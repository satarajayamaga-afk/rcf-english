O/L English

Vocabulary

A long list of words you cannot use is worth less than fifty words you can. This page is about the second kind.

  1. Home
  2. O/L English
  3. Vocabulary

Knowing a word means four things

Students often say they "know" a word when they only recognise it. To use a word in the examination you need all four of these.

  1. Meaning — what it refers to.
  2. Spelling — because a misspelt word in your writing costs marks.
  3. Grammar — is it a noun, a verb, an adjective? He is a success. He succeeded. He is successful. Choosing the wrong form is a very common error.
  4. Company — which words go with it. We make a mistake but do homework; we are interested in, not interested for.

Working out a word you do not know

In the examination there is no dictionary. But a passage usually contains enough evidence to work out an unfamiliar word — and comprehension questions often test exactly this.

  1. Decide what kind of word it is

    Is it naming something, describing something, or doing something? The words around it will tell you. The room was spacious. — after was, describing room, so it is an adjective.

  2. Look for a positive or negative signal

    Although he was tired, he remained cheerful. The word although signals a contrast with tired, so cheerful must be positive.

  3. Look for an explanation nearby

    Writers often explain a difficult word immediately: The village was isolated — far from any town, with no bus service and no shop. The explanation follows the dash.

  4. Look inside the word

    un-, in-, dis-, mis- reverse a meaning: unhappy, incorrect, disagree, misunderstand. -less means without: careless, homeless. -ful means full of: careful, helpful. Knowing twenty prefixes and suffixes unlocks hundreds of words.

  5. Test your guess in the sentence

    Put your guessed meaning back into the sentence. If the sentence now makes sense, your guess is probably close enough to answer the question.

Word building: one root, several words

Examination items often ask for a different form of a given word. Learn words in families rather than singly.

decide (verb)
decision (noun), decisive (adjective), decisively (adverb)
Example: They decided quickly. It was a wise decision.
succeed (verb)
success (noun), successful (adjective), successfully (adverb)
Example: She succeeded. Her success was deserved. She was successful.
explain (verb)
explanation (noun), explanatory (adjective)
Example: He explained the rule. His explanation was clear.
advise (verb)
advice (noun) — note the different spelling and sound
Example: He advised me. His advice was useful.
strong (adjective)
strength (noun), strengthen (verb), strongly (adverb)
Example: A strong wall. The strength of the wall. They strengthened it.
able (adjective)
ability (noun), enable (verb), unable (opposite)
Example: She is able to swim. Her ability is remarkable.

Words that are easily confused

These pairs account for a large share of vocabulary errors in student writing.
WordMeaningExample
advice / adviseadvice is the noun; advise is the verbTake my advice. I advise you to rest.
affect / effectaffect is usually the verb; effect is usually the nounThe rain affected the match. It had a bad effect.
its / it'sits shows possession; it's means it isThe dog wagged its tail. It's raining.
their / there / they'rebelonging to them / in that place / they areTheir house is there. They're inside.
lose / looselose means to no longer have; loose means not tightDo not lose the key. The screw is loose.
practice / practisepractice is the noun; practise is the verbDaily practice helps. Practise every day.
stationary / stationerynot moving / writing materialsThe bus was stationary. Buy stationery.
principal / principlehead of a school, or main / a rule or beliefThe principal spoke. A matter of principle.

A method that actually works

Reading is the only reliable way to build a large vocabulary, but reading alone is slow. Add three habits to it.

  1. Keep a small notebook, not a long list. Five new words a week, each written in a sentence of your own. Fifty words a week that you never use will be gone by the end of the month.
  2. Write the word in a sentence about your own life. The bus was overcrowded this morning will stay with you; overcrowded: too full will not.
  3. Come back to old pages. Look at last week's five words at the start of each new week. Repetition after a gap is what moves a word into permanent memory.
