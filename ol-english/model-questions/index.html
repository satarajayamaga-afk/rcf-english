O/L English

Model Questions

Questions to practise on, arranged by task. Write them by hand, to time, and correct them with the checklist from the matching lesson.

  1. Home
  2. O/L English
  3. Model Questions

How to use these questions

  • Read the lesson for that task first. These questions are for step five — writing independently — not for step one.
  • Write by hand. You will write the examination by hand.
  • Set a timer. Practising without time pressure prepares you for an examination that has it.
  • Correct your own work afterwards with the checklist from the lesson. This matters more than doing another question.

Notices

Ten notice questions
  • Write a notice announcing that the school will be closed for two days for repairs to the electrical system.
  • As secretary of the Science Society, write a notice inviting members to a talk by a visiting speaker.
  • Write a notice informing students that applications are open for the school prefects' board.
  • As librarian, write a notice reminding students to return all borrowed books before the term ends.
  • Write a notice announcing a blood donation campaign to be held at your school.
  • As secretary of the Sports Council, write a notice about trials for the school cricket team.
  • Write a notice informing parents of a meeting to discuss the new school timetable.
  • Write a notice announcing that a mobile telephone found on the premises may be claimed at the office.
  • As president of the Environmental Club, write a notice about a tree-planting programme.
  • Write a notice announcing that the inter-house athletics meet has been postponed, with the new date.

Emails

Ten email questions
  • Write an email to your principal requesting permission to organise an English quiz competition.
  • Write an email to a bookshop enquiring whether a particular title is in stock, its price, and whether it can be delivered.
  • Write an email to your class teacher explaining your absence for three days and asking about missed work.
  • Write an email to a friend inviting them to your sister's wedding, giving the date, place and time.
  • Write an email to the manager of a hotel enquiring about rooms and rates for a school trip.
  • Write an email to the editor of a school magazine submitting an article, and describing what it is about.
  • Write an email to a former teacher thanking them for their help before your examination.
  • Write an email to a company complaining that a delivered item was damaged, and asking for a replacement.
  • Write an email to a cousin abroad describing a festival you recently celebrated.
  • Write an email to your school's old boys' or old girls' association asking how to become a member.

Letters

Ten letter questions
  • Write to the Divisional Secretary about the poor condition of a road used by schoolchildren.
  • Write to the editor of a newspaper about the need for a public library in your area.
  • Write to your principal requesting permission to start a school English newspaper.
  • Write to a shop manager complaining about a faulty item and requesting a refund.
  • Write to the Medical Officer of Health about mosquito breeding in your neighbourhood.
  • Write to a friend who has moved to another town, describing your new class teacher.
  • Write to your uncle thanking him for a gift and telling him your examination plans.
  • Write to a hotel making a reservation for your family, giving dates and requirements.
  • Write to the chief occupant of a house advertised for rent, enquiring about the terms.
  • Write to a younger cousin who is discouraged about studies, encouraging them.

Paragraphs and essays

Twelve paragraph topics
  • The value of reading a newspaper every day
  • Why students should learn to cook
  • A tradition in my family
  • The best advice I have been given
  • Why school sports matter
  • The problem of rubbish in our town
  • A place I would like to visit
  • How the internet has changed studying
  • The person I turn to for help
  • Why kindness costs nothing
  • An old building near my home
  • The importance of drinking clean water
Twelve essay titles
  • The importance of learning English in Sri Lanka today
  • Village life and city life
  • Should students be given fewer examinations?
  • The advantages and disadvantages of social media
  • An experience I shall never forget
  • The person I admire most
  • How our school could be improved
  • Is technology making us lazy?
  • The role of women in society today
  • A day that did not go as planned
  • Should school uniforms be compulsory?
  • The greatest problem facing young people today

Picture description, dialogue and speech

Picture description practice

Because a picture cannot be reproduced here, use pictures from any newspaper, magazine or textbook. Choose ones with several people doing different things, which give you more to organise.

  • A busy market, described in about 100 words
  • A railway station platform
  • A classroom during a lesson
  • A village scene after heavy rain
  • A family preparing for a celebration
  • A road accident scene, described factually
  • A beach on a public holiday
  • Workers at a building site
Ten dialogue questions
  • A student and a librarian about a book that is not available
  • A passenger and a bus conductor about incorrect change
  • Two friends deciding how to spend a free Saturday
  • A customer and a shopkeeper returning a faulty item
  • A student and a class teacher about joining a school society
  • A patient and a receptionist making an appointment
  • A parent and a child about spending too long on the telephone
  • Two students discussing how to prepare for an examination
  • A tourist asking a local resident for directions
  • A student and a bank officer about opening a savings account
Eight speech questions
  • A speech at the opening of your school's English Day
  • A farewell speech to a teacher who is retiring
  • A speech to your school on the importance of reading
  • A speech welcoming a new principal
  • A speech at a prize-giving, thanking parents and teachers
  • A speech on protecting the environment, to a school assembly
  • A speech encouraging younger students before their examination
  • A speech at a school society's annual general meeting, reviewing the year
