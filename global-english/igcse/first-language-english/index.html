Cambridge International

Cambridge IGCSE First Language English

Two papers of two hours, eighty marks each, weighted fifty-fifty. One tests reading, the other tests writing, and the second can be replaced by coursework.

Paper 1: ReadingPaper 2: Writing
  1. Home
  2. Global English
  3. IGCSE English
  4. Cambridge IGCSE First Language English

Where these figures come from

Every figure on this page was read from Cambridge International's own syllabus documents: the 0500 syllabus for 2027, 2028 and 2029 and the 0500 syllabus for 2024, 2025 and 2026.

The question-by-question detail comes from the 2027-2029 syllabus only. Paper 1 was restructured for 2027 and Paper 2 Section A was divided into two parts, so a candidate sitting in 2026 should check the 2024-2026 syllabus for the paper they will actually be given. The component table, the durations, the weightings and the mark totals are the same in both.

Cambridge materials are linked, never copied. RCF English is not affiliated with, endorsed by or approved by Cambridge Assessment International Education.

This is not the same qualification as English as a Second Language

First Language English (0500 and 0990) is for students who use English as a first language. It assesses reading and writing only, and expects a first-language command of style.

English as a Second Language (0510, 0511, 0991, 0993) assesses reading, writing, listening and speaking for learners using English alongside another first language.

They are separate syllabuses with separate papers, and a school enters a candidate for one, not both - Cambridge lists them among the syllabuses that cannot be combined in a single examination series. Revision material written for one will mislead you if you are sitting the other.

The components

All candidates take Paper 1, and then either Paper 2 or Component 3. The school decides which. Component 4 is optional and does not count towards the grade.
ComponentTimeMarksWeightingWhat it is
Paper 1: Reading2 hours8050%Structured and extended writing questions on three reading texts. Externally assessed. Every candidate takes this.
Paper 2: Directed Writing and Composition2 hours8050%An extended writing question and a composition task. Externally assessed.
or Component 3: Coursework Portfolio-8050%Three extended writing assignments. Internally assessed, externally moderated.
Component 4: Speaking and Listening TestAbout 10-12 minutes-Separately endorsedOptional. Marks do not contribute to the overall grade; achievement is reported as grades 1 (high) to 5 (low) on the certificate.

The one decision that changes how you prepare

Paper 2 and Component 3 are worth the same and cover much the same writing skills, but they are not the same experience. Paper 2 is two hours of examination writing with a directed task and a composition chosen from four titles. Component 3 is three assignments of 500 to 800 words each, written over time and marked in your school.

This is a decision your school makes at entry, not one you make in the examination hall. Find out which one you are entered for before you spend a term revising for the wrong one - the composition practice transfers, but the timing practice does not.

The assessment objectives

Ten objectives, five for reading and five for writing. They are worth learning by number, because the syllabus attaches them to individual questions and they tell you exactly what each question is buying.

Reading

  • R1 demonstrate understanding of explicit meanings
  • R2 demonstrate understanding of implicit meanings and attitudes
  • R3 analyse, evaluate and develop facts, ideas and opinions, using appropriate support from the text
  • R4 demonstrate understanding of how writers achieve effects and influence readers
  • R5 select and use information for specific purposes

Writing

  • W1 articulate experience and express what is thought, felt and imagined
  • W2 organise and structure facts, ideas and opinions for deliberate effect
  • W3 use a range of vocabulary and sentence structures appropriate to context
  • W4 use language appropriate to purpose and to engage the audience
  • W5 make accurate use of spelling, punctuation and grammar

What the numbering is actually for

R1 and R2 are the difference between what a text says and what it means, and almost every reading question tests both. R4 - how writers achieve effects - is the one students most often leave out, and on Paper 1 it carries part of two separate questions.

On the writing side, W5 is spelling, punctuation and grammar, and it is one of five objectives on every writing task. It is the easiest of the ten to lose and the only one you can protect by leaving five minutes at the end.

The two written papers

Paper 1: Reading

Four questions of twenty marks each, on three texts. Comprehension, summary, a language task and an extended response.

  • 2 hours, 80 marks, 50%
  • Three reading texts: A, B and C
  • Two of the four questions include writing marks
Open Paper 1

Paper 2: Directed Writing and Composition

A directed writing task built on one or two short texts, and a composition chosen from four titles.

  • 2 hours, 80 marks, 50%
  • Section A 40 marks, Section B 40 marks
  • Two descriptive and two narrative titles
Open Paper 2

Component 3: the Coursework Portfolio

The alternative to Paper 2, worth the same 80 marks and the same 50%. Candidates submit three assignments of about 500 to 800 words each, which may be completed in any order.

  • Assignment 1 (30 marks): writing to discuss, argue and/or persuade, in response to a text or texts.
  • Assignment 2 (25 marks): writing to describe.
  • Assignment 3 (25 marks): writing to narrate.
  • It is internally assessed and externally moderated, which means your teacher marks it and Cambridge checks the marking.
  • Assignment 1 is the only one of the three that carries reading marks, because it responds to a text. The other two are assessed on writing alone.

Component 4 counts, but not towards your grade

The Speaking and Listening Test lasts about 10 to 12 minutes and is optional. Its marks do not contribute to the grade you receive for the written components.

Instead, where a candidate performs to an appropriate standard, the certificate records achievement as grades 1 (high) to 5 (low), separately endorsed alongside the main grade. It is worth doing and it is not worth revising at the expense of Paper 1.

What changes for 2027

The first examinations on the new syllabus are in 2027. The shape of the qualification does not change: the same two papers, the same two hours each, the same 80 marks each, the same 50/50 weighting, the same coursework alternative and the same optional speaking test.

What changes is inside the papers.

Paper 1

  • The total mark is unchanged and the duration is unchanged.
  • The paper has been restructured so that candidates can navigate it more efficiently and use their time better.
  • The questions are still based on three reading texts.

Paper 2

  • The total mark is unchanged and the duration is unchanged.
  • Section A: the reading texts are shorter - the total word count has been reduced to 550 to 650 words.
  • The directed writing task has been divided into two parts, 1(a) and 1(b), to help candidates with their comprehension of the text or texts.
  • Question 1(a) assesses R3 and R4, and R4 was not previously assessed in this paper.
  • Question 1(b) carries 10 marks for reading and 25 for writing.
  • Section B is unchanged in what it asks for.
  • The marking criteria have been updated across both sections.

Components 3 and 4

  • Component 3: the forms in which candidates write have been updated and clarified. There are no changes to the mark allocations. The marking criteria have been updated.
  • Component 4: no changes to the tasks or mark allocations. The marking criteria have been updated.

If you are sitting in November 2026

You are on the 2024-2026 syllabus, not the one described question by question on the paper pages here. The components, the timings, the marks and the weightings are identical, so everything on this page applies to you.

What may not apply is the internal structure of Paper 1 and the two-part directed writing task on Paper 2. Check the 2024-2026 syllabus and practise on past papers from your own series rather than on specimen material for 2027.

0500 and 0990: which code is yours

Cambridge publishes First Language English under two codes. The syllabus content is the same; the grading scale is not.

0500 is graded **A* to G, which the syllabus states directly. 0990 is the version graded 9 to 1**. Which one a school enters candidates for depends on the scale it wants on the certificate rather than on any difference in what is taught.

Check your entry code on your statement of entry. If it is 0990, use the 0990 syllabus page on the Cambridge site for the grading information, since this page was built from the 0500 syllabus documents.
