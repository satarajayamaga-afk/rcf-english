Articles in Academic Writing ESL Lesson Plan (C1, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute C1 lesson on Articles in Academic Writing, built round the theme "Urbanisation in Asia".

About this lesson

Who it is for. Advanced students preparing for university or IELTS. Their academic writing is fluent but contains frequent article errors with abstract nouns and of-phrases (The globalisation has changed ..., Impact of technology is ...).

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis, the timed stages with the teacher's own words and instruction-checking questions, the differentiation, the printable handouts and the answer key.

At a glance

Level
CEFR C1 (Advanced)
Learners
Teenagers and adults
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, run "Freer practice" once through without the second round and cut "Feedback and wrap-up" to board-based error correction.
Skills
Writing
Language focus
Grammar: Articles in Academic Writing and Fixed Expressions
Framework
PPP (presentation, practice, production)
Materials
Handout 1 (fixed expressions warm-up), Handout 2 (two paragraphs for analysis), Handout 3 (practice and editing checklist), Handout 4 (writing task: data and paragraph brief).
Preparation
About 10 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 4, one per learner, and read the language analysis. The answer key is for you, not the class.

Aims

Main aim. To improve accuracy with articles in academic writing: abstract nouns in general vs specific use, nouns followed by of-phrases, pre-modified nouns, and fixed expressions.

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can write and self-edit a 150-word academic paragraph with accurate article use.

  • Subsidiary aim. To develop collaborative academic writing and peer editing.
Advertisement

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Key patterns

PatternExample
Abstract or uncountable noun in general: no articleØ Education is essential. Ø Technology is changing fast.
Abstract noun made specific (by of-phrase, relative clause or context): thethe education of girls · the technology that we use
Noun + of-phrase usually takes thethe role of women · the impact of tourism · the growth of cities
Pre-modified noun, general: no articleØ rapid urban growth · Ø online learning
Countable singular noun always needs an articlea / the study, a / the survey (not Study shows ...)
Plural general: no articleØ Studies show that ... Ø Young people ...
Fixed expressionsin the long run · on the whole · at first · by chance · in hospital · at sea · in the meantime · on the other hand · for the time being
Emphatic the (spoken)That's THE best curry in Colombo. /ðiː/

Pronunciation

  • Emphatic the: /ðiː/ with stress.

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
the with general abstract nouns.General vs specific contrast pairs in analysis.
Missing the before noun + of.Highlight of-phrases in the model text.
Bare singular countable nouns (Study shows ...).Include in the editing checklist.
Students think both options are always possible.Discuss genuine cases where both are possible with a change of meaning.

CEFR C1 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults

Procedure: Articles in Academic Writing, step by step

Warm-up5 minutes · Pairs, then whole class

Aim: To activate awareness of articles in fixed expressions.

Give out Handout 1. Pairs complete the five fixed expressions with a, an, the or no article (2 minutes).

Check quickly. Board the expressions on the left.

Presentation20 minutes · Pairs, whole class

Aim: To analyse article use in academic writing and clarify the key patterns.

Part A: Analysis (9 minutes)

Give out Handout 2. Say: "Paragraph A has article errors. Paragraph B is corrected. Find at least ten differences and explain each one."

ICQs: "Which paragraph has errors?" (A.) "Do you just find the differences?" (No, explain them too.)

Pairs work. Monitor and help with explanations.

Part B: Clarification (11 minutes)

Feedback. Build the patterns in the centre of the board:

general abstract or uncountable nouns → no article (Urbanisation, economic growth, housing);

nouns followed by of → usually the (the proportion of, the rapid growth of);

singular countable nouns always need an article (a recent study);

organisations and superlatives (the United Nations, the most important);

fixed expressions (in the long run).

Contrast pairs: Education is essential / the education of girls; Technology changes fast / the technology that we use.

Discuss cases where both are possible with a change of meaning: Research shows ... / The research shows ... (general research vs a specific study already mentioned).

Controlled practice10 minutes · Individual, then pairs, then whole class

Aim: To practise accurate article use.

Give out Handout 3. Say: "Exercise A: correct the article errors. Some sentences are correct. Six minutes."

Pairs check. Check as a class, asking for the rule each time.

Read through the editing checklist (Exercise B) together.

Freer practice18 minutes · Pairs

Aim: To write an academic paragraph with accurate article use and self-edit it.

Give out Handout 4. Say: "Write an academic paragraph (about 150 words) describing the data and discussing one effect of rapid urban growth." (11 minutes.)

ICQs: "How many words?" (About 150.) "Formal or informal?" (Formal, academic.)

Pairs use the editing checklist (Handout 3, Exercise B) to edit their own paragraph and mark every change (4 minutes).

Pairs swap with another pair and check each other's articles (3 minutes).

Monitor and note common errors.

Feedback and wrap-up7 minutes · Whole class

Aim: To give feedback and consolidate.

Two pairs read their paragraphs. Board two or three sentences with interesting article choices; the class decides if they are correct.

Ask: "Which pattern do you most need to check in your writing?"

Set the homework.

Homework Write a 250-word discursive paragraph on a social issue and self-edit for articles using the checklist.

PPP (presentation, practice, production). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Provide an article decision checklist card. Give a paragraph frame for the writing task.
Stretch
Analyse article choices in a real journal abstract. Explore further cases where both options are possible with a change of meaning.

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Keep the pairwork in "Freer practice" in fixed pairs rather than a mingle, and take feedback from four or five pairs instead of the whole room. Monitor one half of the room during "Controlled practice" and the other half during "Freer practice".
A class with no photocopierPut the exercises from Handout 3: Practice and Editing Checklist on the board for learners to copy into their notebooks, and read any text aloud twice instead of printing it. The board plan above is this lesson's minimum - with that on the board, it runs on no paper at all.
An online classSend Handouts 1 to 4 in the chat before the lesson and put the board plan on a shared slide. Use breakout rooms for the pairwork in "Freer practice" and visit each room once. Have learners type the controlled answers in the chat, so you see every answer at once instead of nominating.
A different age groupWith teenagers at this level, cut the academic framing and keep the examples in their own world; the forms do not change. With adults in a work context, swap the lesson's texts for ones from their own field and keep the analysis as it stands.

Checking learning

The final paragraph has no more than one article error. Students can explain each correction using the patterns. 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

Teacher's reflection

Which pattern caused the most errors in the writing? Did self-editing improve the paragraphs?

Handout 1: Fixed Expressions

Complete with a, an, the or – (no article).

  • In ______ long run, renewable energy will be cheaper.
  • On ______ whole, the results were positive.
  • At ______ first, nobody believed the study.
  • We met by ______ chance at a conference.
  • He's been in ______ hospital since Monday.

Handout 2: Two Paragraphs

Paragraph A (contains article errors)

The urbanisation is one of most important trends in the Asia today. According to the recent study by United Nations, proportion of people living in cities will rise significantly by 2050. Main reason for this is the economic growth. In China, for example, the urban population more than doubled between 1990 and 2020. However, the rapid growth of cities creates the serious problems. Traffic congestion and the air pollution are major concerns, and the housing is becoming unaffordable for young people. In long run, governments will need to invest in the public transport.

Paragraph B (corrected)

Urbanisation is one of the most important trends in Asia today. According to a recent study by the United Nations, the proportion of people living in cities will rise significantly by 2050. The main reason for this is economic growth. In China, for example, the urban population more than doubled between 1990 and 2020. However, the rapid growth of cities creates serious problems. Traffic congestion and air pollution are major concerns, and housing is becoming unaffordable for young people. In the long run, governments will need to invest in public transport.

Find at least ten differences and explain each one.

Handout 3: Practice and Editing Checklist

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

A. Correct the article errors. Some sentences may be correct.

B. Editing checklist (use it for your paragraph on Handout 4)

  • The globalisation has changed the way we live. ______________________________
  • Impact of social media on teenagers is significant. ______________________________
  • In long run, the policy will fail. ______________________________
  • Research shows that the unemployment is falling. ______________________________
  • The history of the Sri Lanka is fascinating. ______________________________
  • Study by Harvard found that sleep improves memory. ______________________________
  • The role of the government is to protect citizens. ______________________________
  • On the whole, students preferred online learning. ______________________________
  • The education of girls has improved since 1990. ______________________________
  • Young people are leaving the villages for the cities. (in general) ______________________________
Check✓
General abstract / uncountable nouns have no article
Nouns followed by of usually have the
No bare singular countable nouns (a study / the study)
Country names have no article (except the USA, the UK, etc.)
Fixed expressions are correct (in the long run, on the whole)
Superlatives have the

Handout 2 (differences)
