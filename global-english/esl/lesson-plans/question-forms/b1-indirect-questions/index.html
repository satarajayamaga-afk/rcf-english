Indirect Questions ESL Lesson Plan (B1, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute B1 lesson on Indirect Questions, built round the theme "At the Tourist Information Desk".

About this lesson

Who it is for. Intermediate students who form direct questions accurately. When they try to be polite with strangers they keep question word order and auxiliaries (Could you tell me where is the station? Do you know what time does the bus leave?) or sound too direct.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis, the timed stages with the teacher's own words and instruction-checking questions, the differentiation, the printable handouts and the answer key.

At a glance

Level
CEFR B1 (Intermediate)
Learners
Teenagers and adults
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, run "Freer practice" once through without the second round and cut "Feedback and wrap-up" to board-based error correction.
Skills
Speaking and writing
Language focus
Grammar: Indirect Questions (Could you tell me where ...? Do you know if ...?)
Framework
PPP (presentation, practice, production)
Materials
Handout 1 (two dialogues: direct and polite), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (role cards and information sheets).
Preparation
About 10 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 4, one per learner, and read the language analysis. The answer key is for you, not the class.

Aims

Main aim. To help students work out and use indirect questions (Could you tell me / Do you know / I was wondering / Can you tell me + wh- word or if / whether + statement word order) for polite enquiries.

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can make polite enquiries as tourists and staff, e.g. Could you tell me what time the train leaves? Do you know if the museum is open on Sundays? I was wondering whether you have a map.

  • Subsidiary aim. To practise polite intonation.
  • Subsidiary aim. To develop fluency in a service-encounter role-play.

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Form

Direct questionIndirect question
Where is the station?Could you tell me where the station is?
What time does the bus leave?Do you know what time the bus leaves?
Is the museum open?Do you know if / whether the museum is open?
Can I pay by card?I was wondering if I could pay by card.
How much did it cost?Can you tell me how much it cost?

Form: notes

  • Opening phrases: Could you tell me ...? Can you tell me ...? Do you know ...? Do you have any idea ...? I was wondering ... · I'd like to know ...
  • After the opening phrase: statement word order (subject + verb); no do / does / did.
  • Yes / no questions: if or whether.
  • The question mark depends on the opening: Could you tell me ...? (question) but I was wondering if ... (statement, full stop).

Meaning and use

  • Indirect questions are more polite and less direct; they are used with strangers, in shops and offices, and in formal situations.

Pronunciation

  • Polite intonation: a wide pitch range and a gentle rise: Could you tell me where the station is? ↗
  • Could you /kʊdʒə/; Do you know /djə nəʊ/.

Concept-checking questions

  • Could you tell me where the station is? · Is this more or less polite than Where is the station? (More polite.) · Where is the verb is: before or after the station? (After.) · Do you know if the museum is open? · Is this a yes / no question or a wh- question? (Yes / no.) Which word do we add? (if.)

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
Question word order after the opening (where is the station).Board an arrow moving the verb back after the subject.
Keeping do / does / did (what time does the bus leave).Cross out does and add -s to the verb.
Missing if / whether.Rule: no question word → if / whether.
Wrong punctuation (I was wondering if ...?).Show that the punctuation follows the opening phrase.
Flat, impolite intonation.Drill with exaggerated pitch range.

Board plan

Left: opening phrasesCentre: patternsRight: pronunciation
Could you tell me ...? · Can you tell me ...? · Do you know ...? · Do you have any idea ...? · I was wondering ... · I'd like to know ...
Where is the station? → Could you tell me where the station is? · What time does the bus leave? → Do you know what time the bus leaves? (no does) · Is it open? → Do you know if / whether it's open? · not: where is the station ✗ (after the opening)
Could you /kʊdʒə/ · Could you tell me where the station is? ↗ (wide pitch, polite)

CEFR B1 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults

Procedure: Indirect Questions, step by step

Warm-up5 minutes · Whole class, pairs

Aim: To introduce the topic of politeness.

Say two questions to the class, one rudely and one politely: "Where's the toilet?" and "Excuse me, could you tell me where the toilet is?"

Ask: "Which is more polite? Why?" Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Take ideas.

Presentation20 minutes · Individual, pairs, whole class

Aim: To present indirect questions in context and help students work out the rules.

Part A: Reading (6 minutes)

Give out Handout 1. Say: "Read the two dialogues at the tourist information desk in Kandy. Which tourist gets better help? Why?" (3 minutes.)

Pairs compare. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)

Give out Handout 2. Say: "Compare the direct and indirect questions. Answer the questions with your partner."

ICQs: "Which dialogue has the indirect questions?" (Dialogue 2.)

Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: "Where is the verb? Where is does?"

Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)

Elicit the answers and build the patterns in the centre of the board.

Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.

Highlight: statement word order; no do / does / did; if / whether; punctuation.

Pronunciation. Drill polite intonation with a wide pitch range: Excuse me, could you tell me where the station is? Do you know if the museum's open today?

Controlled practice10 minutes · Individual, then pairs, then whole class

Aim: To practise the form accurately.

Give out Handout 3. Say: "Exercise A: make the questions polite. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."

Pairs check and read their questions aloud with polite intonation. Check as a class.

Freer practice18 minutes · Pairs, then whole class

Aim: To use indirect questions fluently in a service role-play.

Give out Handout 4. Student A is a tourist with a list of things to find out; Student B works at the tourist information desk and has an information sheet. Tourists must use a different opening phrase for each question (7 minutes).

ICQs: "Can the tourist use 'Where is ...?'" (No, use indirect questions.) "How many different openings?" (A different one each time.)

Swap roles with the second set of cards (7 minutes).

Two pairs perform for the class. The class listens for indirect questions and polite intonation (4 minutes).

Monitor and note errors.

Feedback and wrap-up7 minutes · Whole class

Aim: To give feedback and consolidate.

Board questions from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.

Ask: "What two things change in an indirect question?" (Word order; no do / does / did.)

Set the homework.

Homework Write a polite email (120 words) to a hotel asking at least five questions about your stay. Use five different indirect question openings.

PPP (presentation, practice, production). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Give weaker students the opening phrases on a card. Let them use only Could you tell me ...? and Do you know if ...?
Stretch
Stronger students use I was wondering whether and Would you happen to know ...? They answer politely with I'm afraid I don't know whether ...

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Keep the pairwork in "Freer practice" in fixed pairs rather than a mingle, and take feedback from four or five pairs instead of the whole room. Monitor one half of the room during "Controlled practice" and the other half during "Freer practice".
A class with no photocopierPut the exercises from Handout 3: Practice on the board for learners to copy into their notebooks, and read any text aloud twice instead of printing it. Handout 4: Role Cards works read out from the front: learners note what they need rather than holding a card. The board plan above is this lesson's minimum - with that on the board, it runs on no paper at all.
An online classSend Handouts 1 to 4 in the chat before the lesson and put the board plan on a shared slide. Use breakout rooms for the pairwork in "Freer practice" and visit each room once. Have learners type the controlled answers in the chat, so you see every answer at once instead of nominating.
A different age groupWith teenagers, shorten the analysis and get to the task sooner. With adults in a work context, keep the forms exactly as they are and swap the lesson's theme for one from their own field.

Checking learning

In the role-play, students use statement word order in indirect questions. Students use if / whether for yes / no questions. 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

Teacher's reflection

Did students drop do / does in indirect questions? Did their intonation sound polite?

Handout 1: At the Tourist Information Desk

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

Dialogue 1

Tourist 1: Where is the Temple of the Tooth? Assistant: Over there. Ten minutes' walk. Tourist 1: What time does it close? Assistant: 8 pm. Tourist 1: Can I take photos? Assistant: No.

Dialogue 2

Tourist 2: Good morning. Could you tell me where the Temple of the Tooth is? Assistant: Of course! It's just next to the lake, about ten minutes' walk from here. I'll show you on the map. Tourist 2: Thank you. Do you know what time it closes? Assistant: It's open until 8 pm. The evening ceremony is at 6.30. It's beautiful. Tourist 2: Lovely. I was wondering if I could take photos inside. Assistant: You can take photos in most areas, but not in the inner shrine. And please remember to cover your shoulders and knees. Tourist 2: That's very helpful. Do you have any idea how much a tuk-tuk to the botanical gardens costs? Assistant: About 1,000 rupees. Enjoy your day!

Answer the questions.

  • Which tourist gets more information? Why? ______________________________
  • What time is the evening ceremony? ______________________________
  • Where can't visitors take photos? ______________________________

Handout 2: Language Focus

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

Compare the questions.

Answer the questions with your partner.

  • In the indirect questions, does the verb come before or after the subject? ______________________________
  • What happens to does? What happens to the main verb? ______________________________
  • Can I take photos? has no question word. Which word is added in the indirect question? ______________________________
  • Why does I was wondering if I could take photos end with a full stop, not a question mark? ______________________________

Common errors to watch for

  • Tourist 2, because they ask politely and the assistant is more helpful. · 2. At 6.30. · 3. In the inner shrine.
  • After the subject (statement word order). · 2. does disappears; the main verb takes -s (closes). · 3. if · 4. Because the opening I was wondering is a statement, not a question.
  • where the bank is · 2. what time the film starts · 3. there is a pharmacy near here · 4. how much this costs · 5. when the train left · 6. if / whether I could park here · 7. why the shop is closed · 8. if / whether this bus goes to Galle
  • Could you tell me where the post office is? · 2. Do you know what time the museum opens? · 3. I was wondering if you could help me. · 4. Do you know if / whether the restaurant is open?
