Present Simple ESL Lesson Plan (A1, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute lesson on the present simple for A1 classes, built on the PPP framework, with photocopiable handouts and an answer key.

  • CEFR A1
  • 60 minutes
  • Teenagers and adults; works with young learners too
  • Grammar
  • Present Tenses
  • Present Simple

About this lesson

Beginners who know the verb be, numbers, telling the time (o'clock, half past) and basic daily verbs. They may have met the present simple but cannot yet use the third person -s accurately.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis to read before you teach, a stage-by-stage procedure with timings and interaction patterns, and the handouts and answer key at the end, ready to print.

At a glance

Level
A1 (Beginner)
Learners
Teenagers and adults; works with young learners too
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, cut the interview in Stage 5 to eight minutes and set the six written sentences as homework.
Skills
Reading and speaking, with some writing
Language focus
The present simple for routines and habits: form, third-person -s, and the question and negative with do/does
Framework
PPP (Presentation, Practice, Production)
Materials
Handout 1 (Nimal's Day text and activity table), Handout 2 (controlled practice), Handout 3 (interview grid), board, markers in two colours, optional clock face.
Preparation
About 10 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 3, one each per learner.

Aims

Main aim. To present and practise the present simple (affirmative, negative and questions) for daily routines, with a focus on the third person -s.

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can ask and answer questions about daily routines and describe another person's day in 5–6 accurate sentences.

  • Subsidiary aim. To practise speaking (asking and answering about routines) and listening for specific information.
  • Subsidiary aim. To review times and daily routine vocabulary.

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Form

I / You / We / TheyHe / She / It
PositiveI get up at 6.30.She gets up at 6.30.
NegativeI don't (do not) eat breakfast.He doesn't (does not) eat breakfast.
QuestionDo you walk to school?Does she walk to school?
Short answersYes, I do. / No, I don't.Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

Form: notes

Spelling rules for he / she / it:

  • Most verbs add -s: work → works, play → plays, get → gets.
  • Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -s, -x, -o add -es: watch → watches, wash → washes, go → goes, do → does.
  • Consonant + y → -ies: study → studies. (Vowel + y just adds -s: play → plays.)
  • Irregular: have → has.

Meaning and use

We use the present simple for habits and routines (things we do regularly) and for facts. Time expressions: every day, on Mondays, at 7 o'clock, in the morning, in the evening, at night.

Pronunciation

The -s ending has three sounds:

In questions, do you is often weak and joined: /dəjə/. Short answers are stressed: Yes, I DO.

  • /s/ after voiceless sounds: works, gets, eats, walks
  • /z/ after voiced sounds: plays, goes, comes, reads, has /hæz/
  • /ɪz/ after /s, z, ʃ, tʃ, dʒ/: watches, washes, finishes, relaxes

Concept-checking questions

  • Use these for the sentence Nimal gets up at 6.30. · Is this today only? (No.) · Is it every day? (Yes.) · Is it a habit? (Yes.) · Who gets up? Nimal: is he I, you or he? (He.) So what do we add? (-s.)

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
Students forget the -s with he / she (He work in a bank).Highlight -s in a second colour on the board. Use a gesture (a finger drawing an "s" in the air) to prompt self-correction throughout the lesson.
Students add -s after doesn't (She doesn't works).Show that the -s "moves" to does: draw an arrow from works to does on the board.
Students use be with the main verb (I am go to school).Contrast I am a student (be) with I go to school (action verb, no am).
Students struggle with times.Draw a clock on the board in the lead-in and review o'clock, half past, quarter past / to.
Mixed-level class: stronger students finish early.Fast finishers write three extra sentences using usually, always, never (see Differentiation).

I get up at 6.30. / Nimal gets up at 6.30. / He doesn't eat breakfast. / Do you walk? Yes, I do. / Does he walk? No, he doesn't.

CEFR A1 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults; works with young learners too

Procedure: Present Simple, step by step

Lead-in5 min · T → Ss, whole class

Aim: To engage students, introduce the topic of daily routines and activate vocabulary.

Draw a large clock on the board showing 6.30. Say: "This is my morning. Look!" Mime waking up and stretching. Ask: "What am I doing?" Elicit or teach get up.

Mime four more actions in order: have breakfast, go to work, have lunch, go to bed. After each mime, students call out the phrase. Write each phrase on the left of the board.

Ask two or three students: "What time do you get up?" Accept short answers (Six o'clock). Do not correct grammar at this stage.

Transition: "Now let's meet Nimal. He is a student in Kandy."

Presentation, context8 min · Individual, then pairs, then whole class

Aim: To present the target language in a meaningful context and check general understanding.

Give out Handout 1. Point to the table. Say: "These are six things Nimal does every day. They are not in order."

Task 1 (gist): "Read the text. Number the activities 1 to 6. You have three minutes. Work alone."

ICQs: "Do you work alone or with a partner?" (Alone.) "How many activities?" (Six.) "How many minutes?" (Three.)

Students read and number the activities. Monitor and help with vocabulary if needed.

Students compare answers with a partner (1 minute). Then check as a class: read each activity and ask "What number?"

Task 2 (detail): "Read again. Write the times." Students complete the time column on Handout 1. Check answers by asking: "What time does Nimal get up?" Model the full answer: "He gets up at six thirty."

Presentation, language clarification12 min · T → Ss, whole class

Aim: To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the present simple, focusing on the third person -s.

Meaning. Write on the board: Nimal gets up at 6.30. Ask the CCQs from the language analysis (Is this today only? Is it every day? Is it a habit?).

Form (positive). Write I get up at 6.30. above Nimal's sentence. Ask: "What's different?" Elicit the -s. Underline -s in a second colour. Elicit more examples from the text: goes, has, watches, studies. Ask: "Why goes, not gos?" Write the spelling rules on the board (see Language analysis).

Form (negative). Point to the text: He doesn't eat breakfast at home. Ask: "Is there an -s on eat?" (No.) "Where is it?" (In doesn't / does.) Draw an arrow from eats to does. Contrast with I don't eat breakfast.

Form (questions). Write Does Nimal walk to school? and Do you walk to school? Elicit the short answers. Highlight the word order: Do/Does + subject + verb.

Pronunciation. Say works, plays, watches clearly. Ask students to listen: "Are the endings the same?" Write the three columns /s/, /z/, /ɪz/ on the right of the board. Drill chorally, then individually:

works – he works – he works in a bank

plays – she plays – she plays cricket

watches – he watches – he watches TV

Drill the question and short answer: T: "Does Nimal walk to school?" Ss: "Yes, he does." (Back-chain if needed.)

Transition: "Now let's practise. Take Handout 2."

Practice, controlled10 min · Individual, then pairs, then whole class

Aim: To give students accurate written practice of the form.

Give out Handout 2. Say: "Exercise A: complete the sentences with the verb in brackets. Look at the person: I or he/she? Five minutes. Work alone."

ICQs: "Alone or with a partner?" (Alone.) "Do you need to add -s for I?" (No.)

Monitor. Note common errors on a piece of paper for later.

Students check with a partner (1 minute). Nominate students to read sentences aloud. Write answers on the board. Ask the class to correct any errors.

Exercise B (spelling and sound): students write the he/she form and tick the sound (/s/, /z/ or /ɪz/). Check quickly as a class and drill the trickier ones (finishes, goes, studies).

Production, freer speaking18 min · Pairs, then new pairs, then individual writing

Aim: To give students freer, personalised speaking practice using the target language.

Give out Handout 3. Model first: ask a strong student the first question: "What time do you get up?" Write the answer in the grid on the board. Then show how to report it: "Ayesha gets up at 5.45."

Say: "Ask your partner all the questions. Write the answers in the grid. You have six minutes. Then change: your partner asks you."

ICQs: "Do you write your answers or your partner's answers?" (My partner's.) "Do you show your partner the paper?" (No, ask and listen.)

Students interview each other. Monitor actively: listen for errors with do/does, word order and times. Write errors and good examples on your notes.

Change partners. Say: "Stand up. Find a new partner. Tell your new partner about your first partner." Model: "Ayesha gets up at 5.45. She doesn't have breakfast. She goes to school by bus." (5 minutes.)

Writing (4 minutes): students write 5–6 sentences about their first partner at the bottom of Handout 3. Fast finishers add always, usually, sometimes, never.

Feedback and wrap-up7 min · T → Ss, whole class

Aim: To give feedback on language used in the production stage and consolidate learning.

Ask two or three students to read their sentences aloud. Praise accurate -s endings.

Error correction: write four sentences from your notes on the board, two correct and two incorrect (e.g. She work in a shop. / Does he plays football?). Ask pairs: "Which are correct? Correct the wrong ones." Check as a class.

Good language: board one or two good sentences you heard and praise them.

Set the homework (see below). Ask a final question round the class: "What time does your mother/father get up?" as students leave.

Homework Write 6–8 sentences about a family member's day. Use at least two negatives and one question you would like to ask them. Example: My brother gets up at 7 o'clock. He doesn't eat breakfast. He goes to work by train ...

PPP (Presentation, Practice, Production). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Give a verb bank with the he/she forms already written. Let them use the sentence frames on Handout 3: He/She ___ at ___. Pair them with a supportive partner for the interview.
Stretch
Add frequency adverbs: She usually gets up at 6. He never eats breakfast. (Adverb before the main verb.) Ask two follow-up questions with Why? or How? (How do you go to school?). Write a paragraph comparing their routine with their partner's.

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Keep the interview in Stage 5 in fixed pairs instead of a mingle: learners turn to the person beside them, then to the person behind. Check answers by asking four or five pairs rather than going round the room.
No photocopierWrite Nimal's day on the board as six short sentences and read it aloud twice while learners copy the table headings only. The practice items can be dictated; learners write just the verb.
An online classPut Handout 1 on the screen and use the chat for the tick task so you can see every answer at once. Use breakout rooms of two for the interview, and ask one learner from each room to report.
A different age groupWith young learners, cut the interview to three questions and add a mime round: one child acts a routine, the class says the sentence. With adults, change the routine vocabulary to working life, including shift times.

Checking learning

Students are successful if they can: Check through monitoring in Stage 5 and the written sentences collected at the end. produce the he/she form with -s correctly in at least 4 out of 5 sentences; form Do/Does questions with correct word order; use short answers (Yes, I do / No, she doesn't).

Teacher's reflection

Did students achieve the learning outcome? How do I know? Which stage worked best? Which needed more or less time? Which errors were most common? What will I review next lesson?

Handout 1: Nimal's Day

Name: ___________________________ Date: _____________

Read about Nimal. Number the activities 1–6 in the order Nimal does them. Then write the time for each one.

Answer these questions.

  • Does Nimal eat breakfast at home? ___________________________
  • How does he go to school? ___________________________
  • What does he do in the afternoon? ___________________________

The text

Nimal is 15 years old. He lives in Kandy with his family. He gets up at half past six every day. He doesn't eat breakfast at home. He has a cup of tea and goes to school by bus. School starts at 7.30.

Nimal has lunch at school at 12 o'clock. He usually eats rice and curry. School finishes at 1.30, and he comes home at 2 o'clock. In the afternoon, he plays cricket with his friends.

In the evening, Nimal studies and does his homework. He watches TV at 8 o'clock. His favourite programme is a cricket show. He goes to bed at 10 o'clock.
