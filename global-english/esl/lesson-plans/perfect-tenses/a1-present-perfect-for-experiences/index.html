Present Perfect for Experiences ESL Lesson Plan (A1, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute A1 lesson on Present Perfect for Experiences, built round the theme "Our Experiences".

  • CEFR A1
  • 60 minutes
  • Teenagers and adults

About this lesson

Who it is for. Beginners (high A1) who can use the present simple and the past simple of common verbs. They have not studied the present perfect and cannot ask about life experiences.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis, the timed stages with the teacher's own words and instruction-checking questions, the differentiation, the printable handouts and the answer key.

At a glance

Level
CEFR A1 (Beginner)
Learners
Teenagers and adults
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, run "Freer practice" once through without the second round and cut "Feedback and wrap-up" to board-based error correction.
Skills
Speaking
Language focus
Grammar: Present Perfect for Experiences (Have you ever ...?)
Framework
PPP (presentation, practice, production)
Materials
Handout 1 (speech bubbles), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (class survey), picture cards: a plane, snow, an elephant, the sea, a mountain, a famous building.
Preparation
About 10 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 3, one per learner, and read the language analysis. The answer key is for you, not the class.

Aims

Main aim. To present and practise the present perfect with ever and never to talk about life experiences.

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can ask Have you ever ...?, answer with short answers and say 5 sentences about their own experiences.

  • Subsidiary aim. To learn ten common past participles.
  • Subsidiary aim. To practise short answers.
  • Subsidiary aim. To develop speaking through a class survey.

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Form

PositiveNegativeQuestionShort answers
I / You / We / TheyI have (I've) been to Kandy.I haven't been to India. / I've never been to India.Have you ever been to India?Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.
He / She / ItShe has (She's) eaten durian.He hasn't eaten sushi. / He's never eaten sushi.Has she ever eaten durian?Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't.

Form: notes

have / has + past participle. Common participles:

Meaning and use

  • We use the present perfect for experiences in our life up to now. The exact time is not important and is not said.
  • ever = at any time in your life (in questions). never = at no time (in positive sentences with negative meaning).
  • If we say when, we change to the past simple: I've been to Galle. I went there last year.

Pronunciation

  • Contractions: I've /aɪv/, she's /ʃiːz/, haven't /ˈhævnt/.
  • Have you ever is joined: /həv juː ˈevə/.
  • Short answers are stressed: Yes, I HAVE.

Concept-checking questions

  • I have been to Kandy. Did I go to Kandy? (Yes.) · Do we know when? (No.) · Is the time important? (No, it's about my life.)
  • I've never seen snow. Have I seen snow in my life? (No.)

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
Using the past simple form after have (Have you ever went?).Board the participle table; drill go – went – been.
Using a time word with the present perfect (I have been to Galle last year).Show the rule: time = past simple.
never + negative verb (I haven't never ...).Board: never = not ever; one negative only.
been vs gone confusion.At A1, teach been for experiences only; note gone later.

CEFR A1 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults

Procedure: Present Perfect for Experiences, step by step

Warm-up5 minutes · T → Ss, whole class

Aim: To engage students and introduce the idea of life experiences.

Hold up the picture cards one by one: a plane, snow, an elephant, the sea, a mountain, a famous building.

For each, ask: "Who has seen this? Raise your hand!" Students raise hands. Count them: "Ten students have seen the sea!"

Say: "Interesting! Let's read about some teenagers' experiences."

Presentation20 minutes · Individual, pairs, whole class

Aim: To present the target language in context. To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the present perfect for experiences.

Part A: Context (8 minutes)

Give out Handout 1. Say: "Read the speech bubbles. Match each person to an experience." (3 minutes.)

ICQs: "Do you write or match?" (Match.) "Alone or in pairs?" (Alone.)

Pairs compare. Check as a class.

Ask detail questions: "Has Nadia been to India?" Model the answer: "Yes, she has."

Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)

Meaning. Board I have been to Kandy. Ask the CCQs. Draw a timeline from birth to NOW with a cross somewhere in between and a question mark under it: "When? Not important."

Form. Elicit: have / has + past participle. Highlight has for he/she. Elicit participles from Handout 1 and complete the participle table on the left of the board.

Negative and never. Board I've never flown in a plane and I haven't flown in a plane. Ask: "Same meaning?" (Yes.) Point out: never = no n't.

Questions. Board Have you ever seen snow? Elicit short answers.

The time rule. Board I've been to Galle. I went there last year. Ask: "Which sentence has a time?" (The second.) "Which tense?" (Past simple.)

Pronunciation. Drill: Have you ever been to Jaffna? Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. Use back-chaining: Jaffna – been to Jaffna – ever been to Jaffna – Have you ever been to Jaffna?

Controlled practice10 minutes · Individual, then pairs

Aim: To practise form accurately.

Students complete Handout 2, Exercises A and B (6 minutes).

ICQs: "In Exercise A, do you write have or has?" (Both; look at the person.)

Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers. Drill any difficult participles.

Quick-fire drill: say a base verb; students say the participle (eat → eaten, see → seen).

Freer practice18 minutes · Mingle, then groups, then individual

Aim: To use the language in a personalised speaking task.

Give out Handout 3. Model with a strong student: "Have you ever ridden an elephant?" "No, I haven't." "Have you ever eaten durian?" "Yes, I have." Write the name in the box.

Say: "Stand up. Ask your classmates. Find a name for each question. When someone says yes, ask: When? Use the past simple!" (8 minutes.)

ICQs: "What do you ask when they say yes?" (When?)

Monitor and note errors.

Groups of four compare results and make a class poster: Five students have been to Jaffna. Nobody has flown in a helicopter. (5 minutes.)

Students write five sentences about their own experiences at the bottom of Handout 3 (4 minutes).

Feedback and wrap-up7 minutes · Whole class

Aim: To give feedback and consolidate.

Groups read their posters.

Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Pairs correct.

Set homework.

Homework Write 6 sentences about things you have and haven't done. Write one past simple sentence giving more information about one experience.

PPP (presentation, practice, production). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Give weaker students a participle list during the survey. Allow them to ask the questions on the sheet without follow-up questions.
Stretch
Stronger students ask two follow-up questions in the past simple (When did you go? Who did you go with?). They write a short paragraph: Three things I've never done but want to do.

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Keep the pairwork in "Freer practice" in fixed pairs rather than a mingle, and take feedback from four or five pairs instead of the whole room. Monitor one half of the room during "Controlled practice" and the other half during "Freer practice".
A class with no photocopierPut the exercises from Handout 2: Practice on the board for learners to copy into their notebooks, and read any text aloud twice instead of printing it. The board plan above is this lesson's minimum - with that on the board, it runs on no paper at all.
An online classSend Handouts 1 to 3 in the chat before the lesson and put the board plan on a shared slide. Use breakout rooms for the pairwork in "Freer practice" and visit each room once. Have learners type the controlled answers in the chat, so you see every answer at once instead of nominating.
A different age groupWith young learners, cut the writing at the end and give that time to the speaking task; keep the drilling short, and let them draw or mime where the plan allows it. With adults, keep the writing and ask for a short spoken summary in place of anything drawn or mimed.

Checking learning

Students form Have you ever ...? questions correctly during the survey. Students use the correct participle in 8 of 10 items on Handout 2. Students switch to the past simple when they give a time.

Teacher's reflection

Which participles caused problems? Did students switch to the past simple for details?

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

Read the speech bubbles. Match each person to one experience: India, an elephant, a mountain, the sea, a plane.

Kasun (15): I've ridden an elephant. It was at a festival in Kandy. I've never flown in a plane.

Ayesha (13): I've climbed Adam's Peak with my family. Have you ever climbed a mountain at night? It's amazing!

Ruwan (16): I've swum in the sea many times. I've never eaten sushi, but I want to try it.

Dilini (14): I've flown in a plane. I went to Dubai last year. I've also seen snow on a trip to Japan!
