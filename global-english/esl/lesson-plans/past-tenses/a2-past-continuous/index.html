Past Continuous ESL Lesson Plan (A2, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute lesson on the past continuous for A2 classes, built on the ESA boomerang framework, with photocopiable handouts and an answer key.

  • CEFR A2
  • 60 minutes
  • Teenagers and adults
  • Grammar
  • Past Tenses
  • Past Continuous

About this lesson

Elementary students who use the past simple with reasonable accuracy and know the present continuous. They have not yet learned the past continuous and use the past simple for all past actions.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis to read before you teach, a stage-by-stage procedure with timings and interaction patterns, and the handouts and answer key at the end, ready to print.

At a glance

Level
A2 (Elementary)
Learners
Teenagers and adults
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, keep the alibi game to one round without swapping roles, and halve the preparation time.
Skills
Speaking and reading
Language focus
The past continuous for actions in progress at a past moment and for actions interrupted by the past simple, with when and while
Framework
ESA boomerang (Engage, Activate, Study, Activate)
Materials
Handout 1 (news story), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (alibi role cards and detective question sheet).
Preparation
About 15 minutes: print Handouts 1 and 2 per learner, and one copy of Handout 3 per group of four, cut into the suspects' card and the detectives' sheet.

Aims

Main aim. To clarify and practise the past continuous for actions in progress at a past time and for actions interrupted by a past simple event (when / while).

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can say what they were doing at a specific past time and describe an interrupted action, e.g. I was cooking when the lights went out.

  • Subsidiary aim. To develop fluency in a game-like speaking task (an alibi game).
  • Subsidiary aim. To read a short news story for specific information.
Advertisement

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Form

PositiveNegativeQuestion
I / He / She / ItI was cooking.He wasn't sleeping.Was she working? Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't.
You / We / TheyThey were watching TV.We weren't listening.Were you studying? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.

Meaning and use

  • An action in progress at a specific time in the past: At 8 pm last night, I was watching TV.
  • A longer action interrupted by a shorter one: I was walking home when it started to rain. (past continuous = long action; past simple = short action)
  • when usually goes before the short action; while usually goes before the long action: While she was sleeping, the phone rang.
  • Two actions in progress at the same time: While I was cooking, my brother was doing his homework.

Pronunciation

  • was and were are usually weak in positive sentences: /wəz/, /wə/. I was cooking /aɪ wəz ˈkʊkɪŋ/.
  • They are strong in short answers and negatives: Yes, I was /wɒz/. wasn't /ˈwɒznt/.

Concept-checking questions

  • I was cooking when the lights went out. Which action started first? (Cooking.) · Which action was short? (The lights went out.) · Did the lights going out interrupt the cooking? (Yes.) · Which is in the past continuous? (Cooking.)

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
Missing -ing (I was watch TV).Board formula with three boxes. Finger gesture: three fingers for was – watch – ing.
Using the continuous for both actions (When I was arriving, they were eating).Timeline: one long wavy line, one cross. CCQ: Which is short?
Wrong choice of when / while.Highlight the general pattern on the board; accept when with both in speech.
Alibi game: students forget their story.Give each pair time to write a short alibi plan on the card.

CEFR A2 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults

Procedure: Past Continuous, step by step

Engage7 min · Whole class, then pairs

Aim: To generate interest and get students talking about a past moment.

Turn off the classroom lights (or mime it). Say: "Imagine: last night at 8 o'clock, all the lights in the city went out! A power cut!"

Ask: "Do you remember a power cut? Where were you? How did you feel?" Take two or three answers.

Write 7 o'clock this morning on the board. Students tell a partner what they did this morning (1 minute). Listen; don't correct.

Activate 1, first try8 min · Pairs

Aim: To let students try a communicative task with the language they have, and to diagnose needs.

Say: "Last night at 8 pm, there was a power cut in our town. Ask your partner: what was your family doing at that moment? Imagine if you don't remember! Find out about four people in your partner's family." (4 minutes.)

Monitor without helping. Note: (a) whether students use the past continuous; (b) errors; (c) any good examples.

Ask two or three pairs to report one thing. Say: "Great. Let's read about the real power cut."

Study, reading and language focus12 min · Individual, pairs, whole class

Aim: To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the past continuous with when / while.

Give out Handout 1. Reading task: "Read the news story. Match each person to what they were doing when the lights went out." (3 minutes.) Pairs check, then class feedback.

Noticing: students underline was/were + -ing verbs in one colour and past simple verbs in another.

Meaning. Board Mr Perera was cooking when the lights went out. Draw a timeline. Ask the CCQs.

Form. Elicit the form table: was/were + verb-ing; negative; question. Board: What were you doing at 8 pm?

when / while. Board two sentences from the text with when and while. Ask: "Which word comes before the long action?" (while). "Which comes before the short action?" (when).

Pronunciation. Drill with weak forms: I was cooking. They were watching TV. Then strong short answers: Yes, I was.

Activate 1 errors. Board two or three sentences you heard in Activate 1. Pairs improve them using today's language.

Study, controlled practice9 min · Individual, then pairs

Aim: To practise the form and the contrast with the past simple.

Students complete Handout 2, Exercises A and B (6 minutes).

Pairs compare and justify: "It's the long action, so past continuous."

Check as a class. Drill two or three of the sentences.

Activate 2, the alibi game17 min · Groups of four (two suspects, two detectives)

Aim: To use the language fluently and accurately in a communicative game, repeating the Activate 1 skill at a higher level.

Set up. Read the situation on Handout 3: "Yesterday between 10.00 and 11.00, a birthday cake disappeared from the staff room. Two students are suspects."

Divide each group: two suspects (Pair S) and two detectives (Pair D). Suspects get the alibi card; detectives get the question sheet.

Preparation (4 minutes). Suspects agree on a detailed alibi: where they were, what they were doing at 10.00, 10.15, 10.30 and 10.45, who they saw. Detectives prepare questions using the question sheet.

Interviews (6 minutes). Detectives interview each suspect separately (the other suspect waits out of earshot). Detectives note answers.

Decision (2 minutes). Detectives compare notes: are there differences? They decide: innocent or guilty, and give reasons: "Suspect A said they were reading in the library at 10.30, but Suspect B said they were eating in the canteen."

If time allows, swap roles and play again.

Feedback7 min · Whole class

Aim: To consolidate and correct.

Each group announces its verdict with one reason.

Board four sentences from the game: two correct, two with errors. Students correct them.

Ask: "Was Activate 2 easier than Activate 1? Why?"

Set homework.

Homework Write a paragraph (80–100 words) about where you were and what you were doing during a memorable event (a big match, a storm, a celebration).

ESA boomerang (Engage, Activate, Study, Activate). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Give suspects a time-plan template to complete before the interviews. Weaker detectives can read questions directly from the sheet.
Stretch
Detectives must ask at least two follow-up questions (Exactly what time? Who were you with? What were they wearing?). Stronger suspects add a while sentence: While I was reading, Kamal was sitting next to me.

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Run the alibi game in groups of four at their desks, all at once, and hear only four verdicts. Keep the suspects in the room but seat the second suspect facing away while the first is questioned.
No photocopierRead the power cut report aloud twice while learners match the four people to four activities written on the board. The alibi game needs only the time plan, which learners can rule up in their own books.
An online classSend the second suspect to a waiting room while the first is interviewed, which makes the game work better online than in a classroom. Put the detectives' questions in the chat.
A different age groupWith young learners, make the missing object a classroom one and keep the interview to three questions. With adults, add the follow-up questions about clothing and other people, which is where the contradictions appear.

Checking learning

In Activate 2, students form past continuous questions and answers accurately most of the time. Students choose past continuous vs past simple correctly in 8 of 10 items on Handout 2. Students use when and while in at least one sentence each.

Teacher's reflection

How much did students improve from Activate 1 to Activate 2? Was the alibi game well organised? How could I set it up faster?

Read the news story. Match each person to what they were doing when the lights went out.

True or false?

The text

CITY IN THE DARK

At exactly 8 pm last night, all the lights in the city went out. The power cut lasted for two hours. We asked some people what they were doing.

"I was cooking dinner for my family," said Mr Perera, 45. "The rice was nearly ready. We ate by candlelight!"

Nimali, 16, was doing her homework when the lights went out. "I was writing an essay on my computer. I didn't save it, so I lost everything!"

While the city was sitting in the dark, two doctors at the General Hospital were working with torches. "We were helping a patient, and we didn't stop," said Dr Silva.

Mrs Fernando, 70, wasn't worried. "I was sleeping in my chair. I didn't notice anything!"
