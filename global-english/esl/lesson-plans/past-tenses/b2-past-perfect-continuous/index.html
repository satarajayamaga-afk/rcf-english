Past Perfect Continuous ESL Lesson Plan (B2, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute lesson on the past perfect continuous for B2 classes, built on the TTT framework, with photocopiable handouts and an answer key.

  • 60 minutes
  • Teenagers and adults

  • Past Tenses

About this lesson

Upper-intermediate students who know the past perfect simple but rarely use the past perfect continuous, and confuse the two when talking about causes and duration.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis to read before you teach, a stage-by-stage procedure with timings and interaction patterns, and the handouts and answer key at the end, ready to print.

At a glance

Level
B2 (Upper-intermediate)
Learners
Teenagers and adults
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, let groups explain their scene aloud instead of writing it, and hear two groups.
Skills
Speaking and reading, with some writing
Language focus
The past perfect continuous against the past perfect simple: duration and visible evidence against a completed result, and the stative verbs that stay simple
Framework
TTT (Test, Teach, Test)
Materials
Handout 1 (Test 1), Handout 2 (detective's notes), Handout 3 (language reference and practice), Handout 4 (scene cards for Test 2).
Preparation
About 15 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 3 per learner and one scene card (Handout 4) per group of four. Read Test 1 first so you can note problem items while monitoring.

Aims

Main aim. To diagnose and improve students' use of the past perfect simple and continuous to explain causes, visible results and duration before a past moment.

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can explain a past scene logically, e.g. The floor was wet because it had been raining and someone had left the window open.

  • Subsidiary aim. To develop fluency in speculating and explaining.
  • Subsidiary aim. To practise weak forms of had been.
Advertisement

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Form

Past perfect simplePast perfect continuous
PositiveShe had finished the report.She had been working all night.
NegativeThey hadn't eaten.They hadn't been sleeping well.
QuestionHad you met him before?How long had you been waiting?

Meaning and use

UseExample
Completed action before a past moment (result, number)By 10 pm, he had written three emails.
Activity continuing up to a past moment (duration)She had been working there for ten years when she resigned.
Visible result of a recent activityHis eyes were red because he had been crying.
State verbs: simple onlyI had known him for years. (not had been knowing)

Meaning and use: notes

Time markers: by the time, by (10 pm), for, since, already, just, when, before.

Pronunciation

  • had been is weak and contracted: She'd been working /ʃid bɪn ˈwɜːkɪŋ/.

Concept-checking questions

  • The ground was wet. It had been raining. Was it raining at the moment I looked? (Not necessarily; maybe it had stopped.) · Was the rain before the moment I saw the ground? (Yes.) · Can we see a result? (Yes, the wet ground.)
  • By the time I arrived, they had finished the meeting. Was the meeting finished when I arrived? (Yes.) · Is the focus on the activity or the completion? (Completion.)

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
State verbs in the continuous (had been knowing).Mini state verb list on the board.
Continuous with a number of things (had been writing three emails).Contrast: number = result = simple.
Using past simple instead (He was tired because he ran).Accept as possible in some cases; show that the past perfect makes the order clear.
Test 2 groups produce weak logic.Provide clue prompts on the scene cards.

Board plan

Left: Test 1 problemsCentre: rulesRight: pronunciation
(add the item numbers from Diagnose)
Simple: had + pp → completed result / number: had written three emails · Continuous: had been + -ing → duration / activity / visible evidence: had been raining · State verbs → simple only: had known
She'd been working /ʃid bɪn/ · had been /əd bɪn/

CEFR B2 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults

Procedure: Past Perfect Continuous, step by step

Lead-in5 min · Pairs

Aim: To engage interest with a visual puzzle.

Describe or show a photo: A man arrives at the office. He's soaking wet, exhausted, his shoes are muddy and he's holding a broken umbrella.

Ask: "Why? What had happened to him before he arrived?" Students speculate with a partner (2 minutes). Don't correct.

Take a few ideas.

Test 110 min · Individual

Aim: To diagnose students' current knowledge.

Say: "This is a test for me, not for you: I want to know what you need. Work alone. Seven minutes." Give out Handout 1.

Monitor and record which items many students get wrong.

Diagnose5 min · Pairs, whole class

Aim: To identify the main problem areas.

Pairs compare answers and discuss differences.

Poll the class on each item ("Who has had finished? Who has had been finishing?"). Do not confirm.

Board the item numbers that caused most disagreement.

Teach13 min · Individual, pairs, whole class

Aim: To clarify the problem areas identified.

Context. Give out Handout 2 (detective's notes). Say: "Read the detective's notes. What had the suspect been doing before 10 pm?" (3 minutes.) Check answers.

Noticing. Students underline all past perfect forms and sort them into simple and continuous.

Clarify. Draw timelines for one example of each. Ask the CCQs. Focus especially on the items from the Diagnose stage.

Elicit the rules: result / number → simple; duration / activity / evidence → continuous; state verbs → simple.

Pronunciation. Drill weak forms: She'd been working. They'd been waiting for hours.

Students do Handout 3, Exercise A (4 minutes), and check in pairs.

Give the answers to Test 1. Students mark their own work.

Test 2, deduction game20 min · Pairs, then groups of four, then whole class

Aim: To check improvement through a freer speaking and writing task.

Pairs correct their Test 1 answers (3 minutes).

Give each group a scene card (Handout 4). Say: "You are detectives. Look at the clues. Explain exactly what had happened and what people had been doing before the police arrived. Write your explanation: at least six sentences using both past perfect forms." (10 minutes.)

ICQs: "How many sentences?" (At least six.) "Which forms?" (Both past perfect forms.)

Monitor and help with logic and language.

Groups present their explanation (1 minute each). Other groups challenge: "But if he had been sleeping, why was the coffee hot?"

Class votes for the most convincing explanation.

Feedback7 min · Whole class

Aim: To compare Test 1 and Test 2 and give feedback.

Board sentences heard during the presentations: correct and incorrect. Students correct them.

Ask: "What do you understand now that you didn't understand in Test 1?"

Set homework.

Homework Write the detective's final report (180 words) explaining what had happened in your group's scene.

TTT (Test, Teach, Test). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Give weaker groups the timeline images on their scene cards. Provide sentence starters: The window was open because someone had ... . The floor was wet because ... had been ...
Stretch
Stronger groups include passive forms: The window had been forced. They use modal deductions: He must have been sleeping.

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Poll Test 1 by show of hands and board only the two or three items that split the class. Give each group of four one scene and hear four explanations, not all of them.
No photocopierDictate the eight Test 1 items. Read the detective's notes aloud twice while learners listen for what the suspect had been doing. Describe one scene aloud for the whole class to explain together.
An online classTest 1 by private message. Breakout rooms of four for the deduction task with one scene pasted into each room, then let groups challenge each other's explanations in the main room, which works well on screen.
A different age groupWith teenagers, use the school office scene and keep it light. With adults, the bus station and the villa produce longer explanations; invite them to add a passive form, which comes naturally here.

Checking learning

Fewer errors in Test 2 than in Test 1 on the simple / continuous distinction. Correct use of state verbs in the simple form. 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

Teacher's reflection

Did Test 1 identify the right problems? Was the deduction game motivating?

Handout 1: Test 1

Name: ___________________________ Date: _____________

Complete with the past perfect simple or the past perfect continuous of the verb in brackets.

  • The ground was wet. It ____________________ (rain) all night.
  • By the time I arrived, they ____________________ (finish) the meeting.
  • She was tired because she ____________________ (study) since dawn.
  • I ____________________ (know) him for years before we became friends.
  • They ____________________ (wait) for two hours when the bus finally came.
  • By midnight, he ____________________ (write) four chapters of his book.
  • How long ____________ you ____________________ (live) there before you moved?
  • Her hands were covered in paint. She ____________________ (decorate) her room.

Handout 2: The Detective's Notebook

Name: ___________________________ Date: _____________

Questions

  • What had the suspect been doing before 10 pm? How do we know? ___________________________
  • What had someone done to the lock? ___________________________
  • Why don't the detectives believe his story about the film? ___________________________

The text

Detective Jayasinghe, 11 March, 10.15 pm

When we arrived at the flat at 10 pm, the door was open. Someone had forced the lock. The suspect, Mr Dias, was sitting on the sofa. He was breathing heavily, and his shirt was wet: he had obviously been running.

The kitchen was a mess. Someone had been cooking: there was rice on the floor, and the cooker was still warm. Two glasses were on the table. Mr Dias said he had been watching a film all evening, but the TV was cold. He hadn't been watching anything.

His neighbour told us she had heard shouting at about 9.30. She had been living next door for five years and had never heard anything like it before.

By the end of the interview, Mr Dias had changed his story three times.

Handout 3: Language Focus and Practice

Name: ___________________________ Date: _____________

Language reference

A. Choose the correct form.

B. Write an explanation for each situation using the past perfect continuous.

  • When I got home, my sister had cooked / had been cooking dinner, so we ate immediately.
  • Her eyes were red. She had cried / had been crying.
  • We had walked / had been walking for hours when we found the village.
  • By 2015, he had visited / had been visiting twelve countries.
  • I had known / had been knowing her since primary school.
  • The children were dirty because they had played / had been playing in the garden.
  • She had read / had been reading the book twice before the film came out.
  • How long had you waited / had you been waiting when the doctor arrived?
  • He had a sunburn. → ___________________________
  • The teacher's voice was very tired. → ___________________________
  • The streets were full of water. → ___________________________
Past perfect simple (had + pp)Past perfect continuous (had been + -ing)
Completed action / result: Someone had forced the lock.Activity up to a past moment: He had been running.
Number or quantity: He had changed his story three times.Duration: She had been living there for five years.
State verbs: I had known him for years.Visible evidence: His shirt was wet; he had been running.

Handout 4: Scene Cards (Test 2)

Scene 2: The school office The principal arrives at 7 am. Papers are all over the floor. The computer is on and warm. There's a cup of coffee, still hot. The cleaner is sitting outside, looking nervous. Exam papers are missing.

Scene 3: The wedding hall The guests arrive at 6 pm. The bride is crying. The decorations are only half finished. There are flowers on the floor. The cake has fallen over. The DJ is asleep behind his equipment.

Scene 4: The bus station At 9 pm, a bus arrives three hours late. The passengers look exhausted and angry. The bus is covered in mud. One tyre is new. The driver's clothes are wet.

Common errors to watch for
