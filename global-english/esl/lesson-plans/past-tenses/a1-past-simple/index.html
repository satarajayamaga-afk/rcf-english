Past Simple ESL Lesson Plan (A1, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute lesson on the past simple for A1 classes, built on the PPP framework, with photocopiable handouts and an answer key.

  • Teenagers and adults; works with young learners too

About this lesson

Beginners who can use the present simple for routines and know common activity verbs. They have heard was/were and some past forms but cannot yet form past simple sentences, negatives or questions.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis to read before you teach, a stage-by-stage procedure with timings and interaction patterns, and the handouts and answer key at the end, ready to print.

At a glance

Level
A1 (Beginner)
Learners
Teenagers and adults; works with young learners too
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, cut the mingle in Stage 5 to five minutes and set the six written sentences as homework.
Skills
Reading and speaking, with some writing
Language focus
The past simple of be and of regular and irregular verbs, with the -ed endings and did questions and negatives
Framework
PPP (Presentation, Practice, Production)
Materials
Handout 1 (Sunil's diary), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (Find someone who ...), irregular verb flashcards (go/went, have/had, see/saw, eat/ate, get/got, buy/bought, make/made, do/did).
Preparation
About 15 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 3 per learner and make eight irregular verb flashcards (go/went, have/had, see/saw, eat/ate, get/got, buy/bought, make/made, do/did).

Aims

Main aim. To present and practise the past simple of be and of regular and common irregular verbs to talk about last weekend.

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can say and write 6 sentences about their last weekend and ask a classmate Did you ...? questions.

  • Subsidiary aim. To practise the pronunciation of -ed endings.
  • Subsidiary aim. To develop speaking (asking and answering about past activities).

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Form: be

PositiveNegativeQuestion
I / He / She / ItI was at home.She wasn't tired.Was he happy? Yes, he was. / No, he wasn't.
You / We / TheyThey were at the beach.We weren't late.Were you at school? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't.

Form: other verbs (the same for all persons)

Example
Regular positiveI played cricket. She visited her aunt.
Irregular positiveWe went to Galle. He had lunch. They saw a film.
NegativeI didn't (did not) go out. He didn't watch TV.
QuestionDid you go out? Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
Wh- questionWhere did you go? What did you do?

Form: other verbs (the same for all persons): notes

Spelling of regular verbs:

  • Most verbs add -ed: play → played, watch → watched.
  • Verbs ending in -e add -d: like → liked, dance → danced.
  • Consonant + y → -ied: study → studied (but play → played).
  • One vowel + one consonant: double it: stop → stopped, plan → planned.

Meaning and use

We use the past simple for finished actions at a finished time in the past: last weekend, yesterday, on Saturday, last night, two days ago.

Pronunciation of -ed

  • /t/ after voiceless sounds: walked, watched, cooked, danced
  • /d/ after voiced sounds: played, cleaned, listened, stayed
  • /ɪd/ after /t/ or /d/: visited, wanted, needed, started

Concept-checking questions

  • I played cricket on Saturday. Is Saturday now or in the past? (In the past.) · Is it finished? (Yes.)
  • I didn't go out. Did I go out? (No.) · After didn't, is the verb go or went? (Go.)

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
Regularising irregular verbs (goed, eated).Flashcard drill in the presentation; irregular verb list on Handout 2.
Past form after didn't or did (Did you went?).Board: did + base verb. Gesture: hold up one finger for "only one past".
Pronouncing every -ed as /ɪd/ (play-id).Three-column sound sort; drill minimal examples.
Using was with other verbs (I was go).Contrast I was at home (place/feeling) with I went home (action).

CEFR A1 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults; works with young learners too

Procedure: Past Simple, step by step

Lead-in5 min · T → Ss, whole class

Aim: To engage students and set the context of last weekend.

Write Saturday and Sunday on the board and draw an arrow pointing backwards. Say: "Last weekend. Not this weekend. Last weekend."

Tell the class three things you did, with mime: "On Saturday, I cleaned my house. I went to the market. On Sunday, I watched a film."

Ask: "Did I watch a film?" (Yes.) "Did I go to the beach?" (No.) Students just answer yes or no.

Transition: "Now read about Sunil's weekend."

Presentation, context8 min · Individual, pairs, whole class

Aim: To present the language in context.

Give out Handout 1. Say: "Read Sunil's diary. Tick the things he did. Three minutes."

ICQs: "Do you tick things he did or didn't do?" (Did.)

Students read and tick. Pairs compare. Check answers by asking "Did Sunil go to the beach?" and modelling "Yes, he did."

Students answer the three comprehension questions.

Presentation, language clarification12 min · Whole class

Aim: To clarify meaning, form and pronunciation.

was/were. Board from the text: I was very tired. The beach was beautiful. My cousins were there. Elicit: was for I/he/she/it; were for you/we/they.

Regular verbs. Students underline verbs ending in -ed in the diary. Board: visited, played, walked, cooked, watched, stayed. Elicit the rule: add -ed. Show the spelling rules.

Irregular verbs. Ask: "Is there an -ed on went?" (No.) Board go → went. Use flashcards: show the base form; students call out the past form. Repeat quickly three times.

Negatives. Board I didn't go to the cinema. CCQ: "Did he go?" (No.) "Is it went or go after didn't?" (Go.)

Questions. Board Did you have a good weekend? Yes, I did. and Where did you go? Highlight did + subject + base verb.

Pronunciation. Say walked, played, visited. Ask: "Are the endings the same?" Draw three columns. Students sort the six verbs from the text. Drill chorally and individually.

Practice, controlled10 min · Individual, then pairs

Aim: To practise the form accurately.

Students complete Handout 2, Exercise A (gap fill) and Exercise B (irregular verbs) alone (6 minutes).

Pairs check. Nominate students to read sentences aloud; correct pronunciation of -ed as you go.

Oral drill: say a sentence; students make it negative. T: "I went to the beach." Ss: "I didn't go to the beach." Then: T: "You / go out?" Ss: "Did you go out?"

Production, freer speaking18 min · Mingle, then whole class, then individual

Aim: To use the language in a personalised speaking task.

Give out Handout 3. Model the first item with a strong student: "Did you watch a film last weekend?" "Yes, I did." "What did you watch?" Write the name in the box.

Say: "Stand up. Ask your classmates. Find one name for each box. Ask one more question. You have eight minutes."

ICQs: "Do you ask one person or many people?" (Many.) "What do you write?" (Names.)

Students mingle. Monitor and note errors with Did you ...? and past forms.

Students sit down. Ask several students to report: "Kasun watched a film. He watched an action film." (4 minutes.)

Writing: students write 6 sentences about their own weekend at the bottom of Handout 3 (4 minutes).

Feedback and wrap-up7 min · Whole class

Aim: To give feedback and consolidate.

Board four sentences from your notes, two correct and two incorrect. Students correct them in pairs.

Quick irregular verb review with the flashcards.

Set homework.

Homework Write 6 sentences about your last weekend. Include one sentence with was/were, two irregular verbs and one negative.

PPP (Presentation, Practice, Production). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Give weaker students the verb list with past forms during the mingle. Allow them to answer Yes, I did / No, I didn't without follow-up questions.
Stretch
Stronger students add time phrases (in the morning, after lunch, at night). They ask two follow-up questions with Where? / Who with? / What?

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Replace the Find someone who mingle with a seated version: learners ask the four people nearest them and need only four names, not eight. Drill the irregular verbs chorally by row.
No photocopierTell Sunil's weekend as a story twice while learners tick the activities listed on the board. Dictate the practice items. The flashcards can be words written on the board and rubbed out one at a time.
An online classUse the chat for the gap fill so you see every answer. For the mingle, use breakout rooms of three and ask for two names instead of eight; the follow-up question matters more than the number.
A different age groupWith young learners, keep to six regular verbs plus went and had, and make the sound sort a physical one: learners stand for one ending and sit for another. With adults, use last weekend's real plans and allow longer answers.

Checking learning

Students use correct past forms in 5 of 6 written sentences. Students form Did you ...? questions correctly during the mingle. Students pronounce at least the /ɪd/ endings correctly.

Teacher's reflection

Which irregular verbs caused the most problems? Did the mingle give enough speaking practice for everyone?

The text

Saturday

In the morning, I visited my grandmother in Galle. We had lunch together: she cooked my favourite fish curry. In the afternoon, my cousins and I went to the beach. The beach was beautiful, and the water was warm. We played volleyball and walked along the beach. I was very tired in the evening, so I stayed at home and watched TV.

Sunday

I got up late, at 10 o'clock! I didn't have breakfast. I studied English for two hours, and then I helped my father in the garden. In the evening, my friends wanted to go to the cinema, but I didn't go. I finished my homework and went to bed at 9.

Common errors to watch for

  • ✓ visit his grandmother · ✗ go to the cinema · ✓ play volleyball · ✗ have breakfast on Sunday · ✓ study English · ✓ help his father
  • 1. He went to Galle / visited his grandmother. 2. They played volleyball and walked along the beach. 3. At 10 o'clock.
  • visited · 2. went · 3. didn't watch · 4. Did ... have · 5. were · 6. studied · 7. was · 8. didn't go · 9. did ... eat · 10. stopped ... got
  • /t/: walked, cooked, watched
  • /d/: played, cleaned, stayed
  • /ɪd/: visited, wanted, needed
