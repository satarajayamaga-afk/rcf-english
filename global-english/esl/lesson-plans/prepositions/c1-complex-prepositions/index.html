Complex Prepositions ESL Lesson Plan (C1, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute C1 lesson on Complex Prepositions, built round the theme "A Report to the Principal".

About this lesson

Who it is for. Advanced students whose formal reports rely on because of, about and but. They misuse due to + clause and in spite of + clause.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis, the timed stages with the teacher's own words and instruction-checking questions, the differentiation, the printable handouts and the answer key.

At a glance

Level
CEFR C1 (Advanced)
Learners
Teenagers and adults
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, run "Freer practice" once through without the second round and cut "Feedback and wrap-up" to board-based error correction.
Skills
Speaking and writing
Language focus
Grammar: Complex Prepositions and Prepositional Phrases in Formal Writing
Framework
PPP (presentation, practice, production)
Materials
Handout 1 (informal and formal sentences), Handout 2 (model report for analysis), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (writing task: survey results and report brief).
Preparation
About 10 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 4, one per learner, and read the language analysis. The answer key is for you, not the class.

Aims

Main aim. To develop accurate use of complex prepositions (with regard to, in accordance with, on behalf of, owing to, in spite of, regardless of, by means of) and formal prepositional phrases (under review, at stake, on the grounds that), and preposition placement in formal relative clauses.

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can write a 200-word formal report using at least eight complex prepositions accurately.

  • Subsidiary aim. To write a formal report collaboratively.
  • Subsidiary aim. To develop awareness of register.
Advertisement

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Complex prepositions (+ noun / -ing, not a clause)

FunctionPhrasesExample
Referencewith regard to, regarding, with respect to, as regards, in terms ofWith regard to your email, ...
Causeowing to, due to, on account of, as a result of, in view of, in the light ofOwing to the strike, trains were cancelled.
Contrast / concessionin spite of, despite, regardless of, notwithstanding, as opposed to, in contrast toIn spite of the cost, they bought it.
Means / agencyby means of, on behalf of, in accordance with, with the aid ofI'm writing on behalf of the class.
Addition / alternativein addition to, as well as, instead of, apart fromIn addition to the canteen, ...

Clauses vs phrases

  • Clause after because / although: because it was raining; although it was late.
  • Noun / -ing after complex prepositions: due to the rain; in spite of being late; in spite of the fact that it was late.

Formal phrases

under review, under consideration, in jeopardy, at stake, on the grounds that, in the event of, with a view to + -ing.

Prepositions in relative clauses

  • Informal: the person I spoke to. Formal: the person to whom I spoke.

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
Due to it was rainingBoard: phrase + noun; clause needs because or due to the fact that.
With regards ofCorrect form: with regard to / regarding.
Over-formal, unnatural texts.Discuss balance; limit to one phrase per sentence.
to who in formal relatives.to whom after a preposition.

Board plan

Left: functionsCentre: patternsRight: formal phrases
REFERENCE: with regard to, regarding · CAUSE: owing to, due to, on account of, in the light of · CONTRAST: in spite of, regardless of · MEANS: on behalf of, in accordance with, by means of · ADDITION: in addition to
complex preposition + noun / -ing: owing to the strike · in spite of being late · in spite of the fact that + clause · because / although + clause · the person to whom I spoke
under review · at stake · on the grounds that · with a view to + -ing · in the event of

CEFR C1 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults

Procedure: Complex Prepositions, step by step

Warm-up5 minutes · Pairs, then whole class

Aim: To raise awareness of formal language.

Give out Handout 1. Pairs match the informal sentences to the formal versions (3 minutes).

Check. Ask: "What makes the formal versions formal?" Accept ideas.

Presentation20 minutes · Individual, groups, whole class

Aim: To present complex prepositions and formal prepositional phrases in a model report and clarify their meaning and grammar.

Part A: Reading (5 minutes)

Give out Handout 2. Say: "Read the report. What are the main problems with the canteen? What does the council recommend?"

Pairs compare answers.

Part B: Analysis (7 minutes)

Say: "Find the complex prepositions and put them in the table by function."

ICQs: "How many functions?" (Five columns.) "Alone or in groups?" (Groups.)

Monitor and help.

Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)

Feedback. Build the functions on the left of the board.

Phrase vs clause. Board Due to it was raining ✗. Ask: "What's wrong?" Elicit: complex prepositions take a noun or -ing; clauses need because / although or the fact that.

Show formal relative clauses: the man I wrote to → the man to whom I wrote.

Highlight formal phrases from the report: under review, at stake, with a view to + -ing.

Controlled practice10 minutes · Individual, then pairs, then whole class

Aim: To practise the forms accurately.

Give out Handout 3. Say: "Exercise A: rewrite using the phrase in brackets. Six minutes."

Pairs check. Feedback as a class.

Exercise B: pairs rewrite the informal paragraph formally. One pair reads theirs aloud.

Freer practice18 minutes · Groups of three, then whole class

Aim: To write a formal report using complex prepositions accurately.

Give out Handout 4. Say: "You are the student council. Write a formal report (about 200 words) to the principal about the canteen survey and your recommendations. Use at least eight complex prepositions."

ICQs: "Who is the report for?" (The principal.) "How many complex prepositions?" (At least eight.)

Groups plan (3 minutes) and write (10 minutes).

Two groups read their reports aloud. The class identifies the most formal-sounding phrases (5 minutes).

Monitor and note errors.

Feedback and wrap-up7 minutes · Whole class

Aim: To give feedback and consolidate.

Board strong phrases and two or three errors from the reports. Students correct the errors.

Discuss balance: "Is it possible to be too formal?"

Set the homework.

Homework Write a formal letter of complaint (220 words) using eight complex prepositions.

PPP (presentation, practice, production). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Provide a phrase bank grouped by function. Give a report template with headings.
Stretch
Use on the grounds that, with a view to + -ing, in the event of. Compare phrases in a real policy document.

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Keep the pairwork in "Freer practice" in fixed pairs rather than a mingle, and take feedback from four or five pairs instead of the whole room. Monitor one half of the room during "Controlled practice" and the other half during "Freer practice".
A class with no photocopierPut the exercises from Handout 3: Practice on the board for learners to copy into their notebooks, and read any text aloud twice instead of printing it. The board plan above is this lesson's minimum - with that on the board, it runs on no paper at all.
An online classSend Handouts 1 to 4 in the chat before the lesson and put the board plan on a shared slide. Use breakout rooms for the pairwork in "Freer practice" and visit each room once. Have learners type the controlled answers in the chat, so you see every answer at once instead of nominating.
A different age groupWith teenagers at this level, cut the academic framing and keep the examples in their own world; the forms do not change. With adults in a work context, swap the lesson's texts for ones from their own field and keep the analysis as it stands.

Checking learning

The final report uses at least eight complex prepositions accurately. No due to / in spite of + clause errors. 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

Teacher's reflection

Did students over- or under-use formal phrases? Was the phrase vs clause distinction clear?

Handout 1: Informal and Formal

Match each informal sentence (1–5) to a formal version (a–e).

Answers: 1 ___ 2 ___ 3 ___ 4 ___ 5 ___

InformalFormal
1. About your email, I can confirm the date.a. Owing to the strike, all trains were cancelled.
2. Because of the strike, the trains were cancelled.b. I am writing on behalf of the whole class.
3. I'm writing for the whole class.c. In spite of the cost, the school approved the plan.
4. It was expensive, but the school said yes.d. With regard to your email, I can confirm the date.
5. The man I wrote to has replied.e. The gentleman to whom I wrote has replied.

Handout 2: Model Report

Report on the School Canteen

Purpose: This report has been prepared on behalf of the student council with regard to the school canteen. It is based on a survey of 300 students conducted in accordance with school guidelines.

Findings: In spite of recent improvements, only 35% of students are satisfied with the quality of food. Owing to rising costs, 72% feel that prices are too high. In addition to price, healthy eating is a major concern: 81% want more healthy options. Queues are also a problem on account of the short lunch break.

Recommendations: In the light of these findings, we recommend that the menu be placed under review. Regardless of the cost, fresh fruit should be available daily. With a view to reducing queues, a pre-order system could be introduced by means of a simple mobile app. The success of the canteen is at stake, and we would be grateful for the principal's support.

Find and categorise the complex prepositions.

ReferenceCauseContrastMeans / agencyOther

Common errors to watch for

  • Reference: with regard to · Cause: owing to, on account of, in the light of · Contrast: in spite of, regardless of · Means / agency: on behalf of, in accordance with, by means of · Other: in addition to, with a view to, under review, at stake

In spite of the cost / being expensive, they bought it. · 5. The man to whom I wrote has replied. · 6. We will act in accordance with the school rules. · 7. The decision is under review. · 8. Regardless of your age, you can apply.

In spite of it was late ...In spite of the late hour ... / Although it was late ...
