Used To, Would ESL Lesson Plan (B1, 60 Minutes)

A complete 60-minute B1 lesson on Used To, Would, built round the theme "Childhood Holidays".

  • CEFR B1
  • 60 minutes
  • Teenagers and adults
  • Grammar

  • Used To, Would

About this lesson

Who it is for. Intermediate students who use used to accurately in all forms. They don't yet use would for past habits, or they overuse it for states (I would have a dog, We would live in Kandy) and for single events (One day we would go to Yala). They repeat used to in every sentence of a story.

Everything the lesson needs is on this page: the language analysis, the timed stages with the teacher's own words and instruction-checking questions, the differentiation, the printable handouts and the answer key.

At a glance

Level
CEFR B1 (Intermediate)
Learners
Teenagers and adults
Length
60 minutes. For 45 minutes, run only "Tell" of the two tasks in "Freer practice" and cut "Feedback and wrap-up" to board-based error correction.
Skills
Speaking and writing
Language focus
Grammar: Used To, Would and the Past Simple for Past Habits and States
Framework
PPP (presentation, practice, production)
Materials
Handout 1 (a memoir extract), Handout 2 (language focus), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (memory prompts).
Preparation
About 10 minutes: print Handouts 1 to 4, one per learner, and read the language analysis. The answer key is for you, not the class.

Aims

Main aim. To clarify and practise the difference between used to (past habits and states), would (past repeated actions only, not states) and the past simple (single events, and also habits when a time period is given).

Learning outcome. By the end of the lesson, students can describe childhood memories using a natural mix of forms, e.g. We used to have a big garden. Every April, we'd go to my grandparents' village. My grandmother would wake us up at dawn. One year, we saw an elephant on the road!

  • Subsidiary aim. To practise the contraction 'd /d/.
  • Subsidiary aim. To develop a nostalgic narrative in speaking and writing.
Advertisement

Language analysis

What to know before the lesson: the meaning, form and pronunciation of the target language, and the problems learners usually have with it.

Form and use

Habits (repeated actions)States (be, have, live, like, know, believe)Single past event
used to✓ We used to swim every day.✓ We used to live in Kandy.✗
would ('d)✓ We'd swim every day.✗ We would live in Kandy.✗
past simple✓ We swam every day.✓ We lived in Kandy.✓ One day, we saw an elephant.

Form and use: notes

  • would needs a clear past context (often set up first with used to or a time phrase: When I was a child, ... / Every summer, ...).
  • would for habits is common in storytelling and memories; it sounds nostalgic.
  • Past simple must be used with a specific number or period: I went there five times (not I used to go there five times).

Pronunciation

  • would is usually contracted: we'd /wiːd/, she'd /ʃiːd/. The /d/ can be very light.

Concept-checking questions

  • We'd swim in the river every afternoon. Did we swim many times or once? (Many times.) Is it past? (Yes.) Could I say used to swim? (Yes.)
  • ***We would live in a small house.* ✗** Is live an action or a state? (A state.) So can we use would? (No; used to live / lived.)
  • One morning, we saw a leopard. How many times? (Once.) So used to or would? (Neither; past simple.)

Anticipated problems and solutions

ProblemSolution
would with states (I would have a dog).Board a list of state verbs with ✗ under would.
would for single events.Timeline: repeated dots (used to / would) vs one cross (past simple).
Confusing past would with conditional would.Context: past time phrase → habit.
Repeating used to in every sentence.Model a paragraph: used to first, then would, then past simple for events.

Board plan

Left: habitsCentre: statesRight: single events
used to swim ✓ · 'd swim ✓ · swam ✓ · • • • • (repeated) · Every April, we'd go to the village.
used to live ✓ · lived ✓ · would live ✗ · state verbs: be, have, live, like, know, believe, own
saw ✓ · used to see ✗ · would see ✗ · ✕ (once) · One day, we saw an elephant. · I went there five times (not used to)

CEFR B1 · 60 minutes · Teenagers and adults

Procedure: Used To, Would, step by step

Warm-up5 minutes · Pairs, whole class

Aim: To engage students.

Ask: "Where did you go for school holidays when you were a child? What did you do there?"

Pairs share (2 minutes). Take ideas.

Presentation20 minutes · Individual, pairs, whole class

Aim: To present used to, would and the past simple in a memoir and clarify the differences.

Part A: Reading (6 minutes)

Give out Handout 1. Say: "Read Malini's memories of holidays in her grandparents' village. What was special about them? Three minutes."

Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)

Give out Handout 2. Say: "Find the verbs with used to, would ('d) and the past simple. Complete the table and answer the questions."

ICQs: "How many forms do you look for?" (Three.)

Monitor and help.

Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)

Elicit answers. Build the three-column board.

Ask the CCQs. Draw the timeline.

Board We would live in Kandy ✗ and One day, we would see an elephant ✗. Correct them.

Show how the memoir starts with used to and then switches to would.

Pronunciation. Drill we'd go, she'd cook, he'd tell us stories.

Controlled practice10 minutes · Individual, then pairs, then whole class

Aim: To practise the choice accurately.

Give out Handout 3. Say: "Exercise A: which forms are possible? Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."

Pairs check. Check as a class.

Freer practice18 minutes · Individual, then groups of three, then whole class

Aim: To use the forms in personal narratives.

Give out Handout 4. Students choose a memory prompt and make notes: two states (used to), three habits (would), one special event (past simple) (4 minutes).

Tell (8 minutes). In groups of three, students tell their memories. Listeners note one would, one used to and one past simple and ask a follow-up question.

ICQs: "What do the listeners note?" (One example of each form.)

Share (6 minutes). Each group chooses the best memory and the speaker retells it to the class.

Monitor and note language use.

Feedback and wrap-up7 minutes · Whole class

Aim: To give feedback and consolidate.

Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct them.

Quick fire: say a verb (have, swim, see a leopard once); students say which forms are possible.

Set the homework.

Homework Write a memory (150 words) about a place you used to visit as a child. Use used to for states, would for habits, and the past simple for one special event.

PPP (presentation, practice, production). Stage times add up to 60 minutes.

Differentiation

Support
Give weaker students the three-column board on a card. Let them use used to and the past simple only, adding would when confident.
Stretch
Stronger students use would always / would often: She would always save the biggest mango for me. They add used to questions and negatives in their stories.

Adapting the lesson

ForWhat to change
A large class (40 or more)Keep the pairwork in "Freer practice" in fixed pairs rather than a mingle, and take feedback from four or five pairs instead of the whole room. Run "Tell" once, with two pairs reporting, rather than hearing every pair. Monitor one half of the room during "Controlled practice" and the other half during "Freer practice".
A class with no photocopierPut the exercises from Handout 3: Practice on the board for learners to copy into their notebooks, and read any text aloud twice instead of printing it. The board plan above is this lesson's minimum - with that on the board, it runs on no paper at all.
An online classSend Handouts 1 to 4 in the chat before the lesson and put the board plan on a shared slide. Use breakout rooms for "Tell" and visit each room once. Have learners type the controlled answers in the chat, so you see every answer at once instead of nominating.
A different age groupWith teenagers, shorten the analysis and get to the task sooner. With adults in a work context, keep the forms exactly as they are and swap the lesson's theme for one from their own field.

Checking learning

8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3. In their stories, students do not use would for states or single events. Students use a mix of all three forms.

Teacher's reflection

Did the timeline help students see habits vs single events? Did the stories sound natural and nostalgic?

Handout 1: Holidays in the Village

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

When I was a child, my grandparents used to live in a small village near Kurunegala. They had a big house with a coconut garden, and we used to spend every April holiday there.

Every morning, my grandmother would wake us up at dawn. She'd make kiribath, and we'd eat it on the veranda while the birds sang. In the afternoons, my cousins and I would swim in the tank and climb the mango trees. My grandfather used to know the name of every bird, and he'd tell us stories about the old kings.

I didn't use to like the long bus journey, but I loved the village. One year, on the way home, we saw a wild elephant on the road. The bus stopped for twenty minutes!

My grandparents don't live there any more, but I still dream about those holidays.

Answer the questions.

  • When did Malini's family go to the village? ______________________________
  • What would they do in the afternoons? ______________________________
  • What happened one year on the way home? ______________________________

Common errors to watch for

  • Every April holiday. · 2. They would swim in the tank and climb the mango trees. · 3. They saw a wild elephant on the road; the bus stopped for twenty minutes.
  • No; live is a state verb. · 2. No; it happened once. · 3. used to sets the past context; then would is natural for repeated actions.
  • used to live, lived · 2. used to take, would take, took · 3. broke · 4. used to have, had · 5. used to play, would play, played · 6. went
