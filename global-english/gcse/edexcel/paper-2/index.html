Edexcel GCSE English Language

Edexcel English Language Paper 2

Two hours and five minutes, ninety-six marks, sixty per cent of the qualification. Nine questions, of which you answer eight. This is the paper that decides the grade.

RCF Model Paper 2

  5. Edexcel English Language Paper 2

About the examples on this page

Every example and practice task here was written by RCF English. They are not Edexcel questions or Edexcel source texts, and no Edexcel mark scheme wording is reproduced. RCF English is not affiliated with, endorsed by or approved by Pearson Edexcel.

Paper 2 is called Non-fiction and Transactional Writing, paper code 1EN0/02. It lasts 2 hours 5 minutes, carries 96 marks and is worth 60% of the GCSE. Section A gives you two unseen non-fiction extracts - one from the 20th century and one from the 21st, one of them literary non-fiction - and asks seven questions worth 56 marks. Section B asks for one piece of transactional writing worth 40 marks.

The timings on the paper

The paper advises about 1 hour 20 minutes on Section A and about 45 minutes on Section B. Those are Edexcel's figures. The per-question minutes in the table are an RCF recommendation derived from the marks.

The four short questions: six marks in ten minutes

Questions 1, 2, 4 and 5 are retrieval, and together they are worth 6 marks. Two are on Text 1 and two on Text 2, and each names the lines you must answer from.

  • Answer from the named lines only. Material from elsewhere earns nothing.
  • Give exactly the number asked for - two things, or one thing.
  • A short phrase is enough. Full sentences waste time you need for the 15-mark questions.
  • Questions 4 and 5 are worth one mark each. Do not write a paragraph for one mark.

Question 3: analyse language and structure (15 marks)

On Text 1 only. The question asks how the writer uses language and structure to interest and engage the reader. Both words are in the question and both are marked.

A shape that fits 15 marks

  1. Two paragraphs on language: a short quotation, what the writer is doing, the effect on the reader, then one development.
  2. One paragraph on structure: how the extract opens, where the focus shifts, how it ends.
  3. One further paragraph on either, taken deeper than the others.
  4. Four developed paragraphs is a full answer in about 22 minutes.

What separates the top band

Not more quotations. A second reading of a single word - why that one rather than the plainer alternative the writer could have used. The language analysis page shows the same quotation answered at three levels.

Question 6: evaluation (15 marks)

On Text 2 only. The question states a purpose the writer attempts - to show the impact of something, to persuade you of something - and asks you to evaluate how successfully it is achieved, supported by detailed reference.

  • Answer the question in your first sentence. Say how successful, then justify it.
  • Three or four developed paragraphs, each with a judgement and a short quotation.
  • Use one paragraph to qualify: something the text does less well, or achieves by an unexpected route.
  • Analysis without judgement is the commonest way to lose marks here. The writer uses a metaphor is analysis; the metaphor works because it makes an abstract problem physical is evaluation.

Question 7: the only question using both texts (20 marks)

Question 7 comes in two parts and is worth 20 marks between them. Students routinely treat the two parts as one job. They are not.

7(a): synthesis, 6 marks

This asks what the two texts have in common, with evidence from both. It is about content, not method. Do not analyse technique here.

  • Three clear similarities is a full answer.
  • Each one: state the similarity, quote briefly from Text 1, quote briefly from Text 2.
  • Keep it factual and quick. Six marks in about ten minutes.

7(b): comparison, 14 marks

This asks you to compare how the writers present their ideas and perspectives. The paper gives you bullets: the ideas and perspectives, how they are presented, and how they are similar or different.

Now method matters. Both halves - what the writers think and how they show it - are marked.

  • Three paragraphs, and both writers in every paragraph.
  • Name the difference in perspective first, then analyse how each writer conveys it.
  • Use comparative connectives: where Text 1 asserts, Text 2 concedes.
  • End at least one paragraph by weighing the two. The comparing viewpoints page has a full worked example.

Section B: transactional writing (40 marks)

Choose one of the two tasks. The tasks are linked by a theme to the reading extracts, and Pearson notes that the same form may appear on both tasks with a different focus or audience - so read both before choosing.

Text types include articles, letters, reviews, texts for speeches, and sections for guides, leaflets or booklets.

  • Identify the form, the purpose and the audience before you plan anything else.
  • The form must be visible from the first three lines - a headline, a salutation, an address to an audience.
  • Plan three arguments and one concession. Five minutes.
  • Leave five minutes to check accuracy. The transactional writing method transfers directly from the AQA page.

Edexcel Paper 2

Do this

  • Check which text each question refers to before answering.
  • Spend about ten minutes total on Questions 1, 2, 4 and 5.
  • Give Questions 3 and 6 about twenty minutes each - they are 30 of the 56 reading marks.
  • Handle both writers in every paragraph of 7(b).
  • Choose one Section B task and commit to it.

Avoid this

  • Do not analyse method in 7(a). It asks for similarities of content.
  • Do not describe technique without judgement in Question 6 - it is an evaluation question.
  • Do not answer Question 3 on Text 2, or Question 6 on Text 1.
  • Do not write both Section B tasks.
  • Do not run Section A over time. Section B is 40 marks and it comes last.

Questions about Edexcel Paper 2

What is the difference between Question 7a and 7b on Edexcel Paper 2?

Question 7(a) is worth 6 marks and asks what similarities the two texts share, using evidence from both. It is about content. Question 7(b) is worth 14 marks and asks you to compare how the writers present ideas and perspectives, so it needs analysis of method as well as content. Answering 7(b) as a second synthesis caps the mark.

How many marks is Edexcel English Language Paper 2?

96 marks in total: 56 for Section A reading and 40 for Section B writing. The paper is worth 60% of the GCSE, and it lasts 2 hours 5 minutes.

How long are the two texts on Edexcel Paper 2?

Pearson's specification states that the word count across the two extracts will be approximately 1,000 words, and that the minimum length of an extract will be 300 words. One text is from the 20th century and one from the 21st, and one of the two is literary non-fiction.

Which questions on Edexcel Paper 2 are worth the most?

Question 3 and Question 6 are worth 15 marks each, and Question 7 is worth 20 marks across its two parts. Those three questions carry 50 of the 56 reading marks. The four short retrieval questions carry 6 marks between them.
