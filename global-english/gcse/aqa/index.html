GCSE English

AQA GCSE English Language

Two papers, ten questions, 160 marks. No coursework and no set texts to learn - every source is unseen. That makes this a skills examination, and skills can be practised.

Paper 1Paper 2
  1. Home
  2. Global English
  3. GCSE English
  4. AQA GCSE English Language

AQA GCSE English Language (specification 8700) is assessed by two written papers of 1 hour 45 minutes each, worth 80 marks and 50% of the GCSE apiece. Each paper has a reading section worth 40 marks and a writing section worth 40 marks. There is no coursework. Spoken Language is assessed separately and does not count towards your 9-1 grade.

Where these figures come from

Every duration, mark and question on these pages was read from AQA's own published material: the 8700 specification and scheme of assessment on aqa.org.uk, and the specimen question papers and specimen mark schemes on AQA's file store.

Per-question marks were confirmed against the top mark band of each level-marked question in the specimen mark schemes, and they add up to the section totals printed on the papers.

RCF English is not affiliated with, endorsed by or approved by AQA. No AQA question, source text or mark scheme wording is reproduced anywhere on this site.

The timings AQA prints on the paper

Both papers carry the same advice on the front cover and at the head of each section. This is AQA's own wording, not a revision guide's suggestion, so it is worth following.

  • About 15 minutes reading through the source or sources and all five questions.
  • About 45 minutes on Section A, the reading section.
  • About 45 minutes on Section B, the writing section.
  • Leave time to check your answers.

AQA does not publish a time for each question

It gives you the section times above and leaves the split to you. Where these pages suggest a minute count for an individual question, that is an RCF recommendation worked out from the marks - roughly a minute a mark in Section A, plus planning time - and it is labelled as such. Nobody will mark you down for spending your 45 minutes differently.

The assessment objectives, in plain English

Reading

  • AO1 - find information and ideas, both stated and implied, and bring evidence together from more than one text.
  • AO2 - explain and analyse how writers use language and structure to affect the reader, using the right terms.
  • AO3 - compare writers' ideas and viewpoints, and how they put them across, across two or more texts.
  • AO4 - evaluate a text critically and back your judgement with references.

Writing

  • AO5 - communicate clearly and imaginatively, choosing tone, style and register to suit the form, purpose and audience. This is the 24 marks for content and organisation.
  • AO6 - use a range of vocabulary and sentence structures, with accurate spelling and punctuation. This is the 16 marks for technical accuracy, and AQA states it must be 20% of the marks for the qualification.

Spoken Language

AO7, AO8 and AO9 cover presenting formally, responding to questions and using spoken Standard English. AQA assesses this through a prepared presentation of no more than ten minutes, marked by your teacher as Pass, Merit or Distinction. It is reported separately and carries no weighting towards the 9-1 grade.

Questions about AQA GCSE English Language

How long is AQA GCSE English Language Paper 1?

1 hour 45 minutes, for 80 marks. AQA advises spending about 15 minutes reading the source and all five questions, about 45 minutes on Section A and about 45 minutes on Section B. Paper 2 is exactly the same length and carries the same advice.

How many marks is each question worth on AQA English Language?

On Paper 1: Question 1 is 4 marks, Question 2 is 8, Question 3 is 8, Question 4 is 20 and Question 5 is 40. On Paper 2: Question 1 is 4 marks, Question 2 is 8, Question 3 is 12, Question 4 is 16 and Question 5 is 40. On both papers the writing question is split into 24 marks for content and organisation and 16 for technical accuracy.

Do I need to learn any texts for AQA English Language?

No. Every source in both papers is unseen, and you meet it for the first time in the examination. That is why revision here means practising skills and question methods rather than memorising content. English Literature is the GCSE with set texts.

Is there a Foundation and Higher tier for GCSE English Language?

No. GCSE English Language is untiered: every student sits the same papers and the full grade range from 9 to 1 is available on them.

Does the Spoken Language endorsement affect my grade?

No. AQA assesses it through a prepared formal presentation of no more than ten minutes, marked by your teacher as Pass, Merit or Distinction on a competency basis. It is reported separately on your certificate and has a 0% weighting towards the 9-1 grade.

Can I use a dictionary in the exam?

No. Both specimen papers state plainly that you must not use a dictionary.
