GCSE English

GCSE English is two separate qualifications, English Language and English Literature, and four boards set them differently. Find your board first, then the paper structure follows.

  1. Home
  2. Global English
  3. GCSE English

GCSE English Language and GCSE English Literature are two different GCSEs, and most students in England sit both. English Language is assessed on unseen texts and your own writing; English Literature is assessed on set texts you have studied, plus unseen poetry. Neither has coursework: both are examined at the end of the course.

The four boards, and why the difference matters

AQA, Pearson Edexcel, OCR and Eduqas (part of WJEC) all offer GCSE English Language and GCSE English Literature. All four are regulated to the same standard and graded 9 to 1, so no board is 'easier'. What differs is the shape of the papers - how many there are, how long they last, which texts are set, and how the marks are divided.

  • A revision video or model answer written for AQA Paper 1 Question 5 does not describe any question on the OCR paper.
  • Set texts for English Literature are chosen by your school from the board's list, so two schools using the same board may still study different novels.
  • Spoken Language is assessed at GCSE English Language, but as a separate endorsement that does not count towards your 9-1 grade.

What each AQA Language paper asks of you

Paper 1: reading fiction, then writing your own

The reading section works from one literature fiction text. The writing section asks for a piece of descriptive or narrative writing - this is the paper where a well-planned short description earns more than a rushed adventure story.

Paper 2: reading two non-fiction texts, then writing to a viewpoint

The reading section uses one non-fiction text and one literary non-fiction text, normally from different centuries, and asks you to compare. The writing section asks you to present a point of view, so the form - a letter, an article, a speech - matters as much as the argument.

About these paper links

GCSE English Language and English Literature are not tiered: there is no Foundation or Higher paper in either subject, so every candidate sits the same papers. Tiering applies to some other GCSE subjects, not to these.

Examination boards own their question papers, mark schemes and examiner reports. This site links to the official page and never copies, re-uploads or mirrors them, and it does not link to sites that republish them without permission.

Original RCF model papers for GCSE will follow the format now being trialled for Cambridge IGCSE English as a Second Language, once that template has been checked.

Resources being developed

The question-by-question GCSE guides - creative writing, transactional writing, reading analysis, the Literature essay method, poetry comparison and unseen poetry - are being written now, one at a time, and will appear under this page as each is finished. Original RCF model papers for GCSE English Language and GCSE English Literature are part of that work.

Rather than publish a shelf of thin pages, this hub gives you the codes, the paper shapes and the official links today. In the meantime the Cambridge IGCSE English as a Second Language guide is complete, and much of its writing-format teaching - articles, reports, reviews, formal and informal letters - transfers directly to GCSE transactional writing.

GCSE English questions

How many GCSE English exams are there?

If you are entered for both GCSEs, you normally sit four written papers: two for English Language and two for English Literature. The Spoken Language endorsement is assessed separately by your teacher during the course and is reported alongside your grade rather than inside it. Exact numbers depend on your board, so check your specification code.

Which GCSE English board is the easiest?

None of them. All four boards are regulated to a common standard and grade boundaries are set each year so that the same performance earns the same grade whichever board set the paper. What differs is the format, and the best board for you is the one your school teaches, because the teaching is matched to the paper.

Do I have to read the whole book for GCSE English Literature?

Yes. English Literature is a closed-book examination on most papers, which means you write about texts you have read in full and can quote from memory. Summaries are useful for revision but cannot replace reading, because the questions reward specific detail and personal response.

Does the Spoken Language endorsement affect my GCSE grade?

No. At AQA, and at the other boards, Spoken Language is reported as a separate endorsement graded Pass, Merit or Distinction, with a 0% weighting towards the 9-1 grade for GCSE English Language. It still appears on your certificate, so it is worth preparing for.
