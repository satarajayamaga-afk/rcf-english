A/L Literature

Prescribed Texts

Prescribed texts change. This page is built so that texts can be added and removed without disturbing anything else on the site.

  1. Home
  2. A/L Literature
  3. Prescribed Texts

The list changes — check it

The prescribed texts for A/L English Literature are set by the examination authority and are revised periodically. Always confirm the list for your own examination year against the current official syllabus.

Do not prepare a text because a website, a study guide or an older student's notes include it. Every year some candidates lose a great deal of work this way.

Texts on RCF English

Each text page follows the same fifteen-section structure used throughout the site, so that everything belonging to one text stays together.

Texts are added here as they are prepared. Each text is set out in the same fifteen sections described above, so you always know where to look. Nothing is published for a text until the material has been written and checked, and no copyrighted text is reproduced in full.

What each text page contains

  1. Introduction — what the work is, and what to watch for.
  2. Author or playwright — verified facts only.
  3. Background — when and where written, and the context a reader needs.
  4. Summary or plot — what happens, in order.
  5. Characters — who they are, what they want, how they change.
  6. Themes — stated as arguable sentences, not as single words.
  7. Literary or dramatic techniques — named, illustrated, and tied to effect.
  8. Important quotations — short, accurate, and chosen for usefulness.
  9. Context questions — on printed extracts.
  10. Concise context answers — of the right length.
  11. Essay questions — of the kind the paper sets.
  12. Developed model answers — with notes on how each is built.
  13. Common student mistakes — on this particular text.
  14. Revision exercises.
  15. Related past-paper questions.

The prescribed poetry

The poems below are the twenty-four set for A/L English Literature under the 2017 cycle, listed in the order the official NIE Anthology of Poetry gives them.

We do not reproduce the poems here. The anthology prints them in full under permissions granted to the National Institute of Education — Larkin's Money, for instance, appears by permission of Faber and Faber. Those permissions belong to the NIE, not to this website. Use the anthology issued by your school, and check the list against the syllabus for your own examination year.

Preparing a text that is not yet on this site

Use the fifteen sections as your own note-making structure. Build them yourself, in that order, in a notebook for each text.

This is not a second-best option. Notes you construct yourself are recalled far more reliably than notes you read, and building them is the revision.
