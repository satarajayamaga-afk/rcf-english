GRAMMAR TOPIC: GERUNDS AND INFINITIVES · B2 (Upper-intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Verbs that Change Meaning (remember, forget, stop, try, regret, go on + -ing or to)

*Lesson theme: Memories, Mistakes and Second Chances*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Gerunds and Infinitives |
| Grammar point | Verbs that Change Meaning (remember, forget, stop, try, regret, go on + -ing or to) |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who know most verb patterns. They treat remember, stop, try and regret as interchangeable with either form (I remember to visit Kandy as a child, I stopped to smoke meaning quit), causing misunderstandings. |
| Main aim | To develop students' understanding and use of verbs whose meaning changes with -ing or to + infinitive: remember, forget, stop, try, regret, go on, mean; and to contrast them with verbs where the difference is small (like, love, hate, prefer, begin, start). |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in telling anecdotes about memories and mistakes; to practise stress for contrast. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can tell short anecdotes using the verbs accurately, e.g. I'll never forget seeing elephants at Minneriya. I stopped to take a photo. I regret not bringing a better camera. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (forum posts: My biggest mistakes), Handout 2 (timeline reference and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (anecdote cards). |

## Language analysis

### Meaning changes

| **Verb** | **+ -ing** | **+ to + infinitive** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| remember | remember a past action (memory): I remember visiting Kandy as a child. | remember a duty first, then do it: Remember to lock the door. |
| forget | (usually never forget) a past experience: I'll never forget seeing the sea. | forget a duty: I forgot to call her. (I didn't call.) |
| stop | finish an activity: He stopped smoking. (He doesn't smoke now.) | stop one activity in order to do another: He stopped to smoke. (He stopped walking and smoked.) |
| try | experiment, test a solution: Try restarting the computer. | make an effort (maybe difficult): I tried to open the window, but it was stuck. |
| regret | be sorry about a past action: I regret shouting at him. | formal: be sorry to announce: We regret to inform you that ... |
| go on | continue the same activity: She went on talking. | change to a new activity: After school, she went on to study medicine. |
| mean | involve: This job means working weekends. | intend: I didn't mean to upset you. |

### Little or no change in meaning

- *like, love, hate, prefer, begin, start, continue* + *-ing* or *to*: *It started raining / to rain.*

### Pronunciation

- Contrastive stress: *I REMEMBERED to lock it* (I didn't forget) vs *I remember LOCKING it* (I have the memory).

### Concept checking questions

*I remember locking the door.*

1. Did I lock the door? (Yes.)
2. Which came first: locking or remembering? (Locking.)

*Remember to lock the door.*

1. Has the person locked it yet? (No.)
2. Which comes first: remembering or locking? (Remembering.)

*He stopped to buy a drink.*

1. Did he buy a drink? (Yes.)
2. Why did he stop? (To buy a drink.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Confusing remember / forget patterns. | Timeline: -ing = action before memory; to = memory before action. |
| stop to do misunderstood as quit. | CCQ: "Does he still smoke?" |
| try -ing vs try to | Contrast: experiment (it's easy to do, but does it work?) vs effort (is it possible?). |
| regret to overused. | Note: regret to is formal and mainly inform / tell / announce. |

## Board plan

| **Left: timelines** | **Centre: verb + -ing / + to** | **Right: pronunciation** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| -ING: action → then memory (I remember locking) · TO: memory → then action (Remember to lock) | remember / forget: memory of past ↔ duty · stop: quit ↔ stop in order to · try: experiment ↔ effort · regret: sorry about past ↔ sorry to announce · go on: continue ↔ next step · mean: involve ↔ intend · like / start / begin: little change | I REMEMBERED to lock it. · I remember LOCKING it. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic and personalise it. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Board two questions: *What's your earliest memory? What's something you often forget to do?*
2. Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Take two answers and board them exactly as students say them.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the verbs in context and clarify the differences in meaning. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the forum posts. Whose mistake was the worst? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Find the pairs of sentences with the same verb but different patterns. What's the difference in meaning?"* Pairs discuss.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **remember / forget.** Draw two timelines on the board. Ask the CCQs.
2. **stop.** Mime: walking, then stopping to drink water. Ask: *"Did I stop walking or stop drinking?"*
3. **try.** Board *Try restarting the computer* and *I tried to open the window, but it was stuck.* Ask: *"Which one is an experiment? Which one is an effort?"*
4. **regret, go on, mean.** Elicit the differences from the forum posts.
5. Show that *like, start, begin* change little.
6. Return to the sentences from Stage 1 and check them.
7. **Pronunciation.** Drill contrastive stress: *I REMEMBERED to call her. I remember CALLING her.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing the correct form. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct form and explain the meaning. Exercise B: complete the sentences. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs justify their answers. Check as a class.
3. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the verbs accurately in personal anecdotes. **Interaction:** Groups of three, then whole class

1. Give each group a set of anecdote cards (Handout 4). Students take turns to take a card and tell a short story (true or invented) for one minute: *"Tell us about something you'll never forget seeing."*
   - ICQs: *"How long do you speak?"* (One minute.) *"Must it be true?"* (No.)
2. After each story, the listeners ask one follow-up question using another verb from today: *"Do you regret not ...?" "Did you try ...?"* (12 minutes.)
3. Each group retells the best story to the class (4 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors and explain the meaning.
2. Ask: *"What's the difference between stop smoking and stop to smoke?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write a forum post (180 words) about a mistake you made and what you learned. Use at least five verbs from today with the correct pattern.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the reference table and timelines on a card.
- Focus on *remember, forget, stop* and *try* only.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *need -ing* (passive meaning): *The car needs washing.*
- They use perfect gerunds: *I regret having said that.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- Students use the correct pattern for the intended meaning in their anecdotes.
- Students can explain the difference in meaning between pairs.

## Teacher's reflection

- Were the timelines clear for *remember / forget*?
- Which verb caused the most difficulty?

# Handout 2: Reference and Practice

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Quick reference**

| **Verb** | **+ -ing** | **+ to** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| remember / forget | memory of a past action | a duty (remember first, then do) |
| stop | quit an activity | stop in order to do something |
| try | an experiment (a possible solution) | an effort (something difficult) |
| regret | sorry about the past | formal: sorry to announce |
| go on | continue the same activity | do something new next |
| mean | involve | intend |

**A. Choose the correct form. Then explain the meaning to your partner.**

1. Did you remember **locking / to lock** the door? I can't find the key!
2. I'll never forget **meeting / to meet** my favourite cricketer.
3. We stopped **having / to have** lunch at a café on the way to Ella.
4. My grandfather stopped **driving / to drive** when he was 80.
5. If your phone is slow, try **deleting / to delete** some apps.
6. I tried **lifting / to lift** the box, but it was too heavy.
7. We regret **informing / to inform** you that the flight has been cancelled.
8. After a short break, the speaker went on **talking / to talk** about climate change.

**B. Complete with the correct form of the verb in brackets.**

1. I forgot ______________ (buy) milk, so we had black tea.
2. I remember ______________ (play) in the rain when I was little.
3. She regrets ______________ (not / study) harder for her exams.
4. I didn't mean ______________ (hurt) your feelings.
5. Taking this job will mean ______________ (move) to Colombo.
6. He started his career as a teacher and went on ______________ (become) a principal.
