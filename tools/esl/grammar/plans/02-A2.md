GRAMMAR TOPIC: PAST TENSES · A2 (Elementary) · 60 minutes

# Past Continuous

*Lesson theme: What Were You Doing When ...?*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Past Tenses |
| Grammar point | Past Continuous |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who use the past simple with reasonable accuracy and know the present continuous. They have not yet learned the past continuous and use the past simple for all past actions. |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise the past continuous for actions in progress at a past time and for actions interrupted by a past simple event (when / while). |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in a game-like speaking task (an alibi game); to read a short news story for specific information. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can say what they were doing at a specific past time and describe an interrupted action, e.g. I was cooking when the lights went out. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (news story), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (alibi role cards and detective question sheet). |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Positive** | **Negative** | **Question** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| I / He / She / It | I was cooking. | He wasn't sleeping. | Was she working? Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't. |
| You / We / They | They were watching TV. | We weren't listening. | Were you studying? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. |

### Meaning and use

- An action **in progress at a specific time** in the past: *At 8 pm last night, I was watching TV.*
- A **longer action interrupted** by a shorter one: *I was walking home when it started to rain.* (past continuous = long action; past simple = short action)
- **when** usually goes before the short action; **while** usually goes before the long action: *While she was sleeping, the phone rang.*
- Two actions in progress at the same time: *While I was cooking, my brother was doing his homework.*

### Pronunciation

- *was* and *were* are usually weak in positive sentences: /wəz/, /wə/. *I was cooking* /aɪ wəz ˈkʊkɪŋ/.
- They are strong in short answers and negatives: *Yes, I was* /wɒz/. *wasn't* /ˈwɒznt/.

### Concept checking questions

*I was cooking when the lights went out.*

1. Which action started first? (Cooking.)
2. Which action was short? (The lights went out.)
3. Did the lights going out interrupt the cooking? (Yes.)
4. Which is in the past continuous? (Cooking.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Missing -ing (I was watch TV). | Board formula with three boxes. Finger gesture: three fingers for was – watch – ing. |
| Using the continuous for both actions (When I was arriving, they were eating). | Timeline: one long wavy line, one cross. CCQ: Which is short? |
| Wrong choice of when / while. | Highlight the general pattern on the board; accept when with both in speech. |
| Alibi game: students forget their story. | Give each pair time to write a short alibi plan on the card. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To generate interest and get students thinking about a moment in the past. **Interaction:** Whole class, then pairs

1. Turn off the classroom lights (or mime it). Say: *"Imagine: last night at 8 o'clock, all the lights in the city went out! A power cut!"*
2. Ask: *"Do you remember a power cut? Where were you? How did you feel?"* Take two or three answers.
3. Students tell a partner where they were at 8 o'clock last night (1 minute). Don't correct.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the past continuous in context and clarify its meaning, form and pronunciation, including when and while. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the news story. Match each person to what they were doing when the lights went out. Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many people?"* (Four.) *"Do you write or match?"* (Match.)
2. Pairs check, then check as a class. Students do the True / False questions.

**Part B: Noticing (3 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline was/were + -ing in one colour and the past simple verbs in another."* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **Meaning.** Board *Mr Perera was cooking when the lights went out.* Draw a timeline: a long wavy line (cooking) and a cross (the lights went out). Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
2. **Form.** Elicit was/were + verb-ing; the negative (*wasn't, weren't*); the question: *What were you doing at 8 pm?* Write the form table on the board.
3. **when / while.** Board two sentences from the text. Ask: *"Which word comes before the long action?"* (while) *"Which comes before the short action?"* (when)
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill with weak forms: *I was cooking. They were watching TV.* Then strong short answers: *Yes, I was. No, I wasn't.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form and the contrast with the past simple. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: past simple or past continuous. Exercise B: when or while. Six minutes. Work alone."*
2. Pairs compare and justify: *"It's the long action, so past continuous."*
3. Check as a class. Drill two or three sentences.
4. Students answer Exercise C about themselves and read one answer to a partner.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the past continuous fluently and accurately in a communicative game. **Interaction:** Groups of four (two suspects, two detectives)

1. **Set up (2 minutes).** Read the situation on Handout 3: *"Yesterday between 10.00 and 11.00, a birthday cake disappeared from the staff room. Two students are suspects."* Divide each group: two suspects and two detectives.
2. **Preparation (4 minutes).** Suspects agree on a detailed alibi: where they were and what they were doing at 10.00, 10.15, 10.30 and 10.45. Detectives prepare questions with the question sheet.
   - ICQs: *"Suspects, must your answers be the same?"* (Yes.) *"Detectives, do you interview them together?"* (No, separately.)
3. **Interviews (8 minutes).** Detectives interview each suspect separately; the other suspect waits out of earshot. Detectives note answers.
4. **Decision (2 minutes).** Detectives compare notes and decide: innocent or guilty, with reasons: *"Suspect A said they were reading in the library at 10.30, but Suspect B said they were eating in the canteen."*
5. Each group announces its verdict to the class (2 minutes).

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences from the game: two correct, two with errors. Students correct them.
2. Ask a final question round the class: *"What were you doing at 7 o'clock this morning?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a paragraph (80–100 words) about where you were and what you were doing during a memorable event (a big match, a storm, a celebration).

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give suspects a time-plan template to complete before the interviews.
- Weaker detectives can read questions directly from the sheet.

**Stretch**

- Detectives must ask at least two follow-up questions (*Exactly what time? Who were you with? What were they wearing?*).
- Stronger suspects add a *while* sentence: *While I was reading, Kamal was sitting next to me.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the alibi game, students form past continuous questions and answers accurately most of the time.
- Students choose past continuous vs past simple correctly in 7 of 8 items on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- Students use *when* and *while* in at least one sentence each.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the timeline make the long action / short action clear?
- Was the alibi game well organised? How could I set it up faster?

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Read the news story. Match each person to what they were doing when the lights went out.**

**CITY IN THE DARK**

At exactly 8 pm last night, all the lights in the city went out. The power cut lasted for two hours. We asked some people what they were doing.

"I was cooking dinner for my family," said Mr Perera, 45. "The rice was nearly ready. We ate by candlelight!"

Nimali, 16, was doing her homework when the lights went out. "I was writing an essay on my computer. I didn't save it, so I lost everything!"

While the city was sitting in the dark, two doctors at the General Hospital were working with torches. "We were helping a patient, and we didn't stop," said Dr Silva.

Mrs Fernando, 70, wasn't worried. "I was sleeping in my chair. I didn't notice anything!"

| I was watch TV. | I was watching TV. |
