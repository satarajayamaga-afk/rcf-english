# Must, Have To, Don't Have To and Can't (Rules and Obligation)

*Lesson theme: Rules in Public Places*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Modal Verbs |
| Grammar point | Must, Have To, Don't Have To and Can't (Rules and Obligation) |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who know can for ability and permission. They confuse mustn't (prohibition) with don't have to (no obligation) and say I must to go. |
| Main aim | To present and practise must / have to (obligation), mustn't / can't (prohibition) and don't have to (no obligation), and had to for the past. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read signs and rules for gist and detail; to develop speaking by designing rules for a club. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can explain the rules of places they know and write 8 rules for a new club using the four structures correctly. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (rules from three places), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (club rules planning sheet), pictures of common signs (no smoking, wear a helmet, free entry, no swimming). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Meaning** | **Form** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Obligation (necessary) | must + base verb · have to / has to + base verb | You must wear a seat belt. I have to wear a uniform. She has to work on Saturdays. |
| Prohibition (not allowed) | mustn't + base verb · can't + base verb | You mustn't smoke here. You can't use phones in the exam. |
| No obligation (not necessary, but OK) | don't / doesn't have to + base verb | You don't have to come on Sunday. He doesn't have to pay. |
| Past obligation | had to + base verb | I had to stay late yesterday. (No past form of must.) |
| Questions | Do I have to ...? Does she have to ...? | Do we have to wear a uniform? |

### Meaning and use

- *must* often shows the speaker's own feeling or written rules (*Visitors must sign in*); *have to* often shows external rules (*I have to wear a uniform at school*). At A2, treat them as very similar.
- **Key contrast:** *You mustn't go* (= don't go!) vs *You don't have to go* (= it's not necessary, you can choose).

### Pronunciation

- *have to* /ˈhæftə/; *has to* /ˈhæstə/; *had to* /ˈhættə/.
- *mustn't* /ˈmʌsnt/ – the first *t* is silent.

### Concept checking questions

*You mustn't swim here.*

1. Can I swim here? (No.) Is it allowed? (No.)

*You don't have to come on Sunday.*

1. Is it necessary to come? (No.)
2. Can I come if I want? (Yes.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| mustn't and don't have to confused. | Traffic-light chart: red = mustn't; green = don't have to; CCQs. |
| I must to go. | Board: must + base verb (no to); have to has its own to. |
| She have to work. | Highlight has to for he / she / it. |
| I musted go. | Past: had to only. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic of rules. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Show pictures of four signs (or draw them). Ask pairs: *"Where can you see this sign? What does it mean?"* (2 minutes.)
2. Take answers in simple language; don't correct.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the target language in context and clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the three sets of rules. Where are they from: a library, a swimming pool or a school? Two minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many sets of rules?"* (Three.) *"What do you decide?"* (Where they are from.)
2. Pairs check. Then students answer the True / False questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Language clarification (13 minutes)**

1. Board three sentences from the text: *You must show your library card. You mustn't eat in the library. You don't have to pay to borrow books.*
2. Draw a three-column chart: *Necessary ✓ / Not allowed ✗ / Not necessary (you choose)*. Students tell you where each sentence goes.
3. Ask the CCQs. Focus on the key contrast: *mustn't* vs *don't have to*.
4. **Form.** Elicit: *must + base verb* (no *to*); *have to / has to*; questions with *Do / Does ... have to ...?*; past *had to*.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *I have to go* /ˈhæftə/, *She has to work* /ˈhæstə/, *You mustn't run* /ˈmʌsnt/.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing the correct form. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct answer. Exercise B: write about your school. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check and explain using the chart. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the structures to create and discuss rules. **Interaction:** Groups, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Your group is starting a new club. Choose a club and write 8 rules: necessary things, things not allowed and things that are not necessary."* (10 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"How many rules?"* (Eight.) *"Do you only write things that are not allowed?"* (No, all three types.)
2. Groups present their club and rules. Other groups ask questions: *"Do we have to pay? Can we bring friends?"* (8 minutes.)
3. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. The class votes for the best club.
2. Board errors heard, especially *mustn't / don't have to* confusion. Students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write the rules for your home (8 sentences). Include two things you had to do when you were younger.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide the meaning chart with icons (✓, ✗, ?) on a card.
- Weaker groups write 5 rules instead of 8.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students compare rules in two countries: *In Sri Lanka you have to ..., but in the UK you don't have to ...*
- They use *had to* to talk about past rules at primary school.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- The club rules use all three meanings correctly.
- Students no longer confuse *mustn't* and *don't have to* in the presentations.

## Teacher's reflection

- Was the *mustn't / don't have to* contrast clear?
- Did the club task produce accurate rules?
