GRAMMAR TOPIC: LINKING WORDS · B1 (Intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Contrast Linkers (although, even though, however, despite, in spite of)

*Lesson theme: Should Students Have Smartphones?*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Linking Words |
| Grammar point | Contrast Linkers (although, even though, however, despite, in spite of) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use but well. In writing they now try more formal linkers but mix their grammar: Despite it was raining, Although it was late, but we continued, However I like it (no comma, used as a conjunction). |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise the grammar of contrast linkers: although / even though + clause; despite / in spite of + noun or -ing; however + comma at the start of a new sentence. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise punctuation with each linker; to develop a short for-and-against paragraph. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can write a balanced paragraph using contrast linkers accurately, e.g. Although smartphones are useful for research, they can be distracting. Despite the risks, many parents buy them. However, schools should set clear rules. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (two students' opinions), Handout 2 (language focus), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (argument cards and writing frame). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Linker** | **What follows** | **Position and punctuation** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| although / even though | subject + verb (a clause) | start or middle; comma between clauses when it starts | Although it was raining, we played. / We played although it was raining. |
| despite / in spite of | noun / -ing / the fact that + clause | start or middle; comma when it starts | Despite the rain, we played. / In spite of feeling tired, she finished. |
| however | new sentence | start of the second sentence + comma | It was raining. However, we played. |
| but | clause | middle (comma before) | It was raining, but we played. |

- Never use *although* and *but* together: *Although it was late, we continued* (not *Although ..., but ...*).
- *even though* is stronger than *although* (more surprising).
- *despite* has **no** *of*: *despite the rain* (not *despite of*). *in spite of* has *of*.
- *despite the fact that* + clause: *Despite the fact that it was raining, we played.*

### Pronunciation

- Pause after *However*: *However, | we played.*
- *although* /ɔːlˈðəʊ/; *despite* /dɪˈspaɪt/.

### Concept checking questions

*Although it was raining, we played football.*

1. Was it raining? (Yes.) Did we play? (Yes.) Is that surprising? (A bit; you don't expect to play in the rain.)

*Despite the rain, we played.*

1. What comes after *despite*: a noun or a clause? (A noun: *the rain*.) Can I say *Despite it was raining*? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Despite + clause (Despite it was raining). | Board: despite + noun / -ing / the fact that. |
| Although ..., but ... | Cross out but: one linker per contrast. |
| however as a conjunction (I like it however it's expensive). | Board: full stop (or semicolon) + However + comma. |
| despite of | Board: in spite of / despite (no of). |

## Board plan

| **Left: + clause** | **Centre: + noun / -ing** | **Right: new sentence** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| although / even though + subject + verb · Although it was raining, we played. · We played although it was raining. · not: Although ..., but ... ✗ | despite / in spite of + noun / -ing · Despite the rain, we played. · In spite of feeling tired, she finished. · despite the fact that + clause · not: despite of ✗ · not: Despite it was raining ✗ | Sentence. However, sentence. · It was raining. However, we played. · not: ..., however we played ✗ |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students with the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Ask: *"At what age should children have their own smartphone?"* Quick class vote: under 12 / 12–15 / over 15.
2. Pairs give one reason for and one against (2 minutes).

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present contrast linkers in context and clarify their grammar and punctuation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the opinions of two students, Sachini and Yohan. Who is for smartphones? Who is against? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Find the contrast linkers in the opinions. What comes after each one: a clause (subject + verb) or a noun / -ing? Where are the commas?"*
   - ICQs: *"How many linkers are there?"* (Five.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers and build the three-column board.
2. Ask the CCQs.
3. **Transformation.** Board *It was raining, but we played.* and rewrite it with each linker together.
4. Board the three common errors and cross them out.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *However, | we played.* with a pause.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the grammar accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct linker. Exercise B: rewrite the sentences. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use contrast linkers in speaking and writing. **Interaction:** Pairs, then individual, then whole class

1. **Argue (6 minutes).** Give out Handout 4. Pairs take turns: Student A reads an argument card; Student B answers with a contrast: *"Phones help with homework." "Although they help with homework, they can be very distracting."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you agree or disagree with the card?"* (Give the other side.)
2. **Write (9 minutes).** Students write a for-and-against paragraph (100–120 words) using the writing frame and at least four different linkers.
3. Two students read their paragraphs aloud (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct them.
2. Quick fire: say a linker; students say *clause* or *noun / -ing*.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a for-and-against essay (180 words) on *"Should homework be banned?"* Use *although, even though, despite, in spite of* and *however* at least once each.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the three-column board on a card.
- Let them use only *although* and *however* in the paragraph.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *whereas / while* for comparison: *Some students use phones for study, whereas others only play games.*
- They use *nevertheless* and *on the other hand*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 3.
- The paragraph uses at least four different contrast linkers with correct grammar and punctuation.
- No *although ... but* and no *despite* + clause.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the transformation exercise make the grammar clear?
- Were students' paragraphs balanced?

# Handout 1: Two Opinions

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Sachini, 15: "Students should have smartphones."**

Although some people think phones are a distraction, I use mine for studying every day. I watch science videos and use a dictionary app. Despite the risks of social media, most teenagers use their phones sensibly. My parents can also call me after tuition classes. However, I agree that phones should be switched off during lessons.

**Yohan, 16: "Phones do more harm than good."**

Even though phones are useful, I think they cause more problems. In spite of having a phone for "studying", my cousin spends five hours a day on games. Many students sleep badly because they use their phones late at night. Phones are expensive, too. However, I understand that some parents want to contact their children.

**Answer the questions.**

1. How does Sachini use her phone for studying? ______________________________
2. What problem does Yohan's cousin have? ______________________________
3. What do both students agree about? ______________________________

| in spite of | -ing | In spite of having a phone |
