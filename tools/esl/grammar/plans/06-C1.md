*Lesson theme: A Report to the Principal*

## Language analysis

### Complex prepositions (+ noun / -ing, not a clause)

| **Function** | **Phrases** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Reference | with regard to, regarding, with respect to, as regards, in terms of | With regard to your email, ... |
| Cause | owing to, due to, on account of, as a result of, in view of, in the light of | Owing to the strike, trains were cancelled. |
| Contrast / concession | in spite of, despite, regardless of, notwithstanding, as opposed to, in contrast to | In spite of the cost, they bought it. |
| Means / agency | by means of, on behalf of, in accordance with, with the aid of | I'm writing on behalf of the class. |
| Addition / alternative | in addition to, as well as, instead of, apart from | In addition to the canteen, ... |

### Clauses vs phrases

- Clause after *because / although*: *because it was raining; although it was late*.
- Noun / -ing after complex prepositions: *due to the rain; in spite of being late; in spite of the fact that it was late*.

### Formal phrases

*under review, under consideration, in jeopardy, at stake, on the grounds that, in the event of, with a view to + -ing*.

### Prepositions in relative clauses

- Informal: *the person I spoke* ***to***. Formal: *the person* ***to whom*** *I spoke*.

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Due to it was raining | Board: phrase + noun; clause needs because or due to the fact that. |
| With regards of | Correct form: with regard to / regarding. |
| Over-formal, unnatural texts. | Discuss balance; limit to one phrase per sentence. |
| to who in formal relatives. | to whom after a preposition. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To raise awareness of formal language. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Pairs match the informal sentences to the formal versions (3 minutes).
2. Check. Ask: *"What makes the formal versions formal?"* Accept ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present complex prepositions and formal prepositional phrases in a model report and clarify their meaning and grammar. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (5 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the report. What are the main problems with the canteen? What does the council recommend?"*
2. Pairs compare answers.

**Part B: Analysis (7 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Find the complex prepositions and put them in the table by function."*
   - ICQs: *"How many functions?"* (Five columns.) *"Alone or in groups?"* (Groups.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the functions on the left of the board.
2. **Phrase vs clause.** Board *Due to it was raining* ✗. Ask: *"What's wrong?"* Elicit: complex prepositions take a noun or *-ing*; clauses need *because / although* or *the fact that*.
3. Show formal relative clauses: *the man I wrote to → the man to whom I wrote.*
4. Highlight formal phrases from the report: *under review, at stake, with a view to + -ing.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite using the phrase in brackets. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Feedback as a class.
3. **Exercise B:** pairs rewrite the informal paragraph formally. One pair reads theirs aloud.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To write a formal report using complex prepositions accurately. **Interaction:** Groups of three, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Say: *"You are the student council. Write a formal report (about 200 words) to the principal about the canteen survey and your recommendations. Use at least eight complex prepositions."*
   - ICQs: *"Who is the report for?"* (The principal.) *"How many complex prepositions?"* (At least eight.)
2. Groups plan (3 minutes) and write (10 minutes).
3. Two groups read their reports aloud. The class identifies the most formal-sounding phrases (5 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board strong phrases and two or three errors from the reports. Students correct the errors.
2. Discuss balance: *"Is it possible to be too formal?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a formal letter of complaint (220 words) using eight complex prepositions.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide a phrase bank grouped by function.
- Give a report template with headings.

**Stretch**

- Use *on the grounds that, with a view to + -ing, in the event of*.
- Compare phrases in a real policy document.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The final report uses at least eight complex prepositions accurately.
- No *due to / in spite of* + clause errors.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students over- or under-use formal phrases?
- Was the phrase vs clause distinction clear?

# Handout 2: Model Report

**Report on the School Canteen**

**Purpose:** This report has been prepared on behalf of the student council with regard to the school canteen. It is based on a survey of 300 students conducted in accordance with school guidelines.

**Findings:** In spite of recent improvements, only 35% of students are satisfied with the quality of food. Owing to rising costs, 72% feel that prices are too high. In addition to price, healthy eating is a major concern: 81% want more healthy options. Queues are also a problem on account of the short lunch break.

**Recommendations:** In the light of these findings, we recommend that the menu be placed under review. Regardless of the cost, fresh fruit should be available daily. With a view to reducing queues, a pre-order system could be introduced by means of a simple mobile app. The success of the canteen is at stake, and we would be grateful for the principal's support.
