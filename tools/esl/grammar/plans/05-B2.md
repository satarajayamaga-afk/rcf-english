# Articles with Place Names, Organisations and Generic Nouns

*Lesson theme: Geography and Inventions Quiz*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Articles |
| Grammar point | Articles with Place Names, Organisations and Generic Nouns |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who are generally accurate with articles but make errors with geographical names (the Sri Lanka, Himalayas) and don't know the generic singular the (The tiger is endangered). |
| Main aim | To improve students' use of articles with place names, organisations and generic singular nouns. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in team quiz games; to read encyclopedia-style texts. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can write and answer quiz questions about geography and inventions using articles correctly. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (encyclopedia extracts), Handout 2 (reference chart and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (quiz-writing sheet), a world map (optional). |

## Language analysis

### Place names

| **the** | **No article** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Oceans, seas, rivers, canals: the Indian Ocean, the Nile, the Suez Canal | Most countries: Sri Lanka, India, Japan |
| Mountain ranges and island groups: the Alps, the Himalayas, the Maldives | Single mountains: Mount Everest, Adam's Peak |
| Deserts: the Sahara | Lakes: Lake Victoria, Beira Lake |
| Countries with Republic / Kingdom / States / plural names: the USA, the UK, the Netherlands, the Philippines | Continents: Asia, Africa |
| Regions with of: the north of India, the Middle East | Cities, towns, streets: Colombo, Galle Road |
| Many buildings and organisations: the British Museum, the United Nations, the Taj Mahal | Buildings with a person's or place name + building type: Colombo Airport, Kandy Station, Buckingham Palace |

### Generic reference

- *The* + singular noun for a whole species or invention (formal / encyclopedic): **The tiger** is endangered. **The smartphone** changed our lives.
- Plural without article (general, neutral): **Tigers** are endangered.
- *A* + singular (any one example): **A tiger** can swim well.

### Pronunciation

- *the* /ði/ before vowels: *the Indian Ocean, the Alps, the USA* /ði ˌjuː es ˈeɪ/.

### Concept checking questions

*The tiger is endangered.*

1. Are we talking about one particular tiger? (No, tigers as a species.)
2. Is this formal or informal style? (Quite formal, like an encyclopedia.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| the Sri Lanka, the Mount Everest | Two-column chart kept on the board. |
| Missing the with ranges / oceans (Himalayas). | Point out plurals and water usually take the. |
| Generic the sounds wrong to students. | Show encyclopedia examples; compare with plural generic. |
| Exceptions (the Netherlands, The Hague). | Teach as exceptions; don't over-focus. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students with a quick quiz. **Interaction:** Teams

1. Ask three quick questions: *"What's the longest river in Africa? What's the highest mountain in the world? Which ocean is around Sri Lanka?"* Teams write answers.
2. Check the facts without focusing on articles yet.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present article use with place names and generic nouns in encyclopedia texts and clarify the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (5 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the encyclopedia extracts. What surprised you?"*
2. Pairs share one surprising fact.

**Part B: Noticing (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Find all the place names in the extracts. Put them in the table: the or no article."*
   - ICQs: *"Where do you find the names?"* (In Handout 1.) *"How many columns?"* (Two.)
2. Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (9 minutes)**

1. Build the chart on the board category by category: oceans and rivers; ranges and island groups; deserts; plural or *Republic / Kingdom / States* countries; organisations; versus countries, continents, single mountains, lakes, cities.
2. Present the generic *the* from the extracts (*The Asian elephant ..., The printing press ...*) and contrast with the plural generic (*Elephants are ...*) and *a* (*An elephant can ...*). Ask the CCQs.
3. Mention exceptions briefly: *the Netherlands, The Hague.*
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill *the* /ði/ before vowels: *the Indian Ocean, the Alps, the USA.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Say: *"Do Exercise A on Handout 2: correct the mistakes. Then Handout 3: write the or no article."* (7 minutes.)
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use articles accurately in a motivating team quiz. **Interaction:** Teams

1. Give out Handout 4. Teams write a 10-question quiz about geography and inventions. Each question must include at least one place name or generic noun (8 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"How many questions?"* (Ten.) *"What must each question include?"* (A place name or generic noun.)
2. Teams exchange quizzes and answer them (6 minutes).
3. Teams mark each other: 1 point for the correct fact and 1 point for correct articles (4 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Announce the winning team.
2. Review the trickiest items from the quizzes on the board.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a 150-word travel guide entry for a region you know, including at least eight place names.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker teams the chart during the quiz.
- Provide question frames: *Which river flows through ...? Who invented ...?*

**Stretch**

- Stronger teams research three exceptions and explain them.
- They write questions with generic *the*: *When was the telephone invented?*

## Assessment and success criteria

- Teams' quiz questions and answers use place-name articles accurately.
- Correct generic *the* in at least two quiz questions.
- 6 out of 8 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which categories were most problematic?
- Did the quiz motivate accurate article use?
