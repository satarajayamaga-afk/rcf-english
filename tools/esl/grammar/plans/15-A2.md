# How Much / How Many, Much, Many and A Lot Of

*Lesson theme: Cooking a Family Recipe*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Countable and Uncountable Nouns and Quantifiers |
| Grammar point | How Much / How Many, Much, Many and A Lot Of |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who know countable and uncountable nouns and some / any. They say How many sugar? and How much eggs?, use much in positive sentences (I drink much water) and say a lot of without of (a lot sugar). |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise How much ...? (uncountable) and How many ...? (countable), and much / many (negatives and questions) versus a lot of (all sentences, especially positive). |
| Subsidiary aims | To learn cooking vocabulary and quantities; to practise weak of /əv/ in a lot of; to develop speaking through a recipe information gap. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can ask and answer about the ingredients of a recipe, e.g. How much flour do we need? Not much, about 200 grams. How many eggs? Three. We need a lot of coconut milk. |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Countable (eggs, onions)** | **Uncountable (sugar, milk)** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Question | How many eggs do we need? | How much sugar do we need? |
| Negative | We don't need many eggs. | We don't need much sugar. |
| Positive | We need a lot of eggs. | We need a lot of sugar. |
| Short answer | Not many. / A lot. / Three. | Not much. / A lot. / 200 grams. |

- *How much* + uncountable; *How many* + plural countable.
- *much / many*: mainly in **negatives and questions**. In positive sentences, use *a lot of* (*I drink a lot of water*, not *I drink much water*).
- *a lot of* works with both countable and uncountable nouns.
- *How much is it? / How much are they?* = price (a related use).

### Pronunciation

- *a lot of* /ə ˈlɒt əv/, often /əˈlɒtə/ before consonants: *a lot of sugar* /əˈlɒtə ˈʃʊgə/.
- *How much* and *How many* questions fall: *How many eggs do we need? ↘*

### Concept checking questions

*How much sugar do we need?*

1. Can we count sugar? (No.) So *much* or *many*? (*Much*.)

*We need a lot of eggs.*

1. Is this a big number or a small number? (Big.)
2. Is this sentence positive or negative? (Positive.) So can we say *many eggs* here? (It's possible, but *a lot of* is more natural.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| How many sugar / How much eggs. | Colour-code: blue = countable → many; red = uncountable → much. |
| much in positive sentences (I drink much water). | Board: + a lot of · – not much / not many · ? much / many. |
| a lot sugar (missing of). | Drill a lot of as one chunk /əˈlɒtə/. |
| Plural noun missing after How many (How many egg?). | Highlight the -s: How many eggs? |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate vocabulary and engage students. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Ask: *"What's your favourite cake or sweet? Can you make it?"*
2. Pairs tell each other (2 minutes). Take ideas. Board three dishes the class mentions.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present *how much / how many, much / many* and *a lot of* in context and clarify the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Kasun and his grandmother are making love cake. Read the conversation. How many eggs do they need? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students complete the ingredients list. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Find all the questions with How. Which ones have much? Which have many? Why?"* Pairs discuss.

**Part C: Clarification (9 minutes)**

1. **How much / How many.** Board two questions from the text. Elicit: *many* + countable, *much* + uncountable. Ask the CCQs.
2. **much / many / a lot of.** Board three sentences from the text: *We need a lot of eggs. We don't need much time. Do we need many cashew nuts?* Add + / – / ? symbols.
3. Board *I drink much water* ✗ → *I drink a lot of water*.
4. **Short answers.** Elicit *Not much. Not many. A lot.*
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *a lot of* /əˈlɒtə/ and the questions with falling intonation.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: much or many? Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Rice: much or many?"* (Much.)
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language in a recipe information gap and a personal recipe. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. **Information gap (8 minutes).** Give out Handout 3. Student A and Student B have the same recipe (pol roti) with different information missing. They ask and answer: *"How much flour do we need?" "About 500 grams." "How many onions?" "Two."*
   - ICQs: *"Can you look at your partner's card?"* (No.)
2. **My family recipe (6 minutes).** Students write a short ingredient list for a dish from their family on Handout 4. In groups of three, they ask each other: *"How much rice do you use? Do you use a lot of chilli?"*
3. **Report (4 minutes).** Two students tell the class about a partner's recipe.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Quick quiz: say a noun; students make a *How much / How many* question.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write the recipe for your favourite dish (60–80 words). Include a list of ingredients and 4 questions and answers with *How much* and *How many*.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a list of the nouns marked C (countable) or U (uncountable).
- In the information gap, give them the question frames *How much ... do we need? / How many ... do we need?*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *too much / too many* and *not enough*: *There's too much sugar!*
- They use *lots of* and *plenty of* as alternatives to *a lot of*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2.
- In the information gap, students use *How much* with uncountable nouns and *How many* with countable nouns.
- Students use *a lot of* (not *much*) in positive sentences.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students stop using *much* in positive sentences?
- Was the information gap well balanced?
