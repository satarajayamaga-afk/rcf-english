# Defining Relative Clauses (who, which, that, where; leaving out the pronoun)

*Lesson theme: My Favourite People and Places*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Relative Clauses |
| Grammar point | Defining Relative Clauses (who, which, that, where; leaving out the pronoun) |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who can define words with a person who and a thing that. They don't use where for places, they repeat the object (the book that I read it), and they don't know that the pronoun can be left out. |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise defining relative clauses with who, which, that (subject and object) and where, and leaving out the pronoun when it is the object. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read a personal blog for specific information; to develop speaking about favourite people, places and things. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can describe their favourite people, places and things, e.g. Kandy is the city where I was born. Amma is the person who makes the best curry. This is the song (that) I listen to every morning. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (blog: My Favourite Things), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (personal survey), Handout 4 (sentence-joining cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Pronoun** | **Used for** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| who / that | people | She's the teacher who helped me. |
| which / that | things | It's the book which changed my life. |
| where | places | This is the café where we first met. |

### Subject or object?

|  | **Example** | **Can we leave out the pronoun?** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Subject (pronoun + verb) | the girl who lives next door | No: the girl lives next door ✗ |
| Object (pronoun + subject + verb) | the film (that) I watched | Yes: the film I watched ✓ |

- Don't repeat the object: *the book that I read it* ✗.
- *where* = *in which / at which*: *the town where I live*.

### Meaning and use

- Defining relative clauses tell us **which** person, thing or place we mean. Without them, the sentence is incomplete or unclear: *That's the girl.* (Which girl?) *That's the girl who won the race.*

### Pronunciation

- *who, that, which* are weak in connected speech: *the girl who lives next door* /ðə ɡɜːl hu lɪvz/.
- When the pronoun is left out, the clause follows the noun without a pause: *the film I watched*.

### Concept checking questions

*This is the café where we first met.*

1. Is the café a person, a thing or a place? (A place.) So which word? (*where*.)

*That's the film I watched yesterday.*

1. Who watched the film? (I did.) Is *I* the subject of *watched*? (Yes.)
2. Can we add *that*? (Yes: *the film that I watched*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Repeating the object (the book that I read it). | Board the error; cross out it. |
| Leaving out a subject pronoun (the girl lives next door is ...). | Test: Is there a new subject after the pronoun? If not, keep it. |
| which for places (the town which I live). | Board where for places + subject + verb. |
| which for people. | People → who / that. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Show a photo (real or described) of your favourite place. Say: *"This is the beach where I go every weekend."*
2. Ask: *"What's your favourite place? Your favourite food?"* Pairs share (2 minutes).

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present defining relative clauses in context and clarify form, meaning and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read Nethmi's blog. What is her favourite place? Who is her favourite person? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline who, which, that and where. Circle the noun before each one: is it a person, a thing or a place?"* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **who / which / that / where.** Build the three columns on the board from the blog examples.
2. **Subject or object.** Board *the teacher who helped me* and *the song (that) I listen to*. Ask the CCQs. Show the test: if there is a new subject after the pronoun (*I*), you can leave it out.
3. Find a sentence in the blog without a pronoun (*the curry my grandmother makes*). Ask: *"Where's the pronoun? Can we add one?"*
4. **No repeated object.** Board *the book that I read it* ✗.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *the girl who lives next door, the town where I was born, the film I watched.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the pronoun. Exercise B: can you leave it out? Exercise C: join the sentences. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use defining relative clauses in personalised speaking and writing. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then groups

1. Give out Handout 3. Students complete the sentences about their own lives (5 minutes): *The place where I feel happiest is ... · The person who makes me laugh most is ...*
   - ICQs: *"Do you write about you or your partner?"* (About me.)
2. **Pairs (6 minutes).** Students share their answers and ask a follow-up question each time: *"Why is the beach the place where you feel happiest?"*
3. **Groups (5 minutes).** Students use the cards (Handout 4) to make a chain of definitions about their town: *Kandy is the city where ... The Temple of the Tooth is the place that ...*
4. Two students share their most interesting sentence (2 minutes).
5. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences heard in Stage 4 (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Ask: *"When can we leave out who or that?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a blog post (100 words): *My favourite things*. Use at least six relative clauses with *who, which, that* and *where*, and leave out the pronoun at least once.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the three-column table on a card.
- In Handout 3, let them complete only the first four sentences.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *whose* (preview): *She's the friend whose mother is a doctor.*
- They use prepositions at the end: *the friend I go to school with*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 2.
- Students use *where* for places and leave out object pronouns correctly.
- No repeated objects in the written sentences.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students understand when the pronoun can be left out?
- Was the personal survey motivating?

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Nethmi's Blog: My Favourite Things**

My favourite place is Unawatuna. It's the beach where my family goes every holiday. There's a small café there which sells the best mango juice in the world!

My favourite person is my grandmother. She's the person who taught me to cook. The curry my grandmother makes is better than any restaurant curry.

My favourite teacher is Mr Perera. He's the teacher who helped me with English when I was in Grade 6. He gave me a book that I still read every year: *Charlotte's Web*.

My favourite song is one that my brother wrote for me on my birthday. It's the song I listen to every morning!

1. Where does Nethmi's family go every holiday? ______________________________
2. Who taught Nethmi to cook? ______________________________
3. Which book does she still read every year? ______________________________
