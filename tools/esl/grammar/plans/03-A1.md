# Present Perfect for Experiences (Have you ever ...?)

*Lesson theme: Our Experiences*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Perfect Tenses |
| Grammar point | Present Perfect for Experiences (Have you ever ...?) |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Beginners (high A1) who can use the present simple and the past simple of common verbs. They have not studied the present perfect and cannot ask about life experiences. |
| Main aim | To present and practise the present perfect with ever and never to talk about life experiences. |
| Subsidiary aims | To learn ten common past participles; to practise short answers; to develop speaking through a class survey. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can ask Have you ever ...?, answer with short answers and say 5 sentences about their own experiences. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (speech bubbles), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (class survey), picture cards: a plane, snow, an elephant, the sea, a mountain, a famous building. |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Positive** | **Negative** | **Question** | **Short answers** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| I / You / We / They | I have (I've) been to Kandy. | I haven't been to India. / I've never been to India. | Have you ever been to India? | Yes, I have. / No, I haven't. |
| He / She / It | She has (She's) eaten durian. | He hasn't eaten sushi. / He's never eaten sushi. | Has she ever eaten durian? | Yes, she has. / No, she hasn't. |

**have / has + past participle.** Common participles:

| **Base verb** | **Past participle** | **Base verb** | **Past participle** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| be | been | see | seen |
| eat | eaten | ride | ridden |
| go | been / gone | swim | swum |
| fly | flown | meet | met |
| visit | visited | play | played |

### Meaning and use

- We use the present perfect for **experiences in our life up to now**. The exact time is not important and is not said.
- *ever* = at any time in your life (in questions). *never* = at no time (in positive sentences with negative meaning).
- If we say **when**, we change to the past simple: *I've been to Galle. I went there last year.*

### Pronunciation

- Contractions: *I've* /aɪv/, *she's* /ʃiːz/, *haven't* /ˈhævnt/.
- *Have you ever* is joined: /həv juː ˈevə/.
- Short answers are stressed: *Yes, I HAVE.*

### Concept checking questions

*I have been to Kandy.*

1. Did I go to Kandy? (Yes.)
2. Do we know when? (No.)
3. Is the time important? (No, it's about my life.)

*I've never seen snow.*

1. Have I seen snow in my life? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Using the past simple form after have (Have you ever went?). | Board the participle table; drill go – went – been. |
| Using a time word with the present perfect (I have been to Galle last year). | Show the rule: time = past simple. |
| never + negative verb (I haven't never ...). | Board: never = not ever; one negative only. |
| been vs gone confusion. | At A1, teach been for experiences only; note gone later. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and introduce the idea of life experiences. **Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Hold up the picture cards one by one: a plane, snow, an elephant, the sea, a mountain, a famous building.
2. For each, ask: *"Who has seen this? Raise your hand!"* Students raise hands. Count them: *"Ten students have seen the sea!"*
3. Say: *"Interesting! Let's read about some teenagers' experiences."*

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Part A: Context (8 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To present the target language in context. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the speech bubbles. Match each person to an experience."* (3 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"Do you write or match?"* (Match.) *"Alone or in pairs?"* (Alone.)
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.
3. Ask detail questions: *"Has Nadia been to India?"* Model the answer: *"Yes, she has."*

**Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the present perfect for experiences. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. **Meaning.** Board *I have been to Kandy.* Ask the CCQs. Draw a timeline from birth to NOW with a cross somewhere in between and a question mark under it: *"When? Not important."*
2. **Form.** Elicit: *have / has + past participle*. Highlight *has* for he/she. Elicit participles from Handout 1 and complete the participle table on the left of the board.
3. **Negative and never.** Board *I've never flown in a plane* and *I haven't flown in a plane*. Ask: *"Same meaning?"* (Yes.) Point out: never = no *n't*.
4. **Questions.** Board *Have you ever seen snow?* Elicit short answers.
5. **The time rule.** Board *I've been to Galle. I went there last year.* Ask: *"Which sentence has a time?"* (The second.) *"Which tense?"* (Past simple.)
6. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *Have you ever been to Jaffna? Yes, I have. / No, I haven't.* Use back-chaining: *Jaffna – been to Jaffna – ever been to Jaffna – Have you ever been to Jaffna?*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs

1. Students complete Handout 2, Exercises A and B (6 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"In Exercise A, do you write have or has?"* (Both; look at the person.)
2. Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers. Drill any difficult participles.
3. **Quick-fire drill:** say a base verb; students say the participle (*eat → eaten, see → seen*).

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language in a personalised speaking task. **Interaction:** Mingle, then groups, then individual

1. Give out Handout 3. Model with a strong student: *"Have you ever ridden an elephant?" "No, I haven't." "Have you ever eaten durian?" "Yes, I have."* Write the name in the box.
2. Say: *"Stand up. Ask your classmates. Find a name for each question. When someone says yes, ask: When? Use the past simple!"* (8 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"What do you ask when they say yes?"* (When?)
3. Monitor and note errors.
4. Groups of four compare results and make a class poster: *Five students have been to Jaffna. Nobody has flown in a helicopter.* (5 minutes.)
5. Students write five sentences about their own experiences at the bottom of Handout 3 (4 minutes).

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Groups read their posters.
2. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Pairs correct.
3. Set homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write 6 sentences about things you have and haven't done. Write one past simple sentence giving more information about one experience.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a participle list during the survey.
- Allow them to ask the questions on the sheet without follow-up questions.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students ask two follow-up questions in the past simple (*When did you go? Who did you go with?*).
- They write a short paragraph: *Three things I've never done but want to do.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students form *Have you ever ...?* questions correctly during the survey.
- Students use the correct participle in 8 of 10 items on Handout 2.
- Students switch to the past simple when they give a time.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which participles caused problems?
- Did students switch to the past simple for details?
