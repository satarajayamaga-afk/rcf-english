# Past Perfect (Narrative Tenses)

*Lesson theme: Telling a Story: The Day I Missed My Flight*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Past Tenses |
| Grammar point | Past Perfect (Narrative Tenses) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use the past simple and past continuous but rarely use the past perfect, so their stories are hard to follow when events are not told in order. |
| Main aim | To help students understand and use the past simple, past continuous and past perfect together to tell a clear personal story. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise sequencing words; to develop fluency in storytelling; to practise contracted 'd. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can tell a two-minute personal story using all three narrative tenses and at least four sequencers. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (story strips, cut up), Handout 2 (language focus), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (story prompt cards and listener sheet). |

## Language analysis

### Form and use

| **Tense** | **Form** | **Use in stories** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Past simple | verb-ed / irregular | Main events, in order | I arrived at the airport at 6. |
| Past continuous | was / were + verb-ing | Background, scene-setting, interrupted actions | It was raining and everyone was rushing. |
| Past perfect | had + past participle | An action before another past action | I realised I had left my passport at home. |

Sequencers: *at first, then, after that, suddenly, as soon as, by the time, eventually, in the end*.

### Pronunciation

- *had* is usually contracted in speech: *I'd left* /aɪd left/, *she'd gone*. Students often don't hear the *'d*.
- Weak *had* in full form: /əd/.

### Concept checking questions

*When I got to the gate, the plane had left.*

1. Which happened first: I got to the gate, or the plane left? (The plane left.)
2. Did I see the plane leave? (No.)
3. Which tense shows the earlier action? (Past perfect.)

*It was raining when I left home.*

1. Did the rain start before I left? (Yes.)
2. Is it a main event in the story or the background? (Background.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Not using the past perfect (When I arrived, the plane left = it left as I arrived). | Contrast the two meanings with timelines; CCQs. |
| Using past perfect for everything. | Emphasise: only for the earlier event, when the order is not clear. |
| Confusing 'd (had / would). | Show that 'd + past participle = had. |
| Stories are too short. | Give prompt questions on the story cards; listeners must ask two follow-up questions. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage interest and personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"Have you ever had a problem on a journey? Missed a bus, lost a ticket?"* Give your own one-sentence example.
2. Students share with a partner in one or two sentences (2 minutes).
3. Take two examples from the class.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present narrative tenses in a story and help students work out when to use the past simple, past continuous and past perfect. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

**Part A: Story strips (6 minutes)**

1. Give each pair the cut-up story strips (Handout 1). Say: *"This story is called The Day I Missed My Flight. Put the strips in order. Look for clues in the verbs and the sequencing words."*
   - ICQs: *"How many strips?"* (Eight.) *"What clues help you?"* (Verbs and sequencing words.)
2. Pairs compare with another pair. Read the story aloud in the correct order for students to check. Ask: *"Did the story have a happy ending?"*

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the four sentences from the story. Answer the questions with your partner."*
2. Monitor. Help struggling pairs by asking: *"Which happened first? Draw it."* Pairs draw timelines for sentences 3 and 4.

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit the answers. Build the timeline and rules on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. **Meaning contrast:** board *When I got to the gate, the plane left* vs *When I got to the gate, the plane had left.* Ask: *"In which sentence did I see the plane leave?"* (The first.)
4. **Form:** had + past participle; contraction *'d*. Elicit three irregular participles (*left, forgotten, gone*).
5. **Pronunciation:** drill *I'd left my passport at home*, slowly and then at natural speed.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing and forming the tenses accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: complete the story. Exercise B: join the sentences with the past perfect. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class and ask for timeline explanations for two items.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use narrative tenses fluently in personal stories. **Interaction:** Groups of three, then whole class

1. Give each group a set of story cards (Handout 4). Each student chooses one card and plans a story for 2 minutes, making notes (not full sentences).
2. Model briefly: tell the first two sentences of your own story using all three tenses.
3. Students take turns to tell their stories (about 2 minutes each). Listeners complete the listener sheet: tick each tense they hear and write one follow-up question.
   - ICQs: *"What do listeners tick?"* (The tenses they hear.) *"What do they write?"* (A follow-up question.)
4. After each story, listeners ask their questions.
5. Each group nominates the best story; one or two are retold briefly to the class.
6. Monitor and note errors and good examples.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board errors and good sentences from your notes for students to correct and notice.
2. Ask students to explain the past perfect to a partner in one sentence.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a 150-word story that begins: *It was a perfectly normal day until ...*

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a story skeleton: *It was ... (background). I was ... when ... . I realised I had ... . In the end, ...*
- Allow them to write their story notes in more detail.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add feelings with adjectives and adverbs (*terrified, luckily, unfortunately*).
- They try the past perfect continuous: *I had been waiting for an hour.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students use the past perfect correctly at least once in their story.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- Listeners can identify all three tenses in a partner's story.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the story strips work as a context?
- Could students explain the past perfect in their own words?

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| When I arrived, the film already started. | When I arrived, the film had already started. |
| I was knowing him for years. | I had known him for years. |
| Suddenly, I was hearing a noise. | Suddenly, I heard a noise. |
| I had went home. | I had gone home. |
