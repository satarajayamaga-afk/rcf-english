# Indirect Questions (Could you tell me where ...? Do you know if ...?)

*Lesson theme: At the Tourist Information Desk*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Question Forms |
| Grammar point | Indirect Questions (Could you tell me where ...? Do you know if ...?) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who form direct questions accurately. When they try to be polite with strangers they keep question word order and auxiliaries (Could you tell me where is the station? Do you know what time does the bus leave?) or sound too direct. |
| Main aim | To help students work out and use indirect questions (Could you tell me / Do you know / I was wondering / Can you tell me + wh- word or if / whether + statement word order) for polite enquiries. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise polite intonation; to develop fluency in a service-encounter role-play. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can make polite enquiries as tourists and staff, e.g. Could you tell me what time the train leaves? Do you know if the museum is open on Sundays? I was wondering whether you have a map. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (two dialogues: direct and polite), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (role cards and information sheets). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Direct question** | **Indirect question** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Where is the station? | Could you tell me where the station is? |
| What time does the bus leave? | Do you know what time the bus leaves? |
| Is the museum open? | Do you know if / whether the museum is open? |
| Can I pay by card? | I was wondering if I could pay by card. |
| How much did it cost? | Can you tell me how much it cost? |

- Opening phrases: *Could you tell me ...? Can you tell me ...? Do you know ...? Do you have any idea ...? I was wondering ... · I'd like to know ...*
- After the opening phrase: **statement word order** (subject + verb); **no do / does / did**.
- Yes / no questions: **if** or **whether**.
- The question mark depends on the opening: *Could you tell me ...?* (question) but *I was wondering if ...* (statement, full stop).

### Meaning and use

- Indirect questions are more polite and less direct; they are used with strangers, in shops and offices, and in formal situations.

### Pronunciation

- Polite intonation: a wide pitch range and a gentle rise: *Could you tell me where the station is? ↗*
- *Could you* /kʊdʒə/; *Do you know* /djə nəʊ/.

### Concept checking questions

*Could you tell me where the station is?*

1. Is this more or less polite than *Where is the station?* (More polite.)
2. Where is the verb *is*: before or after *the station*? (After.)

*Do you know if the museum is open?*

1. Is this a yes / no question or a wh- question? (Yes / no.) Which word do we add? (*if*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Question word order after the opening (where is the station). | Board an arrow moving the verb back after the subject. |
| Keeping do / does / did (what time does the bus leave). | Cross out does and add -s to the verb. |
| Missing if / whether. | Rule: no question word → if / whether. |
| Wrong punctuation (I was wondering if ...?). | Show that the punctuation follows the opening phrase. |
| Flat, impolite intonation. | Drill with exaggerated pitch range. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic of politeness. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Say two questions to the class, one rudely and one politely: *"Where's the toilet?"* and *"Excuse me, could you tell me where the toilet is?"*
2. Ask: *"Which is more polite? Why?"* Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Take ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present indirect questions in context and help students work out the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the two dialogues at the tourist information desk in Kandy. Which tourist gets better help? Why?"* (3 minutes.)
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Compare the direct and indirect questions. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Which dialogue has the indirect questions?"* (Dialogue 2.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"Where is the verb? Where is does?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit the answers and build the patterns in the centre of the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Highlight: statement word order; no *do / does / did*; *if / whether*; punctuation.
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill polite intonation with a wide pitch range: *Excuse me, could you tell me where the station is? Do you know if the museum's open today?*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: make the questions polite. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check and read their questions aloud with polite intonation. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use indirect questions fluently in a service role-play. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Student A is a tourist with a list of things to find out; Student B works at the tourist information desk and has an information sheet. Tourists must use a different opening phrase for each question (7 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Can the tourist use 'Where is ...?'"* (No, use indirect questions.) *"How many different openings?"* (A different one each time.)
2. Swap roles with the second set of cards (7 minutes).
3. Two pairs perform for the class. The class listens for indirect questions and polite intonation (4 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board questions from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"What two things change in an indirect question?"* (Word order; no *do / does / did*.)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a polite email (120 words) to a hotel asking at least five questions about your stay. Use five different indirect question openings.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the opening phrases on a card.
- Let them use only *Could you tell me ...?* and *Do you know if ...?*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *I was wondering whether* and *Would you happen to know ...?*
- They answer politely with *I'm afraid I don't know whether ...*

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the role-play, students use statement word order in indirect questions.
- Students use *if / whether* for yes / no questions.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students drop *do / does* in indirect questions?
- Did their intonation sound polite?

# Handout 1: At the Tourist Information Desk

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Dialogue 1**

**Tourist 1:** Where is the Temple of the Tooth? **Assistant:** Over there. Ten minutes' walk. **Tourist 1:** What time does it close? **Assistant:** 8 pm. **Tourist 1:** Can I take photos? **Assistant:** No.

**Dialogue 2**

**Tourist 2:** Good morning. Could you tell me where the Temple of the Tooth is? **Assistant:** Of course! It's just next to the lake, about ten minutes' walk from here. I'll show you on the map. **Tourist 2:** Thank you. Do you know what time it closes? **Assistant:** It's open until 8 pm. The evening ceremony is at 6.30. It's beautiful. **Tourist 2:** Lovely. I was wondering if I could take photos inside. **Assistant:** You can take photos in most areas, but not in the inner shrine. And please remember to cover your shoulders and knees. **Tourist 2:** That's very helpful. Do you have any idea how much a tuk-tuk to the botanical gardens costs? **Assistant:** About 1,000 rupees. Enjoy your day!

**Answer the questions.**

1. Which tourist gets more information? Why? ______________________________
2. What time is the evening ceremony? ______________________________
3. Where can't visitors take photos? ______________________________
