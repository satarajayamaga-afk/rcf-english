# Separable, Inseparable and Intransitive Phrasal Verbs for Travel (set off, look after, run out of)

*Lesson theme: A Road Trip That Went Wrong*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Phrasal Verbs |
| Grammar point | Separable, Inseparable and Intransitive Phrasal Verbs for Travel (set off, look after, run out of) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use separable phrasal verbs with pronouns correctly (turn it off). They now overgeneralise and separate every phrasal verb (look my sister after, get the bus on), add objects to intransitive ones (The car broke down the engine), and drop the preposition in three-part verbs (We ran out petrol). |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise three types of phrasal verbs: intransitive (no object: set off, break down, get back), separable (pick someone up, fill it up, drop someone off) and inseparable, including three-part verbs (look after, get on / off, run out of, look forward to). |
| Subsidiary aims | To learn travel phrasal verbs; to develop narrative speaking through a "story chain"; to practise using a dictionary to check verb type. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can tell a travel story using phrasal verbs with correct word order, e.g. We set off at 5. On the way, the van broke down. A farmer picked us up and dropped us off in Ella. We ran out of money, but we're looking forward to going back. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (a blog post), Handout 2 (language focus), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (story chain cards). |

## Language analysis

### Three types

| **Type** | **Word order** | **Examples** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Intransitive (no object) | verb + particle | set off (start a journey), break down (stop working: a car), get back (return), check in (at a hotel), stop off (stop briefly) |
| Separable (object can go in the middle; pronoun must) | verb + object + particle / verb + particle + noun | pick someone up (collect), drop someone off (take and leave), fill it up (petrol tank), put up a tent / put it up, call someone back |
| Inseparable (object always after) | verb + particle + object | look after (take care of), get on / get off (a bus or train), come across (find by chance) |
| Three-part (always inseparable) | verb + particle + preposition + object | run out of (have none left), look forward to (+ noun / -ing), get on with (have a good relationship), catch up with (reach) |

- A good dictionary shows the type: *pick sb/sth up* (separable), *look after sb* (inseparable).
- *look forward to* + **-ing**: *I'm looking forward to seeing you* (not *to see*).
- Some verbs have more than one meaning and type: *pick up* (collect someone, separable) / *The weather picked up* (intransitive: improved).

### Pronunciation

- Stress usually falls on the particle: *set OFF, break DOWN*. In three-part verbs, stress the particle: *run OUT of, look FORward to*.

### Concept checking questions

*The van broke down.*

1. Can the van move now? (No.) Is there an object? (No.) Can we say *broke down the engine*? (No.)

*Could you look after my dog?*

1. Can I say *look my dog after*? (No, it's inseparable.)

*We ran out of petrol.*

1. Do we have any petrol now? (No.) Can we say *ran out petrol*? (No, we need *of*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Separating inseparable verbs (look my sister after). | Three colour-coded board columns; check type in a dictionary. |
| Adding objects to intransitive verbs. | "Does it need what?" test. |
| Missing preposition (run out petrol). | Teach three-part verbs as chunks. |
| look forward to see | Board: to is a preposition here → -ing. |
| get on vs get in. | get on / off a bus, train, plane, bike; get in / out of a car, taxi. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and activate travel vocabulary. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Ask: *"Have you ever had a problem on a trip? What happened?"*
2. Pairs share (2 minutes). Take two stories briefly.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the three types of phrasal verbs in context and clarify word order. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read Ishara's blog about a road trip to Ella. What went wrong? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Find the phrasal verbs in bold. Do they have an object? Can the object go in the middle? Put them in the table."*
   - ICQs: *"How many columns?"* (Three / four.) *"Where do you find the verbs?"* (In bold in the blog.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers and build the three columns on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs.
3. Highlight *look forward to* + *-ing* and *get on / off* vs *get in / out of*.
4. Show how a learner's dictionary marks the type (*pick sb/sth up*, *look after sb*).
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *We set OFF at 5. The van broke DOWN. We ran OUT of petrol.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the word order accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: put the words in order. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the phrasal verbs fluently in narratives. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then whole class

1. **Story chain (10 minutes).** Give out Handout 4. Groups of four share a set of phrasal verb cards face down. Student 1 starts a travel story: *"Last year my family set off for Jaffna ..."*. Each student turns over a card and continues the story using that verb. Groups do two rounds.
   - ICQs: *"Can you choose your card?"* (No, turn over the next one.) *"What must you use?"* (The verb on the card.)
2. **Retell (8 minutes).** Each group writes the best version of its story in 8–10 sentences and one student tells it to the class. The class listens for word order mistakes.
3. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct them.
2. Quick test: say a verb; students say *separable, inseparable* or *no object*.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a blog post (150 words) about a trip that went wrong (real or imaginary). Use at least eight phrasal verbs from today, including two with pronouns and two three-part verbs.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students Handout 2 (completed) as a reference in Stage 4.
- Let them focus on six verbs: *set off, break down, pick up, look after, get on, run out of*.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *come up with, put up with, turn up* (arrive), *hold up* (delay).
- They use a dictionary to classify five new phrasal verbs.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3.
- In the story, students do not separate inseparable verbs and do not add objects to intransitive verbs.
- Students use *of* in *run out of* and *-ing* after *look forward to*.

## Teacher's reflection

- Was the three-column board clear?
- Did the story chain produce natural use of the verbs?

# Handout 1: Ishara's Travel Blog

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**The road trip from (almost) hell**

Last April, four of us decided to drive from Colombo to Ella in my uncle's old van. My little sister wanted to come, but someone had to **look after** our grandmother, so she stayed at home.

We **set off** at 5 am. First, we **picked** my friend Nuwan **up** in Ratnapura. Then we **stopped off** for breakfast. Just after Balangoda, we **ran out of** petrol! We walked for 20 minutes to a petrol station, bought a can of petrol and walked back.

An hour later, the van **broke down** on a mountain road. Luckily, a tea farmer **came across** us and took us to his village. His son fixed the van and **filled it up** with petrol, and they didn't want any money!

We finally arrived in Ella at 7 pm and **checked in** at our guest house. The next day, we **got on** the famous blue train to Nanu Oya, and **got off** to take photos.

We **got back** to Colombo two days late, but I'm already **looking forward to** going again (by train next time!).

1. Why didn't Ishara's sister go? ______________________________
2. What two problems did they have with the van? ______________________________
3. How did the farmer's son help? ______________________________
