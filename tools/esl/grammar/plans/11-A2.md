GRAMMAR TOPIC: COMPARATIVES AND SUPERLATIVES · A2 (Elementary) · 60 minutes

# Superlative Adjectives (the biggest, the most beautiful)

*Lesson theme: Record Breakers*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Comparatives and Superlatives |
| Grammar point | Superlative Adjectives (the biggest, the most beautiful) |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who use comparatives with than. They form superlatives without the (It is biggest), use more instead of most, and use of or than instead of in (the tallest of the world). |
| Main aim | To present and practise superlative adjectives (the + -est / the most + adjective, the best / the worst) with in and of, and to contrast them with comparatives. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read a short fact text for specific information; to develop speaking through a quiz and a class survey (the ... I've ever ... as a fixed phrase). |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can ask and answer quiz questions such as What's the longest river in the world? and describe superlatives in their own lives. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (world records fact file), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (record quiz), Handout 4 (class superlatives survey). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Adjective** | **Comparative** | **Superlative** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| tall | taller | the tallest |
| big | bigger | the biggest |
| happy | happier | the happiest |
| nice | nicer | the nicest |
| expensive | more expensive | the most expensive |
| good | better | the best |
| bad | worse | the worst |
| far | further / farther | the furthest / farthest |

- **the + -est / the most + adjective**.
- **in** + a place or group: *the tallest building* ***in*** *the world, the best student* ***in*** *the class*.
- **of** + a period or number: *the hottest day* ***of*** *the year, the best* ***of*** *the three*.
- *the ... I've ever ...* (fixed phrase): *It's the best film I've ever seen.*

### Meaning and use

- Superlatives compare **one thing with all the others** in a group (three or more).
- Comparatives compare **two** things.

### Pronunciation

- *-est* is weak: /ɪst/: *tallest* /ˈtɔːlɪst/. *the* /ðə/ before consonants, /ði/ before vowels: *the oldest* /ði ˈəʊldɪst/.

### Concept checking questions

*Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.*

1. Are we comparing two mountains or all mountains? (All mountains.)
2. Is there a higher mountain? (No.)

*K2 is higher than Mont Blanc.*

1. How many mountains? (Two.) Comparative or superlative? (Comparative.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Missing the (It's biggest). | Board the in colour; drill. |
| more instead of most (the more beautiful). | Table with three columns side by side. |
| of the world | Board: in + place / group. |
| Confusing comparatives and superlatives. | Two vs all the others; controlled practice mixes both. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To create interest and review comparatives. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Write *Sigiriya · Adam's Peak · Pidurutalagala* on the board. Ask: *"Which is higher: Sigiriya or Adam's Peak?"* (Adam's Peak.)
2. Ask: *"Which is the highest of the three?"* (Pidurutalagala.) Let students guess.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present superlatives in context and clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the record facts. Which fact is the most surprising? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline all the superlatives. What comes before them? What comes after them?"* Pairs compare. Elicit *the* before; *in / of* after.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **Meaning.** Board *Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.* Ask the CCQs. Contrast with a comparative (two things).
2. **Form.** Build the three-column table on the board: adjective, comparative, superlative. Elicit the rules from the comparatives they already know.
3. **in / of.** Board *the highest mountain in the world* and *the hottest day of the year.* Board *of the world* ✗.
4. **the ... I've ever ...** Board *It's the best film I've ever seen.* Practise with two students.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *the tallest, the oldest, the most dangerous, the best in the world.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: complete the table. Exercise B: comparative or superlative? Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use superlatives in a quiz and in personalised speaking. **Interaction:** Teams, then mingle

1. **Record quiz (8 minutes).** Give out Handout 3. Teams complete the questions with superlatives and choose the answers. Then teams take turns to ask another team.
   - ICQs: *"First you write the question, then you ...?"* (Choose the answer.)
2. **Class superlatives (8 minutes).** Give out Handout 4. Students mingle and ask: *"What's the best film you've ever seen? What's the most delicious food you've ever eaten?"* They write names and answers.
3. Students report one interesting answer: *"Ravi says the best film he's ever seen is Spider-Man."* (2 minutes.)
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences heard in Stage 4 (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Ask: *"Two things: comparative or superlative? All the others?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a short paragraph (80 words) about your country's records: the highest mountain, the longest river, the biggest city, the most popular sport, the best food.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the three-column table during the quiz.
- In the survey, let them answer with short phrases.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *one of the + superlative + plural noun*: *Colombo is one of the busiest cities in Asia.*
- They use *the least*: *the least expensive restaurant*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 2, Exercise B.
- Students use *the* and *in / of* correctly in quiz questions.
- Students choose comparative or superlative correctly.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students remember *the*?
- Was the quiz motivating?
