# Question Tags, Negative Questions and Echo Questions

*Lesson theme: Small Talk and Checking Facts*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Question Forms |
| Grammar point | Question Tags, Negative Questions and Echo Questions |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who are accurate with direct and indirect questions but sound abrupt in conversation. They use isn't it? or no? for every tag (You live in Galle, isn't it?), misunderstand negative questions (Don't you like it? – Yes meaning No), and rarely show interest with reply questions. |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of question tags (form and intonation), negative questions (for surprise and to check a belief) and echo / reply questions (Did you? Have you really?) to sound natural and engaged in conversation. |

## Language analysis

### Question tags

| **Statement** | **Tag** | **Rule** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| You're from Jaffna, | aren't you? | positive statement → negative tag |
| She doesn't eat meat, | does she? | negative statement → positive tag |
| They went to Kandy, | didn't they? | past simple → did |
| He's been here before, | hasn't he? | same auxiliary |
| You can swim, | can't you? | modal → same modal |
| I'm late, | aren't I? | special: I am → aren't I |
| Let's go, | shall we? | special |
| Open the window, | will you? / could you? | imperative |
| Nobody called, | did they? | nobody, nothing = negative → positive tag |

### Intonation

- **Falling** tag ↘: the speaker is fairly sure and expects agreement (common in small talk): *Lovely day, isn't it? ↘*
- **Rising** tag ↗: a real question; the speaker isn't sure: *You're coming tomorrow, aren't you? ↗*

### Negative questions

- Surprise or annoyance: *Don't you like it?* (I thought you did!) *Haven't you finished yet?*
- Checking a belief: *Isn't it very hot there?* (I think it is.)
- Answering: answer about the fact: *Don't you like it? –* ***No*** *(I don't). /* ***Yes*** *(I do).*

### Echo / reply questions

- Short questions to show interest or surprise: ***"I've been to Japan." – "Have you?*** *What was it like?"* · ***"She doesn't eat rice." – "Doesn't she?"***

### Concept checking questions

*You're from Jaffna, aren't you? ↘*

1. Is the speaker sure? (Fairly sure.) What answer does she expect? (Yes.)

*Don't you like spicy food?*

1. Is the speaker surprised? (Yes.)
2. If you don't like it, do you say yes or no? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Universal isn't it? (You live in Galle, isn't it?). | Rule: repeat the auxiliary and the subject pronoun. |
| Wrong auxiliary (She went home, wasn't she?). | Find the auxiliary first (did). |
| Confusing answers to negative questions. | Answer about the fact; practise with true / false facts. |
| Flat intonation on tags. | Contrast ↘ and ↗ with gestures (hand up / down). |
| Overusing tags. | Balance: one or two per short conversation. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce small talk. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Ask: *"At a wedding or a party, how do you start a conversation with someone you don't know?"*
2. Pairs list three opening lines (2 minutes). Take ideas and board one exactly as a student says it.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present tags, negative questions and echo questions in context and clarify their form, meaning and intonation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the conversation between two guests at a wedding in Colombo. What do they have in common? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline the question tags, circle the negative questions and box the short reply questions."* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **Tags.** Build the rules on the left of the board from the conversation. Elicit the special cases (*aren't I, shall we, nobody ... did they*).
2. **Intonation.** Model ↘ and ↗ with hand gestures. Ask the CCQs.
3. **Negative questions.** Board *Don't you like spicy food?* Ask the CCQs. Practise answering: *"Don't you like it?" "No, I don't." / "Yes, I do!"*
4. **Echo questions.** Model: *"I've been to Japan." "Have you?"* Students respond to three more statements.
5. Return to the opening line from Stage 1 and add a tag.
6. **Pronunciation.** Drill tags with both intonations.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: add the question tags. Exercise B: write echo questions. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check and say the sentences with falling intonation. Check as a class.
3. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the structures fluently in natural conversation. **Interaction:** Pairs, mingle, whole class

1. **Guess the facts (6 minutes).** Give out Handout 4. Students write what they think is true about a partner they don't know well, then check with tags: *"You've got two brothers, haven't you? ↗" "You don't like cricket, do you? ↘"* One point for each correct guess.
   - ICQs: *"Do you ask a full question or use a tag?"* (A tag.)
2. **Wedding small talk (9 minutes).** Students take a small talk card (a new identity) and mingle as guests at a wedding. They must use at least two tags, one negative question and two echo questions in each conversation.
3. Students report the most interesting person they met (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"When does the tag go down? When does it go up?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write a short dialogue (12 lines) between two people meeting at a party, using four tags, two negative questions and two echo questions.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the tag rules on a card.
- In the mingle, allow them to focus on tags with *be* and *do*.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use "same-way" tags for interest: *So you're a doctor, are you?*
- They use tags with modals and perfect forms: *You couldn't help me, could you? She'd already left, hadn't she?*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- In conversation, students use the correct auxiliary in tags and appropriate intonation.
- Students answer negative questions correctly.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students stop using *isn't it?* for every tag?
- Did the conversations sound more natural and engaged?

# Handout 1: At the Wedding

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Kavindi:** Beautiful wedding, isn't it? **Arjun:** It's lovely. You're the bride's cousin, aren't you? **Kavindi:** That's right. And you work with the groom, don't you? **Arjun:** Yes, we're both engineers. I've just moved here from Jaffna. **Kavindi:** Have you? Isn't it very hot in Jaffna at this time of year? **Arjun:** It is! Colombo feels cool in comparison. **Kavindi:** Really? I thought Colombo was hot! You haven't tried the food yet, have you? **Arjun:** No, I haven't. Is it good? **Kavindi:** It's amazing. Don't you like spicy food? **Arjun:** No, I love it! I'm from Jaffna, remember? **Kavindi:** Of course. Let's get some before it's finished, shall we? **Arjun:** Good idea. Nobody's tried the watalappan yet, have they?
