# Present Perfect vs Past Simple (just, already, yet)

*Lesson theme: Sharing News*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Perfect Tenses |
| Grammar point | Present Perfect vs Past Simple (just, already, yet) |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who know the present perfect for experiences and the past simple. They mix the two tenses and use time words like yesterday with the present perfect. |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise the difference between the present perfect (news, no time, recent with just / already / yet) and the past simple (finished time, details). |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in sharing news and asking follow-up questions; to read social media posts for specific information. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can share a piece of news in the present perfect and give or ask for details in the past simple, e.g. I've bought a new phone! I bought it yesterday. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (social media posts), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (news cards). |

## Language analysis

### Meaning and use

| **Present perfect** | **Past simple** |
| :-- | :-- |
| News, no time given: I've lost my keys! | Finished time given: I lost my keys yesterday. |
| Recent actions with just: I've just finished. | Details and questions about the news: Where did you lose them? |
| already (sooner than expected): She's already left. | Time words: yesterday, last week, in 2022, ago, when |
| yet (questions and negatives): Have you finished yet? I haven't finished yet. |  |
| Unfinished time: this week, today, this year |  |

### Word position

- *just* and *already* go between *have* and the participle: *I've* ***just*** *eaten. She's* ***already*** *gone.*
- *yet* goes at the end: *Have you done it* ***yet? I haven't done it*** ***yet.***

### been vs gone

- *She's* ***been*** *to Paris.* (She went and came back.)
- *She's* ***gone*** *to Paris.* (She is there now.)

### Pronunciation

- *I've just* /aɪv dʒʌst/ – the *'ve* is short but must be heard.
- *yet* at the end often has rising intonation in questions: *Have you finished yet?* ↗

### Concept checking questions

*I've lost my keys!*

1. Do I have my keys now? (No.)
2. Do we know when I lost them? (No.)
3. Is it news? (Yes.)

*I lost my keys yesterday.*

1. Do we know when? (Yes, yesterday.)
2. Is yesterday finished? (Yes.) So which tense? (Past simple.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Present perfect with finished time (I have seen him yesterday). | Time-word sort; rule on the board: finished time = past simple. |
| Did you finish yet? | Show yet belongs with the present perfect (in British English). |
| Wrong position of just / already. | Colour-code the position in board examples. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To generate interest in sharing news. **Interaction:** Whole class, then pairs

1. Act excited. Say: *"Guess what! I've bought a new bicycle!"* Let students react. Ask: *"What questions can you ask me?"* Answer naturally in the past simple: *"I bought it on Saturday. It cost 40,000 rupees."*
2. Ask: *"Do you have any news this week?"* Students tell a partner (1 minute).

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the present perfect and past simple in context and clarify the difference, including *just, already, yet* and *been / gone*. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the posts. What is each person's news? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many people?"* (Four.) *"What do you find?"* (Their news.)
2. Pairs check; then students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (3 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline present perfect verbs with a straight line and past simple verbs with a wavy line."* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (11 minutes)**

1. **Meaning.** Board Priya's post: *I've passed my driving test! I took it this morning.* Ask the CCQs. Elicit the rule: news with no time → present perfect; details and finished time → past simple.
2. **Time words.** Pairs sort the time words in Handout 2, Exercise A. Check and build the left of the board.
3. **just / already / yet.** Board examples from the posts. Ask: *"Where does just go? Where does yet go? Do we use yet in positive sentences?"* (No: questions and negatives.)
4. **been vs gone.** Board *Kamal has gone to Jaffna* and *Kamal has been to Jaffna.* Ask: *"Where is Kamal now?"*
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *I've just passed my test! Have you finished yet? She's already left.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing the correct tense and placing *just, already, yet*. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Say: *"Now do Exercises B and C on Handout 2. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs compare and explain: *"Yesterday is finished time, so past simple."*
3. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the present perfect for news and the past simple for details in fluent conversation. **Interaction:** Pairs, then mingle

1. Give each student two news cards (Handout 3) or let them use real news. Model with a strong student: *"I've adopted a puppy!" "Really? When did you get it?" "I got it on Sunday."*
2. **Pairs (7 minutes):** students share their news in the present perfect; the partner asks at least three past simple questions. Swap.
   - ICQs: *"Which tense for the news?"* (Present perfect.) *"Which tense for the questions?"* (Past simple.)
3. **Mingle (7 minutes):** students share one real piece of news from this week with three different people. Each listener asks one question.
4. Ask: *"Who had the best news?"* Students report: *"Ravi has adopted a puppy! He got it on Sunday."*
5. Monitor throughout and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences heard in Stage 4 (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Quick test: say a time expression; students say *present perfect* or *past simple*.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a social media post (80 words) with a piece of news. Add three comments from friends asking questions, and your replies.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide question starters: *When did you ...? Where did you ...? How much did it ...? Who did you ...?*
- Allow weaker students to read the news cards directly.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *just, already, yet* at least once each in the freer practice.
- They write a short social media post with three comments and replies.

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students introduce news with the present perfect and give details in the past simple.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise B.
- Correct position of *just, already, yet* in written practice.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students understand the news / details distinction?
- Did they understand *yet* in questions and negatives?
