# Adverbs of Manner and Adjectives vs Adverbs (quick / quickly, good / well)

*Lesson theme: How Do You Do It?*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Adjectives and Adverbs |
| Grammar point | Adverbs of Manner and Adjectives vs Adverbs (quick / quickly, good / well) |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who use adjectives accurately before nouns and after be. They use adjectives to describe actions (He drives careful, She sings good), put adverbs between the verb and object (She speaks very well English) and over-regularise (fastly, hardly for hard). |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise adverbs of manner (slowly, carefully, well, fast, hard) to describe how people do things, contrasting them with adjectives, and their position after the verb or object. |
| Subsidiary aims | To learn spelling rules (happy → happily, careful → carefully); to develop speaking through a mime game and a "class survey". |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can describe how people do things, e.g. My brother is a careful driver. He drives carefully. Nadeesha sings beautifully. I can't run fast. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (a sports day report), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (mime cards and survey), adverb cards. |

## Language analysis

### Adjective or adverb?

| **Adjective (describes a noun)** | **Adverb (describes a verb: how?)** |
| :-- | :-- |
| He is a careful driver. | He drives carefully. |
| She is a beautiful singer. | She sings beautifully. |
| It's a slow bus. | The bus moves slowly. |

### Form

- Most adverbs: adjective + **-ly**: *slow → slowly, quiet → quietly, careful → carefully*.
- *-y* → *-ily*: *happy → happily, easy → easily*.
- *-le* → *-ly*: *terrible → terribly, gentle → gently*.
- **Irregular**: *good → well · fast → fast · hard → hard · late → late · early → early*.
- *hardly* means "almost not" (a different word), not *hard*.

### Position

- After the verb: *She runs fast.*
- After the object: *He speaks English well* (not *He speaks well English*).
- *very / really* + adverb: *very carefully, really well*.

### Pronunciation

- *-ly* is weak /li/: *carefully* /ˈkeəfəli/, *quietly* /ˈkwaɪətli/.

### Concept checking questions

*He drives carefully.*

1. Does this describe the man or how he drives? (How he drives.) So adjective or adverb? (Adverb.)

*She speaks English well.*

1. Can I say *She speaks good English*? (Yes: *good* + noun.) *She speaks English good*? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Adjective after a verb (He drives careful). | Question test: What kind of driver? (adjective) / How does he drive? (adverb). |
| goodly / fastly / hardly (for hard). | Board the irregular list in a box. |
| Adverb between verb and object (speaks well English). | Board: verb + object + adverb. |
| Spelling (happyly, carefuly). | Spelling rules with examples; dictation. |

## Board plan

| **Left: adjective → adverb** | **Centre: grammar** | **Right: irregular** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| slow → slowly · quiet → quietly · careful → carefully · happy → happily · easy → easily · terrible → terribly | ADJECTIVE + noun: a careful driver · ADVERB + verb: He drives carefully. · verb + object + adverb: She speaks English well. · not: She speaks well English ✗ · not: He drives careful ✗ · What kind? → adjective · How? → adverb | good → well · fast → fast · hard → hard · late → late · early → early · hardly = almost not! |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Walk across the room slowly, then quickly. Ask: *"How did I walk?"* Accept *slow / fast*.
2. Whisper, then shout a sentence. Ask: *"How did I speak?"*

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present adverbs of manner in context and contrast them with adjectives. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the report about the school sports day. Who won the race? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline the words that tell us how people did things. What do they end in?"* Pairs compare. Elicit *-ly* and the irregular ones (*fast, well, hard*).

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. Board *Kasun is a fast runner.* and *Kasun runs fast.* Then *Dilini is a careful player.* and *Dilini plays carefully.* Ask: *"Which words describe a person? Which describe an action?"*
2. Ask the CCQs. Teach the question test (*What kind? / How?*).
3. Build the spelling rules and the irregular box.
4. Board *She speaks well English* ✗ → *She speaks English well*.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *carefully, quietly, beautifully* with weak *-ly*.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise form and choice accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: adjective or adverb? Exercise B: write the adverb. Exercise C: put the words in order. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use adverbs fluently. **Interaction:** Groups, then mingle, then whole class

1. **Mime game (8 minutes).** Give out the mime cards from Handout 3. In groups, a student takes a verb card and an adverb card (e.g. *eat* + *slowly*) and mimes. Others guess: *"You're eating slowly!"*
   - ICQs: *"How many cards do you take?"* (Two: a verb and an adverb.)
2. **Class survey (6 minutes).** Students mingle and ask the survey questions: *"Can you swim well?" "Do you get up early?" "Do you drive carefully?"*
3. **Report (4 minutes).** Students report: *"Three people can sing well. Nobody gets up late!"*
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Quick fire: say an adjective; students say the adverb.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write about three people you know (8 sentences): what they do and how they do it. *My grandmother cooks very well. My cousin drives fast.*

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the adjective–adverb list during practice.
- In the mime game, let them choose from five adverbs only.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use comparatives: *She runs faster than me. He speaks more slowly.*
- They use *hardly* correctly: *I hardly sleep before exams.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 12 out of 14 or better on Handout 2.
- Students use adverbs (not adjectives) to describe actions.
- Students put the adverb after the object.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the question test (*What kind? / How?*) help students choose?
- Were the mimes clear and fun?
