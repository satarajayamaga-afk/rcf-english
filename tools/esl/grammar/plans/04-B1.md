GRAMMAR TOPIC: FUTURE FORMS · B1 (Intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Present Continuous and Present Simple for the Future; Future Time Clauses

*Lesson theme: The School Trip to Sigiriya*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Future Forms |
| Grammar point | Present Continuous and Present Simple for the Future; Future Time Clauses |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use will and going to but don't use the present continuous for arrangements or the present simple for timetables, and say I'll call you when I will arrive. |
| Main aim | To help students discover and use the present continuous for fixed arrangements, the present simple for timetables, and the present simple after when, as soon as, before, after, until in future time clauses. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read an itinerary for specific information; to develop fluency in arranging to meet; to write a confirmation email. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can arrange a meeting using the present continuous and write an email with at least two future time clauses. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (trip itinerary and diary), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4A and 4B (busy-week diaries). |

## Language analysis

### Form and use

| **Use** | **Form** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Fixed arrangement (with people, place, time) | Present continuous | I'm meeting Dilshan at 6 on Friday. |
| Timetable / schedule | Present simple | The bus leaves at 7.15. The exam starts on Monday. |
| Intention / plan | be going to | I'm going to take lots of photos. |
| Future time clause | when / as soon as / before / after / until / by the time + present simple, main clause with will | I'll call you when I arrive. (not when I will arrive) |

### Pronunciation

- Arrangement questions: *What are you doing on Saturday?* /wɒt ə jə ˈduːɪŋ/ (weak *are you*).
- *as soon as* /əz ˈsuːn əz/.

### Concept checking questions

*I'm meeting Dilshan at 6 on Friday.*

1. Is this now or in the future? (Future.)
2. Have we agreed the time and place? (Yes, it's arranged.)

*The bus leaves at 7.15.*

1. Is it a personal plan or a timetable? (Timetable.)

*I'll call you when I arrive.*

1. Is arriving in the future? (Yes.)
2. Which tense follows *when*? (Present simple.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| will after when / as soon as (when I will arrive). | Highlight the rule clearly; include several items in practice. |
| Present simple for personal arrangements (I meet my friend tomorrow). | Contrast personal arrangement vs timetable. |
| Thinking the present continuous is only for now. | CCQs with future time words. |
| Students choose going to every time. | Explain that going to is often possible; continuous emphasises the arrangement. |

## Board plan

| **Left: time conjunctions** | **Centre: rules** | **Right: arranging phrases** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| when · as soon as · before · after · until · by the time → + present simple | ARRANGEMENT (diary): I'm meeting Dilshan at 6. · TIMETABLE: The bus leaves at 7.15. · PLAN / INTENTION: I'm going to take photos. · TIME CLAUSE: I'll call you when I arrive. (not will arrive) | Are you doing anything on ...? · Sorry, I'm ...ing. · How about ...? · That works for me. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"What are you doing this weekend? Is anything already arranged, with a time and a place?"* Model: *"I'm having lunch with my cousins on Sunday at 1."*
2. Pairs share (2 minutes). Take two examples.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present present tenses for the future and time clauses in context, and help students work out the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the trip itinerary and Nethmi's diary. What time does the bus leave on Saturday? What is Nethmi doing on Monday?"*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Are the sentences about the past or the future?"* (Future.) *"Alone or in pairs?"* (Pairs.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"Is this from a timetable or a personal diary?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit the answers. Build the four rules in the centre of the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Board *I'll call you when I will arrive.* Ask: *"What's wrong?"* Elicit the rule and list the time conjunctions on the left.
4. Drill: *What are you doing on Saturday? I'm meeting my cousins. I'll text you as soon as I get there.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing the correct future form. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the best form. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare and justify. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the forms fluently in a realistic task and in writing. **Interaction:** Pairs, then individual

1. Give Student A Handout 4A and Student B Handout 4B (busy diaries). Teach the arranging phrases on the right of the board.
2. Say: *"You want to meet to plan a class party. Find a free two-hour slot. Don't show your diary!"* Model one exchange (8 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Can you show your diary?"* (No.) *"How long do you need?"* (Two hours.)
3. When they find a time, each student writes a short confirmation email to their partner, including two time clauses: *I'll bring the list when I come. As soon as I hear from the teacher, I'll message you.* (7 minutes.)
4. Partners swap emails and check the time clauses (3 minutes).

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Pairs report their meeting time.
2. Board errors from the role-play and emails; students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write an email (100 words) to a friend confirming a weekend arrangement. Include one timetable fact and two time clauses.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Mark the free slots in Handouts 4A/4B with a pencil dot so weaker pairs succeed.
- Give an email frame.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students negotiate with alternatives: *How about Thursday instead? I could ... if ...*
- They add *by the time* and *until* to their emails.

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the role-play, students use the present continuous for diary arrangements.
- In the email, students use the present simple after time conjunctions.
- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 3.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students find the difference between arrangement and timetable?
- How many still used *will* after *when*?

# Handout 2: Language Focus

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Look at these sentences.**

1. The bus **leaves** at 7.15 am.
2. The guided tour **starts** at 11.30.
3. I**'m sitting** next to Ishara on the bus.
4. Dad**'s picking** me **up** at 8.15 pm.
5. I**'m going to take** lots of photos!
6. I**'ll call** him as soon as we **get** to the expressway.

**Answer the questions with your partner.**

1. Are all these sentences about the future? ______________
2. Which sentences come from a timetable or programme? What tense are they? ______________________________
3. Which sentences describe arrangements with other people (a time and maybe a place are agreed)? What tense are they? ______________________________
4. Which sentence is an intention (Nethmi's own plan, not arranged with others)? ______________________________
5. In sentence 6, *get* is in the future. Why isn't it *will get*? Find the word before it. ______________________________
6. Complete the rule: after *when, as soon as, before, after, until* we use the ______________________ to talk about the future.
