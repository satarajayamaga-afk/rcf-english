GRAMMAR TOPIC: PRESENT TENSES · B2 (Upper-intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Present Continuous with Always; State and Action Verbs

*Lesson theme: Habits, Annoyances and Changing States*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Present Tenses |
| Grammar point | Present Continuous with Always; State and Action Verbs |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who are confident with basic present tenses. They have rarely used always + continuous for complaints, and they are inconsistent with verbs that have both stative and dynamic meanings. |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of present forms for (a) annoying habits (always + continuous), (b) gradual change (getting + comparative), (c) stative/dynamic verb meanings and (d) the present simple for timetables. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency and appropriate intonation in a polite complaint role-play; to read a blog for writer's attitude. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can make a polite but honest complaint about someone's habits, using always + continuous and appropriate softening language. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (blog: Things My Flatmate Does), Handout 2 (language reference and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (role-play cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form, meaning and use

| **Use** | **Form** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Annoying or surprising habit | always / constantly / forever + present continuous | She's always borrowing my charger! |
| Neutral habit | always + present simple | She always borrows books from the library. |
| Gradual change | get / become + comparative, continuous | It's getting hotter every year. |
| Temporary behaviour | be being + adjective | You're being very quiet today. |
| Stative meaning | present simple | I see what you mean. She has a car. This tastes good. |
| Dynamic meaning | present continuous | I'm seeing the doctor at 4. She's having a hard time. The chef is tasting the sauce. |
| Timetables and schedules | present simple | The train leaves at 8. |

### Pronunciation

- *always* + continuous: the main stress falls on *always*, often with a fall-rise to show irritation: *He's ALways leaving the lights on!*
- Softening language is stressed less and spoken more quickly: *It's just that ..., I don't know if you've noticed, but ...*

### Concept checking questions

*He's always leaving dirty dishes in the sink.*

1. Does it happen often? (Yes, very often, maybe too often.)
2. How does the speaker feel? (Annoyed.)
3. Does it really happen every single time? (Probably not; the speaker is exaggerating.)

*You're being very quiet today.*

1. Are you usually quiet? (No.)
2. Is it just your behaviour today? (Yes.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Word order: He always is losing his keys. | Show the position on the board: be + always + verb-ing. |
| Overuse of always + continuous making complaints sound rude. | Teach softening language (I've noticed that ..., It's just that ...) before the role-play. |
| Confusion between have (possession) and have (activity). | Give pairs of sentences and ask: Can you own it? Is it an activity? |
| Too many uses in one lesson. | Prioritise always + continuous and stative/dynamic verbs; treat timetables briefly. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic of annoying habits and engage students personally. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Say: *"Think about people on buses, in class, at home or online. What habits annoy you?"* Give one example: *"People who talk loudly on the phone on the bus."*
2. Students list three annoying habits with a partner (2 minutes).
3. Take ideas from the class and board key words only (*phone, late, borrow, noise*).

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the target language in context and clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the blog. How does the writer feel about her flatmate? Is she being fair? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs discuss. Elicit that she is annoyed but also funny and kind.
3. Students answer the three questions under the blog. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline every present form in the blog."* Pairs compare. Elicit examples and write them in groups on the board: *always + -ing*; *getting + comparative*; *being + adjective*; verbs like *have, see, taste*.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **always + continuous.** Write *She's always borrowing my charger!* and *She always leaves the lights on.* Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis. Elicit that the first shows annoyance. Board the word order: be + always + verb-ing.
2. **Gradual change.** Write *Our bill is getting higher and higher.* Ask: *"Is it changing? Is it finished?"*
3. **Stative / dynamic.** Board pairs: *I see what you mean / I'm seeing the doctor*; *She has a cat / She's having a difficult time*; *This curry tastes strange / The chef is tasting the curry.* Students explain the difference to a partner.
4. **being + adjective.** *I'm being patient. You're being very quiet today.* CCQ: *"Are you usually quiet?"* (No.)
5. **Timetables.** Board *The concert starts at 7.30.* Ask: *"Present or future? Why present simple?"* (A fixed timetable.)
6. **Pronunciation.** Model the irritated intonation: *He's ALways leaving the lights on!* Students repeat chorally, then say it with different feelings (annoyed, amused).

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the reference box. Then do Exercise A: choose the best form. Four minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Then students do Exercise B (rewrite neutral sentences as complaints).
3. Check both exercises as a class.
4. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it as part of the homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language fluently and appropriately in a polite complaint role-play. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Teach the complaint language on the right of the board and drill two phrases.
2. Say: *"You are flatmates. Student A has a problem with Student B's habits. Make a polite but honest complaint. Student B, respond, explain and suggest a change. Use at least three phrases from the board."*
   - ICQs: *"Are you angry or polite?"* (Polite but honest.) *"Who suggests a change?"* (Student B.)
3. Students read their cards and prepare for 2 minutes.
4. **Role-play 1** (4 minutes). Monitor and note language use.
5. Students swap roles and use Scenario 2 (4 minutes).
6. Two or three pairs perform for the class. The class listens and notes: *How many examples of always + continuous? Was the complaint polite?*

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences heard during the role-plays. Students identify and correct errors.
2. Board one or two very natural complaints and praise them.
3. Ask: *"Which phrase from today will you use outside class?"*
4. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write a humorous blog post (150 words) about someone's annoying habits. Use *always* + continuous at least three times, *getting* + comparative once and one stative/dynamic pair.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide sentence frames for the role-play: *I've noticed that you're always ... . Could you try to ...?*
- Allow weaker students to do the role-play in a group of three, with one student as an observer.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add sarcastic stress patterns and hedging (*a bit, kind of, sort of*).
- Ask them to use *forever* and *constantly* as alternatives to *always*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students use *always* + continuous accurately and appropriately in the role-play.
- Students choose the correct stative or dynamic form in 7 of 8 items on Handout 2.
- Complaints include at least one softening phrase.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which use of the present caused the most difficulty?
- Were the complaints polite as well as accurate?
- What will I recycle next lesson?

**Language reference**

| **Use** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Annoying habit | She's always borrowing my charger! |
| Neutral habit | She always leaves at 8. |
| Gradual change | It's getting more difficult every week. |
| Temporary behaviour | I'm being patient. |
| Stative meaning | I see what you mean. She has a cat. This tastes strange. |
| Dynamic meaning | She's having a difficult time. I'm seeing the doctor. |

**A. Choose the best form.**

1. He **always loses / is always losing** his keys! It's so frustrating.
2. My father **always reads / is always reading** the newspaper at breakfast. It's his routine.
3. Look, the sky **gets / is getting** darker. It's going to rain.
4. I **have / am having** two sisters and a brother.
5. We **have / are having** a party on Saturday. Can you come?
6. You **are / are being** silly. Stop it!
7. This fish **smells / is smelling** bad.
8. I **think / am thinking** about changing jobs.

# Handout 4: Role-play Cards

**Scenario 1: Student A**

You share a flat with Student B. You are unhappy because B: leaves dishes in the sink, borrows your clothes without asking, and plays games late at night. Make a polite but honest complaint. Use *always* + continuous and softening language.

**Scenario 1: Student B**

You share a flat with Student A. Listen to A's complaint. Explain your side (you are having a busy time at work, you are getting tired, etc.). Suggest one change and ask for one change from A.

**Scenario 2: Student A**

You work in an office with Student B. B is a nice colleague, but B: talks loudly on the phone, takes your stapler and arrives late to meetings. Make a polite complaint.

**Scenario 2: Student B**

You work with Student A. Listen to A's complaint. Explain (you are being given more work, your bus is always late, etc.). Agree to one change. Mention one habit of A's that also annoys you!

**Useful language**

- *I've noticed that you're always ...*
- *It's just that ...*
- *I don't mean to complain, but ...*
- *Would you mind not ...-ing?*
- *Could you try to ...?*
- *I'm sorry, I didn't realise. I'm having a really busy time at the moment.*
