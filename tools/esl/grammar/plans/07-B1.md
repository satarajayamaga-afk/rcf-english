# Should, Ought To, Had Better and Suggestions (Advice)

*Lesson theme: The Advice Column*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Modal Verbs |
| Grammar point | Should, Ought To, Had Better and Suggestions (Advice) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use should but say You should to call him and How about go ...? They don't know had better or the difference in strength between advice forms. |
| Main aim | To present and practise forms for giving advice and making suggestions: should, ought to, had better, could, Why don't you ...?, How about + -ing?, If I were you, I'd ... |
| Subsidiary aims | To read problem letters and replies; to develop fluency in giving and responding to advice; to practise polite intonation. |

## Language analysis

### Form and strength

| **Strength** | **Structure** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Gentle suggestion | could + base verb | You could try yoga. |
| Suggestion | Why don't you + base verb? · How about / What about + -ing? | Why don't you join a club? How about asking your teacher? |
| Advice | should / shouldn't + base verb · ought to + base verb | You should see a doctor. You ought to apologise. |
| Personal advice | If I were you, I'd + base verb | If I were you, I'd talk to her. |
| Strong advice / warning | had better ('d better) / had better not + base verb | You'd better hurry or you'll miss the bus. |

### Accuracy points

- No *to* after *should, could, had better*: *You should call* (not *should to call*).
- *ought* takes *to*: *You ought to call.*
- *How about* + **-ing**: *How about going ...?*
- *had better* refers to now / the future (not the past), and often implies a bad result if you don't.

### Pronunciation

- *You'd better* /jʊd ˈbetə/ – the *'d* is often hard to hear.
- Polite suggestion intonation: rising-falling, friendly: *Why don't you join a CLUB?*

### Concept checking questions

*You'd better hurry.*

1. Is it a gentle suggestion or strong advice? (Strong.)
2. What will happen if I don't hurry? (Something bad: I'll be late.)

*How about asking your teacher?*

1. Is it an order? (No, a suggestion.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| should to / had better to | Board the patterns with a crossed-out to. |
| How about go | Highlight -ing after about. |
| Overuse of should only. | Advice reply must use five different forms. |
| had better thought to be past. | CCQ: Is it about the past or now? |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic and personalise it. **Interaction:** Pairs

1. Say: *"Tell your partner about a small problem you have, for example: I can't sleep. I'm always late."*
2. The partner gives any advice. Students swap (2 minutes). Take two examples.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present advice and suggestion forms in context and clarify their strength, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the three problem letters and the replies. Do you agree with the advice? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many letters?"* (Three.)
2. Pairs discuss. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline every phrase the writer uses to give advice."* Pairs compare. Elicit and board the phrases.

**Part C: Clarification (9 minutes)**

1. Pairs rank the phrases from gentle to strong. Build the strength scale on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. **Form.** Highlight: no *to* after *should, could, 'd better*; *ought to*; *How about* + *-ing*; *If I were you, I'd* + base verb.
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill with polite intonation: *Why don't you join a club? How about asking your teacher? You'd better hurry!*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: complete the advice. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give advice fluently in speaking and writing. **Interaction:** Pairs, groups

1. **Problem cards (8 minutes).** Give each pair a set of problem cards (Handout 3). Student A reads a problem as if it's theirs; Student B gives advice using two different structures. Swap.
   - ICQs: *"How many different structures?"* (Two each time.)
2. **Agony aunt (10 minutes).** Groups write a short problem letter, pass it to the next group, and that group writes a reply on Handout 4 using at least five different structures. Groups read the best replies aloud.
3. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Discuss which advice was most helpful.
2. Board errors (*should to, How about go*); students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a reply to an advice letter (130 words) using at least five different structures from today.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide the strength scale on a card.
- Weaker students use three structures in the reply instead of five.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add reasons with *otherwise* and *so that*: *You'd better leave now, otherwise you'll miss the bus.*
- They use *shouldn't have* for past mistakes (preview).

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- The agony aunt reply uses at least five different structures accurately.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students understand the difference in strength?
- Were *How about + -ing* and *had better* used accurately?

# Handout 1: The Advice Column

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Problem 1:** *I'm 15 and I can't sleep before exams. I lie in bed and worry. What can I do? – Dinuka* **Reply:** You should try to relax before bed. Why don't you read a book instead of looking at your phone? You'd better not drink tea or coffee in the evening. If I were you, I'd make a study plan, so you don't feel so worried.

**Problem 2:** *My best friend is angry with me because I forgot her birthday. – Malsha* **Reply:** You ought to apologise as soon as possible. How about making her a card or a small present? You could also invite her for an ice cream. Don't wait too long!

**Problem 3:** *I've just moved to a new school and I don't have any friends. – Arun* **Reply:** That's hard, but it will get better. You should join a club, like football or drama. Why don't you sit with different people at lunch? You could also ask your teacher to introduce you.

**Answer the questions.**

1. What shouldn't Dinuka drink in the evening? ______________________________
2. What should Malsha do first? ______________________________
3. Where could Arun meet new people? ______________________________
