# Perfect Infinitives and Perfect Gerunds (to have done / having done)

*Lesson theme: The Missing Painting*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Perfect Tenses |
| Grammar point | Perfect Infinitives and Perfect Gerunds (to have done / having done) |
| Level | C1 (Advanced) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Advanced students who are accurate with perfect tenses but rarely use perfect infinitives (seems to have left, is said to have been stolen) or perfect gerunds (denied having taken), so their reporting sounds simple and repetitive. |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of perfect infinitives and perfect gerunds to speculate, report and refer to earlier events, especially in news-style reporting. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop collaborative speaking (a press conference role-play) and formal written reporting. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can write and present a short news report about a mystery using at least six perfect infinitive or gerund forms. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (the scene), Handout 2 (model news article for analysis), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (case file and role cards for the press conference). |

## Language analysis

### Form and use

| **Structure** | **Form** | **Example** | **Use** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Perfect infinitive after seem / appear / happen / claim / pretend | to have + past participle | The thief seems to have entered through the roof. | Refers to an action earlier than the main verb |
| Passive perfect infinitive after reporting passives | be said / thought / believed / reported + to have been + pp | The painting is believed to have been stolen at night. | Objective, distanced reporting of past events |
| Perfect gerund after deny / admit / regret / remember / forgive and prepositions | having + past participle | The guard denied having seen anything. He was praised for having raised the alarm. | Emphasises that the action is earlier and completed |
| Passive perfect gerund | having been + pp | She was proud of having been chosen. | Passive, earlier action |
| Perfect infinitive after would like / would love / would rather | would like + to have + pp | I would like to have seen his face! | Past wish that did not happen |

### Accuracy points

- After *deny, admit, regret*, the simple gerund is often possible too (*He denied taking it*); the perfect gerund makes the time order explicit and sounds more formal.
- Not: *He is said to steal the painting yesterday.* → *He is said to have stolen the painting yesterday.*
- Not: *It is said him to have ...* → *He is said to have ...* / *It is said that he has ...*

### Pronunciation

- *to have* is weak: /tə həv/ or /tu‿əv/: *seems to have left* /siːmz tə həv left/.
- *having been* /ˈhævɪŋ bɪn/.

### Concept checking questions

*The thief seems to have entered through the roof.*

1. Did the thief enter before now? (Yes.)
2. Are we sure? (No; it seems so.)

*The guard denied having seen anything.*

1. What did the guard say? (I didn't see anything.)
2. Did the seeing (or not seeing) happen before the denial? (Yes.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Using the simple infinitive for a past action (He seems to leave). | Timeline: main verb now, perfect infinitive earlier. |
| Wrong form after reporting passives (is said to steal). | Board the two patterns and transformation practice. |
| Overloading sentences with passives. | Discuss clarity in feedback; one or two per paragraph is enough. |
| The press conference stalls. | Give question cards to journalists. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic and engage students. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Give out Handout 1. Read the description of the scene and the headline.
2. Ask: *"What happened? What do you think the guard did, or didn't do?"* Pairs speculate for 2 minutes. Take ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present perfect infinitives and perfect gerunds in a news article and clarify their form, meaning and use. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (5 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the news article. Was your guess right? What do the police think happened?"*
2. Pairs compare their ideas with the article.

**Part B: Analysis (7 minutes)**

1. Say: *"In groups, answer the analysis questions under the article."*
   - ICQs: *"How many structures do you look for?"* (Three types.) *"Do you also explain why?"* (Yes, question 4.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the patterns in the centre of the board.
2. Draw a timeline for *The thief seems to have entered through the roof*: *seems* = now; *to have entered* = earlier. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Discuss: *"Why does the writer say is believed to have been stolen and not was stolen?"* (Distance; not yet proven.) *"What's the difference between He denied taking it and He denied having taken it?"* (Very little; the second is more formal and stresses the earlier time.)
4. Highlight *would like to have seen* (a past wish that didn't happen).
5. **Pronunciation:** drill weak forms: *seems to have left* /siːmz tə həv left/; *having been* /ˈhævɪŋ bɪn/.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite the sentences using the words given. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Feedback as a class.
3. **Exercise B:** pairs rewrite the two sentences in news style. Two pairs share.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the structures fluently in a press conference role-play and a short news report. **Interaction:** Groups of four or five

1. Give each group the case file and role cards (Handout 4): Museum Director, Head of Security, Police Inspector, and one or two Journalists. Students read their cards (3 minutes).
2. **Press conference (8 minutes).** Journalists ask questions; officials answer using their cards and their own speculation. Encourage today's structures: *"The thieves appear to have used a van." "Mr Silva denies having seen anything."*
   - ICQs: *"Who asks the questions?"* (The journalists.) *"Can officials invent details?"* (Yes, if they fit the case file.)
3. **News report (7 minutes).** Each group writes a 100-word news report about the press conference using at least four structures from today. One group reads its report aloud.
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board the most effective sentences from the reports and one or two with errors. Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Where would you see these structures?"* (News, reports, formal writing.)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a 220-word news report on an unsolved mystery (real or invented) using at least six of today's structures.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give a pattern card with the five structures and an example of each.
- Assign weaker students the Journalist role, with question cards to read.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use the continuous perfect infinitive: *The thieves appear to have been planning this for months.*
- They write the follow-up article a week later, using *would like to have* and *regret having*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The group report includes at least four perfect infinitive or gerund forms used accurately.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- Students can explain why reporters use distancing structures.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the article make the structures noticeable?
- Did students use the structures naturally in the press conference?

# Handout 1: The Scene

**Look at the scene. What happened?**

The National Art Gallery, 7 am. In Room 4, an empty golden frame hangs on the wall. Above it, a skylight in the roof is broken. Glass is on the floor. There is a ladder against the wall that does not belong to the gallery. The alarm is silent. A security guard stands in the doorway, holding a cup of tea, looking shocked.

**Headline:** PRICELESS PAINTING VANISHES OVERNIGHT

**Discuss:** What do you think happened? What might the guard have done (or not done)?

# Handout 2: Model News Article

**MASTERPIECE STOLEN IN DARING ROOFTOP RAID**

A painting worth $3 million is believed to have been stolen from the National Art Gallery in the early hours of Monday morning. The thieves appear to have entered through a skylight in the roof, and they are thought to have used a ladder to climb down into Room 4.

The gallery's alarm system seems to have been switched off at 1.12 am. Police say whoever did this is likely to have known the security code. The night guard, Mr Silva, has denied having seen anything unusual and admitted having fallen asleep at around midnight. He has not been accused of any crime.

A delivery van is reported to have been seen near the gallery at 1 am. The thieves appear to have been planning the raid for months.

The gallery director said she regretted not having upgraded the alarm last year. "I would like to have seen this coming," she told reporters.

**Analysis questions**

1. Find four examples of *be + said / thought / believed / reported + to have (been) + pp*. ______________________________
2. Find two examples of *seem / appear + to have + pp*. ______________________________
3. Find three examples of *having + pp*. Which verbs come before them? ______________________________
4. Why does the writer avoid saying *Mr Silva didn't see anything* or *Thieves stole the painting*? ______________________________
5. What does *I would like to have seen this coming* mean? Did she see it coming? ______________________________

# Handout 4: Case File and Role Cards

**Case file**

- Painting: *Woman at the Window* (1890), valued at $3 million.
- Last seen: 10 pm on Sunday by the night guard, Mr Silva.
- Discovered missing: 7 am on Monday.
- Clues: broken skylight; ladder; alarm switched off at 1.12 am; muddy footprints of two different sizes; a delivery van seen nearby at 1 am; Mr Silva says he fell asleep at about midnight.

**Museum Director**

You are embarrassed. You want to protect the museum's reputation. You believe the thieves were professionals and had help from inside. You regret not upgrading the alarm last year.

**Head of Security**

You defend your team. You say Mr Silva has worked for you for 15 years and is honest. You think the alarm was switched off by someone who knew the code.

**Police Inspector**

You are careful and do not want to say too much. You can say: two people probably entered through the roof; they probably used the van; they may have been planning this for months. A cleaner is being questioned.

**Journalists**

Ask difficult questions. Find out: How did the thieves get in? Who switched off the alarm? Did the guard really fall asleep? Has anyone been arrested? Does the museum regret anything?

# Answer Key

**Handout 2**

1. is believed to have been stolen; are thought to have used; is reported to have been seen; (also: is likely to have known)
2. appear to have entered; seems to have been switched off (also: appear to have been planning)
3. denied having seen; admitted having fallen; regretted not having upgraded
4. The facts are not yet proven; the writer avoids accusing people and keeps a distance.
5. She wishes she had predicted it. No, she didn't.

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. The thief is believed to have worked alone.
2. He seems to have left the country.
3. He denied having taken the money.
4. The bridge is reported to have been damaged in the storm.
5. She admitted having lied.
6. The company appears to have lost millions.
7. I would like to have been there to see it.
8. They were happy about / at having been chosen.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| He is said to have leaving. | He is said to have left. |
| It is said him to be rich. | He is said to be rich. |
| The thief is believed to escape yesterday. | The thief is believed to have escaped yesterday. |
| He denied to have taken it. | He denied having taken it. |
