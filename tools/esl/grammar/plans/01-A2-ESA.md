# Present Continuous vs Present Simple

*Lesson theme: What Are They Doing Now?*

## Lesson overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grammar topic** | Present Tenses |
| **Grammar point** | Present Continuous vs Present Simple |
| **Level** | A2 (Elementary) |
| **Duration** | 60 minutes |
| **Framework** | ESA boomerang: Engage, Activate 1, Study, Activate 2. Students try a speaking task first with the language they have, study the language they needed, then repeat a similar task more successfully. |
| **Class profile** | Elementary students who can use the present simple for routines and know common action verbs. Most have met the present continuous but confuse it with the present simple and often leave out *be*. |
| **Main aim** | To clarify and practise the present continuous for actions happening now, contrasted with the present simple for routines. |
| **Subsidiary aims** | To develop fluency in describing scenes; to practise reading for specific information (text messages); to practise *-ing* spelling rules. |
| **Learning outcome** | By the end of the lesson, students can describe what people are doing at the moment and contrast it with what they usually do, e.g. *He usually walks to work, but today he's taking a taxi.* |
| **Materials** | Handout 1 (text messages), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3A and 3B (café scene cards for the information gap), a busy photo for the Engage stage (any market or street photo projected or printed, or a board sketch). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| | Positive | Negative | Question | Short answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | I**'m** (am) cook**ing**. | I**'m not** cooking. | **Am** I cooking? | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. |
| **He / She / It** | She**'s** (is) cook**ing**. | She **isn't** cooking. | **Is** she cooking? | Yes, she is. / No, she isn't. |
| **You / We / They** | They**'re** (are) cook**ing**. | They **aren't** cooking. | **Are** they cooking? | Yes, they are. / No, they aren't. |

Spelling of *-ing*:
- Most verbs add *-ing*: read → reading, play → playing.
- Verbs ending in *-e* drop the *e*: make → making, write → writing.
- One vowel + one consonant (stressed) doubles the consonant: sit → sitting, run → running, swim → swimming.
- *-ie* → *-ying*: lie → lying, die → dying.

### Meaning and use
- **Present continuous:** actions happening at the moment of speaking or around now: *Look! It's raining. I'm reading a good book this week.*
- **Present simple:** routines, habits and permanent facts: *I take the bus every day.*
- **Contrast:** *He usually walks to work, but today he's taking a taxi.*
- Time words: *now, right now, at the moment, today, this week, Look! Listen!* vs *every day, usually, always, often, on Mondays*.

### Pronunciation
- Contractions are normal in speech: *I'm /aɪm/, she's /ʃiːz/, they're /ðeə/*.
- *-ing* is pronounced /ɪŋ/ with no hard /g/ at the end.
- In questions, *are* is weak: *What are you doing?* /wɒt ə jə ˈduːɪŋ/.

### Concept checking questions
For *Ravi is taking a taxi today.*
1. Is Ravi in the taxi now? (Yes.)
2. Does he take a taxi every day? (No.)
3. Is this a habit? (No, it's just today / now.)

For *Ravi usually walks to work.*
1. Does he walk most days? (Yes.)
2. Is he walking now? (We don't know / not necessarily.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Missing *be* (*He reading a book*). | Board the three parts in separate boxes: subject + be + verb-ing. Use a finger-counting gesture: three fingers for *He – is – reading*. |
| Using the continuous for routines (*I am going to school every day*). | Use the timeline contrast and highlight time words. Students sort time words into two columns. |
| Spelling errors (*siting, makeing*). | Quick spelling race on the board in Study; add the spelling rules box to Handout 2. |
| Students run out of ideas in Activate 1. | Provide the prompt questions on the board: *Who? What are they doing? Where?* |
| Info-gap students look at each other's cards. | Seat pairs back to back or ask them to hold the card up close and not show it. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Engage (7 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To create interest in the topic and get students talking about actions in a scene.
**Interaction:** T → Ss, then pairs

1. Show a busy market or street photo (or sketch a simple scene on the board: a man on a phone, a girl running, a woman buying fruit). Say: *"Look at this photo. Where is it? Is it busy?"* Take two or three quick answers.
2. Say: *"Look for 30 seconds. Then close your eyes."* After 30 seconds, hide the photo. Ask: *"How many people can you remember? What are they doing?"* Accept any answers and do not correct grammar.
3. Ask: *"Are you a busy person? What do you usually do on Saturday morning?"* Students tell a partner in one minute.

**Transition:** *"OK, now let's try a speaking game."*

### Stage 2: Activate 1, first try (8 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To let students use the language they have in a communicative task, and to diagnose what they need.
**Interaction:** Pairs

1. Give out Handout 3A to Student A and Handout 3B to Student B in each pair. Say: *"This is a café. You have a card with eight people. Your partner has a different card. Don't show your partner! Tell your partner what people are doing. Find the differences. You have four minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Can you show your card?"* (No.) *"What do you find?"* (Differences.)
2. Students try the task. **Do not help or correct.** Monitor and write down: (a) sentences without *be*; (b) wrong use of present simple; (c) good sentences.
3. Stop after four minutes. Ask: *"How many differences did you find?"* Collect a number from each pair. Say: *"Keep your cards. We will do this again later."* Collect the cards or ask students to turn them over.

**Transition:** *"Let's look at some messages. They will help us with the language."*

### Stage 3: Study, reading and language focus (12 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the present continuous and contrast it with the present simple.
**Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. **Reading task:** *"Read the four messages. Where is each person? Write the place. Two minutes."* Students check in pairs, then check as a class. (Answers in the Answer Key.)
2. **Noticing:** *"Underline the verbs that end in -ing."* Elicit: *I'm sitting, It's raining, We're having, She's taking ...*
3. **Meaning.** Write on the board: *Ravi usually walks to work. Today he's taking a taxi.* Draw a timeline: crosses for every day, a wavy line at NOW. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
4. **Form.** Elicit the three parts: subject + am / is / are + verb-ing. Elicit the negative and the question from the messages. Write the form table on the board.
5. **Spelling.** Write *make, sit, lie, play* on the board. Students come to the board and write the *-ing* form. Elicit the rules.
6. **Time words.** Students sort the time words (Handout 2, Exercise A) into two columns with a partner. Check quickly.
7. **Pronunciation.** Drill chorally and individually with contractions: *I'm sitting on the bus. She's taking a taxi. What are you doing?* Model the weak form of *are*.
8. **Error feedback from Activate 1.** Board two or three errors you noted in Stage 2 (e.g. *The man reading a newspaper*). Students correct them in pairs.

### Stage 4: Study, controlled practice (9 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To give accurate written practice of the form and the contrast.
**Interaction:** Individual, then pairs

1. Students complete Handout 2, Exercises B and C, individually. Say: *"Exercise B: choose the correct form. Exercise C: write the -ing form. Six minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"In B, do you write or circle?"* (Circle.) *"Alone or in pairs?"* (Alone, then check.)
2. Monitor and help weaker students by pointing back to the time words.
3. Pairs compare answers. Nominate students to read answers aloud and explain choices: *"Every day, so present simple."*

### Stage 5: Activate 2, second try (17 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To give students the chance to repeat the communicative task with improved accuracy and confidence, then to personalise the language.
**Interaction:** New pairs, then groups

1. Students change partners (Student A works with a new Student B). Return the café cards. Say: *"Do the café game again. Use the language from today. You have five minutes."*
2. Monitor and note improvements. Stop when most pairs have found all six differences. Ask: *"Did you find more differences this time? Was it easier?"*
3. **Personalised extension (8 minutes):** Write on the board: *What are you doing this week / this month that is different from usual?* Model: *"I usually drink coffee, but this week I'm drinking green tea."* In groups of three, students tell each other two things they usually do and one thing they are doing differently this week.
4. Each group reports one interesting sentence about a group member to the class.

### Stage 6: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To consolidate the lesson and give feedback.
**Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences heard in Activate 2: two correct, two with errors. Pairs decide which are correct and fix the others.
2. Ask: *"What's different between Activate 1 and Activate 2?"* Elicit that students used *be + -ing* more accurately.
3. Set homework.

## Differentiation

**Support**
- Give weaker students a verb list for the café task: *carry, drink, read, talk, eat, write, sleep, wait, pay, laugh*.
- Allow them to write two sentences before speaking in Activate 2.

**Stretch**
- Stronger students add a reason or a contrast: *She's drinking tea because she usually doesn't sleep well after coffee.*
- They write a short text message describing what they are doing right now, including one contrast with their routine.

## Assessment and success criteria
Students are successful if they:
- use *be + verb-ing* with the correct form of *be* in most sentences in Activate 2;
- choose the present simple for routines and the continuous for now in Handout 2 (8 out of 10 or better);
- find more differences, with fewer errors, in Activate 2 than in Activate 1.

## Homework
Take or find a photo of a busy place. Write 6 sentences about what is happening, and 2 sentences about what usually happens there.
*Example: A man is selling mangoes. Two boys are playing. On Sundays, this market is usually very busy.*

## Teacher's reflection
- Was the difference between Activate 1 and Activate 2 clear to me and to the students?
- Which errors from Activate 1 disappeared? Which remained?
- Did Study take too long? What would I cut next time?
