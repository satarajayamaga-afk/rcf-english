# Will vs Going To (Decisions, Offers and Predictions)

*Lesson theme: At the Café and Looking at the Sky*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Future Forms |
| Grammar point | Will vs Going To (Decisions, Offers and Predictions) |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who know going to for plans and have met will, but use them interchangeably, e.g. I will go to the cinema tonight, I bought tickets. |

## Language analysis

### Form

- **will / won't + base verb:** *I'll help you. It won't rain.* (Same form for all persons.)
- **be going to + base verb:** *I'm going to paint my room. It's going to rain.*

### Meaning and use

| **will** | **going to** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Instant decision (decided at the moment of speaking): The phone's ringing. I'll answer it. | Plan (decided before speaking): I've bought paint. I'm going to paint my room. |
| Offer: That bag looks heavy. I'll carry it. | Prediction with present evidence: Look at those clouds! It's going to rain. |
| Ordering: I'll have the rice, please. |  |
| Prediction based on opinion: I think Sri Lanka will win. |  |
| Promise: I won't tell anyone. |  |

Useful with *will*: *I think ..., I don't think ... will, probably, definitely, I'm sure ...*

### Pronunciation

- *I'll* /aɪl/, *you'll* /juːl/, *it'll* /ˈɪtl/; *won't* /wəʊnt/ (different from *want* /wɒnt/).

### Concept checking questions

*A: The phone's ringing. B: I'll answer it.*

1. Did B plan to answer the phone before? (No.)
2. When did B decide? (Now, at the moment of speaking.)

*Look at those clouds! It's going to rain.*

1. Can we see something now? (Yes, clouds.)
2. Is it a guess based on evidence? (Yes.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| will for planned events. | Ask: "Did you decide before?" Board the decision flowchart. |
| will + to (I will to help). | Highlight base verb only. |
| won't pronounced like want. | Minimal-pair drill: I won't go / I want to go. |
| Students only use will in the café role-play. | Role cards include a plan question (What are you going to do after lunch?). |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To create interest and introduce decisions and predictions. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Describe a scene in words or with a quick sketch: *a man on a ladder leaning too far to one side.* Ask: *"What's going to happen?"* Accept any answers.
2. Pretend to drop a pile of books. Ask: *"Can anyone help me?"* A student helps; elicit or say for them: *"I'll help you!"*
3. Ask two students: *"What are you doing after class? Do you have plans?"*

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present *will* and *going to* in context and clarify the difference in meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the three dialogues. For each underlined sentence, write P (plan), D (decision now), O (offer) or PR (prediction). Four minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many dialogues?"* (Three.) *"What do you write?"* (P, D, O or PR.)
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Clarification (14 minutes)**

1. **Decisions and plans.** Board *The phone's ringing. I'll answer it.* and *I've bought paint. I'm going to paint my room.* Ask the CCQs. Build the decision flowchart on the left of the board: *"Did you decide before? → going to. Now? → will."*
2. **Offers and orders.** Board *I'll carry them. I'll have the rice, please.* Elicit: *will* for offers and ordering.
3. **Predictions.** Contrast *Look at those clouds! It's going to rain* (evidence) with *I think it'll look great* (opinion). Ask: *"Which one has evidence you can see?"*
4. **Form.** Elicit *will / won't + base verb* (same for all persons) and *be going to + base verb*.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *I'll get it! I'll have the rice. It won't hurt.* Minimal pair: *I won't go / I want to go.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing the correct form. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: choose will or going to. Exercise B: complete the sentences. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs compare and justify using the flowchart.
3. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use *will* and *going to* fluently in a role-play and in making predictions. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups

1. **Café role-play (9 minutes).** Give out Handout 3. Student A is a waiter; Student B is a customer. Customers must order a drink, food and a dessert; waiters must make two offers; both must ask about each other's plans for the afternoon. Model one exchange. Swap roles after four minutes.
   - ICQs: *"How many offers does the waiter make?"* (Two.) *"What do you ask about at the end?"* (Plans.)
2. **Predictions (6 minutes).** Give out Handout 4. Groups decide what is going to happen in each situation (evidence), then make three opinion predictions about their classmates' futures: *I think Ravi will become a famous chef!*
3. Groups share their funniest prediction (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences heard in Stage 4 (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Ask: *"How do you decide between will and going to?"* Elicit the flowchart.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a short dialogue (8–10 lines) in which one person offers help, one person orders something and both talk about their plans.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a phrase card: *I'll have ..., I'll get you ..., Shall I ...?, I'm going to ...*
- Let them play the customer (the simpler role) first.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *probably / definitely* in the correct position (*I'll probably ..., I probably won't ...*).
- They use *Shall I ...?* for offers.

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the role-play, students use *I'll* for ordering and offers and *going to* for plans.
- 10 out of 13 or better on Handout 2.
- Students distinguish evidence and opinion predictions in the group task.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use the decision test (before / now) on their own?
- Was the café role-play realistic and useful?

# Handout 2: Practice

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**A. Choose will or going to.**

1. The phone's ringing. I (will / am going to) answer it.
2. I've bought paint. I (will / am going to) paint my room.
3. Watch out! You (will / are going to) fall!
4. I think Sri Lanka (will / is going to) win the match.
5. This bag is heavy. I (will / am going to) carry it for you.
6. We've booked a hotel. We (will / are going to) stay in Ella for three days.
7. I'm thirsty. I think I (will / am going to) have a lime juice.
8. Don't worry, I (won't / am not going to) tell anyone. I promise.

**B. Complete with will ('ll), won't or the correct form of going to.**

1. A: I don't have any money. B: Don't worry, I ______________ lend you some.
2. Look at that boy on the wall! He ______________ fall!
3. We ______________ visit Kandy next month. We booked the train last week.
4. I'm sure you ______________ pass the exam. You studied hard.
5. A: Would you like tea or coffee? B: I ______________ have coffee, please.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| I will to help you. | I will help you. |
| I will go to the cinema tonight. I bought the tickets last week. | I'm going to go to the cinema tonight. I bought the tickets last week. |
| It will rain. Look at the clouds! | It's going to rain. Look at the clouds! |
| I want help you. (meaning I'll) | I'll help you. |
