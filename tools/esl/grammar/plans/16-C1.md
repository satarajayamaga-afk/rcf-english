# Phrasal Verbs and Register (phrasal verbs vs formal equivalents, and nouns from phrasal verbs such as setback, outcome, breakdown)

*Lesson theme: From Chat to Report*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Phrasal Verbs |
| Grammar point | Phrasal Verbs and Register (phrasal verbs vs formal equivalents, and nouns from phrasal verbs such as setback, outcome, breakdown) |
| Level | C1 (Advanced) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Advanced students who use many phrasal verbs accurately, but they mix registers: they write We found out that sales went down in formal reports, and sound stiff in speech (Let us postpone the meeting). | Subsidiary aims | To practise stress in phrasal verbs vs their nouns (break DOWN vs BREAKdown); to transform a text from one register to another. |

## Language analysis

### Phrasal verbs and formal equivalents

| **Phrasal verb (neutral / informal)** | **Formal equivalent** |
| :-- | :-- |
| find out | discover, ascertain |
| go up / go down | increase, rise / decrease, decline |
| put off | postpone, defer |
| call off | cancel |
| carry out | conduct |
| look into | investigate, examine |
| set up | establish |
| bring about | cause |
| come up with | devise, propose |
| put up with | tolerate |
| make up (of) | constitute, comprise |
| cut down on | reduce |

- Phrasal verbs are not "wrong" in formal writing: *carry out*, *set up* and *point out* are common in academic and business texts. Highly idiomatic ones (*mess up, pull off, fob off*) are inappropriate.
- Formal writing often prefers **nominalisation**: *The machines broke down repeatedly* → *There were repeated breakdowns.*

### Nouns from phrasal verbs

| **Verb** | **Noun** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| set back | a setback | The flood was a major setback. |
| come out | the outcome (reversed) | The outcome of the review was positive. |
| break down | a breakdown | a breakdown in communication / of costs |
| turn out | (the) turnout | Turnout at the meeting was low. |
| draw back | a drawback | The main drawback is the cost. |
| cut back | cutbacks | budget cutbacks |
| put in | input | We welcome your input. |
| take over | a takeover | a hostile takeover |
| break through | a breakthrough | a medical breakthrough |

- Spelling: closed (*setback, breakdown*) or hyphenated (*check-in, follow-up*); some reverse the order (*outcome, input, outbreak, upkeep*).

### Pronunciation

- Phrasal verb: stress on the particle: *break DOWN, set BACK, turn OUT*.
- Noun: stress on the first part: *BREAKdown, SETback, TURNout*.

### Concept checking questions

*The project suffered a setback.*

1. Did the project move forward or backward? (Backward; it was delayed.) Is *setback* a verb or noun here? (Noun.)

*Would "We messed up the order" be suitable in a report to the board?*

1. (No, it is too informal; *An error was made in the order* or *We made an error in the order*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Believing phrasal verbs are always informal. | Show corpus-style examples of carry out, set up in academic writing. |
| Over-formal speech (Let us postpone ...). | Contrast spoken and written versions of the same message. |
| Wrong stress on nouns (break DOWN as a noun). | Drill verb / noun pairs with clapping. |
| Spelling of derived nouns (out-come, set-back). | Highlight closed, hyphenated and reversed forms. |
| Mismatched formal verbs (ascertain the meeting). | Teach collocations: postpone a meeting, conduct a survey. |

## Board plan

| **Left: neutral / informal** | **Centre: formal** | **Right: nouns and stress** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| find out · go up / down · put off · call off · look into · set up · bring about · come up with · put up with · cut down on · (avoid in reports: mess up, pull off) | discover · increase / decrease · postpone · cancel · investigate · establish · cause · devise / propose · tolerate · reduce · nominalise: broke down → breakdowns | setback · outcome · breakdown · turnout · drawback · cutbacks · input · takeover · breakthrough · verb: break DOWN · noun: BREAKdown |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To raise awareness of register. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Board two sentences: *"Hey, we've had to put off the trip."* and *"The excursion has been postponed."* Ask: *"Who might say or write each one? Where?"*
2. Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Elicit: register depends on audience, purpose and medium.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present phrasal verbs and their formal equivalents in two texts and clarify register and derived nouns. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read a WhatsApp message and a formal report about the same event at a factory in Biyagama. What happened? Which details are only in one text?"*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the formal equivalents of the phrasal verbs, and the nouns in the report that come from phrasal verbs."*
   - ICQs: *"Where are the phrasal verbs?"* (In the message.) *"Where are the formal words?"* (In the report.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the board.
2. Point out that *carry out* and *set up* appear in the report too; they are acceptable in formal writing.
3. **Nominalisation.** Board *The machines broke down three times* → *There were three breakdowns*.
4. **Stress.** Drill verb / noun pairs: *break DOWN / BREAKdown, set BACK / SETback, turn OUT / TURNout*.
5. Ask the CCQs.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise register choices and derived nouns accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: replace the phrasal verbs with formal verbs. Exercise B: complete with a noun from a phrasal verb. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check and read aloud the nouns with correct stress. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To transform texts between registers. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Pair A rewrites the informal voice note transcript as a formal email to the client; Pair B rewrites the formal notice as a friendly message to colleagues (10 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Do you keep the same information?"* (Yes, only the register changes.)
2. **Compare (5 minutes).** A and B pairs join, read each other's versions and check: Is the register consistent? Is the information complete?
3. Two versions are read to the class (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board examples of register mixing from Stage 4. Students make them consistent.
2. Ask: *"When is a phrasal verb the better choice, even in writing?"* (When it is the normal term, e.g. *carry out*, or for a friendlier tone.)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Find a short news article (200 words) and a social media post about the same story. List the phrasal verbs and formal verbs in each. Then rewrite the post in the register of the article.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the formal equivalents list during Stage 4.
- Let them rewrite sentence by sentence rather than a whole text.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add more derived nouns: *outbreak, upkeep, onset, offshoot, write-off, follow-up*.
- They identify phrasal verbs that have no single-word equivalent (*carry on, give up*) and discuss how formal writers handle them.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The transformed text keeps the same content and uses a consistent register.
- At least five formal equivalents (or phrasal verbs, for the informal task) and three derived nouns are used accurately.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students understand that phrasal verbs are not banned in formal writing?
- Was the two-way transformation (formal ↔ informal) useful?

# Handout 4: Register Transformation

**Pair A: rewrite as a formal email to the client.**

*Voice note transcript (sales manager to colleague):* "Hi, so basically we've got to call off Friday's visit to the client's warehouse because the truck broke down. Can you let them know? Also, they want us to come up with a cheaper option, so we need to work out the costs again. Prices have gone up a lot. Sorry, it's been a mess!"

**Pair B: rewrite as a friendly message to colleagues.**

*Notice:* "Staff are advised that the quarterly training session has been postponed owing to the unavailability of the trainer. A revised date will be established following consultation with department heads. Staff are requested to submit their input regarding preferred dates by Wednesday. The organising committee regrets any inconvenience caused."

**Checklist:** ☐ same information ☐ consistent register ☐ at least five changes in verbs or nouns

# Answer Key

**Handout 1**

1. The new packing machine broke down three times (it had been incorrectly installed). · 2. The new delivery date (18 April) / the trial results will be reported next week. · 3. *A team member*; formal reports are impersonal and focus on actions, not individuals.

**Handout 2, Exercise A**

put off: postponed · found out: discovered · set up: installed · looking into: investigated · cut down on: reduction in · gone down: decline · came up with: proposed

**Handout 2, Exercise B**

setback, breakdowns, cutbacks, outcome

**Handout 2, Exercise C**

1. Nominalisation makes writing more formal, impersonal and concise. · 2. No; *set up* is acceptable in formal writing, though *installed* is more precise here.

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. The committee investigated / examined the complaints. · 2. Prices increased / rose by 12% last year. · 3. The school was established / founded in 1925. · 4. We will not tolerate this behaviour. · 5. Women constitute / comprise 60% of the workforce.

**Handout 3, Exercise B**

1. turnout · 2. setback · 3. drawback · 4. breakthrough · 5. breakdown

**Handout 4 (model answers)**

Pair A: *Dear Mr Fernando, I regret to inform you that Friday's visit to your warehouse has been cancelled owing to a vehicle breakdown. In response to your request, we are devising a more economical option and will recalculate the costs, as prices have increased considerably. We apologise for any inconvenience.* Pair B: *Hi all, just to let you know the training session has been put off because the trainer can't make it. We'll set a new date once we've talked to the department heads. Can you send in your preferred dates by Wednesday? Sorry for the hassle!*

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| The results went down significantly. (in a report) | The results declined significantly. |
| a break DOWN (noun stress) | a BREAKdown |
| an out-come / a set-back | an outcome / a setback |
| We ascertained the meeting. | We postponed / cancelled the meeting. |
