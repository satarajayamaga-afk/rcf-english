# Comparison in Formal and Academic Writing (twice as many as, compared with, inversion after than and as)

*Lesson theme: Describing Data*

## Language analysis

### Key structures

| **Function** | **Structure** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Multiples | twice / three times + as + adj / many / much + as | Colombo received twice as many tourists as Kandy. |
|  | twice / three times + comparative + than | Prices were three times higher than in 2010. |
| Fractions | half / a third / a quarter + as + adj / many / much + as | Galle had half as many visitors as Kandy. |
| Comparing with a reference | compared with / to, in comparison with / to, relative to | Compared with 2015, spending rose sharply. |
| Contrast | whereas, while | Tourism grew in the south, whereas it fell in the north. |
| Like with like | that of / those of | Japan's population is older than that of Sri Lanka. |
| Countable / uncountable | fewer + plural; less + uncountable | fewer tourists, less money |
| Formal inversion | than / as + auxiliary + subject | Women spent more than did men. Tourism grew rapidly, as did hotel construction. |
| Precise degree | significantly, considerably, marginally, slightly + comparative; by far, by a wide margin | spending was significantly higher; by far the largest share |
| Superlative + of / in | the highest proportion of, the lowest figure in | the highest proportion of visitors |

### Meaning and use

- These structures allow precise, varied and objective comparison of figures in academic and formal writing.
- *that of / those of* avoids comparing unlike things (a population with a country).
- Inversion after *than* and *as* is optional and formal; it sounds natural with longer subjects.

### Pronunciation

- Not a focus; when presenting data aloud, stress the numbers and the comparative words: *TWICE as many visitors.*

### Concept checking questions

*Colombo received twice as many tourists as Kandy.*

1. If Kandy received 100,000, how many did Colombo receive? (200,000.)

*Japan's population is older than that of Sri Lanka.*

1. What does *that* refer to? (The population.)
2. Why not *older than Sri Lanka*? (We compare populations, not a population and a country.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| two times more bigger, the double of | Board the two multiple patterns; cross out errors. |
| less tourists in formal writing | Countable / uncountable reminder in the checklist. |
| Comparing unlike things | Highlight that of / those of with arrows. |
| Inversion with the wrong auxiliary (than did women spent) | Board than did men (auxiliary + subject only). |
| Repetitive more than | Synonym bank; peer-editing checklist. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate language of comparison with numbers. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Board: *Kandy: 100 · Colombo: 200 · Galle: 50*. Ask: *"How many ways can you compare these numbers?"*
2. Pairs write three sentences (2 minutes). Take ideas and board the most varied ones.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present formal comparative structures in a model text and clarify their form and use. **Interaction:** Pairs, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (5 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Look at the chart and read the description. Is every statement accurate? Find one key feature."*
2. Pairs compare.

**Part B: Analysis (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the structures in the model and complete the table. Then answer the questions."*
   - ICQs: *"Where do you find the examples?"* (In the model description.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the patterns in the centre of the board.
2. **Multiples and fractions.** Ask the CCQs. Cross out *two times more bigger* and *the double of*.
3. **Like with like.** Draw an arrow from *that in Kandy* to *spending*, and compare *Japan's population is older than that of Sri Lanka*.
4. **fewer / less.** Elicit examples from the chart.
5. **Inversion.** Board *Women spent more than did men* and *Tourism grew, as did hotel construction.* Point out that this is formal and optional.
6. Build the degree-word list and the checklist.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the structures accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite each sentence using the word given. Exercise B: correct the errors. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To write an accurate and varied description of data. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups of four, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Say: *"Write a description of the chart (about 150 words). Select the key features and make comparisons. Use at least eight different structures from today."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you describe every number?"* (No, the key features.) *"How many structures?"* (At least eight.)
2. Pairs write (11 minutes).
3. Two pairs join, swap descriptions and check them with the checklist on the board: accuracy of data, like with like, *fewer / less*, variety (4 minutes).
4. Two groups read their best sentences aloud (3 minutes).
5. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board strong sentences and two or three with errors. Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which structure will you use in your next exam essay?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Find a chart or table in a newspaper or report (or use one provided). Write a 170-word description using at least ten different comparative structures.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a pattern card with examples.
- Provide an opening sentence for the writing task: *The chart compares ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use inversion after *than / as* and *by far / by a wide margin*.
- They add approximate language: *just under half, roughly twice as many, well over a third*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The description uses at least eight different comparative structures accurately.
- Data are reported accurately, with like compared with like.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students vary their comparisons?
- Was the *that of / those of* structure used correctly?

# Handout 1: Chart and Model Description

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**International visitors to three Sri Lankan cities (thousands)**

| **City** | **2015** | **2024** | **Average spending per visitor (Rs.) 2024** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Colombo | 400 | 800 | 60,000 |
| Kandy | 300 | 400 | 30,000 |
| Galle | 100 | 400 | 45,000 |

**Model description**

The table compares the number of international visitors to Colombo, Kandy and Galle in 2015 and 2024, together with average spending in 2024.

Colombo attracted the most visitors in both years, and by far the most in 2024, when its figure had doubled. Galle experienced the most dramatic growth: in 2024 it received four times as many visitors as in 2015, whereas Kandy's figure rose only slightly. As a result, Galle and Kandy received the same number of visitors in 2024, although in 2015 Galle had only a third as many as Kandy.

In terms of spending, the average Colombo visitor spent twice as much as the average visitor to Kandy. Spending in Galle was considerably higher than that in Kandy, as was its rate of growth. Overall, fewer visitors went to Kandy than to Colombo, and they spent less money.

**Handout 4 (model description)**

The table compares how students in Colombo and Kurunegala travelled to school in 2014 and 2024. In both years, walking was by far the most common method in Kurunegala, whereas in Colombo the bus was the most popular. However, the proportion of Colombo students who walked halved over the decade, falling from 30% to 15%. Over the same period, the use of private cars in Colombo doubled, and school vans became considerably more popular, rising from 20% to 30%. In Kurunegala, changes were far less dramatic: the figure for walking fell by only ten percentage points, bus use remained unchanged, and van and car use each rose marginally to 15%. In 2024, the proportion of Colombo students travelling by van was twice that in Kurunegala. Overall, fewer students walked to school in both districts, and car use grew more quickly in Colombo than it did in Kurunegala.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| two times more bigger | twice as big / twice as many |
| the double of | twice as many / much as |
| less people (formal writing) | fewer people |
| higher than teachers (salaries) | higher than those of teachers |
| than did men spent | than did men / than men did |
