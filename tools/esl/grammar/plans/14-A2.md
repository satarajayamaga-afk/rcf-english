# Past Simple Questions and Subject vs Object Questions (Who called you? Who did you call?)

*Lesson theme: The Mystery of the Missing Cake*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Question Forms |
| Grammar point | Past Simple Questions and Subject vs Object Questions (Who called you? Who did you call?) |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who form present simple questions correctly. In the past they say Where you went? or Did you went?, and they add did to every question with who / what (Who did eat the cake? meaning Who ate it?). |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise past simple questions with did + base verb, and to contrast subject questions (no did: Who ate the cake?) with object questions (did: Who did you see?). |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in asking follow-up questions; to practise weak did you /dɪdʒə/. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can investigate a mystery by asking and answering past questions accurately, e.g. Who ate the cake? What did you do at 3 o'clock? Who saw you? |
| Materials | Handout 1 (the mystery story), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (role cards for the investigation), Handout 4 (detective's notes). |

## Language analysis

### Past simple questions

| **Type** | **Form** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Yes / no | Did + subject + base verb? | Did you see him? Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. |
| Wh- | Question word + did + subject + base verb? | Where did you go? What time did she leave? |
| be | Was / Were + subject? | Were you at home? Where was she? |

- After *did*, the verb is in the **base form**: *Did you go?* (not *Did you went?*)

### Subject vs object questions

|  | **Question** | **Answer** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Subject question (who / what = the doer) | Who ate the cake? (no did; past verb) | Nimal ate the cake. |
| Object question (who / what = the receiver) | Who did Nimal see? (did + base verb) | Nimal saw the cook. |
| Subject question | What happened? | The cake disappeared. |

- Test: if the answer replaces the **subject** of the sentence, there is no *did*.

### Pronunciation

- *Did you* is often /dɪdʒə/: *What did you do?* /wɒt dɪdʒə duː/.
- Wh- questions fall; yes / no questions rise.

### Concept checking questions

*Who ate the cake?*

1. Do we know who ate it? (No, that's the question.)
2. Is the answer the person who did the action? (Yes.) So is there *did*? (No.)

*Who did Nimal see?*

1. Who did the seeing? (Nimal.)
2. Is the answer the person Nimal saw? (Yes.) So is there *did*? (Yes.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Missing did (Where you went?). | Board arrows: did at the front; drill. |
| Past form after did (Did you went?). | Board: did + base verb; cross out went. |
| did in subject questions (Who did eat the cake?). | Answer test: point to the subject of the answer. |
| What did happen? | Teach What happened? as a fixed chunk. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To create interest in the context. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Say: *"Yesterday, someone ate my lunch at school! I want to find out who. What questions can I ask?"*
2. Take ideas and board two questions exactly as students say them. Don't correct yet.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present past questions and subject / object questions in context and clarify the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the story about the birthday party. What happened to the cake? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Look at the detective's questions at the end. Which questions have did? Which don't?"* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **Past questions.** Board *You went to the kitchen.* → *Did you go to the kitchen?* → *Where did you go?* Highlight *did* + base verb. Cross out *Did you went?*
2. **be.** Board *Were you at home? Where was she?* (no *did* with *was / were*).
3. **Subject vs object.** Board *Somebody ate the cake.* → *Who ate the cake?* and *Nimal saw somebody.* → *Who did Nimal see?* Ask the CCQs. Show the answer test.
4. Teach *What happened?* as a chunk.
5. Return to the questions from Stage 1 and correct them together.
6. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *What did you do? Where did you go? Who ate the cake?*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: write the past questions. Exercise B: subject or object question? Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use past questions fluently in a role-play investigation. **Interaction:** Groups of five, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. In each group, one student is the detective and four are suspects (the cook, a guest, the neighbour, the little brother). Suspects read their role cards (2 minutes).
2. **Investigation (10 minutes).** The detective questions each suspect and takes notes on Handout 4: *"Where were you at 3 o'clock? What did you do? Who saw you? Who opened the kitchen door?"* Other suspects can also ask questions.
   - ICQs: *"Can suspects tell the truth?"* (Yes, except the guilty one!) *"What does the detective do?"* (Asks questions and takes notes.)
3. **Solve it (4 minutes).** Each detective tells the class who ate the cake and why: *"The neighbour ate it! She said she was at home, but the cook saw her in the garden."*
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four questions from Stage 4 (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Ask: *"When do we NOT use did?"* (Subject questions; *was / were*.)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write 8 questions to ask a family member about the best day of their life. Include two subject questions (*Who ...? What happened?*). Write their answers.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give the detective a list of question starters.
- Weaker students play suspects (answering is easier than asking).

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *Why ...?* and *How ...?* questions and follow-up questions.
- They use *Whose* and *Which*: *Whose plate was that? Which door did you use?*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- In the investigation, the detective uses *did* + base verb in object questions and no *did* in subject questions.
- Students use *What happened?* correctly.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students overuse *did* in subject questions?
- Was the mystery role-play engaging?
