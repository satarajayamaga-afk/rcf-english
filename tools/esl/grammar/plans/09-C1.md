# Advanced Passive Forms in Academic Writing (impersonal passives, passive infinitives and gerunds)

*Lesson theme: Writing Up Research*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |

## Language analysis

### Key forms

| **Form** | **Pattern** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Impersonal passive with it | It + is said / thought / believed / reported / claimed / assumed + that-clause | It is widely believed that social media affects sleep. |
| Personal passive + infinitive | Subject + is thought / said / believed + to + infinitive | Social media is believed to affect sleep. |
| ... with perfect infinitive (earlier time) | Subject + is thought + to have + pp | The decline is thought to have begun in 2015. |
| ... with continuous infinitive | Subject + is said + to be + -ing | Usage is reported to be increasing. |
| Passive infinitive | to be + pp | Participants expected to be paid. The results need to be verified. |
| Passive gerund | being + pp | Many respondents disliked being observed. Without being told the purpose, ... |
| Prepositional verbs | verb + preposition stays together | The issue was looked into. The patients were well cared for. |
| Two objects | indirect object becomes subject (most common) | Participants were given a questionnaire. (also: A questionnaire was given to participants.) |
| There + passive infinitive / reporting | There is thought to be ... / There are said to be ... | There are thought to be over 2,000 species. |

### Use in academic writing

- Objectivity: the focus is on the research process and findings, not the researcher.
- Distance and hedging: reporting passives show that a claim comes from others or is not certain.
- Information flow: the passive lets known information (the topic) come first.

### Pronunciation

- Weak forms in reading aloud: *is thought to have* /ɪz θɔːt tə həv/; *being* /ˈbiːɪŋ/.

### Concept checking questions

*Social media is believed to affect sleep.*

1. Is this the writer's personal opinion? (Not necessarily; it's a general belief.)
2. Is it certain? (No, it's believed.)

*Participants disliked being observed.*

1. Who observed whom? (Researchers observed the participants.)
2. Did participants like it? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Mixed patterns (It is believed him to be ...). | Board the two patterns side by side: It is believed that he is ... / He is believed to be ... |
| Wrong infinitive for earlier time (The decline is thought to begin in 2015). | Timeline: thought = now; to have begun = earlier. |
| Active gerund with passive meaning (She hates interrupting meaning others interrupt her). | CCQ: "Who interrupts whom?" |
| Dropping the preposition (The issue was looked). | Highlight the verb + particle as one unit. |
| Overuse making text unclear. | Discuss when the active (This study shows ...) is clearer. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To raise awareness of academic style. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Write two sentences on the board: *We asked 200 students about their sleep.* / *Two hundred students were asked about their sleep.*
2. Ask: *"Which sentence sounds more academic? Why?"* Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Elicit: the focus is on the research, not the researchers.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present advanced passive forms in an academic text and clarify their form, meaning and function. **Interaction:** Pairs, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading and comparison (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the two summaries of the same study. Which one is suitable for a university assignment? List three differences."*
   - ICQs: *"How many summaries?"* (Two.) *"How many differences?"* (Three.)
2. Pairs compare. Elicit differences (personal pronouns, informal words, passive structures).

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the structures in Summary B and complete the table."*
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the patterns in the centre of the board.
2. **Impersonal passives.** Board *It is believed that social media affects sleep* → *Social media is believed to affect sleep.* Ask: *"What changes?"* Cross out *It is believed him to be*.
3. **Time reference.** Draw a timeline for *The decline is thought to have begun in 2015*. Ask the CCQs.
4. **Passive infinitives and gerunds.** Board *Participants expected to be paid* and *They disliked being observed.* Ask the CCQs.
5. **Prepositional verbs and two objects.** Board *The issue was looked into* and *Participants were given a questionnaire.*
6. Discuss function: objectivity, hedging, information flow. Note that the active is still preferred when it is clearer: *This study shows ...*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite each sentence using the word given. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Feedback as a class.
3. **Exercise B:** pairs correct the five errors. Check quickly.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use advanced passive forms in an academic paragraph. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups of four, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Say: *"These are a student's informal notes from a survey. Write an academic paragraph (about 150 words) reporting the method and findings. Use at least six advanced passive forms and hedge any claims."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you write I and we?"* (No, or very rarely.) *"How many advanced passive forms?"* (At least six.)
2. Pairs write (11 minutes).
3. Two pairs join and compare paragraphs. They underline each passive form and choose the clearest sentence from each paragraph (4 minutes).
4. Two groups read their best sentences aloud (3 minutes).
5. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board strong sentences and two or three with errors. Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"When is the active better in academic writing?"* (When the agent matters, or when the passive would be unclear or wordy.)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Find a short news article about a scientific study. Rewrite its main findings as a 200-word academic summary using at least eight advanced passive forms and appropriate hedging.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a pattern card with an example of each structure.
- Provide sentence starters for the writing task: *A total of ... were asked ... · It was found that ... · ... is thought to ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *is considered to have been*, *is estimated to have been -ing* and *There is believed to have been ...*.
- They identify where the active voice would be better and justify their choice.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The paragraph uses at least six advanced passive forms accurately.
- The paragraph contains no first-person pronouns and uses hedging appropriately.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use the infinitive forms with the correct time reference?
- Did the writing become more objective without becoming unclear?

# Handout 1: Two Research Summaries

**Summary A (informal)**

We asked 200 teenagers in Colombo about how they use their phones at night. We gave them a questionnaire and we also interviewed 20 of them. Lots of people think that social media is bad for sleep, and our results agree. Most teens said they hated it when their parents interrupted them, and they didn't like it when we watched them. We looked at the data carefully. We think the problem started around 2015, when smartphones got cheap. People say it is getting worse.

**Summary B (academic)**

A total of 200 teenagers in Colombo were surveyed about their night-time phone use. Participants were given a questionnaire, and 20 were selected to be interviewed. It is widely believed that social media has a negative effect on sleep, and the findings support this view. Most respondents reported disliking being interrupted by their parents, and many were uncomfortable at being observed. The data were carefully looked into. The problem is thought to have begun around 2015, when smartphones became affordable, and it is reported to be worsening.

**List three differences between the summaries.**

# Answer Key

**Handout 1 (possible differences)**

Summary B avoids *we / I*; uses the passive to focus on the research; uses formal vocabulary (*respondents, affordable, worsening*); uses reporting passives to distance the writer from claims (*It is widely believed ...*).

**Handout 2**

- Simple passive: *200 teenagers ... were surveyed* · Two objects: *Participants were given a questionnaire* · Passive infinitive: *20 were selected to be interviewed* · Impersonal: *It is widely believed that ...* · Passive gerund: *disliking being interrupted*; *at being observed* · Prepositional verb: *The data were carefully looked into* · Perfect infinitive: *is thought to have begun* · Continuous infinitive: *is reported to be worsening*
   1. To sound objective; the focus is on the research. · 2. *is thought* = now; *to have begun* = earlier (around 2015). · 3. Parents interrupted the respondents.

**Handout 4 (model paragraph)**

A total of 150 first-year students at a university in Kandy were surveyed about part-time employment and academic performance. All participants were given an online questionnaire, and 15 were selected to be interviewed. It is widely believed that part-time work has a negative effect on grades, and the findings appear to support this view: students who worked more than 15 hours a week were found to have lower marks. Many respondents reported disliking being asked to work extra hours. The trend is thought to have begun after tuition fees were increased in 2019, and the number of working students is reported to be rising. It is recommended that this issue be looked into further.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| It is believed him to be ... | He is believed to be ... / It is believed that he is ... |
| The war is thought to start in 1914. | The war is thought to have started in 1914. |
| The problem was looked. | The problem was looked into. |
| She hates to be interrupting. | She hates being interrupted. |
