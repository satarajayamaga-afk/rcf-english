# Dependent Prepositions (Verbs, Adjectives and Nouns + Preposition)

*Lesson theme: A Workplace Complaint*

## Lesson overview

## Language analysis

### Common combinations

| **Verb + prep** | **Adjective + prep** | **Noun + prep** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| depend on | interested in | reason for |
| rely on | good / bad at | increase / decrease in |
| insist on | afraid of | solution to |
| apologise for | keen on | attitude towards |
| succeed in | responsible for | advantage of |
| accuse someone of | aware of | demand for |
| complain about | angry with (someone) / about (something) | lack of |
| consist of | satisfied with | damage to |

### Preposition + -ing

- After prepositions we use the **-ing** form: *She's good* ***at*** ***managing*** *people. He apologised* ***for*** ***being*** *late. She succeeded* ***in*** ***passing*** *the exam.*

### Pronunciation

- Prepositions are usually weak and link to the next word: *depend on it* /dɪˈpend‿ɒn‿ɪt/; *interested in* /ˈɪntrəstɪd‿ɪn/.

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| L1 transfer (depends of, interested about). | Learn as chunks; dominoes game for memory. |
| Infinitive after preposition (succeeded to pass). | Rule: preposition + -ing. |
| Too many combinations to learn. | Focus on the most common; students make a personal learning list. |
| angry with / about confusion. | Contrast person vs thing. |

## Board plan

| **Left: vocabulary** | **Centre: combinations by preposition** | **Right: rule** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| complain · delay · demand · excuse · discount · customer service | ON: depend, rely, insist, keen · OF: afraid, aware, accuse sb, consist, lack · IN: interested, succeed, increase · FOR: apologise, responsible, reason, demand · AT: good, bad · TO: solution, damage · ABOUT: complain, worried · WITH: satisfied, disappointed | preposition + -ing: good at managing · apologise for being late |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"Tell your partner one thing you are good at, afraid of and interested in."*
2. Take two examples. Board the three adjectives with their prepositions.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present dependent prepositions in context and clarify the most common combinations and the preposition + *-ing* rule. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the email. What is the customer complaining about? What does she want? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check the two questions as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (5 minutes)**

1. Say: *"The prepositions in bold go with a word before them. Underline the word: complain about, insisted on ..."* Pairs list all the combinations.

**Part C: Clarification (9 minutes)**

1. Group the combinations by preposition on the board (centre). Elicit more examples for each group.
2. Point out that these are learned as chunks; there is often no logical rule.
3. Show the preposition + *-ing* rule: *She apologised for being late. He succeeded in passing.*
4. Contrast *angry with* (a person) and *angry about* (a thing).
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill linking: *depend on it, interested in it, good at it.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise and memorise the combinations. **Interaction:** Pairs, then individual, then whole class

1. **Dominoes (4 minutes).** Give each pair the dominoes from Handout 2. Pairs build the chain as fast as possible.
2. **Gap fill (5 minutes).** Give out Handout 3. Students write the missing prepositions alone, then check with a partner.
3. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the combinations fluently in discussion and accurately in writing. **Interaction:** Groups, then individual, then pairs

1. Give out Handout 4. **Discussion (8 minutes):** groups discuss the questions (*What do you rely on most? What are you worried about at the moment? What's the best solution to traffic in your town?*).
   - ICQs: *"Do you answer all the questions?"* (As many as possible.)
2. **Email (8 minutes):** students write a reply to the complaint email as the company manager, using at least eight combinations.
3. Partners check each other's emails for prepositions (2 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board errors from the discussion and emails; students correct them.
2. Each student writes a personal list of five combinations to learn.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Learn 15 combinations and write a sentence about yourself for each.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the reference table during the freer practice.
- Provide an email frame.

**Stretch**

- Add less common combinations: *object to, cope with, take advantage of*.
- Students write discussion questions for other groups.

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the discussion, students use correct prepositions in most combinations.
- The email contains at least eight correct combinations.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which combinations were most problematic?
- Did the dominoes help memory?

# Handout 1: Complaint Email

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Subject:** Delay to my kitchen order

Dear Mr Fernando,

I am writing to complain **about** the delay to my kitchen order. When I placed the order in May, your sales manager insisted **on** a delivery date of 1 July. I relied **on** this date and arranged for workers to install the kitchen.

It is now 20 July and the kitchen has still not arrived. Nobody has apologised **for** the delay or explained the reason **for** it. I am aware **of** the high demand **for** your products, but I am very disappointed **with** your customer service.

I am not interested **in** excuses. I would like to know who is responsible **for** my order and what the solution **to** this problem is. I also expect a discount because of the lack **of** communication.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely, Ms N. Perera

**Questions**

1. What is Ms Perera complaining about? ______________________________
2. What does she want? ______________________________

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| I'm interested about history. | I'm interested in history. |
| It depends of the price. | It depends on the price. |
| She succeeded to pass the exam. | She succeeded in passing the exam. |
| He apologised for to be late. | He apologised for being late. |
