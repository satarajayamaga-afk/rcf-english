# Gerunds as Subjects and after Prepositions; Make, Let and Want Someone to Do

*Lesson theme: Parents, Teenagers and Rules*

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Structure** | **Pattern** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Gerund as subject | verb-ing + verb | Learning a language takes time. |
| it + be + adjective + to-infinitive (alternative) | it is + adj + to + verb | It's important to sleep well. |
| Preposition + gerund | adjective / verb / noun + preposition + -ing | I'm good at drawing. Thanks for helping. Before going out, ... Instead of studying, ... |
| make / let + object + base verb | make / let + sb + verb (no to) | They make me study. They let me go out. |
| want / ask / tell / allow / expect / help + object + to-infinitive | verb + sb + to + verb | They want me to be a doctor. They don't allow me to stay out late. |

- *help* can take both: *help me (to) study*.
- Passive of *make*: *I was made* ***to*** *wait* (note *to* returns) and *let* → *be allowed to*.

### Meaning and use

- *make* = force; *let* = give permission; *allow* is more formal than *let*.
- Gerund subjects are common in general statements; *it is ... to* is more common in speech.

### Pronunciation

- Stress the object pronoun when contrasting: *They make ME do it, not my brother!*
- Weak *to* /tə/ in *want me to* /wɒnt mi tə/.

### Concept checking questions

*My parents make me do my homework.*

1. Do I have a choice? (No.)
2. Is there *to* after *make me*? (No.)

*They let me go out at weekends.*

1. Do they give permission? (Yes.)
2. What's a more formal verb with the same meaning? (*allow me to*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Infinitive after prepositions (good at swim). | Rule: preposition + -ing, always. |
| Base verb as subject (Learn English is ...). | Board Learning English is ... / It's ... to learn. |
| make / let + to (make me to study). | Board make / let + sb + verb; cross out to. |
| let in the passive (I was let to go). | Board I was allowed to go. |
| to as a preposition (look forward to see) | Preview: look forward to seeing (to = preposition). |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"What rules do you have at home? Are they fair?"*
2. Pairs list three rules (2 minutes). Take ideas; board one sentence exactly as a student says it.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the target structures in context and help students work out the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read about three teenagers. Whose parents are the strictest? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Alone or with a partner?"* (With a partner.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"What comes after at? After make me?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers and build the patterns in the centre of the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Highlight: preposition + *-ing*; gerund subject vs *it is ... to*; *make / let* + base verb; *want / allow* + *to*.
4. Show the passive: *I was made to wait. I was allowed to go.*
5. Return to the student's sentence from Stage 1 and check it.
6. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *They make me study. They let me go out. They want me to be a doctor.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the structures accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct form. Exercise B: rewrite the sentences. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the structures fluently in discussion. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. **Discussion (8 minutes).** Groups discuss the questions: *What do your parents make you do? What don't they let you do? What are you good at? Is being a teenager easier now than in the past?*
   - ICQs: *"Do you answer all the questions?"* (As many as possible.)
2. **House rules (7 minutes).** Pairs imagine they are parents and write five fair rules for their future teenage children, using *make, let, allow, expect* and *instead of / before + -ing*.
3. Pairs read their rules; the class votes for the fairest set (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which verbs don't take to?"* (*make, let.*)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a paragraph (120 words): *The rules in my house: fair or unfair?* Use at least two gerund subjects, two prepositions + *-ing*, and *make, let* and *allow*.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the patterns on a card.
- Provide sentence starters for the discussion.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *look forward to, be used to, object to* + *-ing* (to as a preposition).
- They use passive forms: *I was made to apologise. We weren't allowed to leave.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the discussion, students use *make / let* without *to* and *want / allow* with *to*.
- No infinitives after prepositions in the house rules.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students stop using *to* after *make / let*?
- Was the topic engaging without being too personal?
