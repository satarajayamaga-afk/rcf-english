# Comparative Adjectives (bigger than, more expensive than)

*Lesson theme: Animals and Cities*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Comparatives and Superlatives |
| Grammar point | Comparative Adjectives (bigger than, more expensive than) |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | High beginners who know common adjectives (big, small, tall, fast, cheap, expensive, hot, beautiful) and be. They compare things with very or more + short adjective (An elephant is more big) and forget than. |
| Main aim | To present and practise comparative adjectives with -er, -ier, more, and the irregular forms better and worse, with than. |
| Subsidiary aims | To review adjectives for describing animals and places; to practise the weak form of than; to develop speaking through a guessing game. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can write and say 6 sentences comparing animals, places and things, e.g. An elephant is bigger than a horse. Colombo is hotter than Nuwara Eliya. |
| Materials | Pictures of an elephant, a horse, a cheetah, a turtle, Colombo and Nuwara Eliya (or described in words), Handout 1 (Kasun's postcard), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (comparison cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Adjective** | **Rule** | **Comparative** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| One syllable | + -er | small → smaller, tall → taller, fast → faster, cheap → cheaper |
| One syllable ending in -e | + -r | nice → nicer, large → larger |
| One vowel + one consonant | double the consonant + -er | big → bigger, hot → hotter, thin → thinner |
| Two syllables ending in -y | -y → -ier | happy → happier, busy → busier, easy → easier |
| Two or more syllables | more + adjective | expensive → more expensive, beautiful → more beautiful |
| Irregular |  | good → better, bad → worse |

- **comparative + than**: *An elephant is bigger* ***than*** *a horse.*

### Meaning and use

- We use comparatives to compare **two** people, animals or things.

### Pronunciation

- Weak *than* /ðən/: *bigger than* /ˈbɪɡə ðən/.
- Weak *-er* /ə/: *taller* /ˈtɔːlə/.

### Concept checking questions

*An elephant is bigger than a horse.*

1. How many animals are we comparing? (Two.)
2. Which one is big? (Both.) Which one is more big? (The elephant.)
3. Can I say *more big*? (No, *bigger*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| more + short adjective (more big). | Syllable clapping: one clap → -er. |
| Missing than or using that / of (bigger that). | Board -er + than in colour. |
| Spelling (biger, happyer). | Spelling rules table; quick spelling quiz. |
| more better | Highlight irregular forms. |

## Board plan

| **Left: adjectives** | **Centre: rules** | **Right: pronunciation** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| big · small · tall · fast · slow · hot · cold · cheap · expensive · beautiful · heavy · good · bad | short: + -er → taller · big → bigger · happy → happier · long: more + adj → more expensive · good → better · bad → worse · comparative + than · not: more big ✗ | than /ðən/ · bigger than /ˈbɪɡə ðən/ |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate adjectives and introduce the idea of comparing. **Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Show the elephant and the horse. Ask: *"Is the elephant big? Is the horse big?"* (Yes, both.) *"Which is very, very big?"* (The elephant.)
2. Show the cheetah and the turtle. Ask: *"Which is fast?"* Elicit adjectives and board them on the left.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Part A: Context (8 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To present comparatives in context. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read Kasun's postcard from Nuwara Eliya. Is Nuwara Eliya hotter or colder than Colombo?"* (3 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"Who is the postcard from?"* (Kasun.)
2. Students answer the true / false questions. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To clarify form, meaning and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. **Meaning.** Board *An elephant is bigger than a horse.* Ask the CCQs.
2. **Form.** Students find comparatives in the postcard. Sort them on the board: *colder, smaller* (-er); *bigger, hotter* (double); *busier* (-ier); *more beautiful, more expensive* (more); *better* (irregular). Elicit the rules.
3. **Syllables.** Clap the syllables: *cold* (1), *beau-ti-ful* (3). Ask: *"One clap: -er or more?"*
4. **than.** Highlight *than* after the comparative. Board *bigger that* ✗.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill weak *than*: *bigger than, colder than, more expensive than.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: write the comparative. Exercise B: write sentences. Six minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you write than in Exercise B?"* (Yes.)
2. Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers. Check spelling on the board.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use comparatives in a game and in writing. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then individual

1. **Comparison cards (8 minutes).** Give each pair a set of cards (Handout 3). Students take two cards and make as many true comparative sentences as they can: *A bicycle is cheaper than a car. A car is faster than a bicycle.* One point for each correct sentence.
   - ICQs: *"How many cards do you take?"* (Two.)
2. **Guess what I'm thinking (6 minutes).** In groups, one student thinks of an animal. Others ask: *"Is it bigger than a dog?" "Is it faster than a horse?"* until they guess.
3. **Writing (4 minutes).** Students write 4 sentences comparing their town with another town they know.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Two students read their sentences about their town.
2. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write 6 sentences comparing two members of your family or two of your friends (*My brother is taller than me. My sister is busier than my mother.*).

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the rules table on a card.
- Let them use only one-syllable adjectives in the card game.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *much* and *a bit*: *An elephant is much bigger than a horse.*
- They use *less* + long adjective: *A bicycle is less expensive than a car.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- Students use *than* after the comparative in their sentences.
- Students choose *-er* or *more* correctly.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the syllable clapping help with *-er* or *more*?
- Which spelling rules caused problems?

# Handout 1: Kasun's Postcard

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

Dear Amma,

Nuwara Eliya is great! It's colder than Colombo, so I'm wearing my jumper every day. The town is smaller than Colombo and it's quieter too. There are fewer cars, so the air is better.

The tea plantations are more beautiful than in the photos. Lake Gregory is bigger than our lake at home. The hotel is more expensive than our hotel last year, but the food is better!

Tomorrow we're going to Horton Plains. Dad says the walk is longer and harder than the walk at Sigiriya. I'm happier here than in Colombo. It's not hotter, it's not busier, it's perfect!

Love, Kasun

**True (T) or False (F)?**

1. Nuwara Eliya is hotter than Colombo. ______
2. Nuwara Eliya is smaller than Colombo. ______
3. The hotel is cheaper than last year's hotel. ______
4. The walk at Horton Plains is longer than the walk at Sigiriya. ______
5. Kasun is happier in Nuwara Eliya. ______
