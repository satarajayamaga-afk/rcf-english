# Both, Either, Neither, All, None, Each and Every (with and without Of)

*Lesson theme: Choosing a Flat to Share*

## Language analysis

### Quantifiers for two things or people

| **Quantifier** | **+ noun** | **+ of + the / my / these + plural** | **+ of + pronoun** | **Verb** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| both (= the two) | both flats | both (of) the flats | both of them | plural: Both flats are ... |
| either (= one or the other) | either flat | either of the flats | either of them | singular: Either flat is fine. |
| neither (= not one and not the other) | neither flat | neither of the flats | neither of them | singular (formal); plural is common in speech: Neither of them has / have ... |

### Quantifiers for three or more

| **Quantifier** | **+ noun** | **+ of the / of them** | **Verb** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| all | all flats (in general) / all the flats | all of the flats / all of them | plural |
| none (= not one) | (no + noun: no flats) | none of the flats / none of them | singular or plural |
| each (= every one, individually; 2 or more) | each room | each of the rooms / each of them | singular |
| every (= all, as a group; 3 or more) | every room | (not: every of) → every one of them | singular |

### Key points

- *neither* already is negative: *Neither flat has a balcony* (not *doesn't have*).
- *both ... and*, *either ... or*, *neither ... nor*: *Neither the kitchen nor the bathroom is clean.*
- *of* is needed before pronouns: *both of them* (not *both them*); *every* cannot take *of*.
- Short answers: *"Which one do you prefer?" "Either. / Neither. / Both."* and *Me neither.*

### Pronunciation

- *either* /ˈaɪðə/ or /ˈiːðə/; *neither* /ˈnaɪðə/ or /ˈniːðə/; both are correct.
- Stress the quantifier in contrast: *NEITHER of them has a garden.*

### Concept checking questions

*Neither of the flats has a balcony.*

1. Does the first flat have a balcony? (No.) The second? (No.)
2. Why *has*, not *doesn't have*? (*Neither* is already negative.)

*You can choose either room.*

1. How many rooms are there? (Two.) Can you choose one? (Yes, one or the other; it doesn't matter.)

*Each room has a fan.*

1. Do all the rooms have a fan? (Yes.) Are we thinking about the rooms one by one or as a group? (One by one.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Double negative (Neither flat doesn't have). | Board: neither = not + not; positive verb. |
| Both of flats / both them. | Rule: of + the / my / pronoun; drill both of them. |
| Plural verb with each / every (Every students have). | Board every / each + singular noun + singular verb. |
| every of them. | Teach every one of them / each of them. |
| Confusing either (positive choice) and neither. | Two hands: either = pick one ✓; neither = ✗ ✗. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students with the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Ask: *"If you shared a flat with friends, what would be important: location, price, kitchen, a balcony, Wi-Fi?"*
2. Pairs rank their top three (2 minutes). Take ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the quantifiers in context and clarify form, meaning and agreement. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Tharushi has seen two flats in Kandy. Read her email to her friends. Which flat does she prefer? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (5 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline every quantifier in the email: both, either, neither, all, none, each, every. Is the next verb singular or plural?"* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (9 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2 (reference). Elicit the patterns from the email and build the board.
2. **Two vs three or more.** Use two flats, then three flats on the board to show *both / either / neither* vs *all / none*.
3. **of.** Board *both flats · both of the flats · both of them* and *every room · every one of them*. Cross out *every of them* and *both them*.
4. **Agreement.** Highlight *Neither of them has* and *Each room has*. Ask the CCQs. Mention that *neither of them have* and *none of them are* are common in informal speech.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *NEITHER of them has a garden. You can have EITHER room.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct option. Exercise B: rewrite the sentences using the word given. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the quantifiers fluently in a negotiation. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups of four, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. **Compare (5 minutes).** Pairs read three flat adverts and write five sentences comparing them: *All of the flats have Wi-Fi. None of them allows pets. Both Flat B and Flat C have parking.*
2. **Negotiate (8 minutes).** Groups of four are future flatmates. Each student has a secret priority card (budget, parking, quiet, walking distance). They must agree on one flat: *"Neither of those suits me because ... " "Either is fine with me."*
   - ICQs: *"Can you show your priority card?"* (No, say it.) *"How many flats do you choose?"* (One.)
3. **Report (5 minutes).** Each group explains its decision to the class.
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Quick fire: *"Do you like tea or coffee?"* Students answer with *both / either / neither* and *me neither / so do I*.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Compare two phones, two schools or two cities you know (150–180 words). Use *both, either, neither, all, none, each* and *every* at least once each.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students Handout 2 to use during the practice.
- Focus on *both / neither of them* and *all / none of them* first.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *neither ... nor* and *either ... or* with verb agreement: *Neither the landlord nor the tenants were happy.*
- They use *each other / one another* and *whichever*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- In the negotiation, students use *of* correctly before pronouns and no double negatives with *neither*.
- Students use singular verbs after *each* and *every*.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students stop saying *Neither ... doesn't*?
- Did the secret priorities create real negotiation?

# Handout 2: Language Reference

| **Two** | **Three or more** |
| :-- | :-- |
| both flats / both (of) the flats / both of them + plural verb | all the flats / all of them + plural verb |
| either flat / either of them + singular verb (one or the other) | none of the flats / none of them (not one) |
| neither flat / neither of them + singular verb (not one, not the other) | each room / each of them + singular verb |
| both ... and · either ... or · neither ... nor | every room / every one of them + singular verb |

**Remember**

- *Neither* is negative: *Neither flat* ***has*** *parking.* (not *doesn't have*)
- Use *of* before *the / my / these* and pronouns: *both of them, each of the rooms*.
- *every of* ✗ → *every one of*.

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. Both · 2. either · 3. Neither · 4. Every · 5. None · 6. Each · 7. likes · 8. All · 9. or · 10. none

**Handout 3, Exercise B**

1. Neither restaurant was open. / Neither of the restaurants was open. · 2. Both Anil and Sunil can swim. / Both of them can swim. · 3. None of the students passed the test. · 4. Each child got a present. / Each of the children got a present.

**Handout 4 (possible solution)**

No flat meets every priority. Flat B costs Rs. 22,500 each (too expensive for Student 1). Flat A has no parking and is noisy. Flat C (Rs. 17,500 each, quiet, parking) suits Students 1, 2 and 3, but Student 4 must take the bus. Any decision is acceptable if students justify it.
