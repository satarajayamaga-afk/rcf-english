# Stance Adverbs and Adverb + Adjective Collocations in Opinion Writing (admittedly, arguably, frankly; highly unlikely, deeply concerned)

*Lesson theme: Writing an Opinion Column*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |

## Language analysis

### Stance adverbs

| **Function** | **Adverbs** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| certainty | clearly, evidently, undoubtedly, inevitably | Clearly, the system is not working. |
| probability / assumption | arguably, presumably, apparently, seemingly | This is arguably the city's biggest problem. |
| concession (admitting a point against you) | admittedly, granted | Admittedly, buses are cheaper. |
| attitude / evaluation | surprisingly, understandably, regrettably, ironically, unfortunately | Understandably, residents are angry. |
| manner of speaking | frankly, honestly, simply put, broadly speaking | Frankly, the plan is a disaster. |

### Position and punctuation

- **Front**, followed by a comma (most common for emphasis): *Admittedly, it is expensive.*
- **Mid-position**, after *be* / the first auxiliary or before the main verb: *It is arguably the best option. The council has evidently ignored the report.*
- **End**, after a comma (less common, more conversational): *It's too late, frankly.*
- Meaning: *admittedly* = "I accept this point although it weakens my argument" (not "certainly"); *arguably* = "it can be argued" (hedging, not "certainly").

### Adverb + adjective collocations

| **Adverb** | **Common adjectives** |
| :-- | :-- |
| highly | unlikely, likely, successful, effective, controversial, qualified |
| deeply | concerned, worried, disappointed, moving, rooted |
| bitterly | disappointed, cold, opposed |
| widely | accepted, available, used, believed |
| fully | aware, committed, prepared |
| strongly | opposed, recommended (+ past participle) |
| painfully | slow, obvious, aware |

- Unnatural: *strongly unlikely, highly concerned, deeply likely, bitterly happy*.

### Pronunciation

- A front stance adverb is followed by a short pause: *Frankly, | it's a disaster.*

### Concept checking questions

*Admittedly, the scheme is expensive. However, it works.*

1. Is the writer for or against the scheme? (For.) Why mention the cost? (To accept a weakness before arguing.)

*It is arguably the best solution.*

1. Is the writer 100% certain? (No; it's an opinion that can be argued.)

*Is "highly concerned" natural?* (No; we say *deeply concerned*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| admittedly used to mean "definitely". | Concession frame: Admittedly, X. However, Y. |
| Overuse of I think and very. | "Ban list" during writing; replace with stance adverbs and strong collocations. |
| Wrong collocations (highly concerned). | Collocation grid; learn as chunks. |
| Mid-position errors (It arguably is ...). | Board: after be, before main verb. |
| Too many stance adverbs. | One or two per paragraph for impact. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To raise awareness of stance. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Board: *The new bus lane is a failure.* Then: *Frankly, the new bus lane is a failure.* and *The new bus lane is arguably a failure.* Ask: *"How does the writer's attitude change?"*
2. Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Elicit: *frankly* = strong and direct; *arguably* = more cautious.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present stance adverbs and collocations in an opinion column and clarify meaning, position and use. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the opinion column about car-free Sundays in Colombo. What is the writer's opinion? Which counter-argument does she accept?"*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the stance adverbs and classify them by function. Then find six adverb + adjective collocations."*
   - ICQs: *"How many functions?"* (Five.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the board.
2. Focus on *admittedly* and *arguably*. Ask the CCQs.
3. **Position.** Board front, mid and end examples. Highlight the comma.
4. **Collocations.** Board *deeply concerned* ✓ / *highly concerned* ✗.
5. Return to the warm-up sentence and write two more versions.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise meaning, position and collocation accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the best stance adverb. Exercise B: choose the natural collocation. Exercise C: rewrite with the adverb in mid-position. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language in persuasive writing. **Interaction:** Individual or pairs, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Students choose a topic and write an opinion paragraph (150 words) that includes a concession (*Admittedly ... However ...*), at least four stance adverbs and three strong collocations. *I think* and *very* are banned (11 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Which words can't you use?"* (*I think* and *very*.) *"What must you include?"* (A concession, four stance adverbs, three collocations.)
2. **Peer review (4 minutes).** Partners use the checklist to give feedback.
3. Two students read their paragraphs to the class (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (effective and incorrect). Students improve them.
2. Ask: *"What's the difference between 'admittedly' and 'arguably'?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a full opinion column (300 words) on a local issue. Use at least six stance adverbs from different functions and five strong collocations. Underline them.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the function table and the collocation grid during writing.
- Provide a paragraph frame: *Clearly, ... Admittedly, ... However, ... Understandably, ... It is highly unlikely that ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use stance phrases: *to be fair, by all accounts, in all likelihood, needless to say*.
- They vary register: rewrite the paragraph as a cautious academic text using *arguably, presumably, apparently*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The paragraph includes a correctly used concession with *admittedly* (or *granted*).
- At least four stance adverbs are used with the right meaning and position.
- At least three natural adverb + adjective collocations; no *I think* or *very*.
- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 3.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students understand the difference between certainty and hedging adverbs?
- Did the ban on *I think* and *very* improve the writing?

# Handout 1: Opinion Column

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Car-free Sundays: a small step Colombo needs to take** *by S. Wijeratne (fictional columnist)*

Anyone who has sat in Colombo traffic knows the problem. Clearly, the city cannot continue like this: journeys are painfully slow, the air is often unhealthy, and pedestrians are widely regarded as an afterthought.

The proposal to close Galle Road to cars on Sunday mornings is, arguably, the most practical idea in years. Surprisingly, a similar scheme in other Asian cities has proved highly successful, with families walking, cycling and shopping where cars once queued.

Admittedly, some shop owners are deeply concerned about losing customers, and their worries are understandable. However, evidence from elsewhere suggests that people on foot spend more time in shops, not less. Understandably, taxi drivers are bitterly opposed to the idea, but a few hours a week is hardly a disaster.

Frankly, the real obstacle is not money or logistics but political courage. Presumably the council is fully aware of the benefits. It is highly unlikely that the problem will solve itself. Isn't it time we tried?
