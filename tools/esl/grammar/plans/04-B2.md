GRAMMAR TOPIC: FUTURE FORMS · B2 (Upper-intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Future Probability (likely to, bound to, could well, I doubt)

*Lesson theme: Life in 2050*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Future Forms |
| Grammar point | Future Probability (likely to, bound to, could well, I doubt) |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who make predictions only with will and might. They make errors such as It is likely to it will rain and He will bound to win. |
| Main aim | To expand students' range of structures for expressing degrees of future probability and improve their accuracy. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in discussion and justification; to read a news report for specific information. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can discuss future scenarios, expressing different degrees of certainty accurately, e.g. Cash is bound to disappear eventually, but I doubt it'll happen before 2040. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (news report), Handout 2 (certainty scale and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (scenario cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form and degree of certainty

| **Certainty** | **Structure** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| ~100% | will definitely / certainly; be bound to / be sure to + infinitive | Prices are bound to rise. |
| ~80% | will probably; be likely to + infinitive; It's likely that + clause; There's a good chance that ...; could well / may well | Storms are likely to increase. We could well see more flooding. |
| ~50% | may / might / could + infinitive | Cars might fly one day. |
| ~20% | probably won't; be unlikely to; I doubt (whether / if) ...; There's little chance that ... | Cash is unlikely to disappear soon. I doubt robots will replace teachers. |
| ~0% | definitely won't; There's no chance that ... | Humans definitely won't live on the sun. |

### Accuracy points

- Adverb position: *will* **probably** *go* but **probably** *won't go*.
- *likely* is an adjective: *He is likely to win* / *It is likely that he will win* (not *He will likely to win* in British English).
- *bound to* needs *be*: *He's bound to win.*
- *I doubt* + clause (no negative): *I doubt he'll come* (= I think he won't come).

### Pronunciation

- Stress on the probability word: *It's BOUND to happen. It could WELL rain.*

### Concept checking questions

*Prices are bound to rise.*

1. Is the speaker sure? (Yes, very sure.)

*I doubt he'll come.*

1. Does the speaker think he'll come? (No, probably not.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| It is likely to it will rain (mixed patterns). | Board the two separate patterns with colour. |
| Missing be with bound / likely / unlikely. | Highlight be in the formula. |
| Adverb position errors. | Show will probably / probably won't side by side. |
| I don't doubt misunderstood. | Keep to I doubt at this level; mention I have no doubt for stronger students. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Write five predictions on the board: *flying cars, a cashless world, a four-day school week, robots as teachers, holidays on the moon.*
2. Pairs rank them from most likely to least likely by 2050 (2 minutes). Take brief feedback.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present expressions of future probability in context and clarify their degree of certainty and form. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the report. Which prediction is the most certain? Which is the least? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (5 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Put the bold expressions from the report on the certainty scale."*
   - ICQs: *"Where do you put bound to: 100% or 50%?"* (100%.)
2. Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (9 minutes)**

1. Build the certainty scale on the board.
2. Focus on form: *be likely to* + infinitive vs *It's likely that* + clause; *be bound to*; adverb position (*will probably / probably won't*); *I doubt* + positive clause.
3. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill with stress on the probability word: *It's BOUND to happen. It could WELL rain.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the structures accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Say: *"Now do Exercise A on Handout 2: correct the mistakes. Then Exercise B: three predictions about your town."* (7 minutes.)
2. Pairs check. Check Exercise A as a class; two students read their predictions.
3. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use a range of probability structures fluently and accurately in discussion. **Interaction:** Groups, then whole class

1. Give each group a set of scenario cards (Handout 4). Say: *"Discuss each scenario. Agree a group prediction and a certainty level. Use at least five different structures from the scale."* (12 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"Do you need to agree?"* (Yes, a group prediction.) *"How many different structures?"* (At least five.)
2. Each group presents its most controversial prediction with a reason (1 minute each). Other groups agree or disagree using the structures.
3. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which new structure will you use most?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write a 180-word article: *Life in 2050*. Use at least six different probability structures.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Keep the certainty scale visible during the discussion.
- Give weaker groups sentence starters on the scenario cards.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students hedge academically: *It seems likely that ..., There is a strong possibility that ...*
- They challenge other groups: *I'm not convinced that ... is bound to happen, because ...*

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the discussion, students use at least five different probability structures accurately.
- 5 out of 6 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which structures were new to most students?
- Did students use the structures naturally in the discussion?

# Handout 1: News Report

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**COLOMBO 2050: WHAT WILL OUR CITY LOOK LIKE?**

Experts agree that the population of Colombo **is bound to** grow, and traffic **is likely to** become an even bigger problem unless public transport improves. **There's a good chance that** the city will have a metro system by 2040, and electric buses **could well** replace most diesel buses within twenty years.

Climate scientists warn that sea levels **are likely to** rise, and coastal areas **may** need new flood defences. Temperatures **will probably** increase by one or two degrees.

But not everything will change. **I doubt** people will stop eating rice and curry! And cash **is unlikely to** disappear completely, says economist Dr Wijesinghe: "Many small traders still prefer it. Online payments **will definitely** grow, but **there's little chance that** cash will vanish before 2050."

1. Which predictions are the most certain? ______________________________
2. What might coastal areas need? ______________________________
3. What does Dr Wijesinghe think about cash? ______________________________

**Useful language**

- *I think it's bound to happen because ...*
- *There's a good chance that ...*
- *It could well happen, but ...*
- *I doubt it'll happen before ...*
- *It's unlikely to happen in our lifetime.*
- *I'm not so sure. I think ...*

# Answer Key

1. The population is bound to grow; online payments will definitely grow. · 2. New flood defences. · 3. It is unlikely to disappear completely before 2050.

**Handout 2, scale**

- 100%: is bound to, will definitely · 80%: is likely to, there's a good chance that, could well, will probably · 50%: may · 20%: I doubt, is unlikely to, there's little chance that

**Handout 2, Exercise A**

1. It is likely to rain. / It is likely that it will rain. · 2. He is bound to win. · 3. She probably won't come. · 4. I doubt he'll come. · 5. Correct. · 6. They are unlikely to win.

**Handout 3**

1. It is bound to rain this afternoon. · 2. Prices are likely to rise. / It's likely that prices will rise. · 3. I doubt (whether / if) he'll come. · 4. They might cancel the match. · 5. We could / may well move abroad. · 6. Cash is unlikely to disappear soon. · 7. She is unlikely to pass. / She isn't likely to pass. · 8. He is sure to be late.

| :-- | :-- |

| She probably will not come. | She probably won't come. |
