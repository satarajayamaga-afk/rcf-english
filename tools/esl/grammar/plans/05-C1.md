# Articles in Academic Writing and Fixed Expressions

*Lesson theme: Urbanisation in Asia*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Articles |
| Grammar point | Articles in Academic Writing and Fixed Expressions |
| Level | C1 (Advanced) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Advanced students preparing for university or IELTS. Their academic writing is fluent but contains frequent article errors with abstract nouns and of-phrases (The globalisation has changed ..., Impact of technology is ...). |
| Main aim | To improve accuracy with articles in academic writing: abstract nouns in general vs specific use, nouns followed by of-phrases, pre-modified nouns, and fixed expressions. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop collaborative academic writing and peer editing. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can write and self-edit a 150-word academic paragraph with accurate article use. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (fixed expressions warm-up), Handout 2 (two paragraphs for analysis), Handout 3 (practice and editing checklist), Handout 4 (writing task: data and paragraph brief). |

## Language analysis

### Key patterns

| **Pattern** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Abstract or uncountable noun in general: no article | Ø Education is essential. Ø Technology is changing fast. |
| Abstract noun made specific (by of-phrase, relative clause or context): the | the education of girls · the technology that we use |
| Noun + of-phrase usually takes the | the role of women · the impact of tourism · the growth of cities |
| Pre-modified noun, general: no article | Ø rapid urban growth · Ø online learning |
| Countable singular noun always needs an article | a / the study, a / the survey (not Study shows ...) |
| Plural general: no article | Ø Studies show that ... Ø Young people ... |
| Fixed expressions | in the long run · on the whole · at first · by chance · in hospital · at sea · in the meantime · on the other hand · for the time being |
| Emphatic the (spoken) | That's THE best curry in Colombo. /ðiː/ |

### Pronunciation

- Emphatic *the*: /ðiː/ with stress.

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| the with general abstract nouns. | General vs specific contrast pairs in analysis. |
| Missing the before noun + of. | Highlight of-phrases in the model text. |
| Bare singular countable nouns (Study shows ...). | Include in the editing checklist. |
| Students think both options are always possible. | Discuss genuine cases where both are possible with a change of meaning. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate awareness of articles in fixed expressions. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Pairs complete the five fixed expressions with *a, an, the* or no article (2 minutes).
2. Check quickly. Board the expressions on the left.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To analyse article use in academic writing and clarify the key patterns. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

**Part A: Analysis (9 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Paragraph A has article errors. Paragraph B is corrected. Find at least ten differences and explain each one."*
   - ICQs: *"Which paragraph has errors?"* (A.) *"Do you just find the differences?"* (No, explain them too.)
2. Pairs work. Monitor and help with explanations.

**Part B: Clarification (11 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the patterns in the centre of the board:
   - general abstract or uncountable nouns → no article (*Urbanisation, economic growth, housing*);
   - nouns followed by *of* → usually *the* (*the proportion of, the rapid growth of*);
   - singular countable nouns always need an article (*a recent study*);
   - organisations and superlatives (*the United Nations, the most important*);
   - fixed expressions (*in the long run*).
2. Contrast pairs: *Education is essential / the education of girls*; *Technology changes fast / the technology that we use*.
3. Discuss cases where both are possible with a change of meaning: *Research shows ... / The research shows ...* (general research vs a specific study already mentioned).

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise accurate article use. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: correct the article errors. Some sentences are correct. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class, asking for the rule each time.
3. Read through the editing checklist (Exercise B) together.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To write an academic paragraph with accurate article use and self-edit it. **Interaction:** Pairs

1. Give out Handout 4. Say: *"Write an academic paragraph (about 150 words) describing the data and discussing one effect of rapid urban growth."* (11 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"How many words?"* (About 150.) *"Formal or informal?"* (Formal, academic.)
2. Pairs use the editing checklist (Handout 3, Exercise B) to edit their own paragraph and mark every change (4 minutes).
3. Pairs swap with another pair and check each other's articles (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note common errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Two pairs read their paragraphs. Board two or three sentences with interesting article choices; the class decides if they are correct.
2. Ask: *"Which pattern do you most need to check in your writing?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a 250-word discursive paragraph on a social issue and self-edit for articles using the checklist.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide an article decision checklist card.
- Give a paragraph frame for the writing task.

**Stretch**

- Analyse article choices in a real journal abstract.
- Explore further cases where both options are possible with a change of meaning.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The final paragraph has no more than one article error.
- Students can explain each correction using the patterns.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which pattern caused the most errors in the writing?
- Did self-editing improve the paragraphs?

# Handout 2: Two Paragraphs

**Paragraph A (contains article errors)**

The urbanisation is one of most important trends in the Asia today. According to the recent study by United Nations, proportion of people living in cities will rise significantly by 2050. Main reason for this is the economic growth. In China, for example, the urban population more than doubled between 1990 and 2020. However, the rapid growth of cities creates the serious problems. Traffic congestion and the air pollution are major concerns, and the housing is becoming unaffordable for young people. In long run, governments will need to invest in the public transport.

**Paragraph B (corrected)**

Urbanisation is one of the most important trends in Asia today. According to a recent study by the United Nations, the proportion of people living in cities will rise significantly by 2050. The main reason for this is economic growth. In China, for example, the urban population more than doubled between 1990 and 2020. However, the rapid growth of cities creates serious problems. Traffic congestion and air pollution are major concerns, and housing is becoming unaffordable for young people. In the long run, governments will need to invest in public transport.

# Answer Key

**Handout 1**

1. the · 2. the · 3. – · 4. – · 5. –

**Handout 2 (differences)**

The urbanisation → Urbanisation (general) · most important → the most important (superlative) · the Asia → Asia (continent) · the recent study → a recent study (first mention) · United Nations → the United Nations (organisation) · proportion of → the proportion of (of-phrase) · Main reason → The main reason (specific) · the economic growth → economic growth (general) · the serious problems → serious problems (general plural) · the air pollution → air pollution (general) · the housing → housing (general) · In long run → In the long run (fixed expression) · the public transport → public transport (general)
