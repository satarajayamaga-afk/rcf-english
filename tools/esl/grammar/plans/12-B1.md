# Non-defining Relative Clauses (commas, which, who, whose)

*Lesson theme: Famous Sri Lankans*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Relative Clauses |
| Grammar point | Non-defining Relative Clauses (commas, which, who, whose) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use defining relative clauses accurately. Their writing is choppy (many short sentences), and when they add extra information they use that after a comma (Kandy, that is ...), leave out commas, or don't know whose. |
| Main aim | To help students work out and use non-defining relative clauses (with commas, who, which, whose, where; not that; pronoun never omitted) and contrast them with defining clauses. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read short biographies for specific information; to develop writing by combining sentences into a fluent biography. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can write a short biography of a famous person using at least three non-defining relative clauses, e.g. Muttiah Muralitharan, who was born in Kandy, is the highest wicket-taker in Test cricket. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (two short biographies), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (fact files for the writing task). |

## Language analysis

### Defining vs non-defining

|  | **Defining** | **Non-defining** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Purpose | Tells us which one (essential) | Gives extra information (not essential) |
| Commas | No | Yes: before and after (or before and full stop) |
| that | Possible | Not possible |
| Leave out the pronoun? | Yes, if object | Never |
| Example | The player who took the most wickets is Muralitharan. | Muralitharan, who was born in Kandy, took 800 Test wickets. |

### Pronouns in non-defining clauses

- **who** (people), **which** (things), **where** (places), **whose** (possession: people or things).
- *whose* + noun: *Kumar Sangakkara,* ***whose*** *father was a lawyer, studied law.*

### Meaning and use

- Non-defining clauses are common in writing (biographies, news, descriptions) to add information smoothly.
- The sentence still makes sense without the clause: *Muralitharan took 800 Test wickets.*

### Pronunciation

- In speech, non-defining clauses are marked by pauses and a lower, faster voice: *Muralitharan, | who was born in Kandy, | took 800 wickets.*

### Concept checking questions

*Muralitharan, who was born in Kandy, took 800 Test wickets.*

1. Do we know who Muralitharan is without the clause? (Yes.)
2. Is the information about Kandy essential or extra? (Extra.)
3. Can we use *that*? (No.)

*Sangakkara, whose father was a lawyer, studied law.*

1. Whose father was a lawyer? (Sangakkara's.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| that in non-defining clauses (Kandy, that is ...). | Board the rule; cross out that after a comma. |
| Missing commas. | "Remove it" test: if the sentence works without it, use commas. |
| who's / whose confusion. | Contrast who's = who is; whose = possession. |
| Missing second comma. | Highlight both commas in colour. |

## Board plan

| **Left: biography vocabulary** | **Centre: rules** | **Right: pronunciation** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| was born · grew up · studied · became famous · won · retired · record · achievement | DEFINING (which one?): no commas, that OK, can omit object · NON-DEFINING (extra info): , who / which / where / whose ..., · no that · never omit · Remove test: sentence still OK → commas · whose + noun = possession | Muralitharan, |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and activate knowledge. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Board the names: *Muttiah Muralitharan, Sirimavo Bandaranaike, Kumar Sangakkara, Susanthika Jayasinghe*.
2. Ask: *"What do you know about these people?"* Pairs share facts (2 minutes). Take ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present non-defining relative clauses in context and help students work out the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the two biographies. Find one surprising fact in each. Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Alone or with a partner?"* (With a partner.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"Cover the part between the commas. Does the sentence still make sense?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers. Build the defining / non-defining contrast in the centre of the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis. Demonstrate the "remove it" test.
3. Highlight *whose*. Board *who's* vs *whose*.
4. **Pronunciation.** Read a sentence aloud with pauses and a lower voice for the extra information. Students repeat.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: add commas where necessary. Exercise B: join the sentences. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use non-defining relative clauses in writing a biography. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Each pair chooses one fact file and writes a biography (about 100 words) using at least three non-defining relative clauses (10 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"How many non-defining clauses?"* (At least three.) *"Do you copy the notes?"* (No, write full sentences.)
2. **Read and guess (6 minutes).** Pairs read their biography to another pair without saying the name; the other pair guesses who it is.
3. Two pairs read their biographies to the class (2 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors (commas, *that*, *whose*).
2. Ask: *"How do you know when to use commas?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a biography (150 words) of a person you admire (famous or in your family). Use at least four non-defining relative clauses, including one with *whose*.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the rules on a card.
- Give them the first sentence of their biography.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *which* to refer to a whole clause: *He took 800 wickets, which is a world record.*
- They use *when* in non-defining clauses: *in 1996, when Sri Lanka won the World Cup*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The biography includes at least three accurate non-defining clauses with commas.
- No *that* in non-defining clauses.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the "remove it" test help students decide on commas?
- Was *whose* used correctly?

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Muttiah Muralitharan** Muttiah Muralitharan, who was born in Kandy in 1972, is one of the greatest bowlers in the history of cricket. He went to St. Anthony's College, where he started bowling spin when he was 14. In 1996, he was part of the team that won the Cricket World Cup. **Sirimavo Bandaranaike** Sirimavo Bandaranaike, whose husband was also prime minister, became the world's first female prime minister in 1960. She was born in Balangoda, which is in the Sabaragamuwa Province. She served three terms as prime minister. Her daughter, Chandrika, who later became president, continued her political work.

2. What is still a world record? ______________________________
3. Why is Sirimavo Bandaranaike famous? ______________________________
4. Who later became president? ______________________________

# Handout 2: Language Focus

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Look at these sentences.**

1. Muttiah Muralitharan, **who was born in Kandy in 1972**, is one of the greatest bowlers.
2. He went to St. Anthony's College, **where he started bowling spin**.
3. He was part of the team **that won the Cricket World Cup**.
4. Muralitharan, **whose bowling action was very unusual**, took 800 Test wickets.
5. She was born in Balangoda, **which is in the Sabaragamuwa Province**.

**Answer the questions with your partner.**

1. Cover the bold part in sentence 1. Does the sentence still make sense? Do we know who Muralitharan is? ______________________________
2. Now cover the bold part in sentence 3. Do we know which team? Is the clause essential? ______________________________
3. Which sentences have commas? Which one doesn't? Why? ______________________________
4. Which sentence uses *that*? Do any of the sentences with commas use *that*? ______________________________
5. In sentence 4, what does *whose* mean? ______________________________

# Answer Key

**Handout 1**

1. At St. Anthony's College · 2. His 800 Test wickets · 3. She was the world's first female prime minister. · 4. Her daughter, Chandrika.

**Handout 2**

1. Yes; yes, the clause is extra information. · 2. No; it's essential (it tells us which team). · 3. 1, 2, 4 and 5 have commas; 3 doesn't because the clause is essential. · 4. Sentence 3; no. · 5. *his* (Muralitharan's)

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. My brother, who lives in Dubai, is an engineer. · 2. No commas. · 3. Galle, which is on the south coast, is famous for its fort. · 4. No commas. · 5. My grandmother, whose house is near the sea, makes the best fish curry.

**Handout 3, Exercise B**

1. who is a former captain, studied law. / who studied law, is a former captain. · 2. which was built in the 5th century, is visited by thousands of tourists. · 3. where my father grew up. · 4. whose mother is a famous actress, is a singer. · 5. which lasted three hours, was very difficult.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Kandy, that is in the hills, is beautiful. | Kandy, which is in the hills, is beautiful. |
| My mother who is a teacher works hard. (one mother) | My mother, who is a teacher, works hard. |
| My friend, who's father is a doctor, ... | My friend, whose father is a doctor, ... |
| Galle, which is on the coast is famous. | Galle, which is on the coast, is famous. |
