# Rhetorical and Emphatic Questions in Persuasive Speech (Isn't it time ...? How on earth ...? What if ...?)

*Lesson theme: Giving a Persuasive Speech*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Question Forms |
| Grammar point | Rhetorical and Emphatic Questions in Persuasive Speech (Isn't it time ...? How on earth ...? What if ...?) |
| Level | C1 (Advanced) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Advanced students who use all standard question forms accurately but rarely use questions for effect. ## Language analysis

### Rhetorical question structures

| **Structure** | **Function** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Isn't it time (that) + subject + past simple? | urging action | Isn't it time we banned single-use plastic? |
| Is it any wonder (that) + clause? | presenting a consequence as obvious | Is it any wonder that our beaches are dirty? |
| Who would have thought (that) + clause? | expressing surprise | Who would have thought a straw could kill a turtle? |
| What if + clause? | imagining a possibility | What if every family recycled just one bottle a day? |
| How can / could we + verb? | challenging the audience | How can we stand by and do nothing? |
| Negative question (Don't we all ...? Shouldn't we ...?) | inviting agreement | Don't we all want clean water? |
| Question + immediate answer (hypophora) | guiding the argument | So what can we do? The answer is simple. |

### Emphatic questions

- *How / What / Why / Who / Where on earth ...?* (strong surprise or annoyance): *How on earth can we justify this?*
- *Why ever / Whatever / Whoever / However ...?* (as one or two words in emphatic questions): *Why ever would anyone do that?*
- Normal question word order follows: *What on earth* ***are you*** *doing?* (not *What on earth you are doing?*)

### Formal fronted prepositions

- Informal: *Who are we doing this for?* Formal: *For whom are we doing this?* · *What purpose does it serve?* / *To what end ...?*

### Pronunciation

- Rhetorical questions usually **fall** at the end (no answer is expected): *Isn't it time we acted? ↘*
- Pause after the question for effect.
- Stress *on earth*: *How on EARTH ...?*

### Concept checking questions

*Isn't it time we banned single-use plastic?*

1. Does the speaker expect an answer? (No.)
2. Why the past form *banned*? (It's the pattern after *It's time*; it doesn't mean past time.)

*Is it any wonder that our beaches are dirty?*

1. Is the speaker surprised that the beaches are dirty? (No, it's obvious to the speaker.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Present tense after Isn't it time we ... (we ban). | Board: It's time + subject + past simple (unreal). |
| Statement word order in emphatic questions (What on earth you are doing?). | Board normal question order after on earth. |
| Who would think ...? for surprise | Teach Who would have thought ...? as a chunk. |
| Overuse making speeches sound unnatural. | Guideline: one rhetorical question per main point. |
| Rising intonation on rhetorical questions. | Drill falling intonation and pauses. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To raise awareness of questions used for effect. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Ask the class, with dramatic delivery: *"Who wants to spend the whole weekend doing homework? ... Nobody? Exactly."*
2. Ask: *"Did I want an answer? Why did I ask the question?"* Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Elicit: to make a point, to involve the audience.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present rhetorical and emphatic questions in a model speech and clarify their form, function and delivery. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading and listening (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Read the speech aloud with appropriate delivery while students follow. Ask: *"What does the speaker want the audience to do? Was it persuasive?"*
2. Pairs discuss.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the questions in the speech. Match each one to its function. Then answer the questions."*
   - ICQs: *"Where do you find the questions?"* (In the speech.) *"What do you match them to?"* (Functions.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the functions and patterns on the board.
2. **Isn't it time ...?** Ask the CCQs. Highlight the past form after *It's time*.
3. **Emphatic questions.** Board *What on earth you are doing* ✗ → *What on earth are you doing?*
4. **Formal fronted prepositions.** Contrast *Who is this for?* with *For whom is this?*
5. **Delivery.** Model falling intonation and pauses. Students repeat three questions from the speech with dramatic delivery.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the structures accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite the statements as rhetorical questions using the words given. Exercise B: correct the errors. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check and read their questions aloud with persuasive delivery. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use rhetorical and emphatic questions in a persuasive speech. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Pairs choose a topic card and plan a 90-second speech using the planner. They must include at least five structures from today (8 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"How long is the speech?"* (90 seconds.) *"How many question structures?"* (At least five.)
2. **Deliver (6 minutes).** In groups of six (three pairs), one student from each pair delivers the speech. Listeners tick the structures they hear and give one piece of feedback on delivery.
3. Two speakers perform for the whole class. The class votes for the most persuasive speech (4 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board the most effective questions and one or two with errors. Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"How many rhetorical questions is too many?"* Discuss balance.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a persuasive article or speech (250 words) on an issue you care about. Use at least six rhetorical or emphatic question structures and underline them.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a pattern card with examples.
- Allow them to write their speech in full before delivering it.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *Need I remind you that ...? Dare we imagine ...? Must we wait until ...?* (formal modal questions).
- They answer their own questions (hypophora) to structure the speech.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The speech includes at least five rhetorical or emphatic question structures used accurately.
- Rhetorical questions are delivered with falling intonation and pauses.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use the questions for effect rather than as decoration?
- Was the delivery practice useful?

# Handout 1: A Student Speech

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**"OUR BEACHES, OUR RESPONSIBILITY": extract from a speech to the school assembly**

Good morning, everyone. Last weekend, I walked along Mount Lavinia beach. And what did I find? Bottles, bags, straws, and a turtle tangled in a fishing net. Who would have thought that a beach so beautiful could become a rubbish dump?

Every year, millions of tonnes of plastic enter the ocean. Is it any wonder that our fish now contain microplastics? And how on earth can we call ourselves an island nation if we can't protect our own sea?

Some people say, "One person can't make a difference." Really? What if every student in this school used a refillable bottle? That's a thousand fewer plastic bottles every single day.

Don't we all want to swim in clean water? Don't we all want our children to see turtles? So what can we do? The answer is simple: refuse, reuse, recycle.

For whom are we protecting these beaches? Not for ourselves, but for the next generation. Isn't it time we stopped making excuses and started taking action?

Thank you.

# Answer Key

**Handout 2**

- Surprise: *Who would have thought that a beach so beautiful could become a rubbish dump?* · Obvious consequence: *Is it any wonder that our fish now contain microplastics?* · Emphatic: *How on earth can we call ourselves an island nation ...?* · Imagine: *What if every student in this school used a refillable bottle?* · Invite agreement: *Don't we all want to swim in clean water?* · Question + answer: *And what did I find? Bottles ...* / *So what can we do? The answer is simple ...* · Fronted preposition: *For whom are we protecting these beaches?* · Urge action: *Isn't it time we stopped making excuses ...?*
   1. After *It's time* + subject, we use the past simple (unreal; it means "now"). · 2. *Who are we protecting these beaches for?* · 3. No; with falling intonation and a pause.

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. Isn't it time we stopped wasting food? · 2. Is it any wonder (that) students are tired when they sleep only five hours? · 3. Who would have thought (that) a small app could change the whole country? · 4. What if every school planted 100 trees? · 5. Don't we all want a safer city? · 6. How on earth could anyone ignore this? · 7. For whom is this law (intended)? · 8. How can we just do nothing?

**Handout 3, Exercise B**

1. What on earth are you doing? · 2. Isn't it time we stopped talking and started acting? · 3. Who would have thought that a straw could kill a turtle? · 4. Is it any wonder (that) people are angry? · 5. For whom is this money?

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| What on earth you are doing? | What on earth are you doing? |
| Isn't it time we act? | Isn't it time we acted? |
| Who would think that ...? (surprise) | Who would have thought that ...? |
| Is it any wonder why ...? | Is it any wonder (that) ...? |
