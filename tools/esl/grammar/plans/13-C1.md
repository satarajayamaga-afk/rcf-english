## Language analysis

### Key patterns

| **Pattern** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- |
| verb + object + -ing (imagine, mind, resent, can't stand, risk, prevent ... from) | I can't imagine him leaving. Do you mind me asking? |
| possessive + gerund (more formal) | I resent his taking the credit. Their arriving late caused problems. |
| to as a preposition + -ing | I object to working late. She's committed to improving conditions. In addition to managing the team, ... · look forward to hearing |
| sense verbs + object + base verb (complete action) | I saw him cross the road. (I saw the whole action.) |
| sense verbs + object + -ing (action in progress) | I saw him crossing the road. (I saw part of it.) |
| fixed expressions + -ing | It's no use complaining. There's no point (in) waiting. It's (not) worth asking. I had difficulty / trouble finding it. I can't help laughing. |

### Meaning and use

- Object + *-ing* is common in speech; possessive + gerund is more formal.
- Sense verbs (*see, hear, watch, notice, feel*): base verb = the whole event; *-ing* = an action in progress or repeated.
- *to* is a preposition in many expressions; test: can you put a noun after it? (*I object to the decision* → *I object to working*.)

### Pronunciation

- Linking in *no use complaining* /nəʊ ˈjuːs kəmˈpleɪnɪŋ/; *There's no point* /ðeəz nəʊ ˈpɔɪnt/.

### Concept checking questions

*I object to working late.*

1. Is *to* part of an infinitive here? (No, it's a preposition.)
2. Can you put a noun after it? (Yes: *I object to the new rule.*)

*I saw him cross the road.* / *I saw him crossing the road.*

1. In which sentence did I see the complete action? (The first.)
2. In which one did I see the action in progress? (The second.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| to + infinitive after prepositional to (object to work). | Noun test: if a noun can follow, use -ing. |
| It's no use to complain / It's worth to visit. | Board fixed expressions + -ing as chunks. |
| Object + infinitive after imagine / mind (imagine him to leave). | Board imagine / mind + object + -ing. |
| Overusing the possessive in speech. | Note register: object pronoun is natural in speech. |
| Sense verbs + to (I saw him to cross). | Board: no to after sense verbs (active). |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic and personalise it. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"What makes a workplace (or a school) a good place to be? What annoys people most?"*
2. Pairs list three ideas (2 minutes). Take ideas; board one sentence exactly as a student says it.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present complex verb patterns in context and clarify their form, meaning and register. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (5 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the anonymous comments from a staff survey. What are the main complaints? Which comment do you agree with most?"*
2. Pairs compare.

**Part B: Analysis (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the patterns in the comments and complete the table. Then answer the questions."*
   - ICQs: *"Where do you find the examples?"* (In the survey comments.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the patterns in the centre of the board.
2. **to as a preposition.** Board *I object to working late.* Ask the CCQs. Teach the noun test.
3. **Object vs possessive + gerund.** Board *I resent him taking the credit* / *I resent his taking the credit.* Discuss register.
4. **Sense verbs.** Board *I saw him cross / crossing the road.* Ask the CCQs.
5. **Fixed expressions.** Board *It's no use complaining. There's no point in waiting. It's worth asking.* Cross out *It's worth to ask*.
6. Return to the student's sentence from Stage 1 and improve it.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the patterns accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite each sentence using the word given. Exercise B: correct the errors. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the patterns in discussion and formal writing. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. **Discussion (8 minutes).** Groups discuss the four workplace scenarios and decide what they would say to the manager in each case, using structures from today.
   - ICQs: *"How many scenarios?"* (Four.) *"What do you decide?"* (What to say to the manager.)
2. **Email (7 minutes).** Pairs choose one scenario and write a short formal email (about 100 words) to the manager, using at least six patterns from today.
3. Two pairs read their emails aloud (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board strong sentences and two or three with errors. Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"How do you know when to is a preposition?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a formal letter (220 words) to a school principal or company director about a change you would like to see. Use at least eight patterns from today.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a pattern card with examples and the noun test.
- Provide an email frame: *I am writing to express ... · I object to ... · There is no point in ... · I would appreciate your considering ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use passive gerunds: *I object to being treated like this.*
- They use *would you mind my* + *-ing* and *I'd appreciate your* + *-ing* for polite formal requests.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The email uses at least six complex patterns accurately.
- No infinitives after prepositional *to* or after *It's no use / worth*.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the noun test help students with *to* as a preposition?
- Was the possessive gerund used appropriately (formal register)?
