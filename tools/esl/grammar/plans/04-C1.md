GRAMMAR TOPIC: FUTURE FORMS · C1 (Advanced) · 60 minutes

# Formal Future Forms (be to, be due to, be set to, Future in the Past)

*Lesson theme: Writing a Press Release*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Future Forms |
| Grammar point | Formal Future Forms (be to, be due to, be set to, Future in the Past) |
| Level | C1 (Advanced) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Advanced students who use will and going to well but whose formal writing sounds conversational. They don't know be to, be due to, be set to or the future in the past (was to, would). |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of formal future forms in official announcements, news and biography. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop collaborative writing; to raise awareness of register. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can write a press release announcing an event using at least five formal future forms, and a short biography paragraph using the future in the past. |

## Language analysis

### Form and use

| **Form** | **Meaning / use** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| be to + infinitive | Official arrangement or plan; instructions; formal | The President is to visit Jaffna next month. Students are to report to the hall at 8. |
| be due to + infinitive | Expected according to a schedule | The new bridge is due to open in May. |
| be set to + infinitive | Likely / ready to happen (news style) | The company is set to announce job cuts. |
| be on the point / verge of + -ing | About to happen very soon | The talks are on the verge of collapsing. |
| be about to + infinitive | Very near future | The ceremony is about to begin. |
| Future in the past: was / were going to, would, was to | A future seen from a past viewpoint; was to often = destiny | Nobody knew then that she was to become a famous scientist. He said he would call. |
| Should + subject (inversion) | Formal conditional for future possibility | Should you require assistance, please contact reception. |

### Register

- These forms are typical of news, official notices, formal letters and biographies. In conversation they sound stiff: *I'm to have pizza tonight* ✗ → *I'm having pizza tonight.*

### Pronunciation

- Weak *to*: *is to visit* /ɪz tə ˈvɪzɪt/; *was to become* /wəz tə bɪˈkʌm/.

### Concept checking questions

*The President is to visit Jaffna next month.*

1. Is this an official plan? (Yes.)
2. Would you hear this in a casual chat? (Not usually; in the news.)

*Nobody knew then that she was to become famous.*

1. Did she become famous? (Yes.)
2. Is the writer looking at the future from a point in the past? (Yes.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| be to + -ing (is to visiting). | Board: be to + base verb. |
| Using formal forms in casual contexts. | Register sorting task in analysis. |
| Should you will need ... | Highlight: Should + subject + base verb. |
| Confusing due to (because of) and be due to (scheduled). | Contrast two examples. |

| **Left: register** | **Centre: patterns** | **Right: meaning** |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To raise awareness of register. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Give out Handout 1. Read the two notices about the same event (a casual message and an official notice).
2. Ask: *"Where would you see each one? Which words make the second one sound official?"* Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Take ideas; don't name the grammar yet.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present formal future forms in authentic-style texts and clarify their meaning, form and register. **Interaction:** Groups, whole class

**Part A: Analysis (9 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2 (a government press release and a biography extract). Say: *"In groups, find all the future forms and answer the questions."*
   - ICQs: *"How many texts?"* (Two.) *"What do you look for?"* (Future forms.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part B: Clarification (11 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the patterns and meanings on the board.
2. Contrast: *be to* (official arrangement or instruction), *be due to* (scheduled), *be set to* (likely, news style), *be on the verge of* (very soon), *was to* (future seen from the past; often destiny in biographies).
3. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
4. Highlight *Should you require ...* = *If you require ...* and its word order.
5. Return to Notice B on Handout 1 and identify each form.
6. Point out register: *"Would you say 'I'm to have pizza tonight' to a friend?"* (No.)

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite in a more formal style using the word given. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Feedback as a class.
3. Students start Exercise B (biography paragraph); they finish it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use formal future forms in an authentic writing task. **Interaction:** Groups of three, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Say: *"Your school is holding a major event. Write an official press release (120–150 words) announcing it. Use at least five formal future forms."*
   - ICQs: *"Formal or informal?"* (Formal.) *"How many formal future forms?"* (At least five.)
2. Groups choose an event, decide the details and write (12 minutes).
3. Groups read their press releases aloud. The class votes for the most professional one (6 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board the most effective lines and one or two sentences with errors. Students correct them.
2. Ask: *"Which forms would you use in an email to a friend? Which would you avoid?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Finish the biography paragraph (Handout 3, Exercise B). Then write a 200-word press release for a real or invented event.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide a press release template with gaps.
- Give a pattern card.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students include *be on the verge of*, *Should you ...* and a passive (*is to be opened by*).
- They rewrite a casual email as an official notice.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The press release uses at least five formal future forms accurately.
- The register is consistent throughout.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students notice the difference in register?
- Which forms appeared most in the press releases?

# Handout 1: Two Notices

**Notice A (a message in the class WhatsApp group)**

Hey everyone! The new library's opening next Friday at 10. The minister's coming to cut the ribbon, apparently! Be there by 9.30, OK? If you need anything, just ask me.

**Notice B (an official notice)**

The new school library is to be officially opened on Friday 14 March at 10.00 am. The Minister of Education is due to attend the ceremony and is set to announce a new national reading programme. All students are to assemble in the main hall by 9.30 am. Should you require any further information, please contact the school office.

**Discuss:** Where would you see each notice? Which words and phrases make Notice B sound official?

# Answer Key

**Handout 2**

1. *is due to open* (scheduled); *is to perform* (official arrangement); *is set to announce* (likely, news style); *is on the verge of being completed* (very soon); *are to use* (instruction)
2. *Drivers are to use the new toll system.*
3. If you have any questions.
4. In 1960, looking forward. *was to lead* = would later lead (it really happened).
5. *She would later say ...*

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. The Minister is to visit the school next week. · 2. The new library is due to open in June. · 3. The company is set to announce job cuts. · 4. Should you need any help, please contact us. · 5. Nobody knew then that he was to become president. · 6. The two sides are on the verge of reaching an agreement. · 7. All visitors are to sign in at reception. · 8. The ceremony is about to begin.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| The President is to visiting Galle. | The President is to visit Galle. |
| Should you will need help ... | Should you need help ... |
| I'm to have pizza tonight. (casual) | I'm having pizza tonight. |
| The bridge is due opening in May. | The bridge is due to open in May. |
