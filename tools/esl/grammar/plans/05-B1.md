GRAMMAR TOPIC: ARTICLES · B1 (Intermediate) · 60 minutes

# The Zero Article (General vs Specific)

*Lesson theme: Food Around the World*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Articles |
| Grammar point | The Zero Article (General vs Specific) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use a / an / the for first and second mention but say The life is beautiful, I like the football and He speaks the English. |
| Main aim | To help students discover when to use no article (general plural and uncountable nouns; meals, sports, languages; institutions for their purpose) and when to use the (specific). |
| Subsidiary aims | To read a magazine article for specific information; to develop fluency in giving opinions. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can give opinions about general topics without using the incorrectly, e.g. Children love sweets. Money can't buy happiness. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (magazine article), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (opinion cards). |

## Language analysis

### Use

| **No article (Ø)** | **the** |
| :-- | :-- |
| General plural nouns: Ø Dogs are loyal. | Specific plural: The dogs next door bark all night. |
| General uncountable nouns: Ø Rice is cheap here. Ø Life is beautiful. | Specific uncountable: The rice in this shop is old. |
| Abstract nouns in general: Ø Happiness is important. | Specific: the happiness of my children |
| Meals: have Ø breakfast / lunch / dinner | Specific: The lunch we had was delicious. |
| Sports and games: play Ø cricket / chess |  |
| Languages and school subjects: speak Ø Tamil; study Ø maths | the Tamil language |
| Institutions for their main purpose: go to Ø school / hospital / prison / university / church / bed | Visit the building for another reason: My mother went to the school to meet my teacher. |

### Pronunciation

- No special issue; contrast stress on *the* when emphasising specificity: *THE rice in THIS shop*.

### Concept checking questions

*Dogs are loyal.*

1. Are we talking about particular dogs? (No, dogs in general.)
2. Do we use *the*? (No.)

*The dogs next door bark all night.*

1. Do we know which dogs? (Yes, next door.)

*He's in hospital.*

1. Is he a patient or a visitor? (A patient.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| L1 transfer: the with general nouns (The life is hard). | Repeated contrast pairs; opinion task forces general statements. |
| Overcorrection: dropping the for specific nouns. | Keep pairs side by side on the board. |
| Institutions confusing. | Picture contrast: a child going to school vs a parent visiting the school. |
| Students give very short opinions. | Opinion cards include sentence starters. |

## Board plan

| **Left: no article** | **Centre: rule** | **Right: the** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Dogs are loyal. · Rice is cheap. · Life is beautiful. · I play cricket. · I speak Tamil. · I have lunch at 1. · He's in hospital (patient). | GENERAL → no article · SPECIFIC (we know which) → the · Meals, sports, languages → no article · Institution for its purpose → no article | The dogs next door ... · The rice in this shop ... · The lunch we had ... · She went to the hospital to visit him. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Write three questions on the board: *Is coffee healthy? Are cats better than dogs? Is rice better than bread?*
2. Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Take a few opinions; don't correct.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present general and specific noun use in context and help students work out when to use no article. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the article. Which country's food habits surprise you most? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs discuss. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the pairs of sentences. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"How many pairs?"* (Five.) *"Alone or with a partner?"* (With a partner.)
2. Monitor. Help with: *"Which dogs? All dogs, or particular dogs?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers. Build the two columns and the rules on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Draw a quick picture: a child with a school bag (*goes to school*) and a parent at the school gate (*goes to the school*). Ask: *"Who is studying? Who is visiting?"*
4. Elicit meals, sports and languages without articles: *have lunch, play cricket, speak Sinhala.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise article choice. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: the or no article. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs justify: *"General, so no article."* Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To make general statements fluently and accurately in discussion. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then whole class

1. Give each group the opinion cards (Handout 4). Model: *"Money can't buy happiness. I agree, because ... But the money my parents saved helped my education."*
2. Students take turns to read a card, agree or disagree, and give a reason (13 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"What do you do after you read the card?"* (Agree or disagree and give a reason.)
3. Monitor and note article errors.
4. Each group reports the card they disagreed about most.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board errors and good sentences; students correct the errors.
2. Ask students to give one general and one specific sentence about food.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a 120-word paragraph about food habits in your country, using at least five general statements and two specific ones.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give a general / specific checklist: *All of them in general? → no article. Do we know which? → the.*
- Let weaker students write their opinion before speaking.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students write their own pairs of contrasting sentences.
- They use abstract nouns in discussion: *freedom, education, success*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the discussion, students make general statements without *the*.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students stop saying *the life / the people* in general statements?
- Was the institution rule clear?

# Handout 1: Eating Around the World

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**What Does the World Eat?**

Rice is the most important food for more than half of the world's population. In Sri Lanka, people often have rice for breakfast, lunch and dinner! In many European countries, however, bread is more common, and people rarely eat rice every day.

Tea is popular everywhere, but the tea grown in the hills of Sri Lanka is famous around the world. Coffee is the favourite drink in Brazil and Ethiopia.

Children love sweets in every country. However, doctors say that sugar is one of the biggest health problems today. The children in a recent study in the UK ate three times more sugar than they should.

In Japan, people eat a lot of fish, and life expectancy is one of the highest in the world. The fish served in Tokyo's famous market is very fresh, and visitors queue for hours for breakfast there.

**Answer the questions.**

1. What do many Sri Lankans eat three times a day? ______________________________
2. Why is Sri Lankan tea special? ______________________________
3. What did the study in the UK find? ______________________________

# Answer Key

**Handout 1**

1. Rice · 2. It grows in the hills and is famous around the world · 3. Children ate three times more sugar than they should.

**Handout 2**

1. In general · 2. Yes, the extra information tells us (in this shop, in the study, grown in Sri Lanka, in Tokyo's market) · 3. no · 4. *He* is a patient; *I* am a visitor · 5. breakfast, lunch, dinner: no article

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. – · 2. The · 3. – · 4. – · 5. The · 6. – · 7. – · 8. – ... – · 9. – · 10. the

**Handout 3, Exercise B**

1. Life is beautiful. · 2. I like football. · 3. He speaks English very well. · 4. Correct. · 5. My cousin is in prison. · 6. Correct.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| The life is beautiful. | Life is beautiful. |
| I like the football. | I like football. |
| He speaks the English very well. | He speaks English very well. |
| The people are friendly in my country. (in general) | People are friendly in my country. |
