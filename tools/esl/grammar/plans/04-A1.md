GRAMMAR TOPIC: FUTURE FORMS · A1 (Beginner) · 60 minutes

# Be Going To for Plans

*Lesson theme: My Holiday Plans*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Future Forms |
| Grammar point | Be Going To for Plans |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Beginners who use the present simple and am/is/are confidently. They talk about the future with the present simple (Tomorrow I visit my aunt). |
| Main aim | To present and practise be going to + base verb for future plans and intentions. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise future time expressions; to develop speaking by arranging a time to meet. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can say 6 sentences about their plans and ask a partner Are you going to ...? questions. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (Hasini's holiday diary), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (my week planner), teacher's weekend calendar drawn on the board. |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Positive** | **Negative** | **Question** | **Short answer** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| I | I'm going to visit my aunt. | I'm not going to work. | Am I going to see you? | Yes, you are. / No, you aren't. |
| He / She / It | She's going to study. | He isn't going to play. | Is she going to come? | Yes, she is. / No, she isn't. |
| You / We / They | They're going to travel. | We aren't going to stay. | Are you going to swim? | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. |

**am / is / are + going to + base verb**

### Meaning and use

- Plans and intentions: something we have **already decided** to do.
- Time expressions: *tonight, tomorrow, this weekend, next week, next month, next year, on Saturday, in the holidays*.

### Pronunciation

- In fast speech, *going to* often sounds like /ˈɡənə/ (*gonna*). Students should understand it but write *going to*.
- Weak *to*: /tə/. *I'm going to visit* /aɪm ˈɡəʊɪŋ tə ˈvɪzɪt/.

### Concept checking questions

*I'm going to visit my aunt on Saturday.*

1. Is Saturday in the past or the future? (Future.)
2. Did I decide before now? (Yes, it's my plan.)
3. Is it a plan or a guess? (A plan.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Missing be (I going to swim). | Three-box board model: I'm + going to + swim. |
| Adding -s or -ing to the base verb (going to plays). | Highlight: base verb only. |
| Present simple for plans (Tomorrow I visit). | Contrast routine vs plan on a calendar. |
| Pronouncing going to very carefully and slowly. | Model natural speed; allow gonna in speech. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and introduce plans. **Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Draw a calendar for this weekend on the board with three pictures: a bus, a cake, a football.
2. Say: *"This weekend is busy! Guess my plans!"* Students guess freely. Confirm with the target language: *"Yes! I'm going to visit my sister. I'm going to make a cake."*
3. Ask: *"Are you busy this weekend?"* Take a few yes/no answers.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Part A: Context (8 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To present the language in context. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read Hasini's diary. Match the days and the plans."* (3 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"Do you match or write?"* (Match.)
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.
3. Students answer the true/false questions. Check: *"Is Hasini going to go to the beach on Monday?"* Model: *"No, she isn't. She's going to visit her grandmother."*

**Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. **Meaning.** Board *Hasini is going to visit her grandmother on Monday.* Ask the CCQs. Draw a timeline with NOW and an arrow to Monday.
2. **Form.** Elicit the three parts: *am / is / are + going to + base verb*. Write the form table on the board with all persons.
3. **Negative and questions.** Elicit from the diary: *She isn't going to work. Are you going to come?* Elicit short answers.
4. **Time words.** Students find time words in the diary; build the list on the left of the board.
5. **Pronunciation.** Model full and natural speed. Drill chorally and individually: *I'm going to swim. Are you going to come? Yes, I am.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs

1. Students complete Handout 2, Exercises A and B (6 minutes).
2. Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers.
3. **Picture drill:** point to the weekend pictures on the board and a student's name; students make sentences: *"Dinesh is going to play football."*

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language in a personalised, communicative task. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Students write their real or imagined plans for next week in the planner, leaving **one** afternoon free (4 minutes).
2. Model with a strong student: *"Are you going to do anything on Monday afternoon?" "Yes, I'm going to play cricket." "What about Tuesday?"*
3. **Pairs (6 minutes):** students ask each other to find a free afternoon to meet. When they find one, they decide what they are going to do together.
4. **Report (4 minutes):** pairs tell the class: *"We're going to meet on Thursday. We're going to go to the cinema."*
5. **Writing (4 minutes):** students write 6 sentences about their plans at the bottom of Handout 3.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Quick round: each student says one plan for tonight.
3. Set homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write 6 sentences about your plans for the next school holidays.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide picture prompts in the planner.
- Give the question frame: *Are you going to ___ on ___?*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add reasons with *because*: *I'm going to study because I have an exam.*
- They ask *What are you going to do ...? / Where are you going to go?*

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students form *going to* sentences with the correct *be* in 5 of 6 written sentences.
- Students ask and answer *Are you going to ...?* correctly in the meeting task.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use the correct form of *be*?
- Did the meeting task work? Did every pair find a time?
