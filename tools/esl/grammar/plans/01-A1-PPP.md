# Present Simple

*Lesson theme: My Day: Daily Routines*

## Lesson overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grammar topic** | Present Tenses |
| **Grammar point** | Present Simple |
| **Level** | A1 (Beginner) |
| **Duration** | 60 minutes |
| **Framework** | PPP: Presentation, Practice, Production |
| **Class profile** | Beginners who know the verb *be*, numbers, telling the time (o'clock, half past) and basic daily verbs. They may have met the present simple but cannot yet use the third person *-s* accurately. |

## Language analysis

### Form

| | I / You / We / They | He / She / It |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | I **get** up at 6.30. | She **gets** up at 6.30. |
| **Negative** | I **don't** (do not) **eat** breakfast. | He **doesn't** (does not) **eat** breakfast. |
| **Question** | **Do** you **walk** to school? | **Does** she **walk** to school? |
| **Short answers** | Yes, I do. / No, I don't. | Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't. |

Spelling rules for he / she / it:
- Most verbs add *-s*: work → works, play → plays, get → gets.
- Verbs ending in *-ch, -sh, -s, -x, -o* add *-es*: watch → watches, wash → washes, go → goes, do → does.
- Consonant + *y* → *-ies*: study → studies. (Vowel + *y* just adds *-s*: play → plays.)
- Irregular: have → has.

### Meaning and use
We use the present simple for habits and routines (things we do regularly) and for facts. Time expressions: *every day, on Mondays, at 7 o'clock, in the morning, in the evening, at night*.

### Pronunciation
The *-s* ending has three sounds:
- /s/ after voiceless sounds: works, gets, eats, walks
- /z/ after voiced sounds: plays, goes, comes, reads, has /hæz/
- /ɪz/ after /s, z, ʃ, tʃ, dʒ/: watches, washes, finishes, relaxes

In questions, *do you* is often weak and joined: /dəjə/. Short answers are stressed: *Yes, I DO.*

### Concept checking questions (CCQs)
Use these for the sentence *Nimal gets up at 6.30.*
1. Is this today only? (No.)
2. Is it every day? (Yes.)
3. Is it a habit? (Yes.)
4. Who gets up? Nimal: is he *I, you* or *he*? (He.) So what do we add? (-s.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Students forget the *-s* with he / she (*He work in a bank*). | Highlight *-s* in a second colour on the board. Use a gesture (a finger drawing an "s" in the air) to prompt self-correction throughout the lesson. |
| Students add *-s* after *doesn't* (*She doesn't works*). | Show that the *-s* "moves" to *does*: draw an arrow from *works* to *does* on the board. |
| Students use *be* with the main verb (*I am go to school*). | Contrast *I am a student* (be) with *I go to school* (action verb, no *am*). |
| Students struggle with times. | Draw a clock on the board in the lead-in and review o'clock, half past, quarter past / to. |
| Mixed-level class: stronger students finish early. | Fast finishers write three extra sentences using *usually, always, never* (see Differentiation). |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Lead-in (5 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To engage students, introduce the topic of daily routines and activate vocabulary.
**Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Draw a large clock on the board showing 6.30. Say: *"This is my morning. Look!"* Mime waking up and stretching. Ask: *"What am I doing?"* Elicit or teach **get up**.
2. Mime four more actions in order: **have breakfast, go to work, have lunch, go to bed**. After each mime, students call out the phrase. Write each phrase on the left of the board.
3. Ask two or three students: *"What time do you get up?"* Accept short answers (*Six o'clock*). Do not correct grammar at this stage.

**Transition:** *"Now let's meet Nimal. He is a student in Kandy."*

### Stage 2: Presentation, context (8 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To present the target language in a meaningful context and check general understanding.
**Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Point to the table. Say: *"These are six things Nimal does every day. They are not in order."*
2. **Task 1 (gist):** *"Read the text. Number the activities 1 to 6. You have three minutes. Work alone."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you work alone or with a partner?"* (Alone.) *"How many activities?"* (Six.) *"How many minutes?"* (Three.)
3. Students read and number the activities. Monitor and help with vocabulary if needed.
4. Students compare answers with a partner (1 minute). Then check as a class: read each activity and ask *"What number?"*
5. **Task 2 (detail):** *"Read again. Write the times."* Students complete the time column on Handout 1. Check answers by asking: *"What time does Nimal get up?"* Model the full answer: *"He gets up at six thirty."*

### Stage 3: Presentation, language clarification (12 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the present simple, focusing on the third person *-s*.
**Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. **Meaning.** Write on the board: *Nimal gets up at 6.30.* Ask the CCQs from the language analysis (Is this today only? Is it every day? Is it a habit?).
2. **Form (positive).** Write *I get up at 6.30.* above Nimal's sentence. Ask: *"What's different?"* Elicit the **-s**. Underline *-s* in a second colour. Elicit more examples from the text: *goes, has, watches, studies*. Ask: *"Why goes, not gos?"* Write the spelling rules on the board (see Language analysis).
3. **Form (negative).** Point to the text: *He doesn't eat breakfast at home.* Ask: *"Is there an -s on eat?"* (No.) *"Where is it?"* (In doesn't / does.) Draw an arrow from *eats* to *does*. Contrast with *I don't eat breakfast.*
4. **Form (questions).** Write *Does Nimal walk to school?* and *Do you walk to school?* Elicit the short answers. Highlight the word order: Do/Does + subject + verb.
5. **Pronunciation.** Say *works, plays, watches* clearly. Ask students to listen: *"Are the endings the same?"* Write the three columns /s/, /z/, /ɪz/ on the right of the board. Drill chorally, then individually:
   - *works – he works – he works in a bank*
   - *plays – she plays – she plays cricket*
   - *watches – he watches – he watches TV*
6. Drill the question and short answer: T: *"Does Nimal walk to school?"* Ss: *"Yes, he does."* (Back-chain if needed.)

**Transition:** *"Now let's practise. Take Handout 2."*

### Stage 4: Practice, controlled (10 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To give students accurate written practice of the form.
**Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: complete the sentences with the verb in brackets. Look at the person: I or he/she? Five minutes. Work alone."*
   - ICQs: *"Alone or with a partner?"* (Alone.) *"Do you need to add -s for I?"* (No.)
2. Monitor. Note common errors on a piece of paper for later.
3. Students check with a partner (1 minute). Nominate students to read sentences aloud. Write answers on the board. Ask the class to correct any errors.
4. **Exercise B (spelling and sound):** students write the *he/she* form and tick the sound (/s/, /z/ or /ɪz/). Check quickly as a class and drill the trickier ones (*finishes, goes, studies*).

### Stage 5: Production, freer speaking (18 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To give students freer, personalised speaking practice using the target language.
**Interaction:** Pairs, then new pairs, then individual writing

1. Give out Handout 3. **Model first:** ask a strong student the first question: *"What time do you get up?"* Write the answer in the grid on the board. Then show how to report it: *"Ayesha gets up at 5.45."*
2. Say: *"Ask your partner all the questions. Write the answers in the grid. You have six minutes. Then change: your partner asks you."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you write your answers or your partner's answers?"* (My partner's.) *"Do you show your partner the paper?"* (No, ask and listen.)
3. Students interview each other. Monitor actively: listen for errors with *do/does*, word order and times. Write errors and good examples on your notes.
4. **Change partners.** Say: *"Stand up. Find a new partner. Tell your new partner about your first partner."* Model: *"Ayesha gets up at 5.45. She doesn't have breakfast. She goes to school by bus."* (5 minutes.)
5. **Writing (4 minutes):** students write 5–6 sentences about their first partner at the bottom of Handout 3. Fast finishers add *always, usually, sometimes, never*.

### Stage 6: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To give feedback on language used in the production stage and consolidate learning.
**Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Ask two or three students to read their sentences aloud. Praise accurate *-s* endings.
2. **Error correction:** write four sentences from your notes on the board, two correct and two incorrect (e.g. *She work in a shop. / Does he plays football?*). Ask pairs: *"Which are correct? Correct the wrong ones."* Check as a class.
3. **Good language:** board one or two good sentences you heard and praise them.
4. Set the homework (see below). Ask a final question round the class: *"What time does your mother/father get up?"* as students leave.

## Differentiation

**Support (weaker students)**
- Give a verb bank with the *he/she* forms already written.
- Let them use the sentence frames on Handout 3: *He/She ___ at ___.*
- Pair them with a supportive partner for the interview.

**Stretch (stronger students)**
- Add frequency adverbs: *She usually gets up at 6. He never eats breakfast.* (Adverb before the main verb.)
- Ask two follow-up questions with *Why?* or *How?* (*How do you go to school?*).
- Write a paragraph comparing their routine with their partner's.

## Assessment and success criteria
Students are successful if they can:
- produce the *he/she* form with *-s* correctly in at least 4 out of 5 sentences;
- form *Do/Does* questions with correct word order;
- use short answers (*Yes, I do / No, she doesn't*).

Check through monitoring in Stage 5 and the written sentences collected at the end.

## Homework
Write 6–8 sentences about a family member's day. Use at least two negatives and one question you would like to ask them.
*Example: My brother gets up at 7 o'clock. He doesn't eat breakfast. He goes to work by train ...*

## Teacher's reflection (complete after the lesson)
- Did students achieve the learning outcome? How do I know?
- Which stage worked best? Which needed more or less time?
- Which errors were most common? What will I review next lesson?
