# Who and That / Which for Describing People and Things

*Lesson theme: What's the Word? Jobs and Objects*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Relative Clauses |
| Grammar point | Who and That / Which for Describing People and Things |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | High beginners who know jobs and everyday objects and use the present simple. When they don't know a word, they can't describe it, or they use two sentences (He is a person. He helps sick people.). They use which for people. |
| Main aim | To present and practise simple defining relative clauses with who (people) and that / which (things) to describe and define jobs and objects. |
| Subsidiary aims | To review jobs and objects vocabulary; to develop a useful communication strategy (describing a word you don't know); to develop speaking through a guessing game. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can define 6 jobs or objects, e.g. A pilot is a person who flies planes. A fridge is a thing that keeps food cold. |
| Materials | Picture cards of jobs and objects, Handout 1 (picture dictionary definitions), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (guessing game cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Noun** | **Relative pronoun** | **Verb + rest** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| a person | who | + verb | A doctor is a person who helps sick people. |
| a thing / an animal | that / which | + verb | A fridge is a thing that keeps food cold. |
| a place | where | + subject + verb (preview only) | A library is a place where you can read books. |

- **who** for people; **that** or **which** for things and animals.
- The verb after *who / that* agrees with the noun: *a person who helps, people who help*.
- Don't repeat the pronoun: *a person who* ***he*** *helps* ✗.

### Meaning and use

- Relative clauses tell us **which** person or thing, or define a word: *A chef is a person who cooks in a restaurant.*
- Useful when you don't know a word: *I need the thing that opens bottles!*

### Pronunciation

- *who* /huː/ is weak and quick; *that* is weak /ðət/: *a thing that keeps* /ə θɪŋ ðət kiːps/.

### Concept checking questions

*A pilot is a person who flies planes.*

1. Is a pilot a person or a thing? (A person.) So *who* or *which*? (*Who*.)
2. What does a pilot do? (Flies planes.)

*A fridge is a thing that keeps food cold.*

1. Is a fridge a person? (No, a thing.) So *who* or *that*? (*That*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| which for people (a person which). | Two columns on the board: people → who; things → that / which. |
| Repeating the pronoun (a person who he cooks). | Board the error and cross out he. |
| Missing -s (a person who cook). | Highlight -s in colour. |
| Long, confusing definitions. | Model short definitions: noun + who / that + one verb phrase. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate vocabulary and introduce the idea of describing words. **Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Say: *"I'm thinking of a person. This person works in a hospital and helps sick people. Who is it?"* (A doctor / a nurse.)
2. Say: *"I'm thinking of a thing. It tells the time."* (A clock / a watch.)
3. Show the picture cards; students name them quickly.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Part A: Context (8 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To present the target language in context. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the definitions from a picture dictionary. Match each definition to a word."* (4 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"Do you write the word or draw it?"* (Write the word.)
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. **Meaning.** Board *A pilot is a person who flies planes.* Ask the CCQs. Underline *who flies planes*: *"This part tells us what a pilot does."*
2. **who / that / which.** Sort the definitions from the handout into two columns: people (*who*) and things (*that / which*). Board *a person which* ✗.
3. **Form.** Highlight the verb + -s after *who / that*. Board *a person who he cooks* ✗ and cross out *he*.
4. **A useful strategy.** Say: *"You don't know the word? Say: It's a thing that ... / It's a person who ..."* Model: *"I need the thing that opens bottles!"* (A bottle opener.)
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *a person who flies planes, a thing that keeps food cold.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: who or that? Exercise B: join the sentences. Six minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"People: who or that?"* (Who.)
2. Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use relative clauses to describe words in a game. **Interaction:** Groups, then pairs

1. **Guessing game (10 minutes).** Groups of four. Each student takes a card from Handout 3 and describes the word without saying it: *"It's a person who works in a school."* The group guesses. One point for the guesser and one for the describer.
   - ICQs: *"Can you say the word on the card?"* (No.)
2. **My own definitions (5 minutes).** Pairs write definitions for three new words (people or things in the classroom or at home) and read them to another pair to guess.
3. **Writing (3 minutes).** Students write their best three definitions at the bottom of Handout 3.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Two pairs read their definitions; the class guesses.
2. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write definitions for 6 words: 3 people (jobs or family members) and 3 things in your house.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students sentence frames: *It's a person who ... · It's a thing that ...*
- Give them cards with easier words (teacher, chair, phone).

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *where* for places: *It's a place where you buy medicine.*
- They add a second clause: *It's a person who works in a hospital and who helps doctors.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2.
- Students use *who* for people and *that / which* for things in the game.
- Students' definitions are clear enough for others to guess.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use *who* and *that* correctly?
- Did they use the "It's a thing that ..." strategy naturally?
