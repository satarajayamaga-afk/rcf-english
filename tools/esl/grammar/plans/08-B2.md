# Third Conditional and Mixed Conditionals

*Lesson theme: Turning Points*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Conditionals |
| Grammar point | Third Conditional and Mixed Conditionals |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who are confident with the first and second conditionals. They try to talk about past regrets with the second conditional (If I studied harder, I passed) or with would in both clauses (If I would have known, I would have come). |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of the third conditional for imaginary past situations and mixed conditionals for past causes with present results (and present states with past results). |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise the weak forms in would have /ˈwʊdəv/; to develop fluency in telling a personal story about a turning point. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can tell the story of a turning point in their life and imagine how things would be different, e.g. If I hadn't missed the bus, I wouldn't have met my best friend, and I wouldn't be so happy now. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (magazine article), Handout 2 (language reference and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (turning point story planner). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Type** | **If clause** | **Main clause** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Third | If + past perfect | would / could / might have + past participle | If I hadn't missed the bus, I wouldn't have met her. |
| Mixed (past → present) | If + past perfect | would / could + base verb | If I hadn't met her, I wouldn't be so happy now. |
| Mixed (present → past) | If + past simple | would / could have + past participle | If I were braver, I would have applied for the job. |

- Contractions in speech: *If I'd known* (= had), *I'd have come* (= would). *I wouldn't have* /aɪ ˈwʊdnt əv/.

### Meaning and use

- **Third:** an imaginary past. The real past is the opposite: *If I had studied, I would have passed* = I didn't study; I didn't pass.
- Used for regrets, criticism, relief and speculation about the past.
- **Mixed (past → present):** an unreal past cause with an unreal present result.
- **Mixed (present → past):** a present (permanent) state with an unreal past result.

### Pronunciation

- *would have* is weak: /ˈwʊdəv/ or /ˈwʊdə/. *wouldn't have* /ˈwʊdntəv/. *could have* /ˈkʊdəv/.
- The contracted *'d* can mean *had* (in the if clause) or *would* (in the main clause): *If I'd known, I'd have come.*

### Concept checking questions

*If Amaya hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have met Kavindu.*

1. Did Amaya miss the bus? (Yes.)
2. Did she meet Kavindu? (Yes.)
3. Is this about the past or the present? (The past.)

*If she hadn't met him, she wouldn't be a chef today.*

1. Is she a chef now? (Yes.)
2. When did the meeting happen? (In the past.) When is the result? (Now.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| would have in the if clause (If I would have known). | Board the pattern; cross out would in the if clause. |
| Confusing 'd (had / would). | Colour-code: 'd + past participle in the if clause = had; 'd have = would. |
| Students can't hear or say would have. | Back-chain drill: met her → have met her → wouldn't have met her. |
| Mixed conditionals seem confusing. | Use timelines with two points: PAST cause → NOW result. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the theme of chance and turning points. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Tell a short true or invented story: *"Ten years ago, I nearly became a doctor. But I failed my biology exam, so I studied English instead, and I became a teacher."*
2. Ask: *"Was failing the exam bad luck or good luck?"* Pairs discuss small moments that changed something in their lives (2 minutes).
3. Take one or two examples.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the third and mixed conditionals in context and clarify their meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the article. What was the turning point in Amaya's life? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline every sentence with if. For each one, decide: is it about the past, the present or both?"* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **Third conditional.** Board *If Amaya hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have met Kavindu.* Ask the CCQs. Show the real past next to it: *She missed the bus. She met him.*
2. Elicit the form: *If + past perfect, would have + past participle.* Cross out *would* in *If she would have ...*
3. **Mixed conditional.** Board *If she hadn't met him, she wouldn't be a chef today.* Ask the CCQs. Draw a timeline with two points: PAST cause → NOW result.
4. Show the other mixed type briefly: *If she weren't so brave, she wouldn't have opened a restaurant.*
5. Point out *'d* = *had* or *would*.
6. **Pronunciation.** Back-chain drill: *met him → have met him → wouldn't have met him → If she hadn't missed the bus, she wouldn't have met him.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the reference box. Do Exercise A: write a conditional sentence for each situation. Then Exercise B: choose the correct form. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class, asking: *"Past result or present result?"*
3. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the forms fluently in a personal story. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then groups

1. Give out Handout 4. Students choose a turning point in their life (or invent one) and make notes in the planner (4 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Does it have to be true?"* (No, it can be invented.) *"Do you write full sentences?"* (No, notes.)
2. **Pairs (6 minutes).** Students tell their story. The listener asks at least two *What would have happened if ...?* questions.
3. **Groups (6 minutes).** New groups of three. Students retell their partner's story using the third and mixed conditionals: *If Ruwan hadn't broken his leg, he wouldn't have started painting, and he wouldn't be an artist now.*
4. The class chooses the most surprising turning point (2 minutes).
5. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences heard in Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"When do we use the third conditional? When do we use mixed?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write a 180-word blog post about a turning point in your life or in the life of a famous person. Use at least three third conditionals and two mixed conditionals.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the timelines on a card.
- Provide sentence frames: *If I hadn't ..., I wouldn't have ... · If I hadn't ..., I wouldn't be ... now.*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *could have, might have* and *If it hadn't been for ...*.
- They use the present → past mixed type: *If I weren't so shy, I'd have spoken to her.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the story task, students use the past perfect after *if* and *would have* + past participle accurately.
- Students produce at least one mixed conditional with a present result.
- 5 out of 6 or better on Handout 2, Exercise B.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students hear and produce the weak form of *would have*?
- Was the mixed conditional clear after the timelines?

# Handout 1: Magazine Article

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**ONE MISSED BUS, ONE NEW LIFE**

Eight years ago, Amaya Wickramasinghe was a shy accounting student in Kandy. One rainy morning, she missed the bus to university by thirty seconds. While she was waiting for the next one, she started talking to a young man at the bus stop. His name was Kavindu, and he was a chef.

"If I hadn't missed that bus, I would never have met Kavindu," Amaya says. "He invited me to his restaurant, and I fell in love with cooking." She left accounting and trained as a chef. If she had stayed at university, she would have become an accountant like her father.

Today, Amaya runs her own restaurant in Galle. "If I hadn't met Kavindu, I wouldn't be a chef today, and I probably wouldn't be so happy," she says. "And if I weren't so stubborn, I wouldn't have opened my own restaurant. Everyone told me it was a bad idea!"

And Kavindu? He's now her husband.

2. What would Amaya have become if she had stayed at university? ______________________________
3. According to Amaya, why did she open her own restaurant? ______________________________

# Handout 2: Language Focus and Practice

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Language reference**

| **Type** | **Form** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Third (past → past) | If + had + pp, would have + pp | If I hadn't missed the bus, I wouldn't have met him. |
| Mixed (past → present) | If + had + pp, would + verb | If I hadn't met him, I wouldn't be a chef now. |
| Mixed (present → past) | If + past simple, would have + pp | If I weren't so stubborn, I wouldn't have opened a restaurant. |

**A. Write a conditional sentence for each situation.**

1. I didn't set my alarm. I was late for the exam. → If I ______________________________
2. She didn't take the job in Dubai. She lives in Colombo now. → If she ______________________________
3. They didn't bring a map. They got lost. → If they ______________________________
4. He isn't very patient. He shouted at the waiter. → If he ______________________________

**B. Choose the correct form.**

1. If you **told / had told** me, I would have helped you.
2. If we hadn't missed the train, we **would be / would have been** at home now.
3. If she **would have studied / had studied**, she would have passed.
4. If I hadn't eaten so much, I **wouldn't feel / wouldn't have felt** sick now.
5. If he **were / had been** taller, he would have joined the basketball team.
6. I **would have come / would come** to the party if I had known about it.

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| If I would have known, I would have come. | If I had known, I would have come. |
| If I studied harder, I passed the exam. (past) | If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam. |
