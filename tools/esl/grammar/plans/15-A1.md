# Countable and Uncountable Nouns with A / An, Some and Any

*Lesson theme: Going Shopping at the Market*

## Language analysis

### Form and meaning

|  | **Countable (you can count them)** | **Uncountable (you can't count them)** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Singular | a banana, an egg, an onion | rice, milk, water, bread, sugar (no a / an) |
| Plural | bananas, eggs, onions | (no plural: not rices) |
| Positive | There are some eggs. | There is some milk. |
| Negative | There aren't any eggs. | There isn't any milk. |
| Question | Are there any eggs? | Is there any milk? |

- *a* before a consonant sound (*a banana*); *an* before a vowel sound (*an egg, an apple, an onion*).
- *some* in positive sentences; *any* in negatives and questions.
- Uncountable nouns take a singular verb: *There* ***is*** *some rice.*
- To count uncountable nouns: *a bottle of water, a bag of rice, a loaf of bread, a packet of tea.*

### Pronunciation

- *some* is weak in sentences: /səm/: *I need some rice* /aɪ niːd səm raɪs/.
- Linking: *an egg* /ənˈeg/, *an onion* /ənˈʌnjən/.

### Concept checking questions

*There is some rice.*

1. Can I count rice: one rice, two rices? (No.) So is it countable or uncountable? (Uncountable.)
2. Can I say *a rice*? (No.)

*Are there any eggs?*

1. Is this a question? (Yes.) So *some* or *any*? (*Any*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| -s on uncountable nouns (rices, breads). | "Can you count it?" test with real objects; two board columns. |
| a with uncountable nouns (a water). | Teach containers: a bottle of water. |
| some in negatives and questions. | Board symbols: + some, – any, ? any. |
| a before vowels (a egg). | Drill an egg, an apple, an onion with linking. |
| Plural verb with uncountable nouns (There are some milk). | Highlight is in the uncountable column. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate food vocabulary. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Show food picture cards one by one. Students call out the word.
2. Pairs have 1 minute to list as many foods as they can. The pair with the longest list writes it on the board.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present countable and uncountable nouns with *a / an, some* and *any* in context and clarify form, meaning and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Whole class, individual, pairs

**Part A: Sorting (6 minutes)**

1. Hold up a banana (or picture) and say: *"One banana, two bananas."* Then hold up a bag of rice: *"One rice? Two rices? No!"*
2. Students sort the words from Stage 1 into two columns on the board: *Can count / Can't count.* Label them *countable* and *uncountable*.

**Part B: Context (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Look at Nimali's kitchen. Read the text. Is there any milk? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you read or write first?"* (Read.)
2. Students answer the questions. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. **a / an.** Board *a banana, an egg, an onion*. Elicit the rule (vowel sound → *an*).
2. **some / any.** Board three sentences from the text: *There is some bread. There aren't any eggs. Is there any milk?* Add + / – / ? symbols. Ask the CCQs.
3. **Uncountable + is.** Highlight *There* ***is*** *some rice* (not *are*).
4. **Containers.** Show a bottle, a bag, a packet. Board *a bottle of water, a bag of rice, a packet of tea, a loaf of bread*.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *I need some rice. We don't have any eggs. Is there any milk?* with weak *some*.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: write a, an or some. Exercise B: write some or any. Six minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Rice: a or some?"* (Some.)
2. Pairs check. Check as a class; students read the sentences aloud.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language in a shopping role-play. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. **Make a list (5 minutes).** Pairs plan a family dinner (rice and curry). They check Nimali's kitchen (Handout 1) and write a shopping list: *We need some rice ... We don't have any eggs.*
2. **At the market (9 minutes).** Give out Handout 3. Half the class are market sellers with picture cards; half are shoppers. Shoppers ask: *"Do you have any tomatoes?" "Yes, I do. / Sorry, I don't have any." "Can I have a bag of rice, please?"* Then swap roles.
   - ICQs: *"What does the shopper ask?"* (*Do you have any ...?*)
3. **Report (4 minutes).** Pairs tell the class what they bought and what they couldn't find: *"We bought some rice and an onion, but there weren't any..."* (Accept *There wasn't any / There weren't any* as a chunk.)
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Quick game: say a word; students shout *"A!"*, *"An!"* or *"Some!"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Look in your kitchen at home. Write 8 sentences: 4 things you have (*There is some rice. There are some bananas.*) and 4 things you don't have (*There isn't any cheese.*).

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the two-column word list (countable / uncountable) to keep on the desk.
- In the role-play, give them the question frame *Do you have any ...?* on a card.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use more containers: *a tin of fish, a kilo of potatoes, a cup of tea*.
- They ask *How much is it? / How much are they?*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 12 out of 14 or better on Handout 2.
- In the role-play, students use *any* in questions and negatives and no *a* with uncountable nouns.
- Students say *an* before vowel sounds.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students stop adding *-s* to uncountable nouns?
- Was the market role-play lively and well organised?

**Useful language**
