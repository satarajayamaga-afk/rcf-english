# Passive in Different Tenses and with Modals

*Lesson theme: Our Town Is Changing*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | The Passive |
| Grammar point | Passive in Different Tenses and with Modals |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use the present and past simple passive accurately but avoid the passive in other tenses. They say The road is repairing or The bridge has built and avoid modal passives. |
| Main aim | To help students work out and use the passive in the present continuous, present perfect and future (will, going to), and with modals (can, must, should), to describe changes and rules. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read a local news report for specific information; to develop fluency in describing changes and making suggestions. |

## Language analysis

### Form: be + past participle (the tense is shown by *be*)

| **Tense** | **Active** | **Passive** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Present simple | They clean the streets every day. | The streets are cleaned every day. |
| Present continuous | They are repairing the road. | The road is being repaired. |
| Past simple | They opened the park in May. | The park was opened in May. |
| Present perfect | They have built a new bridge. | A new bridge has been built. |
| Future (will) | They will finish the work soon. | The work will be finished soon. |
| going to | They are going to close the road. | The road is going to be closed. |
| Modal | You must wear a helmet. | Helmets must be worn. |

### Meaning and use

- The passive focuses on the thing or the change, not the person: *A new bridge has been built.*
- It is common in news, notices and rules: *Dogs must be kept on a lead.*
- Present continuous passive = a change in progress now; present perfect passive = a recent result.

### Pronunciation

- Weak forms: *has been* /həz bɪn/; *is being* /ɪz ˈbiːɪŋ/; *will be* /wɪl bi/ or *'ll be*.
- Stress falls on the participle: *It's being rePAIRED. It's been OPENED.*

### Concept checking questions

*The road is being repaired.*

1. Is the work finished? (No, it's happening now.)
2. Do we know who is repairing it? (No, and it isn't important.)

*A new bridge has been built.*

1. Is the bridge finished? (Yes.)
2. Is it new or old news? (New, recent.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Missing being or been (The road is repaired now; The bridge has built). | Tense table on the board; highlight the form of be in colour. |
| Active form with passive meaning (The road is repairing). | Ask: "Does the road repair itself?" |
| Modal + to (must to be worn). | Board modal + be + pp, no to. |
| Too many forms at once. | Teach one rule: choose the tense, then change be. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"How has your town changed in the last five years? What's new? What's being built now?"*
2. Pairs discuss for 2 minutes. Take two or three ideas without correcting.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the passive in different tenses and with modals in context and help students work out the form. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the news report. Which changes are finished? Which are happening now? Which are in the future? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many kinds of change are you looking for?"* (Three: finished, now, future.)
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences from the report. Complete the table and answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Alone or in pairs?"* (Pairs.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"Which word shows the tense?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers and build the tense table on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Highlight the rule: *choose the tense → change be → add the past participle.*
4. Show modal passives: *Dogs must be kept on a lead. More trees should be planted.* No *to* after the modal.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *The road's being repaired. A new bridge has been built. The work'll be finished in May.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: complete the sentences with the passive. Exercise B: rewrite the signs as passive rules. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the passive in different tenses fluently in describing change and making suggestions. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. **Spot the changes (7 minutes).** Give out Handout 4. Student A reads the description of Kotagama ten years ago; Student B reads the description of Kotagama today. They describe their town to each other and list as many changes as they can: *"The old cinema has been demolished. A supermarket has been built."*
   - ICQs: *"Can you show your description?"* (No.) *"Which tense for changes from then to now?"* (Present perfect passive.)
2. **Our town plan (8 minutes).** Groups discuss their own town and write five sentences: two changes that have been made, one that is being made now and two suggestions (*More bus stops should be built.*).
3. Groups share their best suggestion (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which word changes to show the tense?"* (*be*.)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a short news report (120 words) about changes at your school or in your town. Use at least five different passive forms.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the tense table on a card.
- In the spot-the-changes task, they can use only the present perfect passive.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add time expressions and *by* + agent where useful: *The park was opened last month by the mayor.*
- They use *need to be* + pp and *could be* + pp in suggestions.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- In the spot-the-changes task, students use the present perfect passive accurately.
- Students produce at least one modal passive in the group suggestions.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which tense caused the most problems?
- Did the "change *be*" rule help students?

# Handout 1: Local News Report

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**KOTAGAMA: A TOWN ON THE MOVE**

Kotagama has changed a lot in the last two years. A new bridge has been built over the river, and the main road has been widened. The old cinema was demolished last year, and a modern library has been opened in its place.

At the moment, the bus station is being renovated, and new trees are being planted in the park. Residents say the town looks much greener.

More changes are planned. A sports centre will be built next year, and the old market is going to be replaced by a covered shopping area. The mayor says that cars will be banned from the town centre on weekends.

However, not everyone is happy. "The town is changing too fast," says Mrs Silva, 72. "More benches should be put in the park for older people, and the bus service must be improved."
