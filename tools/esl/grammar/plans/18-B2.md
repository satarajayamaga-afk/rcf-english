GRAMMAR TOPIC: LINKING WORDS · B2 (Upper-intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Linkers of Cause, Result and Purpose (due to, owing to, as a result, consequently, so that, in order to)

*Lesson theme: Urban Flooding: Causes and Solutions*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Linking Words |
| Grammar point | Linkers of Cause, Result and Purpose (due to, owing to, as a result, consequently, so that, in order to) |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who use because, so and contrast linkers accurately. In formal writing they mix the grammar of cause linkers (due to the rain was heavy), confuse cause and result linkers (Consequently of the rain), and use for + infinitive for purpose (for to reduce). |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise formal linkers of cause (because of, due to, owing to + noun; as, since + clause), result (as a result, consequently, therefore, as a result of + noun) and purpose (to, in order to, so as to + infinitive; so that + clause). |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise sentence-level punctuation with sentence linkers; to write a cause-and-solution paragraph for a report. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can explain causes, results and solutions in formal writing, e.g. Owing to heavy rainfall, several roads were closed. As a result, thousands of commuters were delayed. Drains must be cleaned regularly so that water can flow freely. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (a short report), Handout 2 (language analysis), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (problem cards and writing task). |

## Language analysis

### Cause

| **Linker** | **What follows** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| because of / due to / owing to | noun / -ing / the fact that + clause | Owing to heavy rain, the match was cancelled. |
| as / since | clause | As the drains were blocked, the water rose quickly. |

- *due to* is common after *be*: *The delay was due to the storm.*

### Result

| **Linker** | **Position** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| as a result / consequently / therefore | new sentence (or after a semicolon) + comma | The river overflowed. As a result, many homes were damaged. |
| as a result of + noun | phrase | As a result of the flood, schools closed. (= cause!) |
| so | clause (informal) | It rained, so the roads flooded. |

- *therefore* can also go mid-sentence: *The drains are old and therefore cannot cope.*
- Careful: *as a result of* introduces the **cause**; *as a result* introduces the **result**.

### Purpose

| **Linker** | **What follows** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| to / in order to / so as to | infinitive | The council cleaned the canals (in order) to reduce flooding. |
| in order not to / so as not to | infinitive | They moved the cars so as not to damage them. |
| so that | clause (often with can / could / will / would) | Drains were widened so that water could flow freely. |

- Not *for to reduce* or *for reduce*. *for* + noun is possible: *for safety*.

### Pronunciation

- Pause after sentence linkers: *As a result, | many homes were damaged.*

### Concept checking questions

*Owing to the heavy rain, the roads were closed.*

1. What was the cause? (The heavy rain.) Can I say *Owing to it rained heavily*? (No; noun after *owing to*.)

*As a result of the flood, schools closed.*

1. Is *the flood* a cause or a result? (A cause.)

*They widened the drains so that water could flow.*

1. Why did they widen the drains? (For the water to flow; purpose.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| due to / owing to + clause. | Board: + noun / -ing / the fact that. |
| as a result vs as a result of confusion. | Arrow diagram: cause → As a result, → result; As a result of + cause, result. |
| Sentence linkers used as conjunctions (It rained, consequently the roads flooded). | Full stop or semicolon before them. |
| for to + verb for purpose. | Board: (in order) to + verb; for + noun. |
| so that + infinitive. | Board: so that + subject + can / could. |

## Board plan

| **Left: cause** | **Centre: result** | **Right: purpose** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| because of / due to / owing to + noun / -ing · as / since + clause · The delay was due to the storm. · not: due to it rained ✗ | Sentence. As a result, / Consequently, / Therefore, + sentence. · As a result of + CAUSE, result. · not: ..., consequently the roads ... ✗ (comma splice) | (in order) to / so as to + infinitive · so as not to / in order not to · so that + subject + can / could · not: for to reduce ✗ |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students with the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Show a photo of a flooded street. Ask: *"Why does this happen in cities? What are the effects?"*
2. Pairs list two causes and two effects (2 minutes). Take ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present formal cause, result and purpose linkers and clarify their grammar. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the extract from a report on urban flooding in a fictional city, Riverton. What are the two main causes? What solutions does it suggest?"*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the linkers and classify them: cause, result or purpose. What follows each one?"*
   - ICQs: *"How many categories?"* (Three.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the three columns on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs. Draw the *as a result / as a result of* arrow.
3. Board a comma splice (*It rained heavily, consequently the roads flooded*) and correct it.
4. Board *for to reduce* ✗ → *(in order) to reduce*.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill sentences with a pause after the linker.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the grammar accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct linker. Exercise B: join the sentences using the word in brackets. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the linkers in a short report. **Interaction:** Groups of three, then individual, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Groups choose a problem card (traffic congestion, rubbish on beaches, power cuts, dengue). They brainstorm causes, results and solutions (5 minutes).
2. **Write (10 minutes).** Each student writes a report paragraph (150 words) with at least two cause, two result and two purpose linkers.
   - ICQs: *"How many of each type?"* (At least two.)
3. Partners swap and underline the linkers, checking grammar (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct them.
2. Quick fire: say a linker; students say *cause, result* or *purpose* and what follows.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a full problem–solution report (250 words) on another problem card. Use at least eight different linkers from today.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the three-column table during writing.
- Provide a paragraph frame with gaps for the linkers.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *thus, hence, with the aim of + -ing, for fear of + -ing / that*.
- They use *Given that* and *In view of* for cause.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 3.
- The paragraph includes at least two linkers of each type with correct grammar and punctuation.
- No comma splices with sentence linkers.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the arrow diagram clarify *as a result* vs *as a result of*?
- Did students write in a consistently formal register?

# Handout 1: Report Extract

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Flooding in Riverton: Causes and Recommendations** *(fictional city)*

Riverton has experienced serious flooding in four of the last five monsoon seasons. This is largely due to two factors.

Firstly, owing to rapid construction, many wetlands have been filled in. As these areas once absorbed rainwater, the water now runs directly onto roads. Secondly, many drains are blocked because of plastic waste. Consequently, even moderate rainfall can cause flooding within an hour.

As a result of the floods, thousands of commuters are delayed each year, and some homes have been damaged repeatedly. Therefore, urgent action is needed.

We recommend that the remaining wetlands be protected in order to absorb excess water. Drains should be cleaned before each monsoon so that water can flow freely. In addition, residents should be educated about waste disposal so as not to block drains in the future.

**Answer the questions.**

1. What are the two main causes of flooding? ______________________________
2. What are two effects? ______________________________
3. What are three recommendations? ______________________________

| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| due to · owing to · as · because of · (as a result of + cause) | consequently · therefore | in order to · so that · so as not to |

1. A noun (phrase). · 2. A clause (subject + verb). · 3. A cause (*the floods*). · 4. *so that* + clause (*water can flow*); *in order to* + infinitive (*absorb*).

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Due to it rained, the match was cancelled. | Due to the rain, the match was cancelled. / As it rained, ... |
| It rained, consequently the roads flooded. | It rained. Consequently, the roads flooded. |
| They cleaned the drains for to reduce flooding. | They cleaned the drains (in order) to reduce flooding. |
| As a result the rain, ... | As a result of the rain, ... |
