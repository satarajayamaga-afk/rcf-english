# Present Simple vs Present Continuous: State Verbs

*Lesson theme: Temporary and Permanent: Lifestyles*

## Lesson overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grammar topic** | Present Tenses |
| **Grammar point** | Present Simple vs Present Continuous: State Verbs |
| **Level** | B1 (Intermediate) |
| **Duration** | 60 minutes |
| **Framework** | Guided Discovery: students read a context, work out the rules from examples using guiding questions, the teacher checks and confirms, then students practise. |
| **Class profile** | Intermediate students who know the basic forms of both present tenses but are unsure about temporary vs permanent situations, changing trends and stative verbs. |
| **Main aim** | To help students discover and practise the use of the present simple for permanent situations and the present continuous for temporary situations and changing trends, and to recognise stative verbs. |
| **Subsidiary aims** | To practise reading for specific information; to develop fluency in discussing trends and expressing opinions. |
| **Learning outcome** | By the end of the lesson, students can describe permanent and temporary situations in their lives, talk about trends in their town and avoid stative verbs in the continuous. |
| **Materials** | Handout 1 (three profiles), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (trend discussion cards, cut up). |

## Language analysis

### Form and use

| Use | Tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent situation | Present simple | I **live** in Colombo. She **works** for a bank. |
| Temporary situation (around now) | Present continuous | I**'m staying** with my aunt this month. |
| Changing trend / gradual change | Present continuous | More people **are working** from home. Prices **are rising**. |
| State (opinion, feeling, possession, senses, mind) | Present simple only | I **know** the answer. This bag **belongs** to me. |

### Stative verbs
- **Mind / opinion:** know, believe, understand, think (= have an opinion), remember, mean, agree
- **Feelings:** like, love, hate, want, prefer, need
- **Possession:** have (= own), own, belong
- **Senses / appearance:** seem, look (= appear), taste, smell, sound

### Verbs with two meanings
- *I think it's a good idea.* (opinion: stative) vs *I'm thinking about moving.* (mental process: dynamic)
- *She has a car.* (possession) vs *She's having lunch.* (activity)
- *I see what you mean.* (understand) vs *I'm seeing the doctor tomorrow.* (meeting)

### Pronunciation
- Contractions in speech: *I'm staying, they're working*.
- Stress on the contrast words: *I USUALLY live in Kandy, but THIS month I'm staying in Colombo.*

### Concept checking questions
*I'm staying with my aunt this month.*
1. Is this my permanent home? (No.)
2. Is it true now? (Yes.)
3. Will it change? (Yes, probably soon.)

*I know the answer.*
1. Is knowing an action you can see? (No, it's a state.)
2. Can we say *I'm knowing*? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Stative verbs in the continuous (*I'm knowing, I'm wanting*). | Card sort in the discovery task; keep a stative verb list on the board for the whole lesson. |
| *think* and *have* used wrongly. | Show the meaning pairs side by side; CCQ: *Is it an opinion or an activity?* |
| Students find the discovery questions hard. | Monitor and give the first answer for one example; allow pairs to compare with another pair. |
| Trend discussion becomes one-word answers. | Give the sentence starters on Handout 4. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Lead-in (5 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To generate interest and personalise the topic.
**Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Write on the board: *Always true about me* and *Different at the moment*. Model: *"I always drink tea in the morning. At the moment, I'm learning to swim."*
2. Students tell a partner one thing for each column (2 minutes).
3. Take two or three examples from the class. Write one of each on the board without comment. These will be used later.

### Stage 2: Context, reading (7 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To present the target language in context and check understanding.
**Interaction:** Individual, then pairs

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read about three people. Whose life is changing the most? Three minutes."*
2. Students read and decide. Pairs compare. Take a class vote and ask for reasons.
3. **Detail task:** Students answer the three questions under the profiles. Check as a class.

### Stage 3: Discovery task (10 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To guide students to work out the rules for themselves.
**Interaction:** Pairs

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences from the texts. Answer the questions with your partner. Don't use a grammar book! Think about the meaning."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you work alone?"* (No, in pairs.) *"Do I give you the rules?"* (No, we find them.)
2. Pairs work through Part A (permanent / temporary / trends) and Part B (stative verbs sort).
3. Monitor. If pairs are stuck, ask a guiding question: *"Is Tharindu's job in Melbourne forever?"*
4. When most pairs have finished, pair up the pairs to compare answers (2 minutes).

### Stage 4: Check and clarify (8 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To confirm the rules and clarify meaning, form and pronunciation.
**Interaction:** Whole class

1. Elicit the answers to Part A. Build the rules on the centre of the board as students give them.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Elicit the stative verbs from Part B and board them on the left. Ask: *"Why is I'm knowing wrong?"*
4. Show the verb pairs (think, have, see). Ask: *"Which one is an opinion? Which is an activity?"*
5. Return to the students' sentences from the lead-in on the board. Ask: *"Are these correct? Why?"*
6. Drill the contrast sentence with stress: *I USUALLY live in Kandy, but THIS month I'm staying in Colombo.*

### Stage 5: Controlled practice (9 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To practise choosing the correct form accurately.
**Interaction:** Individual, then pairs

1. Give out Handout 3. Students complete Exercise A (10 items) individually (5 minutes).
2. In pairs, they compare and justify each answer using the rules: *"Temporary, so continuous."*
3. Check as a class. For each stative verb, ask: *"Why not the continuous?"*
4. Fast finishers do Exercise B (error correction).

### Stage 6: Freer practice (15 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To use the target language fluently in a discussion about trends and opinions.
**Interaction:** Groups of three or four

1. Give each group a set of trend cards (Handout 4). Model with one card: *"Online shopping. In my town, more people are shopping online. My mother still goes to the market every day. I believe online shopping is convenient, but I don't think it's good for small shops."*
2. Say: *"Take a card. Talk for one minute: what is changing in your town? What do you think about it? Use the sentence starters."* Each student takes at least two cards.
3. Monitor and note examples of good use and errors (especially stative verbs).
4. Each group chooses the most interesting trend and reports it to the class in two or three sentences.

### Stage 7: Feedback (6 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To give feedback on language from the freer practice.
**Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board three errors and two good sentences from your notes. Pairs correct the errors.
2. Elicit the four rules one last time without looking at the board.
3. Set homework.

## Differentiation

**Support**
- Highlight the key time words (*this month, at the moment, these days*) on Handout 1 before reading.
- Let weaker students do Part A of the discovery task only, then copy Part B from the board.

**Stretch**
- Ask stronger students to find two verbs on Handout 3 that change meaning in the continuous, and write a pair of sentences for each.
- In the discussion, they must use *these days, nowadays, more and more*.

## Assessment and success criteria
- Students can explain (in their own words) when to use each tense.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- In the discussion, students avoid stative verbs in the continuous.

## Homework
Write a 120-word paragraph: *My life at the moment.* Include two permanent facts, three temporary situations, one trend in your town and two opinions using stative verbs.

## Teacher's reflection
- Were the guiding questions clear enough for students to find the rules?
- Did any pairs discover the rules incorrectly? How did I handle it?
- How well did students use stative verbs in the discussion?

**Sentence starters**
- *These days, more and more people are ...*
- *In my town, people are starting to ...*

- *I prefer ... because ...*

# Answer Key

**Handout 1**
1. He comes from Galle; he's living (studying) in Melbourne this year. 2. It's changing the way it works: more meetings are online and people work from home. 3. Young people are leaving the village for the cities.

**Handout 2, Part A**
1. Permanent: 1, 3, 7 · 2. Temporary: 2, 4 · 3. Trends: 5, 6 · 4. Present simple · 5. Present continuous · 6. *this year, now, these days, every month (with the trend)*

**Handout 2, Part B**
- Actions: learn, work, cook, teach, write
- States: love, miss, know, understand, have (= own)

**Handout 2, Part C**
- *know* is a state verb, so we don't use it in the continuous.
- *I think it's a good idea* = my opinion (state). *I'm thinking about moving* = a mental process happening now (action).

**Common errors to watch for**

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|
| I am knowing the answer. | I know the answer. |
| I live with my cousin until July. | I'm living with my cousin until July. |
| Are you understand? | Do you understand? |
| I'm thinking it's a good idea. | I think it's a good idea. |
