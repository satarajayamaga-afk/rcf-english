# Reporting with Say and Tell in the Present (She says she's ...)

*Lesson theme: Passing On Messages*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Reported Speech |
| Grammar point | Reporting with Say and Tell in the Present (She says she's ...) |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | High beginners who use the present simple and be confidently. When they pass on a message, they repeat the exact words (Ravi says "I am late") and confuse pronouns (She says I am hungry meaning she is hungry). They mix say and tell (She says me ...). |
| Main aim | To present and practise reporting messages in the present with says (that) and tells me (that), changing pronouns and verb forms correctly. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise third person -s; to develop listening and speaking through a "telephone" message game. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can pass on 5 simple messages, e.g. Kasun says he's at the bus stop. Mum tells me she's busy. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (phone messages), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (message cards for the game), a toy phone or real phone (optional). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Direct speech** | **Reported speech (present)** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Ravi: "I'm late." | Ravi says (that) he's late. |
| Amma: "I'm busy." | Amma says she's busy. |
| Kasun: "We are at the beach." | Kasun says they're at the beach. |
| Nimmi to me: "I like your bag." | Nimmi tells me (that) she likes my bag. |

- **say** + (that) + clause: *He says he's hungry.* (no person after *say*)
- **tell** + person + (that) + clause: *He tells me he's hungry.* (person after *tell*)
- Change the pronoun: *I → he / she*, *we → they*, *my → his / her*.
- Change the verb to match the new subject: *I like → she likes*, *I am → he is*.
- *that* is optional.

### Meaning and use

- We use *says / tells me* + present to pass on a message that someone **is saying now** or has just sent (a text, a phone call).

### Pronunciation

- *says* /sez/ (not /seɪz/). *tells me* /telz mi/.
- Contractions: *he's* /hiːz/, *she's* /ʃiːz/, *they're* /ðeə/.

### Concept checking questions

*Ravi says he's late.*

1. Who is late: Ravi or me? (Ravi.)
2. Did Ravi say "he's late"? (No, he said "I'm late".)

*Nimmi tells me she likes my bag.*

1. Who is Nimmi talking to? (Me.)
2. Can I say *Nimmi says me*? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Not changing the pronoun (Ravi says I'm late). | Point to Ravi and to yourself; colour-code I → he. |
| Pronunciation /seɪz/. | Drill /sez/ with the sound of Les. |
| say + person (She says me). | Board: say + what, tell + who + what. |
| Missing -s (She says she like). | Highlight -s in a different colour. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the idea of passing on messages. **Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Pretend to read a message on your phone. Say: *"Oh! It's a message from my sister."* Read it aloud: *"I'm at the hospital. I'm OK."*
2. Ask: *"Where is my sister? Is she OK?"* Elicit answers: *She's at the hospital. She's OK.*
3. Say: *"My sister says she's at the hospital. She says she's OK."*

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Part A: Context (8 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To present the language in context. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the phone messages to Dilini. Answer the questions."* (4 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"Who are the messages to?"* (Dilini.) *"Do you answer the questions?"* (Yes.)
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.
3. Read Dilini's message to her mother (the second part of the handout). Ask: *"What does Kasun say?"*

**Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. **Meaning.** Board *Kasun: "I'm at the bus stop."* and *Kasun says he's at the bus stop.* Ask the CCQs.
2. **Pronouns.** Underline *I* and *he*. Ask: *"Why he?"* (Because I'm talking about Kasun.) Build the pronoun list on the left.
3. **Verbs.** Board *Nimmi: "I like your bag."* → *Nimmi says she likes my bag.* Highlight *likes* (-s) and *my*.
4. **say and tell.** Board *Nimmi tells me she likes my bag.* Ask: *"After tell, who?"* (me.) *"After say?"* (no person.) Board *She says me* ✗.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *says* /sez/ and contractions: *He says he's late. She tells me she's busy.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: report the messages. Exercise B: say or tell? Six minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you change I?"* (Yes.)
2. Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers.
3. **Quick drill:** say a sentence about yourself (*"I'm tired."*); students report it: *"She says she's tired."*

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use reported speech in a fun, communicative game. **Interaction:** Groups, then pairs, then whole class

1. **The telephone game (8 minutes).** Groups of four sit in a line. Student 1 takes a message card from Handout 3 and whispers it to Student 2 (*"I'm hungry and I want pizza."*). Student 2 tells Student 3: *"Kasun says he's hungry and he wants pizza."* Student 4 reports the message to the class. Is it the same as the card?
   - ICQs: *"Do you say I or he when you pass on the message?"* (He / she.)
2. **My messages (6 minutes).** Each student writes two real or invented short messages on paper (*I love cricket. My cat is black.*) and gives them to a partner. The partner tells a new partner: *"Ayesha says she loves cricket. She says her cat is black."*
3. **Writing (4 minutes).** Students write 4 reported sentences about their classmates' messages.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Two or three students read their sentences.
2. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Ask three people in your family a question (*How are you? What do you like?*). Write what they say: *My father says he's tired. My sister says she likes music.*

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the pronoun list on a card.
- In the telephone game, use short one-sentence cards.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students report two-part messages with *and* and *but*: *She says she's tired but she's happy.*
- They use *asks* for simple questions (preview): *He asks if you're OK.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2.
- Students change *I* to *he / she* when they pass on messages.
- Students pronounce *says* as /sez/.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students change pronouns and verb forms?
- Was the telephone game clear and fun?

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Ravi says I'm late. (meaning Ravi is late) | Ravi says he's late. |
| She says me she's busy. | She tells me she's busy. / She says she's busy. |
| He says he like pizza. | He says he likes pizza. |
| Kasun says, I am at the bus stop. | Kasun says he's at the bus stop. |
