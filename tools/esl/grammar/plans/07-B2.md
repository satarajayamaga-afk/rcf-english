# Modals of Deduction (must, might, can't – present and past)

*Lesson theme: The Missing Student*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Modal Verbs |
| Grammar point | Modals of Deduction (must, might, can't – present and past) |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who use must and might for present deduction but say He mustn't be at home (meaning can't) and She must have went. |
| Main aim | To present and practise modals of deduction for present and past situations: must, might / may / could, can't / couldn't + base verb / be + -ing / have + past participle. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in speculating and reasoning; to practise connected speech in must have /ˈmʌstəv/. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can solve a mystery by making and justifying deductions about present and past events. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (the mystery scenario), Handout 2 (certainty scale and practice), Handout 3 (clue cards), close-up photos of everyday objects (or descriptions). |

## Language analysis

### Form and certainty

| **Certainty** | **Present** | **Present continuous** | **Past** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| ~100% sure it's true | He must be at home. | She must be sleeping. | They must have left early. |
| possible | It might / may / could be him. | He might be working. | She might / may / could have forgotten. |
| ~100% sure it's not true | It can't be Ravi. | They can't be playing outside. | He can't / couldn't have seen us. |

### Accuracy points

- Negative deduction is *can't*, not *mustn't*: *He can't be at home* (not *He mustn't be at home*).
- Past: modal + *have* + **past participle**: *must have gone* (not *must have went*).
- *couldn't have* and *can't have* are both used for the past.

### Pronunciation

- Weak *have*: *must have* /ˈmʌstəv/, *might have* /ˈmaɪtəv/, *can't have* /ˈkɑːntəv/.

### Concept checking questions

*The lights are off. They must be out.*

1. Am I sure? (Almost sure.) Why? (The lights are off: evidence.)

*He can't have seen us. He didn't say hello.*

1. Am I sure he didn't see us? (Almost sure.)
2. Is it about the past or now? (The past.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| mustn't for negative deduction. | Board the certainty scale: must ↔ can't. |
| must have went | Drill past participles; error correction in practice. |
| Hearing of instead of have (must of). | Show weak /əv/ is written have. |
| Deductions without evidence. | Require because + evidence in the freer task. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students in speculating. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Show (or describe) three close-up photos of everyday objects (the bristles of a toothbrush, the inside of a mango, a coconut shell).
2. Pairs guess: *"It might be ... It can't be ..."* (2 minutes). Reveal the answers.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present modals of deduction in context and clarify certainty, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read about Tharushi. Where do you think she is? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Who is missing?"* (Tharushi.)
2. Pairs share their theories in any language they have.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Read the friends' comments at the bottom. How sure is each speaker? Write 100%, 50% or 0% next to each."* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. Build the certainty scale on the board using the friends' comments.
2. Ask the CCQs. Point out *can't* (not *mustn't*) for negative certainty.
3. **Form.** Show present (*must be*), continuous (*might be studying*) and past (*must have gone*). Elicit the past participle in each past example.
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill weak *have*: *She must have gone to the library. He can't have seen her.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: complete with must, might or can't in the correct form. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check and explain their certainty. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use deductions fluently and logically in a group mystery. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then whole class

1. Say: *"New information about Tharushi arrives. Each time you get a clue card, discuss and update your theory. Use must, might and can't, and always give a reason."*
   - ICQs: *"What must you always give?"* (A reason / the evidence.)
2. Give each group clue card 1 (Handout 3). After 2 minutes, give clue 2, and so on, until all five clues are out (10 minutes).
3. Groups write their final theory in three sentences and present it (6 minutes).
4. Reveal the solution (on the last clue card, which you keep). Groups get a point for each correct deduction (2 minutes).

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences heard during the mystery: correct and incorrect. Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"How do you say you're sure something didn't happen?"* (*can't have* + pp.)
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Find a strange or mysterious photo online or in a newspaper. Write 150 words explaining what might have happened, using at least six modals of deduction.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Keep the certainty scale on the board and give a sentence-frame card: *She must / might / can't have ... because ...*
- Give weaker groups the clues with key words highlighted.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *could have* and *couldn't have*, and *may well have*.
- They challenge other groups' theories: *But if she had gone to the library, her friend would have seen her.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students use *can't* (not *mustn't*) for negative deduction.
- Past deductions use *have + past participle* correctly.
- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students give evidence for their deductions?
- Were past forms accurate?

# Handout 2: Certainty Scale and Practice

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

| **100% YES** | **50%** | **100% NO** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| must + be / be -ing / have + pp | might / may / could + be / be -ing / have + pp | can't / couldn't + be / be -ing / have + pp |

**A. Complete with must, might or can't and the correct form of the verb.**

1. The lights are off. They ______________________ (be) out.
2. He didn't say hello. He ______________________ (see) us.
3. I'm not sure where she is. She ______________________ (be) in the library.
4. The ground is wet. It ______________________ (rain) last night.
5. She ______________________ (forget) the meeting. I reminded her this morning.
6. Don't call him now. He ______________________ (sleep). He works nights.
7. That ______________________ (be) Ravi's bag. His is red, and this one is blue.
8. They ______________________ (take) the bus. It's possible, but they usually walk.
9. You ______________________ (be) hungry. You haven't eaten all day!
10. He ______________________ (lose) his keys. He's looking everywhere.
