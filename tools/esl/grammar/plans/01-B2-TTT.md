# Present Continuous with Always; State and Action Verbs

*Lesson theme: Habits, Annoyances and Changing States*

## Lesson overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grammar topic** | Present Tenses |
| **Grammar point** | Present Continuous with Always; State and Action Verbs |
| **Level** | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| **Duration** | 60 minutes |
| **Framework** | TTT: Test 1 (students try the language with no help), Teach (focus only on what Test 1 showed they need), Test 2 (a freer task to check improvement). |
| **Class profile** | Upper-intermediate students who are confident with basic present tenses. They have rarely used *always* + continuous for complaints, and they are inconsistent with verbs that have both stative and dynamic meanings. |
| **Main aim** | To diagnose and develop students' use of present forms for (a) annoying habits (*always* + continuous), (b) gradual change (*getting* + comparative), (c) stative/dynamic verb meanings and (d) the present simple for timetables. |
| **Subsidiary aims** | To develop fluency and appropriate intonation in a polite complaint role-play; to read a blog for writer's attitude. |
| **Learning outcome** | By the end of the lesson, students can make a polite but honest complaint about someone's habits, using *always* + continuous and appropriate softening language. |
| **Materials** | Handout 1 (Test 1), Handout 2 (blog: Things My Flatmate Does), Handout 3 (Teach: language reference and practice), Handout 4 (role-play cards for Test 2). |

## Language analysis

### Form, meaning and use

| Use | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Annoying or surprising habit | *always / constantly / forever* + present continuous | She**'s always borrowing** my charger! |
| Neutral habit | *always* + present simple | She **always borrows** books from the library. |
| Gradual change | *get / become* + comparative, continuous | It**'s getting hotter** every year. |
| Temporary behaviour | *be being* + adjective | You**'re being** very quiet today. |
| Stative meaning | present simple | I **see** what you mean. She **has** a car. This **tastes** good. |
| Dynamic meaning | present continuous | I**'m seeing** the doctor at 4. She**'s having** a hard time. The chef **is tasting** the sauce. |
| Timetables and schedules | present simple | The train **leaves** at 8. |

### Pronunciation
- *always* + continuous: the main stress falls on *always*, often with a fall-rise to show irritation: *He's ALways leaving the lights on!*
- Softening language is stressed less and spoken more quickly: *It's just that ..., I don't know if you've noticed, but ...*

### Concept checking questions
*He's always leaving dirty dishes in the sink.*
1. Does it happen often? (Yes, very often, maybe too often.)
2. How does the speaker feel? (Annoyed.)
3. Does it really happen every single time? (Probably not; the speaker is exaggerating.)

*You're being very quiet today.*
1. Are you usually quiet? (No.)
2. Is it just your behaviour today? (Yes.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Word order: *He always is losing his keys.* | Show the position on the board: be + always + verb-ing. |
| Overuse of *always* + continuous making complaints sound rude. | Teach softening language (*I've noticed that ..., It's just that ...*) before the role-play. |
| Confusion between *have* (possession) and *have* (activity). | Give pairs of sentences and ask: *Can you own it? Is it an activity?* |
| Test 1 is too difficult for some students and they lose confidence. | Tell students clearly before Test 1 that it is a diagnostic and that mistakes are useful. |
| Teach stage expands to cover everything. | Use the Test 1 results: teach only items that more than a third of the class got wrong. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Lead-in (5 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic of annoying habits and engage students personally.
**Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Say: *"Think about people on buses, in class, at home or online. What habits annoy you?"* Give one example without using the target language: *"People who talk loudly on the phone on the bus."*
2. Students list three annoying habits with a partner (2 minutes).
3. Take ideas from the class and board key words only (*phone, late, borrow, noise*). Do not focus on grammar.

### Stage 2: Test 1 (10 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To diagnose what students already know and what they need.
**Interaction:** Individual

1. Say: *"Before I teach anything, I want to see what you know. This is not an exam, and mistakes are very useful for me. Work alone, no dictionaries. Seven minutes."* Give out Handout 1.
   - ICQs: *"Can you work with a partner?"* (No.) *"Is it a problem if you make mistakes?"* (No.)
2. Monitor closely. Note on a class list which items cause problems for many students.
3. When most students have finished, stop. Do not give answers.

### Stage 3: Diagnose (5 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To identify the language the class needs to focus on.
**Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Pairs compare their answers and discuss any differences (2 minutes).
2. Go through the items quickly. For each, ask: *"Who has A? Who has B?"* Count hands. **Do not confirm answers yet.**
3. Board the numbers of the two or three items where the class disagreed most (left of the board). Say: *"These are our focus today."*

### Stage 4: Teach (13 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the problem areas identified in Test 1.
**Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. **Context.** Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the blog. How does the writer feel about her flatmate? Do you think she's being fair?"* (3 minutes.) Elicit that she is annoyed but also funny.
2. **Noticing.** Students underline every present form in the blog (2 minutes).
3. **Clarify the problem areas.** Focus first on the items from the Diagnose stage. For each one:
   - **always + continuous:** Write *She's always borrowing my charger!* and *She always borrows books from the library.* Ask the CCQs. Elicit that the first shows annoyance. Board the word order: be + always + verb-ing.
   - **gradual change:** Write *It's getting hotter every year.* Ask: *Is it changing? Is it finished?*
   - **stative / dynamic:** Board pairs from the blog (*I see what you mean / I'm seeing someone*; *She has a cat / She's having a party*). Students explain the difference to a partner.
   - **being + adjective:** *You're being very quiet today.* CCQ: *Are you usually quiet?*
4. **Pronunciation.** Model the irritated intonation: *He's ALways leaving the lights on!* Students repeat chorally, then say it with different feelings (annoyed, amused).
5. **Quick practice.** Students do Handout 3, Exercise A (4 minutes) and check in pairs.
6. **Answer Test 1.** Now give the correct answers to Test 1. Students mark their own work.

### Stage 5: Test 2 (20 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To check improvement through a freer, communicative task.
**Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Pairs correct their own Test 1 answers using what they learned (3 minutes).
2. **Prepare the role-play.** Give out Handout 4. Teach the complaint language on the right of the board. Say: *"You are flatmates. Student A has a problem with Student B's habits. Make a polite but honest complaint. Student B, respond, explain and suggest a change. Use at least three phrases from the board."*
   - ICQs: *"Are you angry or polite?"* (Polite but honest.) *"Who suggests a change?"* (Student B.)
3. Students read their cards and prepare for 2 minutes.
4. **Role-play 1** (4 minutes). Monitor and note language use.
5. Students swap roles and use the second scenario (4 minutes).
6. **Performance:** two or three pairs perform for the class. The class listens and notes: *How many examples of always + continuous? Was the complaint polite?*

### Stage 6: Feedback (7 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To compare Test 1 and Test 2 performance and give final feedback.
**Interaction:** Whole class

1. Ask: *"Was it easier to use these structures in the role-play than in Test 1?"*
2. Board four sentences heard during the role-plays. Students identify and correct errors.
3. Board one or two very natural complaints and praise them.
4. Set homework.

## Differentiation

**Support**
- Provide sentence frames for the role-play: *I've noticed that you're always ... . Could you try to ...?*
- Allow weaker students to do Test 2 in a group of three, with one student as an observer.

**Stretch**
- Stronger students add sarcastic stress patterns and hedging (*a bit, kind of, sort of*).
- Ask them to use *forever* and *constantly* as alternatives to *always*.

## Assessment and success criteria
- Test 1 vs Test 2: students use *always* + continuous accurately and appropriately in the role-play.
- Students choose the correct stative or dynamic form in 4 out of 5 Teach practice items.
- Complaints include at least one softening phrase.

## Homework
Write a humorous blog post (150 words) about someone's annoying habits. Use *always* + continuous at least three times, *getting* + comparative once and one stative/dynamic pair.

## Teacher's reflection
- Did Test 1 show clearly what the class needed?
- Did I keep the Teach stage focused, or did I teach things they already knew?
- How much better was Test 2?

# Handout 4: Role-play Cards (Test 2)

**Scenario 1: Student A**
You share a flat with Student B. You are unhappy because B: leaves dishes in the sink, borrows your clothes without asking, and plays games late at night. Make a polite but honest complaint. Use *always* + continuous and softening language.

**Scenario 1: Student B**
You share a flat with Student A. Listen to A's complaint. Explain your side (you are having a busy time at work, you are getting tired, etc.). Suggest one change and ask for one change from A.

**Scenario 2: Student A**
You work in an office with Student B. B is a nice colleague, but B: talks loudly on the phone, takes your stapler and arrives late to meetings. Make a polite complaint.

**Scenario 2: Student B**
You work with Student A. Listen to A's complaint. Explain (you are being given more work, your bus is always late, etc.). Agree to one change. Mention one habit of A's that also annoys you!

**Useful language**
- *I've noticed that you're always ...*
- *It's just that ...*
- *I don't mean to complain, but ...*
- *Would you mind not ...-ing?*
- *Could you try to ...?*
- *I'm sorry, I didn't realise. I'm having a really busy time at the moment.*
