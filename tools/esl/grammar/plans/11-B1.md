# (Not) As ... As, Modifiers and Less / The Least

*Lesson theme: Choosing a Phone*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Comparatives and Superlatives |
| Grammar point | (Not) As ... As, Modifiers and Less / The Least |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who form comparatives and superlatives correctly but compare only with -er / more. They say very bigger, as big than and don't use not as ... as or less. |
| Main aim | To help students work out and use (not) as + adjective + as, modifiers of comparatives (much, far, a lot, a bit, slightly), and less / the least, to make more precise comparisons. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read product reviews for specific information; to develop fluency in comparing and choosing products. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can compare three products precisely and recommend one, e.g. The Nova is slightly cheaper than the Zeta, but its camera isn't nearly as good. The Lumo is by far the least expensive. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (phone comparison chart and reviews), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (product cards for three categories). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Structure** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- |
| as + adjective + as (= the same) | The Nova is as fast as the Zeta. |
| not as / not so + adjective + as (= less) | The Lumo isn't as fast as the Zeta. |
| just as / nearly as / not nearly as / twice as | It's just as good. It isn't nearly as expensive. It's twice as heavy. |
| much / far / a lot + comparative (big difference) | The Zeta is much more expensive. |
| a bit / slightly / a little + comparative (small difference) | The Nova is slightly cheaper. |
| less + adjective (+ than) | The Lumo is less expensive than the Nova. |
| the least + adjective | The Lumo is the least expensive. |
| by far + superlative | The Zeta is by far the best camera. |

### Meaning and use

- *not as ... as* is often more polite than a direct comparative: *My cooking isn't as good as yours* (softer than *Your cooking is better*).
- Modifiers make comparisons precise (a big or small difference).
- *less / the least* are mostly used with longer adjectives: *less expensive, the least comfortable* (with short adjectives we usually use *not as ... as*: *not as tall as*).

### Pronunciation

- Weak *as* /əz/: *as fast as* /əz ˈfɑːst əz/.
- Stress the modifier: *It's MUCH cheaper. It's SLIGHTly bigger.*

### Concept checking questions

*The Lumo isn't as fast as the Zeta.*

1. Which phone is faster? (The Zeta.)
2. Are they the same? (No.)

*The Nova is slightly cheaper than the Zeta.*

1. Is the price difference big or small? (Small.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| very + comparative (very bigger). | Board: much / far / a lot bigger; cross out very. |
| as ... than or as -er as (as bigger as). | Board: as + adjective + as (no -er). |
| less with short adjectives (less tall). | Prefer not as tall as; note that less tall is possible but less common. |
| Confusing not as ... as meaning. | CCQs with a picture of two phones. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"What's important when you choose a phone: the price, the camera, the battery?"* Pairs rank five features (2 minutes).
2. Take ideas and board key words.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the target structures in context and help students work out their form and meaning. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Look at the chart and read the review. Which phone does the reviewer recommend for students? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences from the review and the chart. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you use the chart to check?"* (Yes.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"Which is faster? Look at the chart."*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers and build the patterns in the centre of the board.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
3. Draw a scale for modifiers: *slightly / a bit → much / far / a lot*. Board *very bigger* ✗ and *as big than* ✗.
4. Show politeness: *Your phone isn't as new as mine* vs *My phone is newer than yours.*
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill weak *as* and stressed modifiers: *It isn't as fast as the Zeta. It's MUCH cheaper. It's SLIGHTly heavier.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: rewrite the sentences. Exercise B: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use precise comparisons fluently in a decision-making task. **Interaction:** Groups of three, then whole class

1. Give each group a set of product cards (Handout 4). Each group chooses one category (laptops, bicycles or hotels in Kandy).
2. Groups compare the three products using at least eight structures from today and choose the best one for a given customer (10 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"How many products do you compare?"* (Three.) *"What do you decide?"* (The best one for the customer.)
3. Each group presents its recommendation in one minute: *"The Hilltop Inn isn't as cheap as the Lake View, but it's far quieter. The City Lodge is by far the least comfortable."* (6 minutes.)
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"How do you say there's a small difference? A big difference?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a short review (120 words) comparing three things you know well (phones, restaurants, cricket teams, films). Use at least six structures from today.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the patterns on a card.
- Ask them to compare two products instead of three.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *twice / three times as ... as* and *nowhere near as ... as*.
- They use *the + comparative + of the two*: *the cheaper of the two*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- The group recommendation uses at least eight different structures accurately.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- No *very* + comparative errors in the presentations.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use modifiers to make their comparisons precise?
- Did *not as ... as* appear naturally?
