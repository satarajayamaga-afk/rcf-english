# The Meanings of Particles (up, down, out, off, over) and Phrasal Verbs at Work

*Lesson theme: Managing a Project at Work*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Phrasal Verbs |
| Grammar point | The Meanings of Particles (up, down, out, off, over) and Phrasal Verbs at Work |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who handle phrasal verb word order well but learn every phrasal verb as a separate item and find them random. They avoid phrasal verbs in speech (We postponed the meeting instead of We put it off), and guess meanings literally. |
| Main aim | To help students recognise common meanings of particles (up = completion or increase, down = reduction or refusal, out = solving or completing thoroughly, off = cancelling or starting, over = reviewing or transferring) and use workplace phrasal verbs accurately. |

| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can take part in a project meeting using phrasal verbs naturally, e.g. Let's go over the budget. We need to cut down on costs. Can you sort out the venue? We can't put it off again, so let's call off the extra event. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (meeting minutes), Handout 2 (particle meanings), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (project meeting role cards). |

## Language analysis

### Common particle meanings

| **Particle** | **Meaning** | **Workplace examples** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| up | completion; increase; creation | use up (use all of), sum up (summarise), set up (establish), speed up (go faster), follow up (take further action) |
| down | reduction; refusal; recording | cut down on (reduce), slow down, turn down (refuse), write / note down |
| out | solving; thoroughly; distributing | sort out (solve), work out / figure out (calculate, understand), carry out (do: a survey, a plan), hand out (distribute) |
| off | cancelling; delaying; starting | call off (cancel), put off (postpone), kick off (start, informal), lay off (dismiss workers) |
| over | reviewing; transferring | go over (check carefully), think over (consider), take over (take control), hand over (give responsibility) |

### Key points

- The meanings are tendencies, not rules: *put off* can also mean "discourage" (*The price put me off*).
- Many verbs combine the same particle meanings: *eat up, drink up, use up* (all = completely).
- Word order rules still apply: *Let's put the meeting off / put it off*; *cut down on costs* (three-part, inseparable).
- Phrasal verbs are more informal than single-verb equivalents (*postpone, cancel, distribute*), but very common in spoken business English.

### Pronunciation

- Stress the particle when the verb ends the clause: *We had to call it OFF.*

### Concept checking questions

*They called off the meeting.*

1. Did the meeting happen? (No, it was cancelled.)

*They put off the meeting.*

1. Will the meeting happen? (Yes, but later.) Is it the same as *call off*? (No.)

*Can you sort out the problem with the printer?*

1. Is there a problem now? (Yes.) What do I want? (Someone to solve it.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Confusing put off and call off. | Timeline: put off → later; call off → never. |
| Seeing particle meanings as fixed rules. | Show exceptions; present as "helpful patterns". |
| Literal guessing (take over = carry over something). | Context first; then particle meaning. |
| Avoiding phrasal verbs in speech. | Role-play with a "phrasal verb target" checklist. |
| Separating three-part verbs (cut costs down on). | Board: three-part verbs never separate. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and raise awareness of particles. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Board four sentences: *Drink up! · Prices went up. · Sum up the report. · Set up a company.* Ask: *"What does up mean in each?"*
2. Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Elicit: "complete", "increase", "create". Say: *"Particles often have patterns of meaning."*

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present phrasal verbs at work and help students notice particle meanings. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the minutes of a project meeting at a tea company in Kandy. What are the three main decisions? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, put the phrasal verbs from the minutes in the right row, according to the meaning of the particle."*
   - ICQs: *"How many particles?"* (Five.) *"Where are the verbs?"* (In bold in the minutes.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the table on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs; draw the *put off / call off* timeline.
3. Show one exception: *The smell put me off* (= discouraged).
4. Highlight register: *We postponed the meeting* (formal) / *We put the meeting off* (spoken).
5. **Guess the meaning.** Board three new verbs: *drink up, write off (a debt), hand in (a report)*. Students guess using particle meanings.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise meaning and form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct particle. Exercise B: replace the words in italics with a phrasal verb. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use phrasal verbs fluently in a project meeting. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Groups are the project team for a company's 25th anniversary event. Each member has a role card with problems to raise and a list of phrasal verbs to use (3 minutes to prepare).
2. **Meeting (10 minutes).** Groups hold the meeting and agree on five actions. One student takes notes (the minutes): *Ruwan will sort out the catering.*
   - ICQs: *"What do you need at the end?"* (Five decisions.) *"Who writes them?"* (One note-taker.)
3. **Report (5 minutes).** Each group sums up its decisions for the class.
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which particle meaning was most useful today?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write the minutes of your group's meeting (150 words), using at least eight phrasal verbs from at least four particle groups.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students Handout 2 (completed) during the meeting.
- Let them focus on two particles (*up* and *off*).

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *phase out, iron out, bring forward, back up* (support) and explain the particle logic.
- They rewrite the minutes in a formal register with single-word verbs.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3.
- In the meeting, students use at least six phrasal verbs accurately, including correct word order with pronouns.
- Students distinguish *put off* and *call off*.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the particle meanings help students guess new verbs?
- Did the meeting sound more natural than students' usual speech?

# Answer Key

**Handout 1**

1. Because the design team has used up most of the budget. · 2. Yes, in June (it has been put off). · 3. No, it has been called off. · 4. Dinesh.

**Handout 2**

| **Particle** | **Phrasal verbs** |
| :-- | :-- |
| UP | summing up, used up |
| DOWN | cut down on, turned down |

| OFF | kicked off, put off, called off |
