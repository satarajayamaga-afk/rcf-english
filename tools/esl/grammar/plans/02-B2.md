GRAMMAR TOPIC: PAST TENSES · B2 (Upper-intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Past Perfect Continuous

*Lesson theme: What Had Happened? The Detective's Notebook*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Past Tenses |
| Grammar point | Past Perfect Continuous |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who know the past perfect simple but rarely use the past perfect continuous, and confuse the two when talking about causes and duration. |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of the past perfect simple and continuous to explain causes, visible results and duration before a past moment. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in speculating and explaining; to practise weak forms of had been. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can explain a past scene logically, e.g. The floor was wet because it had been raining and someone had left the window open. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (detective's notebook), Handout 2 (language reference and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (scene cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Past perfect simple** | **Past perfect continuous** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Positive | She had finished the report. | She had been working all night. |
| Negative | They hadn't eaten. | They hadn't been sleeping well. |
| Question | Had you met him before? | How long had you been waiting? |

### Meaning and use

| **Use** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Completed action before a past moment (result, number) | By 10 pm, he had written three emails. |
| Activity continuing up to a past moment (duration) | She had been working there for ten years when she resigned. |
| Visible result of a recent activity | His eyes were red because he had been crying. |
| State verbs: simple only | I had known him for years. (not had been knowing) |

Time markers: *by the time, by (10 pm), for, since, already, just, when, before*.

### Pronunciation

- *had been* is weak and contracted: *She'd been working* /ʃid bɪn ˈwɜːkɪŋ/.

### Concept checking questions

*The ground was wet. It had been raining.*

1. Was it raining at the moment I looked? (Not necessarily; maybe it had stopped.)
2. Was the rain before the moment I saw the ground? (Yes.)
3. Can we see a result? (Yes, the wet ground.)

*By the time I arrived, they had finished the meeting.*

1. Was the meeting finished when I arrived? (Yes.)
2. Is the focus on the activity or the completion? (Completion.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| State verbs in the continuous (had been knowing). | Mini state verb list on the board. |
| Continuous with a number of things (had been writing three emails). | Contrast: number = result = simple. |
| Using past simple instead (He was tired because he ran). | Accept as possible in some cases; show that the past perfect makes the order clear. |
| Groups produce weak explanations in the deduction task. | Provide clue prompts on the scene cards. |

## Board plan

| **Left: vocabulary** | **Centre: rules** | **Right: pronunciation** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| suspect · force a lock · breathe heavily · clue · evidence · explanation | Simple: had + pp → completed result / number: had written three emails · Continuous: had been + -ing → duration / activity / visible evidence: had been raining · State verbs → simple only: had known | She'd been working /ʃid bɪn/ · had been /əd bɪn/ |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage interest with a visual puzzle. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Describe or show a photo: *A man arrives at the office. He's soaking wet, exhausted, his shoes are muddy and he's holding a broken umbrella.*
2. Ask: *"Why? What had happened to him before he arrived?"* Students speculate with a partner (2 minutes). Don't correct.
3. Take a few ideas and board one or two.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the past perfect simple and continuous in context and clarify the difference in meaning and form. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the detective's notes. What had the suspect been doing before 10 pm? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline all the past perfect forms. Put them in two groups: had + past participle and had been + -ing."* Pairs compare. Board the two lists.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. Draw a timeline for *He had been running* (a wavy line ending just before 10 pm) and *Someone had forced the lock* (a cross before 10 pm). Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
2. Elicit the rules: completed result or number → simple; duration, activity or visible evidence → continuous; state verbs → simple only.
3. Contrast: *By the end of the interview, he had changed his story three times* (number → simple) vs *She had been living next door for five years* (duration → continuous).
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill weak forms: *She'd been working. They'd been waiting for hours.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing and forming the two tenses accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the reference box. Do Exercise A: choose the correct form. Then Exercise B: write explanations. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class, asking for the reason each time.
3. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use both forms fluently in a deduction task. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then whole class

1. Give each group a scene card (Handout 4). Say: *"You are detectives. Look at the clues. Explain exactly what had happened and what people had been doing before the police arrived. Write at least six sentences using both past perfect forms."* (10 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"How many sentences?"* (At least six.) *"Which forms?"* (Both past perfect forms.)
2. Monitor and help with logic and language.
3. Groups present their explanation (1 minute each). Other groups challenge: *"But if he had been sleeping, why was the coffee hot?"*
4. The class votes for the most convincing explanation.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences heard during the presentations: correct and incorrect. Students correct them.
2. Ask: *"How do you decide between had done and had been doing?"* Elicit the rules.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write the detective's final report (180 words) explaining what had happened in your group's scene.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker groups the timeline images on their scene cards.
- Provide sentence starters: *The window was open because someone had ... . The floor was wet because ... had been ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger groups include passive forms: *The window had been forced.*
- They use modal deductions: *He must have been sleeping.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the deduction task, students choose simple vs continuous correctly most of the time.
- Correct use of state verbs in the simple form.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Was the difference between the two forms clear after the clarification?
- Was the deduction task motivating?

# Handout 1: The Detective's Notebook

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Detective Jayasinghe, 11 March, 10.15 pm**

When we arrived at the flat at 10 pm, the door was open. Someone had forced the lock. The suspect, Mr Dias, was sitting on the sofa. He was breathing heavily, and his shirt was wet: he had obviously been running.

The kitchen was a mess. Someone had been cooking: there was rice on the floor, and the cooker was still warm. Two glasses were on the table. Mr Dias said he had been watching a film all evening, but the TV was cold. He hadn't been watching anything.

His neighbour told us she had heard shouting at about 9.30. She had been living next door for five years and had never heard anything like it before.

By the end of the interview, Mr Dias had changed his story three times.

# Handout 2: Language Focus and Practice

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Language reference**

| **Past perfect simple (had + pp)** | **Past perfect continuous (had been + -ing)** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Completed action / result: Someone had forced the lock. | Activity up to a past moment: He had been running. |
| Number or quantity: He had changed his story three times. | Duration: She had been living there for five years. |
| State verbs: I had known him for years. | Visible evidence: His shirt was wet; he had been running. |

**A. Choose the correct form.**

1. When I got home, my sister **had cooked / had been cooking** dinner, so we ate immediately.
2. Her eyes were red. She **had cried / had been crying**.
3. We **had walked / had been walking** for hours when we found the village.
4. By 2015, he **had visited / had been visiting** twelve countries.
5. I **had known / had been knowing** her since primary school.
6. The children were dirty because they **had played / had been playing** in the garden.
7. She **had read / had been reading** the book twice before the film came out.
8. How long **had you waited / had you been waiting** when the doctor arrived?

**B. Write an explanation for each situation using the past perfect continuous.**

1. He had a sunburn. → ______________________________
2. The teacher's voice was very tired. → ______________________________
3. The streets were full of water. → ______________________________

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| I had been knowing her for years. | I had known her for years. |
| He was tired because he has been running. | He was tired because he had been running. |
| I had been writing three emails. | I had written three emails. |
| She was tired because she studied all night. (unclear order) | She was tired because she had been studying all night. |
