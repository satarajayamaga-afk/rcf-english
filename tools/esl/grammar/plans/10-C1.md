# Reporting in Academic Writing (stance verbs, tense choice and nominalised reports)

*Lesson theme: Writing a Literature Review*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |

## Language analysis

### Reporting verbs and stance

| **Writer's attitude** | **Verbs** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Neutral | state, report, note, observe, describe, find, explain | Perera (2021) reports that 60% of students ... |
| Tentative (the source is cautious) | suggest, propose, speculate, imply, hypothesise | Fernando (2019) suggests that screen time may affect sleep. |
| Strong (the source is confident) | argue, claim, contend, maintain, assert, insist | Silva (2020) argues that homework should be abolished. |
| Writer agrees / source is right | show, demonstrate, establish, confirm, point out | The study demonstrates that ... (the writer accepts it) |
| Writer doubts | claim, allege, assert (in context) | Some commentators claim that ... (the writer may not agree) |
| Concession | acknowledge, concede, admit, recognise | Silva acknowledges that the data are limited. |
| Disagreement | dispute, challenge, question, reject, refute | Jayasinghe (2022) disputes this conclusion. |

### Patterns

- verb + *that*-clause: *Silva argues that ...*
- verb + noun phrase: *Jayasinghe challenges this view.* / *disputes the findings*.
- *as* + subject + verb: *As Perera (2021) notes, ...*
- *According to* + source: *According to Silva (2020), ...* (do not combine with *argues*: not *According to Silva, he argues*).
- Nominalised reports: *the claim / argument / assertion / finding / suggestion + that*-clause: *Silva's claim that homework is harmful has been widely criticised.*

### Tense choice

- **Present simple**: the most common; the source's view is presented as current: *Silva argues ...*
- **Past simple**: a specific study or event: *Perera (2021) found that ...*
- **Present perfect**: a research area or several studies: *Several studies have shown ...*
- **No backshift** in the reported clause when the content is still considered true: *Perera found that sleep* ***is*** *essential for memory.*

### Pronunciation

- Not a focus; when reading aloud, the author's name and date are often said with a slight pause: *Silva, 2020, argues ...*

### Concept checking questions

*Silva (2020) claims that homework is harmful.*

1. Does the writer definitely agree with Silva? (Not necessarily; *claims* can show doubt.)
2. Would *demonstrates* show more or less agreement? (More.)

*Perera found that sleep is essential for memory.*

1. Why *is* and not *was*? (Because it is still considered true.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Over-using said / says. | Verb bank grouped by stance. |
| Automatic backshift for current truths. | Contrast found that sleep was essential (in that study only) and is essential (generally true). |
| According to X, he argues ... | Board the two patterns separately. |
| Wrong pattern (Silva discusses about, argues that homework to be). | Pattern column in the verb bank. |
| Choosing a verb with the wrong stance (demonstrates for a weak claim). | Analysis task focusing on writer's attitude. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To raise awareness of stance in reporting. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Board three sentences: *Silva says that homework is harmful. / Silva claims that homework is harmful. / Silva demonstrates that homework is harmful.*
2. Ask: *"Which sentence shows that the writer agrees with Silva? Which shows doubt?"* Pairs discuss (2 minutes). Elicit the differences.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present academic reporting structures in context and clarify their stance, patterns and tense choices. **Interaction:** Pairs, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading and comparison (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the two versions of the same literature review paragraph. Which is better for a university essay? Why?"*
   - ICQs: *"How many versions?"* (Two.) *"What do you decide?"* (Which is better and why.)
2. Pairs compare. Elicit: Version B uses varied verbs, shows the writer's view, connects the sources.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the reporting structures in Version B and complete the table."*
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the verb bank by stance on the left of the board.
2. **Patterns.** Show *argues that, disputes this view, As Perera notes, According to ...* Board *According to Silva, she argues* ✗.
3. **Tense choice.** Ask: *"Why does the writer use found for Perera but argues for Silva?"* Elicit present for current views, past for specific studies, present perfect for research areas.
4. **No backshift.** Contrast *Perera found that sleep was essential* (only in her study) and *is essential* (generally accepted). Ask the CCQs.
5. **Nominalised reports.** Highlight *Silva's claim that ...* and *this view*; show how they connect ideas across sentences.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the structures accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the most appropriate verb and form. Exercise B: rewrite using the word given. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check and justify their choices (stance and tense). Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To write a literature review paragraph that reports and evaluates sources. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Say: *"Read the three source summaries. Write a literature review paragraph (about 150 words) on whether school should start later. Report all three sources, show your view of each, and connect them. Use at least six different reporting structures."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you just list the sources?"* (No, connect them and show your view.) *"How many structures?"* (At least six.)
2. Pairs write (11 minutes).
3. Pairs swap with another pair and identify the writer's attitude to each source from the verbs alone (4 minutes).
4. Two pairs read their paragraphs aloud (3 minutes).
5. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board strong sentences and two or three with errors. Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which verb would you choose to report a source you disagree with? A source you trust?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Find three short sources (articles, reports or studies) on a topic of your choice. Write a 250-word literature review paragraph that reports, compares and evaluates them, using at least eight different reporting structures.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the verb bank with patterns on a card.
- Provide a paragraph frame: *Several studies have ... · X (year) argues that ... · However, Y disputes ... · As Z notes, ... · Overall, ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *concede, contend* and *refute* accurately and add evaluative adverbs: *X convincingly argues ..., Y rightly points out ...*
- They use passive reporting: *It has been argued that ...*

## Assessment and success criteria

- The paragraph uses at least six different reporting structures accurately.
- Verb choice reflects a clear stance towards each source.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students choose verbs that matched their attitude to each source?
- Did they avoid automatic backshift?

# Handout 1: Two Literature Review Paragraphs

**Version A**

Silva (2020) said that homework was harmful for children. Perera (2021) said that students who slept more did better in exams. Jayasinghe (2022) said that Silva was wrong. Fernando (2019) said that phones affected sleep. All these writers said interesting things about homework and sleep.

**Version B**

The relationship between homework, sleep and academic performance has been widely debated. Silva (2020) argues that homework is harmful for younger children, although she acknowledges that her sample was small. Jayasinghe (2022) disputes this view, contending that moderate homework improves self-discipline. Silva's claim that homework reduces sleep is, however, supported by Perera (2021), who found that students who sleep more than eight hours perform significantly better in examinations. As Fernando (2019) suggests, the effect of homework on sleep may be less important than that of late-night phone use.

**Which version is better for a university essay? Give three reasons.**

# Answer Key

**Handout 1 (possible reasons)**

Version B uses varied reporting verbs; it shows the writer's attitude to each source; it connects sources (*disputes this view, is supported by*); it uses appropriate tenses; it avoids repetition and automatic backshift.

**Handout 2**

- Present perfect: *has been widely debated* · Strong claim: *argues that homework is harmful*; *contending that ...* · Concession: *acknowledges that her sample was small* · Disagreement: *disputes this view* · Nominalised report: *Silva's claim that homework reduces sleep* · Past simple: *Perera (2021) ... found that* · As + source: *As Fernando (2019) suggests* · Tentative: *suggests ... may be*
   1. Because the finding is presented as still true (no backshift). · 2. With Silva's claim about sleep, partly, because it is *supported by* Perera; the writer reports Jayasinghe neutrally. · 3. Sources are connected and evaluated, not listed.

**Handout 3, Exercise A**

1. Silva claims that homework is harmful. · 2. Perera's study demonstrates that sleep improves exam results. · 3. Jayasinghe disputes Silva's view / conclusion. · 4. Silva's argument that homework is harmful has been criticised.

**Handout 4 (model paragraph)**

Whether schools should start later has attracted growing attention. Gunawardena (2021) found that students who began after 8.30 am slept 45 minutes longer and achieved higher grades, although she acknowledges that the data were self-reported. These findings are partly confirmed by Lim (2022), whose experiment showed improved attention in class; however, Lim found no effect on examination results and suggests that further research is needed. In contrast, Hettiarachchi (2019) argues that a later start would cause traffic problems and reduce time for sport. This claim, however, is not supported by data. Overall, the evidence suggests that a later start benefits sleep and attention, but its effect on academic results remains unclear.

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| According to Silva, she argues that ... | According to Silva, ... / Silva argues that ... |
| Perera found that sleep was essential. (generally true) | Perera found that sleep is essential. |
| Dias discusses about the results. | Dias discusses the results. |
| Silva demonstrates that ... (for an unproven opinion) | Silva argues / claims that ... |
