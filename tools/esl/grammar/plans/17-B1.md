# -ed and -ing Adjectives with Adverbs of Degree (bored / boring, quite, really, extremely)

*Lesson theme: Films, Trips and Experiences*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Adjectives and Adverbs |
| Grammar point | -ed and -ing Adjectives with Adverbs of Degree (bored / boring, quite, really, extremely) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use adjectives and adverbs of manner accurately. They confuse -ed and -ing adjectives (I am boring in maths class, The film was very interested) and use very for everything, including with a bit and enough in the wrong positions. |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise -ed adjectives (how a person feels) and -ing adjectives (what causes the feeling), with adverbs of degree (a bit, quite, fairly, very, really, extremely). |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise the pronunciation of -ed endings (/d/, /t/, /ɪd/); to develop speaking through a "find someone who" and an experience-sharing task. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can describe experiences and feelings accurately, e.g. The film was really boring, so I was bored. I was extremely frightened on the bus because the road was terrifying. The museum was quite interesting. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (two short reviews), Handout 2 (language focus), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (experience cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form and meaning

| **-ed (how a person feels)** | **-ing (the thing / person that causes the feeling)** |
| :-- | :-- |
| I was bored. | The lesson was boring. |
| She's interested in history. | History is interesting. |
| We were tired after the walk. | The walk was tiring. |
| He was frightened. | The film was frightening. |
| I felt embarrassed. | My mistake was embarrassing. |

- Common pairs: *amazed / amazing, annoyed / annoying, confused / confusing, disappointed / disappointing, excited / exciting, relaxed / relaxing, shocked / shocking, surprised / surprising, worried / worrying*.
- A person can be *-ing*: *He's boring* (= he makes other people bored). Careful: *I'm boring* means "I make others bored".

### Adverbs of degree

| **weaker → stronger** |
| :-- |
| a bit / a little → fairly → quite → very / really → extremely |

- *a bit* is mostly used with negative adjectives: *a bit boring, a bit tired* (not *a bit amazing*).
- *really* works with all adjectives: *really interesting, really amazing*.
- *quite* in British English often means "fairly, but not very": *The film was quite good* (= OK).

### Pronunciation

- *-ed* endings: /d/ *bored, worried*; /t/ *relaxed, shocked*; /ɪd/ *interested, excited, disappointed* (after /t/ or /d/).
- Stress: *INteresting* (3 syllables: /ˈɪntrəstɪŋ/).

### Concept checking questions

*I was bored.*

1. Is this about my feeling or about the lesson? (My feeling.)

*The lesson was boring.*

1. Did the lesson make me bored? (Yes.)

*I'm boring.*

1. Do other people feel bored with me? (Yes!) Is this what you want to say in class? (Probably not.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| I am boring (meaning bored). | Arrow diagram: thing (-ing) → person (-ed). |
| The film was very interested. | Ask: "Can a film feel?" (No → -ing.) |
| /ɪd/ added to every ending (relax-id). | Three-column pronunciation sort. |
| a bit with positive adjectives. | Board: a bit + negative adjective. |
| Overuse of very. | Scale on the board; challenge students to vary. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students with the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Ask: *"What was the last film you watched or the last trip you went on? Was it good?"*
2. Pairs share (2 minutes). Board two sentences exactly as students say them.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present -ed and -ing adjectives in context and clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the two short reviews of a trip to Horton Plains. Did they both enjoy it? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Find the -ed and -ing adjectives in the reviews. Who or what do they describe? Answer the questions."*
   - ICQs: *"How many reviews?"* (Two.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Elicit answers. Draw the arrow diagram: *the walk (tiring) → me (tired)*.
2. Ask the CCQs, including *I'm boring*.
3. Build the scale of degree adverbs. Highlight *a bit* + negative.
4. **Pronunciation.** Students sort *bored, relaxed, excited, worried, shocked, disappointed* into /d/, /t/, /ɪd/. Drill.
5. Return to the Stage 1 sentences and correct or improve them.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct adjective. Exercise B: complete with an adverb from the scale and an adjective. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the adjectives fluently to talk about experiences. **Interaction:** Groups of three, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. In groups of three, students take an experience card (*the first day at a new school, a long bus journey, a wedding, an exam*). They talk for one minute about a real or imagined experience, using at least four adjectives and two degree adverbs: *"The journey was extremely tiring and I was really bored ..."* (10 minutes, rotating).
   - ICQs: *"How long do you talk?"* (One minute.) *"How many adjectives?"* (At least four.)
2. Listeners ask one follow-up question each and tick the adjectives they hear.
3. **Report (5 minutes).** Each group tells the class the most surprising experience they heard.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct them.
2. Quick fire: say *"the exam"* or *"me after the exam"*; students say an *-ing* or *-ed* adjective.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a short review (120–150 words) of a film, book, trip or event. Use at least six *-ed / -ing* adjectives and four different degree adverbs.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the arrow diagram and the list of pairs.
- Let them prepare their one-minute talk in note form first.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use ungradable adjectives with *absolutely*: *absolutely terrifying, absolutely exhausted*.
- They use *so / such*: *It was so boring that I fell asleep.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 3.
- In the talks, students use *-ed* for feelings and *-ing* for causes.
- Students use at least two different degree adverbs.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the arrow diagram clear up the confusion?
- Were students' -ed endings pronounced correctly?
