GRAMMAR TOPIC: PERFECT TENSES · B2 (Upper-intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Future Perfect and Future Continuous

*Lesson theme: My Life in Five Years*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Perfect Tenses |
| Grammar point | Future Perfect and Future Continuous |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who use will and going to confidently but rarely use the future perfect or future continuous, and say By 2030 I will graduate. |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of the future perfect (simple and continuous) and the future continuous to talk about achievements and activities at future points in time. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in talking about plans and predictions; to practise contracted will have /ˈwɪləv/. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can describe their life at points 1, 5 and 10 years from now, e.g. By 2030, I'll have graduated. This time next year, I'll be living in Melbourne. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (university brochure), Handout 2 (language reference and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (life timeline and interview questions). |

## Language analysis

### Form and meaning

| **Form** | **Structure** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Future perfect simple | will have + past participle | An action completed before a future point | By 2030, I will have graduated. |
| Future perfect continuous | will have been + verb-ing | Duration up to a future point | By June, I'll have been working here for five years. |
| Future continuous | will be + verb-ing | An action in progress at a future moment | This time next year, I'll be living abroad. |

Time expressions: *by (2030), by the time + present simple, this time next week / year, in five years' time, at 9 pm tomorrow*.

### Accuracy points

- *By the time you* ***arrive,*** (present simple after time conjunctions, not *will arrive*).
- *by* = not later than; *until* = continuing up to.
- The future continuous is also used for polite questions about plans: *Will you be using the car tonight?*

### Pronunciation

- *I'll have* /aɪl əv/; *will have been* /wɪl əv bɪn/. Students should hear the contracted forms.

### Concept checking questions

*By 2030, I will have graduated.*

1. Will I graduate before 2030 or after? (Before, or by 2030.)
2. In 2030, will graduation be finished? (Yes.)

*This time next year, I'll be living in Melbourne.*

1. Will I be in Melbourne next year at this time? (Yes.)
2. Is the living finished? (No, it's in progress.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Using will + base verb instead of the future perfect (By 2030 I will graduate). | Timeline with by marked; CCQs. |
| will after time conjunctions (By the time you will arrive). | Board the rule; include several items in the practice. |
| Not hearing have in fast speech. | Listening and drilling of contracted forms. |
| Students' timelines are too short. | Give an example timeline; encourage both personal and world predictions. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and introduce the theme. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"Imagine it's five years from now. Where are you? What's your life like?"*
2. Students tell a partner (2 minutes). Don't correct.
3. Take two or three ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the three future forms in context and clarify their meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read about the course. What will graduates have done by the end? What will they be doing in their final year? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Draw three timelines on the board (see Board plan) without labels. Say: *"Underline the future forms in the brochure. Match each one to a timeline."* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. Label each timeline with its form and an example from the brochure. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
2. Highlight *by* (= not later than) and *by the time* + present simple. Board *By the time you will arrive* and ask what's wrong.
3. Show the future continuous for polite questions: *Will you be using the car tonight?*
4. **Pronunciation.** Drill contracted forms: *By 2030, I'll have graduated. This time next year, I'll be living abroad. By June, I'll have been working here for five years.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise choosing and forming the three forms accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the reference box. Do Exercise A: choose the best form. Then complete Exercise B about you. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check Exercise A as a class; two students read their Exercise B sentences.
3. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the forms freely in personalised and group speaking. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, groups

1. Give out Handout 4. Students complete their personal timeline for 1, 5 and 10 years from now (4 minutes).
2. **Interviews (7 minutes).** Pairs interview each other with the questions: *"By the time you're 30, what will you have achieved? What will you be doing this time next year?"*
   - ICQs: *"Do you talk about your own timeline or your partner's?"* (Both: we ask and answer.)
3. **World predictions (7 minutes).** Groups make three predictions about technology and the world in 2050 using all three forms, and present the boldest one to the class.
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which form was most useful today?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write a letter to your future self (180 words) to be opened in ten years, using all three forms.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide the three timelines on a card during the freer practice.
- Give sentence frames: *By ___, I'll have ___. This time next ___, I'll be ___ing.*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add hedging: *I'll probably have ..., I doubt I'll have ..., I might be ...*
- They use the future continuous for polite enquiries: *Will you be coming to the meeting?*

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students use the future perfect with *by* correctly in the interviews.
- They use the present simple after *by the time / when* in future clauses.
- 7 out of 8 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.

## Teacher's reflection

- Which form did students find hardest?
- Did the timelines help students understand the difference?
