# Present Continuous vs Present Simple

*Lesson theme: What Are They Doing Now?*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Present Tenses |
| Grammar point | Present Continuous vs Present Simple |
| Level | A2 (Elementary) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Elementary students who can use the present simple for routines and know common action verbs. Most have met the present continuous but confuse it with the present simple and often leave out be. |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise the present continuous for actions happening now, contrasted with the present simple for routines. |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in describing scenes; to practise reading for specific information (text messages); to practise -ing spelling rules. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can describe what people are doing at the moment and contrast it with what they usually do, e.g. He usually walks to work, but today he's taking a taxi. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (text messages), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3A and 3B (café scene cards for the information gap), a busy photo for the warm-up (any market or street photo projected or printed, or a board sketch). |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Positive** | **Negative** | **Question** | **Short answer** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| I | I'm (am) cooking. | I'm not cooking. | Am I cooking? | Yes, I am. / No, I'm not. |
| He / She / It | She's (is) cooking. | She isn't cooking. | Is she cooking? | Yes, she is. / No, she isn't. |
| You / We / They | They're (are) cooking. | They aren't cooking. | Are they cooking? | Yes, they are. / No, they aren't. |

Spelling of *-ing*:

- Most verbs add *-ing*: read → reading, play → playing.
- Verbs ending in *-e* drop the *e*: make → making, write → writing.
- One vowel + one consonant (stressed) doubles the consonant: sit → sitting, run → running, swim → swimming.
- *-ie* → *-ying*: lie → lying, die → dying.

### Meaning and use

- **Present continuous:** actions happening at the moment of speaking or around now: *Look! It's raining. I'm reading a good book this week.*
- **Present simple:** routines, habits and permanent facts: *I take the bus every day.*
- **Contrast:** *He usually walks to work, but today he's taking a taxi.*
- Time words: *now, right now, at the moment, today, this week, Look! Listen!* vs *every day, usually, always, often, on Mondays*.

### Pronunciation

- Contractions are normal in speech: *I'm /aɪm/, she's /ʃiːz/, they're /ðeə/*.
- *-ing* is pronounced /ɪŋ/ with no hard /g/ at the end.
- In questions, *are* is weak: *What are you doing?* /wɒt ə jə ˈduːɪŋ/.

### Concept checking questions

For *Ravi is taking a taxi today.*

1. Is Ravi in the taxi now? (Yes.)
2. Does he take a taxi every day? (No.)
3. Is this a habit? (No, it's just today / now.)

For *Ravi usually walks to work.*

1. Does he walk most days? (Yes.)
2. Is he walking now? (We don't know / not necessarily.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Missing be (He reading a book). | Board the three parts in separate boxes: subject + be + verb-ing. Use a finger-counting gesture: three fingers for He – is – reading. |
| Using the continuous for routines (I am going to school every day). | Use the timeline contrast and highlight time words. Students sort time words into two columns. |
| Spelling errors (siting, makeing). | Quick spelling race on the board in the presentation; add the spelling rules box to Handout 2. |
| Students run out of ideas in the café task. | Provide the prompt questions on the board: Who? What are they doing? Where? |
| Info-gap students look at each other's cards. | Seat pairs back to back or ask them to hold the card up close and not show it. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To create interest in the topic and get students talking about actions in a scene. **Interaction:** T → Ss, then pairs

1. Show a busy market or street photo (or sketch a simple scene on the board: a man on a phone, a girl running, a woman buying fruit). Say: *"Look at this photo for 30 seconds."*
2. Hide the photo. Ask: *"How many people can you remember? What are they doing?"* Accept any answers; do not correct grammar yet.
3. Ask: *"What do you usually do on Saturday morning?"* Students tell a partner in one minute.

**Transition:** *"Let's read some messages. What are these people doing right now?"*

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the present continuous in context and clarify its meaning, form and pronunciation, contrasted with the present simple. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. **Reading for gist:** *"Read the four messages. Where is each person? Write the place. Two minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many messages?"* (Four.) *"What do you write?"* (The place.)
2. Students check in pairs, then check as a class.
3. **Reading for detail:** students answer the three *Find and write* questions. Check answers.
4. **Noticing:** *"Underline the verbs that end in -ing."* Elicit: *I'm sitting, It's raining, We're having, They're cleaning ...*
5. **Meaning.** Write on the board: *Dilini usually walks to school. Today she's sitting on the bus.* Draw a timeline: crosses for every day, a wavy line at NOW. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
6. **Form.** Elicit the three parts: subject + am / is / are + verb-ing. Elicit the negative (*aren't, isn't*) and the question (*Is she ...? Are they ...?*) from the messages. Write the form table on the board.
7. **Spelling.** Write *make, sit, lie, play* on the board. Students come to the board and write the *-ing* forms. Elicit the rules.
8. **Pronunciation.** Drill chorally and individually with contractions: *I'm sitting on the bus. She's taking a taxi. What are you doing?* Model the weak form of *are*.

**Transition:** *"Now let's practise."*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give accurate written practice of the form and the contrast with the present simple. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: put the time words in the right column. Exercise B: circle the correct form. Exercise C: write the -ing form. Seven minutes. Work alone."*
   - ICQs: *"In B, do you write or circle?"* (Circle.) *"Alone or with a partner?"* (Alone, then check.)
2. Monitor and help weaker students by pointing to the time words.
3. Pairs compare. Nominate students to read answers aloud and explain choices: *"Every day, so present simple."*

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the target language fluently in a communicative information-gap task and personalised speaking. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups of three

1. **Information gap (10 minutes).** Give out Handout 3A to Student A and Handout 3B to Student B. Say: *"This is a café. You have eight people on your card. Your partner has a different card. Don't show your partner! Tell your partner what the people are doing. Find six differences."*
   - ICQs: *"Can you show your card?"* (No.) *"How many differences?"* (Six.)
2. Monitor and note errors (missing *be*, wrong tense) and good sentences.
3. Check the differences as a class.
4. **Personalised speaking (8 minutes).** Write on the board: *What are you doing this week that is different from usual?* Model: *"I usually drink coffee, but this week I'm drinking green tea."* In groups of three, students tell each other two things they usually do and one thing they're doing differently this week. Each group reports one sentence about a member to the class.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback on language from the freer practice and consolidate learning. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences heard during Stage 4: two correct, two with errors. Pairs decide which are correct and fix the others.
2. Board one or two good sentences and praise them.
3. Quick review: say a time word; students say *present simple* or *present continuous*.
4. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Take or find a photo of a busy place. Write 6 sentences about what is happening, and 2 sentences about what usually happens there. *Example: A man is selling mangoes. Two boys are playing. On Sundays, this market is usually very busy.*

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students a verb list for the café task: *carry, drink, read, talk, eat, write, sleep, wait, pay, laugh*.
- Let them write two sentences before speaking in the personalised task.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add a reason or a contrast: *She's drinking tea because she usually doesn't sleep well after coffee.*
- They write a short message describing what they are doing right now, including one contrast with their routine.

## Assessment and success criteria

Students are successful if they:

- use *be + verb-ing* with the correct form of *be* in most sentences in the café task;
- choose the present simple for routines and the continuous for now in Handout 2 (8 out of 10 or better);
- find all six differences using accurate sentences.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use *be* consistently by the end of the lesson?
- Which stage took longer than planned?
- What will I review next lesson?
