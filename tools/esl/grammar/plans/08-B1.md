GRAMMAR TOPIC: CONDITIONALS · B1 (Intermediate) · 60 minutes

# Second Conditional (If + past, would)

*Lesson theme: What Would You Do?*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Conditionals |
| Grammar point | Second Conditional (If + past, would) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use the first conditional well. They talk about imaginary situations with will (If I win the lottery, I will buy a house) and use would in both clauses (If I would have money, I would travel). |
| Main aim | To help students work out and use the second conditional (If + past simple, would / could + base verb) for imaginary or unlikely present and future situations, and to contrast it with the first conditional. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise If I were you for advice; to develop fluency in discussing moral dilemmas; to practise contracted 'd. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can discuss imaginary situations and dilemmas, e.g. If I found a wallet in the street, I'd take it to the police. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (magazine quiz), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (dilemma cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **If clause** | **Main clause** |
| :-- | :-- |
| If + past simple | would / could + base verb |
| If I won the lottery, | I'd buy a house for my parents. |
| If I didn't have homework, | I could go out tonight. |
| If I were you, | I'd talk to the teacher. |

- Question: *What would you do if you found a wallet?*
- *were* is used for all persons in formal English and in *If I were you*: *If she were here, she'd help.* (*was* is common in informal speech.)
- *'d* = *would*: *I'd* /aɪd/, *she'd* /ʃiːd/.

### Meaning and use

- Imaginary or unlikely situations in the present or future. The past form does **not** mean past time.
- *If I were you, I'd ...* = advice.
- Compare:

| **First conditional** | **Second conditional** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Real, possible: If I pass the exam, I'll celebrate. | Imaginary or unlikely: If I won a million rupees, I'd travel the world. |
| Speaker thinks it's likely. | Speaker thinks it's unlikely or impossible. |

### Pronunciation

- Contracted *'d* is short and easy to miss: *I'd go* /aɪd ɡəʊ/. Drill so students hear it.
- *wouldn't* /ˈwʊdnt/.

### Concept checking questions

*If I won the lottery, I'd buy a house.*

1. Have I won the lottery? (No.)
2. Do I think I will win? (No, probably not.)
3. Is *won* about the past? (No, it's about an imaginary present or future.)

*If I were you, I'd talk to the teacher.*

1. Am I you? (No, it's impossible.)
2. What am I doing? (Giving advice.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| would in the if clause (If I would have money). | Board the pattern; cross out would in the if clause. |
| Using will for imaginary situations. | Contrast likely vs unlikely with a percentage line (80% vs 5%). |
| Thinking the past form means past time. | CCQs; timeline showing NOW and FUTURE only. |
| If I was you in writing. | Teach If I were you as a fixed phrase. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce imaginary situations and engage students. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Write on the board: *a million rupees · invisible for a day · any job in the world*.
2. Say: *"Imagine! Tell your partner what you'd do."* Students talk for 2 minutes. Don't correct.
3. Take two or three answers and board one sentence exactly as a student says it.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the second conditional in context and help students work out its form and meaning. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Do the magazine quiz. Choose a, b or c for each question. Then read your result."* (4 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"Do you choose one answer or two?"* (One.)
2. Pairs compare answers and results. Ask: *"Are you an angel, a realist or a rebel?"*

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences from the quiz. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Alone or with a partner?"* (With a partner.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"Have you found a wallet? Is it real or imaginary?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit the answers. Build the form in the centre of the board: *If + past simple, would / could + base verb*.
2. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis. Draw a timeline with NOW and FUTURE; write *won* under it to show it isn't past time.
3. **First vs second.** Board *If I pass the exam, I'll celebrate* and *If I won a million rupees, I'd travel the world.* Ask: *"Which one is likely?"* Draw a line from 0% to 100% and place each sentence.
4. **If I were you.** Board it and ask: *"Is this advice or a story?"* (Advice.)
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *I'd* and question intonation: *What would you do if you found a wallet? I'd take it to the police.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form and the contrast accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: complete the sentences. Exercise B: first or second conditional? Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare and justify: *"It's unlikely, so second."*
3. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the second conditional fluently in a discussion of dilemmas. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then whole class

1. Give each group a set of dilemma cards (Handout 4). Model with one card: *"If my best friend copied my homework, I'd ... because ..."*
2. Students take turns to read a card. Each person says what they would do and why. The group decides on the best answer (12 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Does everyone answer each card?"* (Yes.) *"Do you give a reason?"* (Yes.)
3. Each group reports the dilemma they disagreed about most (4 minutes).
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Return to the sentence you boarded in Stage 1. Ask: *"Is it correct now?"* Students correct it if necessary.
2. Board four sentences from Stage 4 (two with errors). Students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a paragraph (120 words): *If I could change one thing about my town, I would ...* Use at least five second conditional sentences.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students sentence starters on the dilemma cards: *If this happened to me, I'd ... · I wouldn't ...*
- Let them answer with the main clause only at first.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students ask follow-up questions: *What would you do if your friend got angry?*
- They use *might* and *could* in the main clause to show different degrees of certainty.

## Assessment and success criteria

- In the discussion, students use the past simple after *if* and *would* in the main clause.
- 9 out of 11 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- Students correctly choose first or second conditional in 4 of 5 items in Exercise B.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students understand that the past form doesn't mean past time?
- Were the dilemmas engaging? Did students give reasons?

# Handout 1: Magazine Quiz

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**ANGEL, REALIST OR REBEL? Do our quiz and find out!**

**1. If you found a wallet with 20,000 rupees in the street, what would you do?** a. I'd take it to the police. b. I'd look for the owner's ID and call them. c. I'd keep the money. Finders keepers!

**2. If your friend gave you a horrible birthday present, what would you say?** a. I'd say, "Thank you! I love it!" b. I'd say thank you, but I wouldn't use it. c. I'd tell them the truth.

**3. If you could be invisible for a day, what would you do?** a. I'd help people secretly. b. I'd go to a concert without a ticket. c. I'd listen to my teachers' conversations!

**4. If a shop assistant gave you too much change, what would you do?** a. I'd give it back immediately. b. I'd give it back if it was a lot of money. c. I wouldn't say anything.

**Results:** Mostly a: You're an angel! · Mostly b: You're a realist. · Mostly c: You're a rebel!

**Answer the questions with your partner.**

4. What comes in the other part of the sentence? ______________ + base verb
5. What does *'d* mean in sentence 3? ______________
6. Look at these two sentences. Which one is more likely? Why?
   - *If I pass the exam, I'll celebrate.*
   - *If I won the lottery, I'd buy a house.*

# Handout 3: Practice

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**A. Complete with the correct form of the verbs.**

1. If I ______________ (have) a car, I ______________ (drive) to school.
2. If she ______________ (live) near the beach, she ______________ (swim) every day.
3. What ______________ you ______________ (do) if you ______________ (see) a snake in your room?
4. If I ______________ (be) you, I ______________ (apologise).
5. They ______________ (not / be) so tired if they ______________ (go) to bed earlier.

**B. First or second conditional? Complete the sentences.**

1. If it ______________ (rain) tomorrow, we ______________ (stay) at home. *(It's quite possible.)*
2. If I ______________ (meet) the President, I ______________ (ask) him for a selfie. *(Very unlikely!)*
3. If you ______________ (hurry), you ______________ (catch) the bus. *(It's possible.)*
4. If humans ______________ (have) wings, we ______________ (not / need) cars. *(Impossible.)*
5. If my mum ______________ (find out), she ______________ (be) angry. *(She probably will!)*

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| If I would have money, I would travel. | If I had money, I would travel. |
| If I win the lottery, I will buy a castle. (imaginary) | If I won the lottery, I would buy a castle. |
| If I was you, I'd go. (formal writing) | If I were you, I'd go. |
| If I had a car, I drive to school. | If I had a car, I would drive to school. |
