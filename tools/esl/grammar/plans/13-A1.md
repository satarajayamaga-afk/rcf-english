# Like, Love, Hate and Don't Mind + -ing

*Lesson theme: Free Time and Hobbies*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Gerunds and Infinitives |
| Grammar point | Like, Love, Hate and Don't Mind + -ing |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | High beginners who know the present simple and common activity verbs (swim, dance, read, cook, play). They say I like swim or I like to swimming and confuse the -ing spelling rules. |
| Main aim | To present and practise love, like, don't mind, don't like, hate + verb-ing to talk about free-time activities. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise -ing spelling rules; to develop speaking through a class survey; to review Do you like ...? questions and short answers. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can say and write 6 sentences about their likes and dislikes, e.g. I love dancing. I don't mind cooking. I hate getting up early. |
| Materials | Activity picture cards (swimming, dancing, reading, cooking, playing cricket, watching TV, shopping, getting up early), a "feelings line" drawn on the board, Handout 1 (pen-friend profiles), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (class survey). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Feeling** | **Verb** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| ♥♥ | love + -ing | I love swimming. |
| ♥ | like + -ing | She likes reading. |
| ~ | don't mind + -ing | I don't mind cooking. |
| ✗ | don't like + -ing | He doesn't like shopping. |
| ✗✗ | hate + -ing | I hate getting up early. |

- **verb + verb-ing**: *I like swimming.* (not *I like swim*)
- Question: *Do you like dancing? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.* *Does she like cooking?*
- *-ing* spelling: *read → reading; dance → dancing* (drop -e); *swim → swimming, run → running* (double the consonant); *lie → lying*.

### Meaning and use

- We use these verbs + *-ing* to talk about activities we enjoy or don't enjoy in general.
- *don't mind* = it's OK; I don't love it and I don't hate it.

### Pronunciation

- *-ing* /ɪŋ/ (no /g/ sound at the end): *swimming* /ˈswɪmɪŋ/.
- *don't mind* /dəʊnt maɪnd/.

### Concept checking questions

*I don't mind cooking.*

1. Do I love cooking? (No.)
2. Do I hate cooking? (No.) So? (It's OK.)

*She hates getting up early.*

1. Is she happy at 5 am? (No!)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Base verb after like (I like swim). | Board like + -ing; drill with picture cards. |
| to + -ing (I like to swimming). | Cross out to on the board. |
| Spelling (swiming, danceing). | Spelling rules table; quick spelling race. |
| -s for he / she (She like reading). | Highlight likes, doesn't like. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and activate vocabulary. **Interaction:** T → Ss, whole class

1. Mime activities (swimming, dancing, cooking, reading). Students guess the verb. Board the base forms on the left.
2. Ask: *"What do you do on Saturdays?"* Take a few answers.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Part A: Context (8 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To present the target language in context. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the pen-friend profiles. Who would be a good friend for you?"* (4 minutes.)
   - ICQs: *"How many profiles?"* (Three.)
2. Students answer the true / false questions. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)**

**Stage aim:** To clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. **Meaning.** Draw the feelings line on the board (♥♥ to ✗✗). Elicit *love, like, don't mind, don't like, hate* from the profiles and put them on the line. Ask the CCQs for *don't mind*.
2. **Form.** Board *I love swimming.* Underline *swimming*. Ask: *"After love, what form?"* (verb + -ing.) Board *I like swim* ✗.
3. **Spelling.** Elicit the *-ing* forms from the profiles and build the spelling column.
4. **Questions.** Board *Do you like dancing? Does she like cooking?* Elicit short answers.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill: *I love swimming. I don't mind cooking. Do you like dancing? Yes, I do.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: write the -ing form. Exercise B: write sentences. Six minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Do you write swim or swimming?"* (Swimming.)
2. Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers.
3. **Picture drill:** hold up a card and a face (☺ or ☹); students make a sentence: *"He hates shopping!"*

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language in a personalised class survey. **Interaction:** Mingle, then pairs, then individual

1. Give out Handout 3. Model: *"Do you like playing cricket?" "Yes, I love it!"* Students mingle and ask classmates, writing names in the table (8 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Do you ask one person or many people?"* (Many people.)
2. **Report (5 minutes).** Pairs share their findings: *"Ravi loves playing cricket. Nimmi hates getting up early."*
3. **Writing (5 minutes).** Students write 6 sentences about themselves and their classmates at the bottom of Handout 3.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Ask: *"What is the most popular activity in our class? The least popular?"*
2. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a pen-friend profile (60 words) about yourself. Say what you love, like, don't mind, don't like and hate doing.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the spelling rules on a card.
- Allow them to use only *like* and *don't like*.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *because*: *I love swimming because it's relaxing.*
- They use *enjoy* and *can't stand*: *I can't stand waiting.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- Students use verb + *-ing* after *love / like / hate* in the survey.
- Students use *don't mind* with the correct meaning.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students use *-ing* after the verbs consistently?
- Which spelling rules caused problems?
