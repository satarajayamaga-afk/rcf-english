# Advanced Comparative Structures (the more ... the more, -er and -er, nowhere near as)

*Lesson theme: Life Then and Now*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Comparatives and Superlatives |
| Grammar point | Advanced Comparative Structures (the more ... the more, -er and -er, nowhere near as) |
| Level | B2 (Upper-intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Upper-intermediate students who use comparatives, superlatives, as ... as and modifiers accurately. They don't use double comparatives (the more ... the more) or progressive comparatives (more and more), and they compare adverbs inaccurately (He drives more careful). |
| Main aim | To develop students' use of double comparatives (the + comparative ..., the + comparative), progressive comparatives (-er and -er, more and more), comparative adverbs, and emphatic structures (nowhere near / nothing like as ... as, considerably, a great deal). |
| Subsidiary aims | To develop fluency in discussing social change; to practise the rhythm of double comparatives. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can discuss how life has changed over three generations, e.g. Life is getting faster and faster. The more technology we have, the less time we seem to have. Our grandparents' lives were nowhere near as stressful. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (interview: three generations), Handout 2 (language reference and practice), Handout 3 (further practice), Handout 4 (discussion cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Structure** | **Pattern** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Double comparative | the + comparative (+ clause), the + comparative (+ clause) | The more you practise, the better you get. The sooner, the better. |
| Progressive comparative | comparative + and + comparative | Prices are getting higher and higher. Life is becoming more and more stressful. |
| Comparative adverbs | -er / more + adverb (+ than) | He drives more carefully than his father. She works harder than anyone. |
| Emphatic (big difference) | nowhere near / nothing like + as ... as; considerably / a great deal / way (informal) + comparative | Life was nowhere near as busy. Phones are considerably cheaper now. |
| Emphatic (small difference) | marginally / fractionally + comparative | Rents are marginally lower this year. |
| Similarity / difference | the same as, similar to, different from, like | My life is completely different from my grandmother's. |

### Meaning and use

- Double comparatives show that two changes are connected: when one increases or decreases, the other changes too.
- Progressive comparatives show continuous change over time.
- Emphatic structures make comparisons stronger or more precise.

### Pronunciation

- Double comparatives have a balanced rhythm with stress on both comparatives and a slight pause in the middle: *The MORE you practise, | the BETter you get.*
- *more and more* /ˌmɔːr ən ˈmɔː/.

### Concept checking questions

*The more you practise, the better you get.*

1. Are practising and getting better connected? (Yes.)
2. If you practise a little, do you get much better? (No.)

*Prices are getting higher and higher.*

1. Is this a single change or a continuous change? (Continuous.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Missing the in double comparatives (More you study, better you get). | Board both the's in colour. |
| Wrong word order (The more I study hard). | Show: the + comparative + subject + verb. |
| Adjective for adverb (more careful). | Contrast careful driver / drives more carefully. |
| different than / to | Teach different from as the safest form in British English. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To introduce the topic of change across generations. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Ask: *"How is your life different from your grandparents' lives when they were your age?"*
2. Pairs list three differences (2 minutes). Take ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the structures in context and clarify their form, meaning and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the interview with three generations of the same family. Who is the most positive about modern life? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the four questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (4 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Underline every comparison. Find a sentence with the ... the, a sentence with -er and -er and a sentence with nowhere near."* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (10 minutes)**

1. **Double comparatives.** Board *The more you practise, the better you get.* Ask the CCQs. Elicit the pattern; show the short form *The sooner, the better.*
2. **Progressive comparatives.** Board *Prices are getting higher and higher.* and *Life is becoming more and more stressful.* Ask the CCQs.
3. **Adverbs.** Board *He drives more carefully than his father.* Contrast adjective and adverb.
4. **Emphatic structures.** Draw a scale: *marginally → considerably → nowhere near / nothing like.*
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill the rhythm: *The MORE you earn, | the MORE you spend. The SOONer, | the BETter.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the structures accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Read the reference box. Exercise A: join the ideas with the ... the. Exercise B: complete the sentences. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.
3. Fast finishers do Handout 3 (further practice); otherwise set it for homework.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the structures fluently in discussion. **Interaction:** Groups of four, then whole class

1. Give each group a set of discussion cards (Handout 4). Students take turns to read a card and give their opinion using at least one structure from today; others agree or disagree (10 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Do you just answer, or do others respond too?"* (Others agree or disagree.)
2. **"The more ..., the more ..." chain (4 minutes).** Groups make a chain: *The more you use your phone, the less you sleep. → The less you sleep, the more tired you are. → The more tired you are, ...*
3. Groups share their longest chain (4 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"Which structure did you find most useful?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Complete Handout 3. Then write an article (180 words): *Is life better now than in our grandparents' time?* Use at least two double comparatives, two progressive comparatives and two emphatic structures.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the patterns on a card.
- Provide sentence frames: *The more ..., the more ... · ... is getting ... and ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use double comparatives with nouns: *The more money you have, the more problems you have.*
- They use *all the more* and *none the wiser*: *It made me all the more determined.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 5 out of 6 or better on Handout 2, Exercise A.
- In discussion, students use double and progressive comparatives accurately.
- Students use comparative adverbs (not adjectives) where needed.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students manage the word order in double comparatives?
- Did the discussion cards generate real disagreement?

# Handout 1: Three Generations

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**THEN AND NOW: one family, three views**

**Grandmother Sumana, 78:** "When I was young, life was nowhere near as busy as it is now. We didn't have much, but we had time for each other. Nowadays, everyone is getting busier and busier. The more things people buy, the less happy they seem."

**Father Nimal, 48:** "Life is considerably more comfortable now. Travel is easier, and phones are far cheaper than they were. But prices are getting higher and higher, and children spend less time outside. The more time my son spends on his phone, the less he talks to us!"

**Son Kavindu, 17:** "My life is completely different from my grandmother's. I can learn anything online, and the more I learn, the more I want to know. Technology is becoming more and more important. Maybe we're more stressed, but we're nothing like as isolated as people think: my friends are always online!"

**Answer the questions.**

1. According to Sumana, how has life changed? ______________________________
2. What does Nimal say about prices? ______________________________
3. What happens when Kavindu learns more? ______________________________
4. Who is the most positive about modern life? ______________________________

**Language reference**

| the + comparative, the + comparative | The more you practise, the better you get. |

3. Exams are becoming ______________ and ______________ (difficult).
4. The new bus is ______________ near as comfortable as the old one.
