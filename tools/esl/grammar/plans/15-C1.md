# Formal Quantity Expressions and Agreement in Report Writing (the majority of, a significant proportion of, the number of vs a number of)

*Lesson theme: Reporting Survey Results*

## Language analysis

### Formal quantity expressions

| **Countable (plural) nouns** | **Uncountable nouns** | **Both** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| a large / significant / growing number of + plural | a great deal of / a substantial amount of / a considerable amount of | the (vast) majority of · a (small) minority of |
| a handful of | a large quantity of | a significant proportion / percentage of |
| fewer / (very) few / scarcely any | less / (very) little / scarcely any | almost all · hardly any · plenty of (less formal) |

### Agreement

- **a number of + plural noun → plural verb**: *A number of students* ***have*** *complained.* (= several)
- **the number of + plural noun → singular verb**: *The number of students* ***has*** *increased.* (the subject is *the number*)
- **the majority / a minority / a proportion / percentage of + plural noun → usually plural verb**: *The majority of respondents* ***were*** *satisfied.* With an uncountable or singular noun → singular verb: *The majority of the land* ***is*** *farmland.*
- **The majority** used alone (no *of*) can take a singular or plural verb (*The majority was / were in favour*).

### Precision with data

- *the vast majority* (≈ 80–99%), *the majority / over half* (> 50%), *almost half*, *a third*, *a significant minority* (≈ 20–40%), *a small minority / a handful* (< 10%), *scarcely any / hardly any* (≈ 0–3%).
- Avoid vague *a lot of / lots of* in formal writing.
- *amount* + uncountable; *number* + countable (not *a large amount of people*).

### Concept checking questions

*The number of students who read daily has fallen.*

1. What has fallen: the students or the number? (The number.) So singular or plural verb? (Singular.)

*A number of students have complained.*

1. Does *a number of* mean a specific figure? (No, it means several or quite a few.) So singular or plural verb? (Plural.)

*Only a small minority of respondents read printed newspapers.*

1. Is this more or less than 50%? (Much less.) Would *a significant proportion* be correct for 4%? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| The number of students have increased. | Underline the head noun number; singular verb. |
| A large amount of people. | Two columns: number (countable) / amount (uncountable). |
| Less students / fewer time. | Rule: fewer + countable, less + uncountable; less with countable is common informally but avoid it in formal writing. |
| Imprecise expressions for figures. | Map expressions onto a percentage scale on the board. |
| Overuse of a lot of in reports. | Substitution task; highlight formal register. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and introduce the topic. **Interaction:** Whole class, pairs

1. Quick class poll with a show of hands: *"Who read a book for pleasure last month? Who reads news on a phone? Who reads a printed newspaper?"* Board the numbers.
2. Pairs describe the results in one sentence each (2 minutes). Board two sentences as students say them (they will probably use *a lot of / most / some*).

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present formal quantity expressions in a report and clarify their meaning, form and agreement. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the extract from a survey report on young people's reading habits. Complete the table with the percentages. Four minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, find the quantity expressions. Put them on the scale. Then answer the questions about the verbs."*
   - ICQs: *"What do you put on the scale?"* (The expressions.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the scale on the board.
2. **Countable vs uncountable.** Board *a great deal of time* vs *a large number of students*. Cross out *a large amount of people*.
3. **Agreement.** Board *The number of students has fallen* and *A number of students have complained*. Ask the CCQs.
4. **the majority of.** Contrast *The majority of respondents were ...* with *The majority of the land is ...*
5. Return to the Stage 1 sentences and rewrite them formally.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the expressions and agreement accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct verb. Exercise B: choose the best expression for each figure. Exercise C: correct the errors. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the expressions accurately in report writing. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Pairs choose Survey A or Survey B and write a report paragraph (120–150 words) describing the key findings. They must use at least six expressions from today and get the figures right (10 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"How many expressions?"* (At least six.) *"Can you write 'a lot of'?"* (No.)
2. **Peer review (5 minutes).** Pairs swap paragraphs with a pair that wrote about the other survey. They check that each expression matches the figure and that the verbs agree.
3. Two pairs read their paragraphs to the class (3 minutes).
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from the reports (correct and incorrect). Students correct the errors.
2. Ask: *"What's the difference between 'a number of' and 'the number of'?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a full report (250 words) on the other survey in Handout 4, with an introduction, findings and conclusion. Use at least eight formal quantity expressions and underline the subject and verb in each.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the percentage scale on a card.
- Let them write sentence by sentence using the data table, not a full paragraph.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use hedged and comparative expressions: *a marginally higher proportion, roughly two in five, a twofold increase, the figure stood at*.
- They use inversion: *Only a handful of respondents ...; Scarcely any ...*

## Assessment and success criteria

- The report paragraph contains at least six formal quantity expressions that accurately match the figures.
- Subject–verb agreement is correct after *the number of / a number of / the majority of*.
- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 3, Exercises A and B.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students match the expressions to the figures precisely?
- Was the peer review useful for catching agreement errors?

**Reading Habits of Young People in Colombo: Key Findings**

*This survey is fictional and was written for this lesson.*

A total of 500 respondents aged 16–24 took part in the survey. The vast majority of respondents (91%) own a smartphone, and over half (56%) said they read news online every day. By contrast, only a small minority (4%) still read a printed newspaper regularly, and scarcely any (2%) subscribe to a magazine.

The number of young people who read books for pleasure has fallen since the previous survey, from 48% to 35%. Nevertheless, a significant proportion of respondents (35%) said they had read at least one book in the past month, and a handful (6%) reported reading more than five.

A great deal of reading time is now spent on social media: respondents reported spending an average of three hours a day on it. A number of participants also commented that they had less time for reading because of study pressure. Finally, almost a third (31%) said they would read more if the local library opened in the evenings.
