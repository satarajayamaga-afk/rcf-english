# Order of Adjectives and Gradable vs Ungradable Adjectives (a lovely old wooden table, very cold / absolutely freezing)

*Lesson theme: Selling Things Online and Lost Property*

|  |  |

## Language analysis

### Order of adjectives

| **Opinion** | **Size** | **Age** | **Shape** | **Colour** | **Origin** | **Material** | **Purpose** | **Noun** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| beautiful | small | antique | round |  |  | brass |  | lamp |
| lovely | large | old |  | brown | Dutch | wooden | dining | table |
| useful |  | new |  | black |  | leather | laptop | bag |

- **Opinion** comes before **fact** (size, age, colour ...).
- In practice, two or three adjectives is normal; four or more sounds unnatural except in descriptions or listings.
- No comma between adjectives from **different** categories (*a small black bag*). A comma or *and* between two of the **same** category (*a warm, friendly welcome*; *a black and white cat*).
- Purpose adjectives are often nouns: *a* ***dining*** *table, a* ***laptop*** *bag, a* ***running*** *shoe*.

### Gradable vs ungradable adjectives

| **Gradable (can be more or less)** | **Ungradable / extreme (already the maximum)** |
| :-- | :-- |
| cold, hot, big, small, dirty, tired, angry, happy, good, tasty, surprised | freezing, boiling, enormous, tiny, filthy, exhausted, furious, delighted, perfect, delicious, astonished |
| a bit / fairly / very / extremely + gradable | absolutely / completely / totally + ungradable |
| very cold, extremely tired | absolutely freezing, completely exhausted |

- *really* works with both: *really cold, really freezing*.
- *quite* + gradable = "fairly"; *quite* + ungradable = "completely": *quite good* (OK) vs *quite perfect* (completely perfect).
- Classifying adjectives (*wooden, Japanese, electric, dead, unique*) are ungradable: not *very wooden*.

### Pronunciation

- Stress the adverb and the extreme adjective for emphasis: *It was ABsolutely FREEZing!*

### Concept checking questions

*It's absolutely freezing.*

1. Is it a little cold or extremely cold? (Extremely.) Can I say *very freezing*? (No.)

*a lovely old wooden table*

1. Which adjective is an opinion? (*lovely*.) Which comes first: opinion or fact? (Opinion.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Unnatural order (a wooden old big table). | OSASCOMP chart; physical card-ordering activity. |
| Too many adjectives. | Rule of thumb: two or three, unless a listing or description. |
| very enormous / absolutely big | Two-column board; matching adverbs with columns. |
| Commas between all adjectives. | Show comma only with same-category adjectives. |
| quite meaning confusion. | Contrast quite tired and quite exhausted. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students with the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Ask: *"Have you ever sold or bought something online? How did the seller describe it?"*
2. Pairs share (2 minutes). Take ideas.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present adjective order and gradability in context and clarify the rules. **Interaction:** Individual, groups, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read three listings on a Sri Lankan buy-and-sell website. Which item would you buy? Why? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Analysis (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"In groups, put the adjectives from the listings in the chart. Then complete the gradable / ungradable table."*
   - ICQs: *"How many columns in the chart?"* (Seven plus the noun.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Feedback. Build the order chart on the board.
2. **Card ordering.** Give four students one adjective card each (*Japanese, small, black, beautiful*) and a fifth student *camera*. The class puts them in order.
3. **Gradability.** Board *very cold / absolutely freezing*. Ask the CCQs. Cross out *very enormous*.
4. Highlight *really* with both and the *quite* contrast.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *It's ABsolutely FREEZing! I'm comPLETEly exHAUSTed!*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise order and adverb choice accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: put the adjectives in order. Exercise B: choose the correct adverb. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the language in describing objects precisely and vividly. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. **Lost property (8 minutes).** Give out Handout 4. Student A has lost an item (picture card) and describes it to Student B, a lost property officer, who must find it among similar pictures: *"It's a small black leather wallet with a red stripe."* Swap.
   - ICQs: *"Can B see A's picture?"* (No.)
2. **Write a listing (7 minutes).** Pairs choose something to sell (real or imaginary) and write a persuasive listing (60–80 words), using at least two three-adjective phrases and two extreme adjectives with *absolutely / completely*.
3. **Gallery (3 minutes).** Pairs read two other listings and "buy" the best one.
4. Monitor and note language use.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board phrases from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct them.
2. Quick fire: say a gradable adjective; students say the extreme version with *absolutely*.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a description (150 words) of a room in your home or a special object in your family, using adjective order correctly and at least four gradable and four ungradable adjectives with suitable adverbs.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the order chart and the gradable / ungradable list.
- Limit them to two adjectives before a noun.

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use compound adjectives: *a hand-made, ten-year-old, well-known brand*.
- They use *utterly, highly, deeply* collocations: *utterly ridiculous, highly unlikely, deeply grateful*.

## Assessment and success criteria

- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 3.
- In the listing, adjectives appear in a natural order.
- Students use *absolutely / completely* with extreme adjectives and *very / extremely* with gradable ones.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the card-ordering activity make the order memorable?
- Did students start using extreme adjectives naturally?
