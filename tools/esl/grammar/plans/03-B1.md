# Present Perfect Continuous (for and since)

*Lesson theme: Clubs and Hobbies*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Perfect Tenses |
| Grammar point | Present Perfect Continuous (for and since) |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use the present perfect simple for experiences and news, but say I am learning English since six years and don't use the present perfect continuous. |
| Main aim | To help students discover and use the present perfect continuous for duration (with for / since) and recent activity, and to contrast it with the present perfect simple for results and quantities. |
| Subsidiary aims | To read interviews for specific information; to develop fluency in interviewing and writing a profile. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can interview a partner about hobbies, work and studies and write a short profile using both perfect forms and for / since. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (club interviews), Handout 2 (discovery task), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (interview sheet). |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Present perfect continuous** | **Present perfect simple** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Positive | I've been running for five years. | I've run three marathons. |
| Negative | She hasn't been sleeping well. | She hasn't slept at all. |
| Question | How long have you been learning English? | How many books have you read? |

**have / has + been + verb-ing**

### Meaning and use

| **Present perfect continuous** | **Present perfect simple** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Duration of an activity up to now: I've been learning English for six years. | Result or quantity: I've learned 500 words. |
| Recent activity with visible evidence: I've been painting (that's why I'm dirty). | Completed action: I've painted the room (it's finished). |
| Question: How long ...? | Question: How many / How much ...? |
| Not with state verbs: I've known her for years (not been knowing). |  |

### for and since

- **for** + a period of time: *for three years, for two hours, for ages*.
- **since** + a point in time: *since 2019, since Monday, since I was ten*.

### Pronunciation

- Weak *been*: /bɪn/. *I've been running* /aɪv bɪn ˈrʌnɪŋ/.
- *How long have you been ...?* is said as one unit, stress on *long* and the main verb.

### Concept checking questions

*I've been learning English for six years.*

1. Did I start in the past? (Yes, six years ago.)
2. Am I still learning? (Yes.)
3. Are we talking about how long or how many? (How long.)

*I've run three marathons.*

1. Are the marathons finished? (Yes.)
2. Is the focus on how long or how many? (How many.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Present simple / continuous for duration (I am living here since 2018). | Contrast with the timeline from past to now; CCQs. |
| for / since confusion. | Card sort; rule: period vs point. |
| Continuous with quantities (I've been writing five emails). | How long vs how many contrast. |
| State verbs in the continuous (I've been knowing her). | Remind of state verbs from earlier lessons. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To personalise the topic and create interest. **Interaction:** Pairs, then whole class

1. Say: *"Tell your partner about a hobby or sport you do. When did you start?"* Model: *"I play badminton. I started when I was twelve."*
2. Students talk for 2 minutes. Take two examples without correcting.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the present perfect continuous in context and help students work out its form and use, contrasted with the present perfect simple. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read about three club members. Who has been a member the longest? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the three questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Look at the sentences from the interviews. Answer the questions with your partner."*
   - ICQs: *"Alone or in pairs?"* (Pairs.) *"Where are the sentences from?"* (The interviews.)
2. Monitor. Ask stuck pairs: *"Is Amal still running? Did he start in the past?"*

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit the answers. Draw the timeline: a line from a point in the past to NOW, with an arrow. Ask the CCQs from the Language analysis.
2. Contrast: *How long have you been running?* vs *How many marathons have you run?*
3. **for / since:** students call out time expressions; the class decides *for* or *since*. Board them on the left.
4. **Pronunciation:** drill *How long have you been learning English? I've been learning it for six years.* Focus on weak *been*.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form and the contrast accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: for or since. Exercise B: simple or continuous. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs compare and justify: *"How long, so continuous."*
3. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use both forms fluently in a personalised interview and writing task. **Interaction:** Pairs, then individual, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Model one question with a strong student: *"How long have you been living in your house?" "Since 2015." "How many times have you moved?"*
2. Pairs interview each other and take notes (9 minutes).
   - ICQs: *"Do you write full sentences or notes?"* (Notes.)
3. Students write a short profile of their partner (5 sentences) using both forms (6 minutes).
4. Two or three students read their profiles aloud (3 minutes).

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board errors and good sentences from monitoring. Students correct them.
2. Quick check: say a time expression; students say *for* or *since*.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a 120-word profile of someone you admire, including how long they have been doing their job or hobby and what they have achieved.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Provide question starters on the board.
- Let weaker students write profile sentences using a frame: *___ has been ___ing for / since ___. He/She has ___ (number) ___.*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students include both forms in one answer: *I've been training for a year and I've run two races.*
- They add a sentence with visible evidence: *You look tired. Have you been studying all night?*

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students choose *for / since* correctly in 7 of 8 items.
- In the profiles, students use both forms appropriately.
- Students avoid the present simple for duration up to now.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students find the rules from the examples?
- Were *for / since* still a problem in the interviews?
