# Past Continuous

*Lesson theme: What Were You Doing When ...?*

## Lesson overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Grammar topic** | Past Tenses |
| **Grammar point** | Past Continuous |
| **Level** | A2 (Elementary) |
| **Duration** | 60 minutes |
| **Framework** | ESA boomerang: Engage, Activate 1 (first try), Study, Activate 2 (second try). |
| **Class profile** | Elementary students who use the past simple with reasonable accuracy and know the present continuous. They have not yet learned the past continuous and use the past simple for all past actions. |
| **Main aim** | To clarify and practise the past continuous for actions in progress at a past time and for actions interrupted by a past simple event (*when / while*). |
| **Subsidiary aims** | To develop fluency in a game-like speaking task (an alibi game); to read a short news story for specific information. |
| **Learning outcome** | By the end of the lesson, students can say what they were doing at a specific past time and describe an interrupted action, e.g. *I was cooking when the lights went out.* |
| **Materials** | Handout 1 (news story), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (alibi role cards and detective question sheet). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| | Positive | Negative | Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / He / She / It | I **was** cook**ing**. | He **wasn't** sleep**ing**. | **Was** she work**ing**? Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't. |
| You / We / They | They **were** watch**ing** TV. | We **weren't** listen**ing**. | **Were** you study**ing**? Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. |

### Meaning and use
- An action **in progress at a specific time** in the past: *At 8 pm last night, I was watching TV.*
- A **longer action interrupted** by a shorter one: *I was walking home when it started to rain.* (past continuous = long action; past simple = short action)
- **when** usually goes before the short action; **while** usually goes before the long action: *While she was sleeping, the phone rang.*
- Two actions in progress at the same time: *While I was cooking, my brother was doing his homework.*

### Pronunciation
- *was* and *were* are usually weak in positive sentences: /wəz/, /wə/. *I was cooking* /aɪ wəz ˈkʊkɪŋ/.
- They are strong in short answers and negatives: *Yes, I was* /wɒz/. *wasn't* /ˈwɒznt/.

### Concept checking questions
*I was cooking when the lights went out.*
1. Which action started first? (Cooking.)
2. Which action was short? (The lights went out.)
3. Did the lights going out interrupt the cooking? (Yes.)
4. Which is in the past continuous? (Cooking.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Missing *-ing* (*I was watch TV*). | Board formula with three boxes. Finger gesture: three fingers for *was – watch – ing*. |
| Using the continuous for both actions (*When I was arriving, they were eating*). | Timeline: one long wavy line, one cross. CCQ: *Which is short?* |
| Wrong choice of *when / while*. | Highlight the general pattern on the board; accept *when* with both in speech. |
| Alibi game: students forget their story. | Give each pair time to write a short alibi plan on the card. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Engage (7 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To generate interest and get students talking about a past moment.
**Interaction:** Whole class, then pairs

1. Turn off the classroom lights (or mime it). Say: *"Imagine: last night at 8 o'clock, all the lights in the city went out! A power cut!"*
2. Ask: *"Do you remember a power cut? Where were you? How did you feel?"* Take two or three answers.
3. Write *7 o'clock this morning* on the board. Students tell a partner what they did this morning (1 minute). Listen; don't correct.

### Stage 2: Activate 1, first try (8 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To let students try a communicative task with the language they have, and to diagnose needs.
**Interaction:** Pairs

1. Say: *"Last night at 8 pm, there was a power cut in our town. Ask your partner: what was your family doing at that moment? Imagine if you don't remember! Find out about four people in your partner's family."* (4 minutes.)
2. Monitor without helping. Note: (a) whether students use the past continuous; (b) errors; (c) any good examples.
3. Ask two or three pairs to report one thing. Say: *"Great. Let's read about the real power cut."*

### Stage 3: Study, reading and language focus (12 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To clarify the meaning, form and pronunciation of the past continuous with *when / while*.
**Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

1. Give out Handout 1. **Reading task:** *"Read the news story. Match each person to what they were doing when the lights went out."* (3 minutes.) Pairs check, then class feedback.
2. **Noticing:** students underline *was/were + -ing* verbs in one colour and past simple verbs in another.
3. **Meaning.** Board *Mr Perera was cooking when the lights went out.* Draw a timeline. Ask the CCQs.
4. **Form.** Elicit the form table: was/were + verb-ing; negative; question. Board: *What were you doing at 8 pm?*
5. **when / while.** Board two sentences from the text with *when* and *while*. Ask: *"Which word comes before the long action?"* (while). *"Which comes before the short action?"* (when).
6. **Pronunciation.** Drill with weak forms: *I was cooking. They were watching TV.* Then strong short answers: *Yes, I was.*
7. **Activate 1 errors.** Board two or three sentences you heard in Activate 1. Pairs improve them using today's language.

### Stage 4: Study, controlled practice (9 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To practise the form and the contrast with the past simple.
**Interaction:** Individual, then pairs

1. Students complete Handout 2, Exercises A and B (6 minutes).
2. Pairs compare and justify: *"It's the long action, so past continuous."*
3. Check as a class. Drill two or three of the sentences.

### Stage 5: Activate 2, the alibi game (17 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To use the language fluently and accurately in a communicative game, repeating the Activate 1 skill at a higher level.
**Interaction:** Groups of four (two suspects, two detectives)

1. **Set up.** Read the situation on Handout 3: *"Yesterday between 10.00 and 11.00, a birthday cake disappeared from the staff room. Two students are suspects."*
2. Divide each group: two suspects (Pair S) and two detectives (Pair D). Suspects get the alibi card; detectives get the question sheet.
3. **Preparation (4 minutes).** Suspects agree on a detailed alibi: where they were, what they were doing at 10.00, 10.15, 10.30 and 10.45, who they saw. Detectives prepare questions using the question sheet.
4. **Interviews (6 minutes).** Detectives interview each suspect **separately** (the other suspect waits out of earshot). Detectives note answers.
5. **Decision (2 minutes).** Detectives compare notes: are there differences? They decide: *innocent* or *guilty*, and give reasons: *"Suspect A said they were reading in the library at 10.30, but Suspect B said they were eating in the canteen."*
6. If time allows, swap roles and play again.

### Stage 6: Feedback (7 minutes)
**Stage aim:** To consolidate and correct.
**Interaction:** Whole class

1. Each group announces its verdict with one reason.
2. Board four sentences from the game: two correct, two with errors. Students correct them.
3. Ask: *"Was Activate 2 easier than Activate 1? Why?"*
4. Set homework.

## Differentiation

**Support**
- Give suspects a time-plan template to complete before the interviews.
- Weaker detectives can read questions directly from the sheet.

**Stretch**
- Detectives must ask at least two follow-up questions (*Exactly what time? Who were you with? What were they wearing?*).
- Stronger suspects add a while sentence: *While I was reading, Kamal was sitting next to me.*

## Assessment and success criteria
- In Activate 2, students form past continuous questions and answers accurately most of the time.
- Students choose past continuous vs past simple correctly in 8 of 10 items on Handout 2.
- Students use *when* and *while* in at least one sentence each.

## Homework
Write a paragraph (80–100 words) about where you were and what you were doing during a memorable event (a big match, a storm, a celebration).

## Teacher's reflection
- How much did students improve from Activate 1 to Activate 2?
- Was the alibi game well organised? How could I set it up faster?

**Read the news story. Match each person to what they were doing when the lights went out.**

> **CITY IN THE DARK**
>
> At exactly 8 pm last night, all the lights in the city went out. The power cut lasted for two hours. We asked some people what they were doing.
>
> "I was cooking dinner for my family," said Mr Perera, 45. "The rice was nearly ready. We ate by candlelight!"
>
> Nimali, 16, was doing her homework when the lights went out. "I was writing an essay on my computer. I didn't save it, so I lost everything!"
>
> While the city was sitting in the dark, two doctors at the General Hospital were working with torches. "We were helping a patient, and we didn't stop," said Dr Silva.
>
> Mrs Fernando, 70, wasn't worried. "I was sleeping in my chair. I didn't notice anything!"

# Handout 2: Practice

Name: ___________________________ Date: _____________

**A. Put the verbs into the past simple or the past continuous.**

1. I ____________ (read) when the lights ____________ (go) out.
2. While we ____________ (eat) dinner, my phone ____________ (ring).
3. What ____________ you ____________ (do) at 9 pm yesterday?
4. She ____________ (fall) while she ____________ (run) for the bus.
5. It ____________ (not / rain) when we left the house.
6. They ____________ (watch) a film when their cousins ____________ (arrive).
7. ____________ you ____________ (sleep) when I called?
8. I ____________ (see) an accident while I ____________ (drive) to work.

1. ____________ I was walking to school, I saw an elephant!
2. I was having a shower ____________ the water stopped.
3. My mother was cooking ____________ my father was cleaning the car.

1. at 7 o'clock this morning? ___________________________

| Error | Correction |
|---|---|

| When I was arriving, they ate. | When I arrived, they were eating. |
