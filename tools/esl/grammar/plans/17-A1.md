GRAMMAR TOPIC: ADJECTIVES AND ADVERBS · A1 (Beginner) · 60 minutes

# Adjectives Before Nouns and After Be, with Very (a big house, The house is very big)

*Lesson theme: My Home and My Things*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Adjectives and Adverbs |
| Grammar point | Adjectives Before Nouns and After Be, with Very (a big house, The house is very big) |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Beginners who know be, have got / have and basic nouns. They put adjectives after the noun (a house big), add -s to adjectives with plural nouns (bigs houses) and leave out be (My house very small). |
| Main aim | To present and practise adjectives in two positions: before a noun (a big house) and after be (The house is big), with very, and with no plural -s on the adjective. |
| Subsidiary aims | To learn common adjectives and their opposites (big / small, old / new, clean / dirty); to practise a / an before adjectives (an old car); to develop speaking through a "describe and guess" game. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can describe their home and things, e.g. I live in a small house. It's very old. My bedroom is blue. I have got a new bike. |
| Materials | Picture cards (houses, rooms, objects), Handout 1 (two houses), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (describe and guess cards). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Position** | **Form** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| before a noun | a / an + (very) + adjective + noun | a big house · an old car · a very small room |
| after be | subject + be + (very) + adjective | The house is big. The rooms are very small. |

- Adjectives have **no plural form**: *two big houses* (not *bigs houses*); *The rooms are small* (not *smalls*).
- *an* before a vowel sound: *an old house, an empty room, an expensive car*.
- *very* goes before the adjective: *very big* (not *big very*).

### Adjectives and opposites

big / small · old / new · old / young (people) · clean / dirty · hot / cold · beautiful / ugly · expensive / cheap · long / short · happy / sad · colours: red, blue, green, white

### Pronunciation

- Stress the adjective in descriptions: *a BIG HOUSE*, *It's VERy OLD*.
- Linking: *an old* /ə nəʊld/.

### Concept checking questions

*It's a big house.*

1. Where is *big*: before or after *house*? (Before.)
2. Can I say *a house big*? (No.)

*The rooms are very small.*

1. Is there *-s* on *small*? (No.) Why not? (Adjectives don't change.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| Adjective after the noun (a house big). | Board arrows; picture cards: adjective card + noun card in order. |
| Plural adjectives (bigs houses). | Board: two big houses (the noun changes, not the adjective). |
| Missing be (My house very small). | Highlight is / are in a different colour. |
| a old car | Drill an old, an expensive with linking. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate vocabulary. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Show two pictures: a big house and a small house. Ask: *"What's different?"* Elicit *big* and *small*.
2. Show more pairs (old / new car, clean / dirty room). Elicit or teach the opposites.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present adjectives in two positions and clarify form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Whole class, individual, pairs

**Part A: Vocabulary (6 minutes)**

1. Stick the adjective opposites on the board. Students repeat. Point to classroom objects: *"Is the board big or small? Is my bag old or new?"*

**Part B: Context (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read about Thilini's house and Ravi's house. Whose house is old? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many houses?"* (Two.)
2. Students answer the true / false questions. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Board *Thilini has got a big house.* and *Her house is big.* Ask: *"Where is big in each sentence?"* (Before the noun / after *is*.) Ask the CCQs.
2. Board *two big houses* and cross out *bigs*.
3. Add *very*: *a very big house · The house is very big.*
4. Board *an old house* and *an expensive car*.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill phrases with stress: *a BIG HOUSE, an OLD CAR, It's VERy CLEAN.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: put the words in order. Exercise B: write the opposite. Exercise C: correct the mistakes. Seven minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"house / big / a: what's the order?"* (a big house.)
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use adjectives to describe homes and things. **Interaction:** Pairs, then groups, then whole class

1. **My home (6 minutes).** Students draw a quick plan of their home and describe it to a partner: *"I live in a small house. The kitchen is very small. My bedroom is green."*
2. **Describe and guess (8 minutes).** Give out Handout 3. In groups, students take a card and describe the object or room without saying its name: *"It's very big. It's white. It's cold inside."* (A fridge!) Others guess.
   - ICQs: *"Can you say the word on the card?"* (No.)
3. **Report (4 minutes).** Two students describe their partner's home to the class.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Quick game: say a noun; students add an adjective: *"car"* → *"a red car!"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write about your home (8 sentences). Use 8 different adjectives, 4 before a noun and 4 after *is / are*.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the adjective opposites with pictures.
- Give sentence frames: *I live in a ... house. My ... is very ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use two adjectives: *a big white house, a small old car*.
- They use *quite* and *really*: *It's quite old. It's really beautiful.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 12 out of 14 or better on Handout 2.
- Students put adjectives before nouns and use *be* before adjectives.
- Students don't add *-s* to adjectives.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the picture cards help students with word order?
- Was the guessing game engaging?

# Handout 1: Two Houses

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Thilini's house**

I live in Colombo. I have got a big new house. It's white and it has got four bedrooms. My bedroom is very beautiful. The kitchen is clean and modern, but the garden is very small.

**Ravi's house**

I live in a village near Galle. My house is very old and small. It's green. It has got two bedrooms and a big garden with a lot of tall trees. The house is very cool, even on hot days.

**True (T) or False (F)?**

1. Thilini has got an old house. ______
2. Thilini's garden is very small. ______
3. Ravi's house is green. ______
4. Ravi has got a small garden. ______
