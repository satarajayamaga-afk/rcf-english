# A Few / A Little, Too Much / Too Many and (Not) Enough

*Lesson theme: Improving Our Town*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Countable and Uncountable Nouns and Quantifiers |
| Grammar point | A Few / A Little, Too Much / Too Many and (Not) Enough |
| Level | B1 (Intermediate) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Intermediate students who use some, any, much, many and a lot of accurately. They confuse a few and a little (a little cars), say too much people and put enough after the noun (money enough). They also use very much where they mean too much. |
| Main aim | To clarify and practise a few (countable) / a little (uncountable), too much / too many (more than we want) and (not) enough + noun to describe problems and needs. |
| Subsidiary aims | To introduce the contrast a few (some, positive) vs few (not many, negative); to develop speaking through a town planning discussion. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can describe problems in a town and suggest improvements, e.g. There's too much traffic and there are too many cars. There aren't enough parks. We need a few more buses and a little more space for cycling. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (a letter to a local newspaper), Handout 2 (language focus), Handout 3 (practice), Handout 4 (town plan and planning sheet). |

## Language analysis

### Form

| **Meaning** | **Countable (cars, parks)** | **Uncountable (traffic, noise)** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| a small amount (positive) | a few parks | a little space |
| not many / not much (negative feeling) | few parks | little space |
| more than we want | too many cars | too much traffic |
| the right amount | enough parks | enough money |
| less than we need | not enough buses | not enough space |

- *enough* goes **before** a noun: *enough money* (not *money enough*); but **after** an adjective: *big enough*.
- *too much / too many* + noun; *too* + adjective: *too noisy*.
- *a few more / a little more* + noun: *We need a few more buses.*

### Meaning

- *a few / a little* = some; a positive idea. *few / little* = almost none; a negative idea. *I have a few friends here* (I'm OK). *I have few friends here* (I'm lonely).
- *too much / too many* is a problem. *a lot of* is neutral. *There's a lot of traffic* (fact) vs *There's too much traffic* (it's a problem).

### Pronunciation

- Stress *too*: *There are TOO many cars.*
- *enough* /ɪˈnʌf/: stress the second syllable.

### Concept checking questions

*There are too many cars.*

1. Are there a lot of cars? (Yes.) Is that a problem? (Yes.)

*There aren't enough parks.*

1. Do we have parks? (Some, maybe.) Do we have the number we need? (No.) Do we need more? (Yes.)

*We need a little more space.*

1. Can we count space? (No.) So *a little* or *a few*? (*A little*.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| a little cars / a few traffic. | Two columns: countable / uncountable; sort the town nouns. |
| too much people. | People is plural countable: too many people. |
| money enough. | Board: enough + noun; adjective + enough. |
| very much for too much (The bus is very much crowded). | Contrast very (a lot) and too (a problem). |
| few / a few confusion. | Two faces: ☺ a few friends · ☹ few friends. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students with the topic. **Interaction:** Pairs, whole class

1. Ask: *"What's good about our town? What's bad about it?"*
2. Pairs list two good things and two bad things (2 minutes). Take ideas. Board two problems exactly as students say them.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the quantifiers in context and clarify their form and meaning. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Reading (6 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read the letter to the newspaper. What are the three biggest problems in Riverside, according to the writer? Three minutes."*
2. Pairs compare. Students answer the questions. Check as a class.

**Part B: Finding the rules (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Find the quantifiers in the letter. Complete the table and answer the questions."*
   - ICQs: *"Where do you find the words?"* (In the letter.)
2. Monitor and help.

**Part C: Clarification (7 minutes)**

1. Elicit the table and build it on the board.
2. Ask the CCQs. Highlight *a lot of* (fact) vs *too much / too many* (problem).
3. Contrast *a few* and *few* with the faces ☺ / ☹.
4. **enough.** Board *enough money* and *big enough*.
5. Return to the problems from Stage 1 and improve them with the new language.
6. **Pronunciation.** Drill *There are TOO many cars. There aren't eNOUGH parks.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 3. Say: *"Exercise A: choose the correct word. Exercise B: rewrite the sentences with too or enough. Seven minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the quantifiers fluently in a planning discussion. **Interaction:** Groups, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 4. Groups of four are the town council. They look at the map of "Riverside" and the facts, and list the five biggest problems: *There's too much ... There aren't enough ...* (5 minutes).
2. **Plan (7 minutes).** The council has Rs. 50 million. Groups decide how to spend it: *We should build a few more ... We need a little more ...* Each item has a cost.
   - ICQs: *"How much money do you have?"* (50 million.) *"Can you buy everything?"* (No.)
3. **Present (6 minutes).** Each group presents its plan in one minute. The class votes for the best plan.
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board sentences from Stage 4 (correct and incorrect). Students correct them.
2. Ask: *"What's the difference between 'a lot of traffic' and 'too much traffic'?"*
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write a letter to your local newspaper (120–150 words) about problems in your town and how to improve them. Use *too much / too many*, *(not) enough* and *a few / a little* at least twice each.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the town nouns already sorted into countable and uncountable.
- Give them sentence frames: *There's too much ... · There are too many ... · There aren't enough ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *few / little* and *very few / very little*: *Very few people use the bus.*
- They use *too few / too little* and *fewer / less*: *We need fewer cars and less noise.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 8 out of 10 or better on Handout 3, Exercise A.
- In the discussion, students use *too much / too many* with the correct noun type.
- Students put *enough* before nouns.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did students understand the difference between *a lot of* and *too much / too many*?
- Did the budget make the discussion more realistic?

# Handout 1: A Letter to the Newspaper

Name: ______________________________ Date: ______________

**Dear Editor,**

I have lived in Riverside for twenty years, and I am worried about our town.

Firstly, there is too much traffic. Every morning there are too many cars on the main road, and it takes forty minutes to drive two kilometres. There aren't enough buses, so people don't have a choice.

Secondly, there is too much rubbish. There are only a few bins in the town centre, and they are always full.

Thirdly, our children don't have enough space to play. There are few parks, and the only playground is old and dangerous.

But there is hope. A few volunteers clean the beach every Sunday, and a little money from the council could make a big difference. We need a few more buses, a little more space for parks, and enough bins for everyone. Is that too much to ask?

**Yours faithfully,** **Mrs R. Perera**
