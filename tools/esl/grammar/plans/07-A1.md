# Can and Can't for Ability

*Lesson theme: The Class Talent Show*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Modal Verbs |
| Grammar point | Can and Can't for Ability |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Beginners who know common action verbs (swim, dance, sing, cook, drive) and the verb be. They add -s or to after can (He cans swim, I can to cook). |
| Main aim | To present and practise can / can't + base verb to talk about abilities. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise the pronunciation of weak can /kən/ and strong can't /kɑːnt/; to develop speaking through a class survey. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can ask and answer Can you ...? questions and describe 6 abilities of their own and their classmates. |
| Materials | Handout 1 (talent show entries), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (class survey), action picture cards (swim, dance, sing, cook, drive, play the guitar, ride a bike, speak Tamil). |

## Language analysis

### Form

|  | **Positive** | **Negative** | **Question** | **Short answers** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| All persons | I / She / They can swim. | He can't (cannot) drive. | Can you cook? | Yes, I can. / No, I can't. |

- **can / can't + base verb** (no *-s*, no *to*): *She can swim.* (not *She cans swim / She can to swim*)
- The same form for all persons.

### Meaning and use

- Ability: something a person is able to do. *Fish can swim. Babies can't walk.*
- Modifiers: *very well, quite well, a little, not at all*: *I can sing quite well. I can't cook at all.*

### Pronunciation

- Positive *can* is weak: /kən/ – *I can SWIM*. The main verb is stressed.
- Negative *can't* is strong: /kɑːnt/ – *I CAN'T swim.*
- Short answers are strong: *Yes, I CAN* /kæn/.

### Concept checking questions

*She can swim.*

1. Is she swimming now? (Not necessarily.)
2. Is she able to swim? (Yes.)

*He can't drive.*

1. Does he know how to drive? (No.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| He cans swim. | Board: can = same for all persons; cross out -s. |
| I can to cook. | Highlight: can + base verb, no to. |
| Do you can dance? | Question: Can goes first. Word cards to reorder. |
| Students can't hear can vs can't. | Listening game: teacher says sentences; students hold up ✓ or ✗ cards. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To engage students and introduce abilities. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Show the action picture cards one by one. Students say the verb (*swim, dance, sing ...*). Stick them on the left of the board.
2. Mime swimming well and singing badly. Ask: *"Am I good at swimming? Am I good at singing?"* Students laugh and answer *yes / no*.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present *can / can't* in context and clarify meaning, form and pronunciation. **Interaction:** Individual, pairs, whole class

**Part A: Context (8 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Our school has a talent show. Read the entries. What can each person do? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"How many people?"* (Five.) *"What do you find?"* (What they can do.)
2. Students complete the table with ✓ and ✗. Pairs compare.
3. Check as a class: *"Can Nethmi sing?" "Yes, she can."*

**Part B: Language clarification (12 minutes)**

1. **Meaning.** Board *Kavindu can play the guitar.* Ask the CCQs.
2. **Form.** Board the form table. Ask: *"He cans or he can?"* (He can.) *"Can to play or can play?"* (Can play.) Cross out wrong forms.
3. **Negative and questions.** Elicit *can't* and the question *Can you ...?* with short answers.
4. **Pronunciation.** Say *I can swim* (weak) and *I can't swim* (strong). Students listen and hold up ✓ for *can* and ✗ for *can't*. Play six rounds.
5. Drill: *I can swim. She can't drive. Can you cook? Yes, I can. / No, I can't.*

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the form accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: complete with can or can't and the verb. Exercise B: put the words in order. Six minutes."*
2. Pairs check. Nominate students to read answers; correct pronunciation of *can / can't*.
3. **Picture drill:** point to a picture card and a student: *"Ravi, swim?"* Student asks a partner: *"Can you swim?"*

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use *can / can't* in a personalised survey and a class talent show plan. **Interaction:** Mingle, then groups

1. Give out Handout 3. Model with a strong student: *"Can you dance?" "Yes, I can." "How well?" "Quite well!"*
2. **Survey (8 minutes):** students mingle and ask about the abilities on the sheet. They write names and how well.
   - ICQs: *"What do you write?"* (Names and how well.)
3. **Talent show plan (6 minutes):** in groups of four, students use their survey results to plan a class talent show: *"Ruwan can sing very well. Maya can dance. Nobody can play the piano."*
4. **Writing (4 minutes):** students write 6 sentences about their own abilities at the bottom of Handout 3.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Groups announce their talent show line-up.
2. Board four sentences heard (two with errors such as *He cans swim*). Students correct them.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write 8 sentences about what your family members can and can't do. Use *very well, quite well* or *not at all* in three sentences.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students picture prompts on the survey sheet.
- Allow short answers only (*Yes, I can / No, I can't*).

**Stretch**

- Stronger students add *how well* in every answer.
- They ask *Could you ... when you were five?* (past ability).

## Assessment and success criteria

- Students use *can / can't* + base verb correctly in 8 of 10 items on Handout 2.
- Students ask and answer *Can you ...?* correctly during the survey.
- Students hear the difference between *can* and *can't* in the listening game.

## Teacher's reflection

- Could students hear and say the difference between *can* and *can't*?
- Did the survey give everyone enough speaking practice?
