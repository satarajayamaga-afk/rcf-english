GRAMMAR TOPIC: LINKING WORDS · A1 (Beginner) · 60 minutes

# And, But, Or and Because

*Lesson theme: Likes, Dislikes and Reasons*

## Lesson overview

|  |  |
| :-- | :-- |
| Grammar topic | Linking Words |
| Grammar point | And, But, Or and Because |
| Level | A1 (Beginner) |
| Duration | 60 minutes |
| Class profile | Beginners who write short, separate sentences (I like tea. I like coffee. I don't like milk.). When they try to join them, they use and for everything (I like tea and I don't like milk) and put because at the start of a separate sentence (Because it is hot.). |
| Main aim | To present and practise four linking words: and (adding), but (contrast), or (choice) and because (reason), to join two ideas in one sentence. |
| Subsidiary aims | To practise or in negatives and questions (I don't like tea or coffee. Tea or coffee?); to practise like / don't like; to develop speaking and writing about preferences. |
| Learning outcome | By the end of the lesson, students can talk and write about likes and dislikes with reasons, e.g. I like cricket and football, but I don't like swimming because the water is cold. Do you want tea or coffee? |
| Materials | Linking word cards, Handout 1 (a pen friend's email), Handout 2 (practice), Handout 3 (likes and dislikes survey). |

## Language analysis

### Form and meaning

| **Word** | **Meaning** | **Example** |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| and | adds a similar idea | I like rice and curry. She plays cricket and he plays football. |
| but | adds a different / opposite idea | I like tea, but I don't like coffee. |
| or | gives a choice; joins ideas in negatives | Do you want tea or coffee? I don't like tea or coffee. |
| because | gives a reason (answers Why?) | I like Sundays because I don't go to school. |

- Usually a comma before *but* in longer sentences: *It's small, but it's beautiful.*
- *because* + subject + verb: *because it is hot* (not just *because hot*).
- *Because ...* alone is a spoken answer to *Why?*: *"Why do you like it?" "Because it's fun."* In writing, join it to the main sentence.

### Pronunciation

- Weak forms: *and* /ən/, *or* /ə/ in fast speech: *tea or coffee* /tiː ə ˈkɒfi/.
- *because* /bɪˈkɒz/, often /kəz/ in speech.

### Concept checking questions

*I like tea, but I don't like coffee.*

1. Do I like both? (No.) Are the two ideas the same or different? (Different.)

*Tea or coffee?*

1. Can I have both? (No, one.) What word gives a choice? (*or*.)

*I'm happy because it's Friday.*

1. Why am I happy? (Because it's Friday.)

## Anticipated problems and solutions

| **Problem** | **Solution** |
| :-- | :-- |
| and for contrast (I like tea and I don't like milk). | Smile / frown faces: same feeling → and; different → but. |
| because sentence alone in writing. | Board: join with an arrow to the main idea. |
| because hot (no subject / verb). | Board: because + it + is. |
| and in negatives (I don't like tea and coffee). | Teach I don't like tea or coffee. |

## Procedure

### Stage 1: Warm-up (5 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To activate vocabulary for likes and dislikes. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Show pictures (cricket, rice, rain, ice cream, maths). For each, say *"I like ..."* or *"I don't like ..."* with a smile or frown.
2. Ask students: *"Do you like rain?"* Take answers.

### Stage 2: Presentation (20 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To present the four linking words in context and clarify their meaning and form. **Interaction:** Whole class, individual, pairs

**Part A: Context (7 minutes)**

1. Give out Handout 1. Say: *"Read Nethmi's email to her new pen friend. What does she like? What doesn't she like? Three minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"Who is the email from?"* (Nethmi.)
2. Students complete the table. Pairs compare. Check as a class.

**Part B: Noticing (5 minutes)**

1. Say: *"Find and circle and, but, or, because in the email."* Pairs compare.

**Part C: Clarification (8 minutes)**

1. Board one example of each from the email. Use the faces: ☺☺ → *and*; ☺☹ → *but*.
2. Ask the CCQs.
3. Hold up two objects: *"A pen or a pencil?"* Teach *or* for choices and negatives.
4. Ask *"Why do you like Saturdays?"* Board a student's answer with *because*. Show *because* + subject + verb.
5. **Pronunciation.** Drill *tea or coffee? · rice and curry · because it's fun*.

### Stage 3: Controlled practice (10 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To practise the linking words accurately. **Interaction:** Individual, then pairs, then whole class

1. Give out Handout 2. Say: *"Exercise A: write and, but, or, because. Exercise B: join the sentences. Seven minutes."*
   - ICQs: *"I like dogs ___ I don't like cats. Which word?"* (*but*.)
2. Pairs check. Check as a class.

### Stage 4: Freer practice (18 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To use the linking words to talk about real likes and dislikes. **Interaction:** Individual, mingle, whole class

1. **Survey (8 minutes).** Give out Handout 3. Students ask three classmates: *"Do you like cricket or football?" "I like cricket, but I don't like football." "Why?" "Because it's exciting."* They note answers.
   - ICQs: *"How many people do you ask?"* (Three.)
2. **Write (6 minutes).** Students write four sentences about one classmate using each linking word.
3. **Report (4 minutes).** Students read one sentence to the class: *"Kasun likes cricket and football, but he doesn't like swimming because the water is cold."*
4. Monitor and note errors.

### Stage 5: Feedback and wrap-up (7 minutes)

**Stage aim:** To give feedback and consolidate. **Interaction:** Whole class

1. Board four sentences from your notes (two with errors). Students correct them.
2. Quick fire: say two sentences; students join them with the right word.
3. Set the homework.

### Stage 6: Homework

**Set at the end of the lesson.**

Write an email to a pen friend (60–80 words) about what you like and don't like. Use *and, but, or* and *because* at least twice each.

## Differentiation

**Support**

- Give weaker students the face symbols with each word.
- Give sentence frames: *I like ... and ... · I like ..., but I don't like ... · ... because ...*

**Stretch**

- Stronger students use *so* for results: *It was raining, so I stayed at home.*
- They use *both ... and*: *I like both tea and coffee.*

## Assessment and success criteria

- 10 out of 12 or better on Handout 2.
- Students use *but* for contrast and *because* + subject + verb.
- In writing, *because* clauses are joined to the main sentence.

## Teacher's reflection

- Did the faces help students choose *and* or *but*?
- Did students use *because* naturally in the survey?

**Common errors to watch for**

| **Error** | **Correction** |
| :-- | :-- |
| I like tea and I don't like milk. | I like tea, but I don't like milk. |
| I like the beach. Because it is near. | I like the beach because it is near. |
| I'm happy because Friday. | I'm happy because it's Friday. |
| I don't like tea and coffee. | I don't like tea or coffee. |
