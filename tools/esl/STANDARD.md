# The RCF English International Resource Standard

Version 1, 22 September 2026. Every international ESL lesson plan, worksheet
and activity page under `global-english/esl/` meets this standard before it
is published. A page that does not meet it is not published.

It was drawn up from a survey of how the recognised authorities and the
largest international resource sites structure their material. What each
source contributed is recorded at the end, so the standard can be checked and
revised against them.

---

## 1. Levels: the CEFR

Every plan and worksheet is set at a level of the Common European Framework
of Reference for Languages (Council of Europe): **A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2**,
grouped as basic (A), independent (B) and proficient (C). This is the
international common language for level: teachers in every country, every
major publisher and the Cambridge and IELTS scales use it.

- The level is stated on the page, in the title where it fits, and in the
  structured data.
- A page may give a main level and an adaptable one: "A2 (adaptable for
  B1)", with a note on what to change.
- The grammar and vocabulary taught, and the language of every instruction,
  are at or below that level. Instructions a learner cannot read defeat the
  worksheet.
- We write our own can-do aims. We do not copy the Council of Europe's
  descriptors.

## 2. The lesson plan

The parts below are the ones found in every serious lesson-plan format
surveyed: the British Council's TeachingEnglish plans, the Cambridge CELTA
plan, and the major commercial resource sites.

**At a glance** (first, before anything else)
- CEFR level; learners (young learners 7 to 12, teenagers 13 to 17, adults);
  lesson length; skills; language focus; materials; preparation time.
- Lengths: a 60-minute main route, with a 45-minute version noted, because
  lessons around the world run 40 to 90 minutes.

**Aims, written as learner outcomes**
- One main aim and up to two subsidiary aims, each finishing the sentence
  "By the end of the lesson, learners will be able to ...". Aims describe
  what learners can do, never what the teacher will cover (CELTA practice).

**Language analysis** (grammar, vocabulary and function lessons)
- Meaning, with concept-checking questions (CCQs).
- Form: affirmative, negative, question, and short answers where they
  apply.
- Pronunciation: the sounds, weak forms and stress that matter.
- Anticipated problems and a planned solution for each.

**Procedure**
- Staged, with an aim, a time and an interaction pattern for every stage:
  whole class, individual, pairs, groups, mingle.
- A named framework chosen to suit the aim: PPP, test-teach-test, or a task
  cycle. The stages are coloured the same way across the site.
- The teacher's key instructions written out, with instruction-checking
  questions (ICQs) where a task could be misunderstood.
- Controlled practice always comes before freer practice.

**Around the lesson**
- Differentiation: support for weaker learners and stretch for stronger.
- Adaptations: a large class (40 or more), a class with no photocopier, an
  online class, and a different age group.
- Checking learning: how the teacher knows the main aim was met; an exit
  task.
- Homework or follow-up.
- A link to the matching worksheet.

## 3. The worksheet

**Each worksheet is its own web page with the full content on it**, not a
download button. The printable copy is an extra.

- Header: title, CEFR level, time, and lines for name and date.
- Three to five tasks, graded from recognition, to controlled use, to freer
  use.
- Every task has a clear, short instruction in the imperative, and the first
  item done as an example ("0").
- Items are numbered; answer spaces are big enough to write in by hand.
- A teacher's page follows the student's: the answer key, timings, how to
  use it, common mistakes, and an extension.
- It fits on one or two A4 pages, and prints on US Letter too.

**Readability** (British Dyslexia Association style guide)
- A clear sans-serif font, at least 12 point in print and 16 pixels on
  screen; line spacing about 1.5; lines of 60 to 70 characters; text left
  aligned, never justified; headings at least 20 per cent larger than body
  text.
- Nothing depends on colour: every worksheet prints in black and white.

## 4. Content that suits classrooms everywhere

- **Written by RCF English, all of it.** No text, exercise or illustration
  is copied from a coursebook, another website or a published test.
- **Names, places and situations from many countries**, not one. No learner
  should find the material is about someone else's life.
- **Topics safe for any classroom.** The long-standing ELT publishing
  convention is followed: avoid politics, alcohol, religion, sex, drugs,
  "isms" and pork (PARSNIP). A teacher in any country can use the page
  without checking it first.
- **British spelling**, with American alternatives noted where learners
  meet them often (for example "colour/color"). Vocabulary that differs is
  flagged.
- Examples are ordinary daily life, work and study, not stereotypes.

## 5. The page

**Title** says what it is, for whom and at what level, the way teachers
search: "Past Simple ESL Lesson Plan (A2, 60 Minutes)". Never a Sri Lankan
grade name on an international page.

**Opening**: two sentences that tell the visitor what the page is, who it
is for and what they will be able to do with it.

**Structured data**: schema.org `LearningResource`, with the CEFR level as an
`educationalAlignment` (framework "CEFR"), `timeRequired`, `teaches`,
`learningResourceType`, `educationalLevel`, and the audience (teacher or
learner). The data describes only what is on the page.

**Links**: every plan links to its worksheet and its worksheet to its plan,
and each links on to the next topic and back to its hub.

**Accessible**: real headings in order, alternative text for every image,
enough contrast, usable by keyboard. The site's accessibility checks apply.

**Advertising**: ad positions are reserved with the site's labelled
advertisement slot, only between sections: never inside a lesson stage,
never inside a worksheet, never beside a download or a button, and never
more than three on a page. An ad must never be mistaken for part of a lesson.

**Premium**: the free page is always complete enough to teach the lesson
from. Premium adds convenience - editable Word files, slides, and
whole-unit packs - never the part of the lesson that makes it work.

## 6. Quality before quantity

Google's spam policies name "scaled content abuse": many pages made mainly
to rank, adding little value. Sites caught by it lose their search traffic,
and AdSense depends on that traffic. So:

- Every page is written for one real teaching purpose and read through in
  full before it goes live.
- Pages are published in small, finished batches, never as hundreds of
  near-identical pages.
- A page with nothing new to add to what already ranks is not written.
- The date the page was last reviewed is shown.

## 7. The publishing checklist

A page goes live only when every line is true.

- [ ] CEFR level stated; language of the page and instructions at or below it
- [ ] Aims written as learner outcomes
- [ ] Language analysis: meaning with CCQs, form, pronunciation, problems
- [ ] Every stage has an aim, a time and an interaction pattern
- [ ] Controlled practice before freer practice
- [ ] Support, stretch, and large-class, no-photocopier and online adaptations
- [ ] Worksheet: graded tasks, example item, answer key, teacher notes
- [ ] Worksheet readable: font, spacing, line length; works in black and white
- [ ] Original throughout; names and places from many countries; PARSNIP-safe
- [ ] Title, opening and structured data describe the page accurately
- [ ] Plan and worksheet link to each other and to the hub
- [ ] At most three ad slots, between sections only
- [ ] Read through in full; review date set

## Sources surveyed

What each contributed to the standard. Structure only was studied; no
content was taken from any of them.

- **Council of Europe, CEFR and its Companion Volume**: the six levels and
  three broad bands; can-do statements as the way to describe outcomes.
  https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages
- **British Council TeachingEnglish, lesson plans**: level, aims, materials,
  procedure, downloadable worksheet; warm-up through to learner feedback.
  https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/planning-lessons-and-courses/lesson-plans
- **Cambridge CELTA lesson-plan conventions**, as documented by CELTA
  trainers: aims as learner outcomes, stage aims, interaction patterns,
  timing, language analysis (meaning, form, pronunciation), anticipated
  problems and solutions.
  https://eltplanning.com/2016/07/10/how-to-write-celta-lesson-plans/
- **Commercial ESL sites (ESL Brains, Linguahouse)**: separate student and
  teacher versions; warm-up, then controlled, semi-controlled and free
  practice; lengths from 45 to 90 minutes.
  https://eslbrains.com/sample-lessons/ ; https://www.linguahouse.com/esl-lesson-plans
- **British Dyslexia Association Dyslexia Style Guide (2023)**: font, size,
  spacing, line length and alignment for printed and screen material.
  https://cdn.bdadyslexia.org.uk/uploads/documents/Advice/style-guide/BDA-Style-Guide-2023.pdf
- **Google Search Central**: people-first, original content; experience and
  expertise; the scaled-content-abuse policy.
  https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies
