General English

Pronunciation

The goal is not to sound English. The goal is to be understood the first time, without repeating yourself.

  1. Home
  2. General English
  3. Pronunciation

First, about accent

Your accent is not a fault. English is spoken as a first or second language across the world, in a great many accents, and no one of them is the correct one. A Sri Lankan English accent is a legitimate English accent.

What matters is intelligibility — whether a listener understands you without effort. That depends far more on word stress and rhythm than on individual sounds, which is why this page begins with stress.

1. Word stress — the most important thing on this page

Every English word of more than one syllable has one syllable said more strongly than the others. Put the stress in the wrong place and a listener may not recognise the word at all, even if every sound was correct.

PHO-to-graph — pho-TO-gra-pher — pho-to-GRAPH-ic. Same root, three different stresses.

When you learn a new word, mark where the stress falls, and say it aloud with the stress exaggerated. That single habit improves intelligibility more than any other.

Stress changes the word

The same spelling, stressed differently, becomes a different part of speech.
Noun (stress first)Verb (stress second)Example
RE-cordre-CORDI keep a record. / Please record the meeting.
PRE-sentpre-SENTA birthday present. / He will present the report.
IN-creasein-CREASEAn increase in prices. / Prices increase every year.
CON-ductcon-DUCTGood conduct. / She will conduct the class.
OB-jectob-JECTA heavy object. / I object to that.
PER-mitper-MITA travel permit. / They do not permit it.

2. Sentence rhythm

English does not give every word equal weight. The words carrying the meaning are said strongly; the small connecting words are swallowed.

I'm GO-ing to the MAR-ket to buy some VE-ge-ta-bles. The words to, the, some almost disappear.

This is why fast English sounds like one long word to a learner: you are listening for the small words, and they are not there to hear. Listen for the strong ones instead, and the meaning arrives.

It also works the other way. If you say every word with equal weight, your English is understandable but tiring to listen to. Letting the small words weaken makes you sound far more fluent than any change of accent would.

3. Sounds worth practising

These are the distinctions that most often cause a listener to hesitate. Practise each pair aloud, slowly, then in a sentence.

  • /v/ and /w/ — vine and wine, very and wary. For v, the top teeth touch the bottom lip; for w, the lips round and the teeth do not touch.
  • /θ/ and /ð/ — the two th sounds: think, three, both (voiceless) and this, that, mother (voiced). The tongue tip is between the teeth.
  • /p/, /t/, /k/ at the start of a word — these need a small puff of air in English: pen, time, cat. Hold your hand in front of your mouth and feel it.
  • Long and short vowels — ship and sheep, full and fool, bit and beat. The difference is real and it changes the word.
  • Final consonants — English keeps them: want not wan, asked not ask, helped not help. Dropping the ending often removes the tense.
  • Consonant clusters — strengths, asked, texts, twelfths. Practise slowly, keeping every consonant, then speed up.

A ten-minute daily practice

  1. Read aloud for five minutes

    Anything — a newspaper, a page of a book. Reading aloud daily is the single most effective pronunciation exercise there is.

  2. Record yourself once a week

    Read the same short passage each week on your telephone and listen back. You will hear things you cannot hear while speaking.

  3. Shadow a speaker

    Play a short clip of clear English and speak along with it, half a second behind, copying the rhythm rather than the words.

  4. Exaggerate the stress

    When practising alone, overdo the stressed syllable. In real speech it will come out about right.

  5. Slow down

    Speaking slightly slower than feels natural makes you far easier to understand, and gives you time to find your words. Speed is not fluency.
