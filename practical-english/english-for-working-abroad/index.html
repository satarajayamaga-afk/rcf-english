Practical English

English for Working Abroad

Getting a job abroad starts with English, and keeping it depends on English too. Ten practical topics, from your CV to your first payslip.

Start with your CVPractise a job interview
  1. Home
  2. Practical English
  3. English for Working Abroad

About this page

This page helps with the English you need for work abroad. It is not legal, visa or employment advice. Before you accept a job or sign anything, check official guidance, for example from the Sri Lanka Bureau of Foreign Employment, and the rules of the country you are going to.

1. Writing a CV

A CV (curriculum vitae) is a short record of your experience and skills. Employers often look at it for less than a minute, so make it clear and easy to scan.

The usual sections

  1. Contact details: name, phone number, email address.
  2. Personal profile: two or three sentences about who you are and the job you want.
  3. Work experience: most recent job first, with dates and what you did.
  4. Education and training: qualifications and certificates.
  5. Skills: languages, computer skills, driving licence, and so on.

Tips

  • Start each duty with an action verb: managed, prepared, repaired, served, trained.
  • Keep it to one or two pages, and check every spelling.
  • CVs are different in different countries (for example, whether to include a photograph). Check what employers expect where you are applying.

Example lines

Prepared meals for up to 200 guests a day in a busy hotel kitchen.

Maintained and repaired air-conditioning units in office buildings.

2. Writing a cover letter or email

A cover letter introduces your CV. Keep it short: say which job you want, why you are suitable, and that you are available for an interview.

Example

Dear Sir or Madam,

I am writing to apply for the position of Hotel Kitchen Assistant advertised on your website.

I have three years' experience in a busy hotel kitchen in Galle, where I prepared food, kept the kitchen clean and followed food safety rules. I work well in a team and I am used to working long shifts.

I have attached my CV, and I would welcome the opportunity to attend an interview.

Yours faithfully,

Chaminda Rathnayake

The name and details are invented for this example.

3. The job interview

Most interviews use similar questions. Prepare an answer to each, and practise saying it aloud, not just reading it.

QuestionA good answer includes
Tell me about yourself.Your experience and skills in two or three sentences, linked to this job.
Why do you want this job?What interests you about the work, not only the salary.
What experience do you have?One or two real examples of what you did.
What are your strengths?A strength with a short example that proves it.
Tell me about a problem you solved at work.The situation, what you did, and the result.
Do you have any questions?Always ask one: for example, about training or working hours.

Practise out loud

The Conversation Partner has a job interview you can answer with your voice, and Speaking Practice lets you practise introducing yourself.

4. Your first days at work

In the first week, people will not expect you to know everything, but they will expect you to ask.

Say thisWhen
Hello, I'm Kamal. I've just started in the stores department.Introducing yourself
What time do I start and finish?Asking about hours
Where should I put my things?Asking about the workplace
Who should I ask if I have a problem?Finding your supervisor
Could you show me how this works, please?Learning a task

5. Understanding and checking instructions

Doing a task wrongly because you were too shy to ask can cost money or cause accidents. Always check instructions you are not sure about. Repeating them back is the best way.

Say thisWhy
Sorry, could you say that again more slowly?You did not catch it
Just to check: you want me to clean the rooms on floor two first?Repeating back
What does urgent mean here? Today, or right now?Checking a word
Could you write that down for me, please?For numbers, names and times
I've finished. Is this correct?Checking your work

6. Safety signs and warnings

Safety signs use short words that you must understand immediately. Learn these before your first day.

Sign or warningMeaning
DANGERSerious risk of injury or death
CAUTION / WARNINGBe careful: there is a risk
WET FLOORThe floor is slippery
NO ENTRY / AUTHORISED PERSONNEL ONLYDo not go in unless you have permission
FIRE EXIT / EMERGENCY EXITThe way out in an emergency
WEAR PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENTWear the gloves, helmet, glasses or boots provided
HIGH VOLTAGEDanger of electric shock
FIRST AIDWhere to find help and medical supplies

Report a problem at once

If something is unsafe, say so immediately: Excuse me, this machine isn't working properly. I think it's dangerous. You are never wrong to report a safety problem.

7. Telephone calls and work messages

When you are ill or late, tell your supervisor as early as possible, clearly and politely.

Calling in sick

Good morning, this is Sunil from the night shift. I'm sorry, but I'm ill today and I can't come to work. I'll let you know how I am tomorrow.

A short message about being late

Hello Ms Lee, my bus has broken down. I'm sorry, I will be about 30 minutes late. Sunil

The names are invented for these examples.

8. Serving customers and handling complaints

In hotels, shops, restaurants and care work, your English is part of the service. When a customer complains, follow four steps: listen, apologise, solve, thank.

StepUseful phrases
GreetGood afternoon. How can I help you?
ListenI understand. Could you tell me exactly what happened?
ApologiseI'm very sorry about that.
SolveLet me fix that for you right away. / I'll ask my manager.
ThankThank you for letting us know.

9. Working with colleagues

Friendly, polite English makes working together easier, especially in teams where people come from many countries.

Say thisWhen
Could you give me a hand with this?Asking for help
No problem, I'll do it now.Agreeing to help
Sorry, I can't right now. I'm finishing this job.Saying no politely
Would it be possible to take Friday off?Asking for time off
Thanks for your help today.Thanking a colleague

10. Words in your contract and payslip

Never sign a contract you do not fully understand. Ask for a copy, take your time, and ask someone you trust to read it with you.

WordMeaning
salary / wagethe money you are paid, often monthly (salary) or hourly (wage)
basic payyour pay before extra money is added
overtimeextra hours worked beyond your normal hours
shifta set period of work, such as a night shift
deductionsmoney taken from your pay, for example for tax or accommodation
annual leavepaid holiday days each year
probation periodthe first weeks or months, when your work is checked
notice periodhow long before leaving you must tell your employer
