IELTS

IELTS Test Types

There is more than one IELTS test, and preparing for the wrong one is the costliest mistake a candidate can make. Find the one your university, employer or visa asks for, then practise that test.

Academic Practice Test 1General Training Practice Test 1Quick practice

  4. IELTS Test Types

Check the official requirements

RCF English is not connected with IELTS, the British Council, IDP: IELTS Australia or Cambridge University Press & Assessment. Test formats, fees, dates and the tests accepted for a particular purpose can change. Before you book, confirm which test you need with the organisation that asked for it, and check the details on ielts.org.

The IELTS tests at a glance

TestUsually needed forWhat it tests
IELTS AcademicUniversity study at undergraduate or postgraduate level, and professional registration (for example, some medical and nursing bodies)Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. Reading and Writing use academic-style material.
IELTS General TrainingWork, training, secondary education and migration to some English-speaking countriesListening, Reading, Writing and Speaking. Reading and Writing use everyday and workplace material.
IELTS for UKVI (Academic or General Training)Some UK visa and immigration applications that require a Secure English Language TestThe same content and format as Academic or General Training, taken at an approved UKVI test centre.
IELTS Life Skills (A1, A2, B1)Some UK visa applications, such as certain family, settlement and citizenship routesSpeaking and Listening only, at one level, with a pass or fail result.

Academic and General Training: what is the same and what is different

The two tests share Listening and Speaking. The difference is in Reading and Writing.
PaperIELTS AcademicIELTS General Training
Listening30 minutes, 4 parts, 40 questions. The same for both.The same as Academic
Reading60 minutes, 3 long passages from books, journals and magazines, 40 questions60 minutes, 40 questions in 3 sections: everyday texts such as notices and advertisements, workplace texts, and one longer general-interest passage
Writing Task 1Describe a graph, table, chart, map or process in at least 150 wordsWrite a letter (formal, semi-formal or personal) in at least 150 words
Writing Task 2An essay in at least 250 words on an academic-style topicAn essay in at least 250 words on a topic of general interest
Speaking11 to 14 minutes, face to face with an examiner, 3 parts. The same for both.The same as Academic

IELTS for UKVI

IELTS for UKVI is not a different kind of English test. It is the Academic or General Training test with the same questions, format and marking, taken at a test centre approved by UK Visas and Immigration, under extra identity and security checks, so that the result can be used for certain UK visa applications.

Prepare for it exactly as you would for Academic or General Training. What matters is booking the UKVI version when your visa route asks for a Secure English Language Test; an ordinary IELTS result may not be accepted for that purpose.

IELTS Life Skills

IELTS Life Skills tests only Speaking and Listening, at one level of the Common European Framework: A1, A2 or B1. It is taken with one examiner and one other candidate, and the result is simply pass or fail.

It is used for some UK visa routes where the Home Office asks for a particular level. The tasks are about everyday life: talking about yourself, your family and your routine, making arrangements, and understanding short spoken information.

Practise speaking in full sentences about familiar topics, asking the other candidate questions, and listening for specific details such as times, places and prices. The IELTS Speaking and IELTS Listening pages on this site will help, as will Everyday English.

Paper, computer and One Skill Retake

Paper-based and computer-delivered. Academic and General Training can be taken on paper or on a computer at a test centre. The questions and the marking are the same. On computer, Listening gives a short time to check answers instead of ten minutes to transfer them to an answer sheet. The Speaking test is always a live conversation with an examiner.

IELTS Online. In some places an online version can be taken at home under remote supervision. Check whether the organisation you are applying to accepts it before choosing it.

One Skill Retake. In some locations, candidates who took the computer-delivered test can retake one of the four skills, within a set period, instead of the whole test. Availability depends on the country and test centre, and not every organisation accepts a retaken result, so check both before relying on it.
