A new A/L question every MondayQuestion of the Week
A/L General EnglishLetter to the editorWeek of 5 October 2026

Plan a letter about roadside litter

Litter along the roads in your town has become a serious problem. Write a letter to the editor of a newspaper (about 200 words) describing the problem, its effects, and two practical solutions.

For this week, write a plan and an opening paragraph only.

Show the model answer

Plan

Paragraph 1: reason for writing; the problem in one sentence (roadside litter along the main road and near the bus stand).

Paragraph 2: the effects: blocked drains and flooding after rain, mosquitoes, a poor impression on visitors.

Paragraph 3: solution 1: covered bins at bus halts, emptied on a fixed schedule by the council.

Paragraph 4: solution 2: school and community clean-up days, with fines for dumping enforced.

Paragraph 5: closing appeal to readers and the authorities.

Opening paragraph

Sir, I write to draw attention to the litter that now lines the main road through our town, particularly near the bus stand and the market. Plastic bags, food wrappers and bottles are thrown from vehicles and left by passers-by, and the problem has grown steadily worse over the past year.

How it would be marked

Format: a letter to the editor opens with Sir or Dear Editor and ends with the writer's name and town. The writer's address and date appear at the top.

Planning earns its time: a five-paragraph plan guarantees that the problem, the effects and two solutions are all covered. Letters written without a plan often give one solution only.

Tone: formal and reasonable. Complaints that insult the council lose the reader's sympathy.

Opening: state the purpose in the first sentence. I am writing this letter to tell you about something delays the point.

A/L LiteratureUnseen poetryWeek of 28 September 2026

An unseen poem: 'Rain on the Tin Roof'

Rain on the Tin Roof (an unseen poem written for this question by RCF English)

At first a knock, polite and small,

a stranger testing at the door;

then fists, then drums, then no pause at all,

the whole sky falling on the floor.

My mother lifts her head and smiles.

The paddy drinks. The well will rise.

Tonight the noise is worth the miles

of dry and dust-white August skies.

How does the poet present the arrival of the rain, and how does the mood change between the two stanzas? Refer closely to language and technique.

Show the model answer

The poet presents the rain's arrival as a growing visitor. In the first stanza it is personified as a stranger who knocks polite and small, suggesting caution and unfamiliarity after a long dry season. The sequence then fists, then drums, then no pause at all builds through repetition and rising force, and the listing imitates the quickening sound on the roof until the hyperbole the whole sky falling on the floor makes the rain overwhelming.

In the second stanza the mood turns from noise and near-alarm to relief. The short, calm sentences The paddy drinks. The well will rise. slow the rhythm and show the practical value of the rain for a farming household. The mother's smile turns the sound into good news. The final lines weigh the noise against miles / of dry and dust-white August skies: the long vowels and the image of dust suggest weeks of hardship, so the rain becomes welcome rather than threatening.

How it would be marked

Credit for: identifying a technique (personification, repetition, hyperbole, sentence length, imagery), quoting it briefly, and explaining its effect. Naming a device without explaining it earns little.

Answer the whole question: both how the rain is presented and how the mood changes. Many answers do the first well and forget the second.

Structure: one paragraph per stanza works well here, with a sentence linking them.

Common mistake: retelling the poem (First the rain comes, then the mother smiles). Every sentence should say how or why, not only what.

A/L General EnglishError correctionWeek of 21 September 2026

Find and correct the error

Each sentence contains one error. Rewrite the sentence correctly.

1. Neither the manager nor his assistants was present at the meeting.

2. She has been working in this company since five years.

3. If I would have known about the delay, I would have taken a taxi.

4. The informations given in the leaflet are out of date.

5. He is one of those students who always tries to help others.

Show the model answer

1. Neither the manager nor his assistants were present at the meeting.

2. She has been working in this company for five years.

3. If I had known about the delay, I would have taken a taxi.

4. The information given in the leaflet is out of date.

5. He is one of those students who always try to help others.

How it would be marked

1. With neither...nor, the verb agrees with the nearer subject: assistants is plural.

2. Since takes a point in time (since 2021); for takes a length of time (for five years).

3. The third conditional uses had + past participle in the if clause, never would have.

4. Information is uncountable: no plural -s, and a singular verb.

5. Who refers to those students, so its verb is plural. Many careful writers still get this wrong, which is why it is often tested.

Marking notes written by RCF English to explain what earns credit. They are not an official marking scheme.

A/L General EnglishSummary writingWeek of 14 September 2026

Shorten a paragraph without losing its meaning

Many young people today believe that success depends mainly on talent, and that those who are not born with a gift for a subject should simply give it up. However, research on learning suggests a different picture. People who improve steadily are usually those who practise regularly, look carefully at their mistakes, and ask for advice from others. Talent may help a person to begin quickly, but it rarely carries anyone very far without effort. In fact, students who believe that ability can grow tend to keep trying when work becomes difficult, while those who believe it is fixed are more likely to stop.

Summarise the paragraph above in about 40 words, using your own words as far as possible.

Show the model answer

Although many young people think success comes mainly from natural talent, steady improvement usually results from regular practice, learning from mistakes and seeking advice. Talent only gives an early start, and students who believe ability can develop persevere longer than those who think it is fixed. (45 words)

How it would be marked

Main points to include: (1) the common belief that talent decides success; (2) improvement comes from practice, studying mistakes and asking advice; (3) talent helps only at the start; (4) believing ability can grow makes students persist.

Own words: change the wording, not the meaning. Seeking advice for ask for advice from others shows understanding; copying whole phrases does not.

Leave out: examples, repetition and phrases such as In fact or research suggests a different picture that add no new point.

Common mistake: adding an opinion (This is true because...). A summary reports the writer's ideas only.
