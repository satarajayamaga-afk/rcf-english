O/L LiteratureContext question techniqueWeek of 5 October 2026

Answering 'Who said this, to whom, and when?'

An extract written for practice by RCF English:

"Keep the change," said the old man, pressing the coin back into the boy's hand. "You carried my bag all the way from the market. That is worth more than a bus fare."

Imagine this extract is from a short story you have studied.

(a) Who is speaking, and to whom?

(b) What has just happened?

(c) What does this speech show about the speaker's character?

Show the model answer

(a) The old man is speaking to the boy.

(b) The boy has carried the old man's bag from the market and has tried to return a coin, which the old man presses back into his hand.

(c) The old man is grateful and generous. He values the boy's effort more than money, saying that the help is worth more than a bus fare, and he insists on rewarding him rather than accepting the coin back.

How it would be marked

Parts (a) and (b) want precise facts: names (or clear descriptions), and the situation immediately before the words. Long background retelling wastes time.

Part (c) carries the most credit. Give a quality (generous, grateful), then evidence from the extract, then a brief explanation.

In the examination, use the characters' names and the situation from your prescribed text. This practice extract is original so that the technique can be shown without reproducing any set text.

Marking notes written by RCF English to explain what earns credit. They are not an official marking scheme.

O/L EnglishReading comprehensionWeek of 28 September 2026

The library under the stairs

When Ms Jayawardena arrived at the village school, the only books were locked in a cupboard in the principal's office. Children were not allowed to touch them in case they were damaged. She asked for permission to move them to the empty space under the staircase, and the principal agreed, although he did not expect the idea to last.

With the help of a carpenter who was also a parent, she built three low shelves. Students from Grade 9 made a cardboard sign and a simple borrowing book. Within a month, the younger children were spending their interval there, and some parents began donating old newspapers and magazines.

By the end of the year, a few books had been torn, but far more had been read. The principal, who had once worried about damage, asked her to write a request to the zonal office for more books.

1. Where were the books kept before Ms Jayawardena arrived?

2. Why were children not allowed to touch them?

3. Who helped to build the shelves?

4. Find a word in paragraph 2 that means giving as a gift.

5. How did the principal's attitude change? Give evidence from the passage.

Show the model answer

1. In a locked cupboard in the principal's office.

2. Because the school was afraid they would be damaged.

3. A carpenter who was also a parent (and Grade 9 students made the sign and borrowing book).

4. donating

5. At first he did not expect the library to last and worried about damage, but by the end of the year he supported it and asked her to request more books from the zonal office.

How it would be marked

Questions 1 to 3 test finding information. Short, exact answers are best; copying the whole paragraph is not.

Question 4: the word must be in the stated paragraph and in the same form. Donate would lose credit if the passage uses donating.

Question 5 is worth more because it asks for two things: the change (before and after) and evidence. Answers that describe only the end lose half the credit.

O/L EnglishGrammar: the passive voiceWeek of 21 September 2026

Change these sentences into the passive

Rewrite each sentence in the passive voice. Keep the same tense.

1. The prefect rings the bell at 7.30 every morning.

2. The students painted the classroom walls last week.

3. The principal will open the new library tomorrow.

4. Someone has stolen my bicycle.

5. The teachers are checking the answer scripts now.

Show the model answer

1. The bell is rung (by the prefect) at 7.30 every morning.

2. The classroom walls were painted by the students last week.

3. The new library will be opened by the principal tomorrow.

4. My bicycle has been stolen.

5. The answer scripts are being checked (by the teachers) now.

How it would be marked

The rule: object of the active sentence + the correct form of be in the same tense + past participle.

Tense of be: simple present is/are; simple past was/were; future will be; present perfect has/have been; present continuous is/are being.

When to leave out 'by': when the doer is unknown or obvious, as in sentence 4. My bicycle has been stolen by someone is not wrong, but it is weak.

Common mistakes: The bell is ring (the past participle is rung); The walls was painted (plural subject needs were).

Marking notes written by RCF English to explain what earns credit. They are not an official marking scheme.

O/L EnglishWriting a noticeWeek of 14 September 2026

A notice for a tree-planting day

Your school Environment Society is organising a tree-planting day. As its secretary, write a notice of about 50 words for the school notice board.

Include: the purpose, the date, time and place, what students should bring, and whom to contact.

Show the model answer

NOTICE

Environment Society, [School name]

Tree-Planting Day

Help us make our school greener! The Environment Society will plant fifty saplings along the school boundary on Saturday, 3rd October, from 8.00 a.m. to 11.00 a.m. Please meet at the main gate. Bring a water bottle, a cap and a small garden tool if you have one.

Contact: N. Perera (Grade 11A)

N. Perera, Secretary, Environment Society — 21st September

How it would be marked

Content: credit goes to each required point that is clearly given: purpose, date, time, place, what to bring, contact. A missing point costs more than a spelling mistake.

Format: a notice needs the word NOTICE, the name of the organisation, a clear heading, the writer's name and position, and the date of issue.

Length: about 50 words means roughly 40 to 60 in the body. A notice that becomes a letter (Dear students, I am writing to...) is the commonest format error.

Language: short, direct sentences. An imperative such as Bring a water bottle is correct and appropriate here.

Marking notes written by RCF English to explain what earns credit. They are not an official marking scheme.
