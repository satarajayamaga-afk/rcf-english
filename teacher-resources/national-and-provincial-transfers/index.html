Moving Between Provincial and National Schools

The two systems are run by different authorities, which is why this is not an ordinary transfer. What follows is what the official sources actually say - and where they say nothing.

  • Transfers
  • Official and service matters
  • Sri Lanka Teachers' Service
  • All 25 districts

  1. Home
  2. Teacher Resources
  3. Moving Between Provincial and National Schools

Read this before you plan anything around it

This page is a guide to the official sources, not an authority in itself. RCF English cannot transfer anyone, and nothing here overrides a circular or an instruction from your Zonal or Provincial Director.

Everything below was checked on 5 October 2026 against the Ministry of Education and provincial ministry websites. Transfer rules change with each circular, so confirm the current position before you act on it.

Get the answer in writing. A verbal assurance from any office is worth nothing if a later officer disagrees with it.

Why this is not an ordinary transfer

A transfer within one system moves you between schools under the same authority. Moving between a provincial school and a national school does not do that - it crosses from one appointing authority to another, and that is a different kind of move altogether.

National schools are administered directly by the Ministry of Education. Provincial schools are administered by the Provincial Ministry or Department of Education, and provincial transfers are recommended to the Provincial Public Service Commission.

So a move between them is not simply a longer transfer. One authority has to release you and the other has to take you on. That is why you will not find a single form for it in the place you would expect, and why teachers lose months looking for one.

Who runs which system

What the official sources do say

Three official sources carry the published detail. None of them is about moving between the two systems, which is the point - but each tells you something you need.

The National School Teacher Transfer portal

This handles transfers within national schools. Applications are made online, and the portal states that no amendment is possible once an application is submitted - so everything is checked before it goes.

It also sets a compulsory threshold: a teacher must apply where the service period at the current school reaches eight years or more by the end of the year in question.

The round is annual. The 2026 window closed on 30 July 2026, so if you are reading this afterwards, watch the portal for the next call rather than assuming it is still open.

The portal publishes notices and instructions in Sinhala, Tamil and English, transfer criteria, medical certificate formats and the national schools list. It carries nothing about provincial schools.

The provincial route

Provincial ministries publish the chain an application travels: teacher, then principal, then Zonal Director of Education, then Provincial Director of Education, then the Provincial Ministry, which recommends to the Provincial Public Service Commission.

The Southern Provincial Ministry lists these documents for an inter-provincial transfer: the application, a certified copy of the letter of appointment, the original regarding audit queries and disciplinary action, and a certified copy of the letter of service confirmation.

That list is for transfers between provinces. Treat it as a guide to the kind of paperwork your own province will want, not as the list for a move to a national school - and confirm it with your own Provincial Ministry, since provinces differ.

The Ministry's circulars index

The Ministry publishes its circulars, with Sinhala, Tamil and English versions. As checked on 5 October 2026, the transfer circulars listed there cover the Sri Lanka Teacher Educators' Service and the Sri Lanka Education Administrative Service, and there are separate circulars for the Principal Service.

A National Teacher Transfer Policy exists and has been reported as intended to balance teacher distribution between national and provincial schools. If movement between the two is opened to you, it will be under an instrument of that kind - which is why the circulars index is the page worth watching.

What is not published - said plainly

As at 5 October 2026, no circular on the Ministry's own index sets out a procedure for a teacher moving between a national school and a provincial school in either direction. The national portal covers national schools; the provincial pages cover provinces.

That absence is worth knowing, because the usual advice online fills it in with detail from news sites and teachers' groups. Some of that may be accurate. None of it is authority, and acting on it can cost you a transfer cycle - a year.

If someone shows you a document that does set out this procedure, it will have a circular number and a date. Ask for those two things. Anything without them is hearsay.

What to do instead

Since there is no published route, the practical approach is to put the question to the people who must answer it, in a form they have to answer in writing.

  1. Start with your own chain of command

    Your principal, then your Zonal Director of Education. They handle transfers constantly and will know whether anyone in your zone has made this move recently, and under what instrument.

    Going around them does not work and does not help you: a transfer file travels through these officers whatever route it eventually takes.

  2. Ask in writing, and ask a precise question

    Not can I move to a national school, which invites a shrug. Ask: under which circular may a teacher of the Sri Lanka Teachers' Service attached to a provincial school be released to a national school, and which authority approves it?

    A question naming a circular has to be answered with a circular, or with an admission that there is not one. Either answer is useful.

  3. Put the same question to the Teacher Transfer Unit

    The Ministry's transfer office is the other half of the answer, because it is the receiving authority. The National School Teacher Transfer portal published the numbers 011 278 4434 and 011 207 5854 as at 5 October 2026.

    Ask them for the circular number. Write down who you spoke to and when.

  4. Watch the circulars index, not social media

    If this route opens properly, it will appear as a numbered circular on the Ministry's own circulars page, in all three languages. That page is the first place it will be true.

    Teachers' groups will have it first and will have it slightly wrong. Use them to know that something has happened, then go and read the circular.

  5. Keep every document certified and ready

    Whatever route opens, the paperwork will be the same family: letter of appointment, service confirmation, and a clear record on audit queries and disciplinary action.

    Getting certified copies takes time you will not have once a window opens. Hold them ready.
