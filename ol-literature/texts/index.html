O/L Literature

Prescribed Texts

Each text has its own page, arranged in the same fifteen sections, so that everything belonging to that poem, story, play or novel stays in one place.

  1. Home
  2. O/L Literature
  3. Prescribed Texts

Prescribed texts change

The list of prescribed texts is set by the examination authority and is revised from time to time. Always check the current official syllabus for your own examination year. Do not prepare a text simply because a website, a guide or an older sibling's notes include it.

Texts on RCF English

Texts appear here as their pages are written and checked. Each one follows the fifteen-section structure described below.

Texts are added here as they are prepared. Each text is set out in the same fifteen sections described above, so you always know where to look. Nothing is published for a text until the material has been written and checked, and no copyrighted text is reproduced in full.

What every text page contains

  1. Introduction

    What kind of text this is and what to watch for as you read.

  2. Author or playwright

    Who wrote it. Only what is known and verifiable — nothing is invented.

  3. Background

    When and where it was written, and any context a reader needs in order to make sense of it.

  4. Summary or plot

    What happens, in order, without interpretation mixed in.

  5. Characters

    Who each character is, what they want, how they change, and how they relate to the others.

  6. Themes

    The ideas about life the text explores, each with the moments in the text that carry it.

  7. Literary or dramatic techniques

    Imagery, symbolism, irony, structure, stagecraft — each named, explained, and tied to its effect.

  8. Important quotations

    Short and accurate, each with a note on which questions it can be used for.

  9. Context questions

    Short questions on printed extracts, of the kind the paper asks.

  10. Concise context answers

    Model answers of the right length — short, because context answers should be short.

  11. Essay questions

    Full questions on the text, of the kind the paper asks.

  12. Developed model answers

    Full essay answers with notes explaining how each is built.

  13. Common student mistakes

    The errors students actually make on this particular text.

  14. Revision exercises

    Activities to check what has stayed, rather than what was merely read.

  15. Related past-paper questions

    Where this text has been examined, so you can see the range of questions asked.

What is and is not reproduced

  • Not reproduced: complete copyrighted poems, stories, plays or novels, or large parts of them. You will need your prescribed book.
  • Reproduced: short quotations, used for study and criticism, with the author and work named.
  • Original work by RCF English: summaries, character notes, themes, analysis, questions, model answers and revision exercises.
  • Nothing on a text page is invented. Where a fact about an author or a text is not certain, it is not stated. See the Copyright and Resource Policy.
